Emeric didn't call for anyone.

Word had a way of finding him anyway, even through walls he'd told everyone not to cross. A plate left outside his door, untouched. A message from Bram, relayed through someone else, asking if he wanted to talk. A knock from Ward that lasted exactly as long as good judgment allowed and then stopped. Emeric had asked not to be disturbed unless something outrageously urgent required him.

His hand found the crystal before his mind decided to look for it, the same habit that had carried it through a pocket for a week straight. Mother's crystal, though he still hadn't found the nerve to say that word about it out loud to anyone. He turned it over once, twice, not thinking, just needing something in his hand that wasn't the memory of Tam's name on a duty roster he'd approved without reading twice.

The crystal warmed. He let it.

He hadn't meant to delve. He wasn't chasing the loop he'd found last week or the branch before that, wasn't trying to be useful. He just wanted the noise in his head to have somewhere else to go, and the workspace had always been good at that, structure where a moment ago there'd been none, the closest thing to quiet his brain still seemed able to produce.

He drifted back to the loop anyway, not on purpose. Whatever passed for hands here kept circling the same structure the way a tongue keeps finding a broken tooth, and somewhere in the third or fourth pass he noticed something that hadn't been there before, or that he hadn't been steady enough to see. The loop wasn't resetting. Every time he circled back through it, some piece of what he'd touched the last pass was still there, waiting, unchanged, holding its shape whether or not he was holding it.

Data that persisted without him watching it. Not a branch. Not the loop itself. Something underneath both, something that just sat there and stayed true whether or not anyone was looking.

He didn't have a name for it yet. He filed it anyway, out of habit, the same reflex that sorted ledgers and mapped wells, because it was easier to file something new than sit with the thing he actually didn't want to look at.

It didn't work. Not really. He surfaced an hour later no lighter than he'd gone in, Tam's name sitting exactly where he'd left it. Except now there was a second thing sitting next to it that hadn't been there before, something his mother's crystal had just handed him without him earning it the usual way.

He wasn't sure that felt like winning either.

Emeric found Ward before Ward could find a reason not to be found.

He was at the practice yard, alone, running drills nobody had asked him to run, well past the hour any sane man would call it a night. His forearm, still healing from the road, wasn't wrapped the way it should have been. He didn't stop when Emeric came into view. He finished the pass first, like discipline was the only thing left he trusted to hold.

"You've taken every shift since," Emeric said. Not a question.

"Someone has to."

"That's not what I asked."

Ward set the practice blade down with more care than it needed. "I built the rotation. I put him on that door." His voice stayed level the way it always did, which was somehow worse than if it hadn't. "You don't have to say anything about whose fault it was. I already know where I've put it."

"It wasn't yours to put anywhere." Emeric heard how thin that sounded the second it left his mouth, and he wasn't people-sharp enough to fix it in the moment, only sharp enough to know it had landed wrong. "That's not- I mean it. But I know saying it doesn't move it."

Ward almost smiled, the kind that didn't reach anywhere near actual humor. "No. It doesn't."

Neither of them said anything else for a while. Emeric didn't leave, and Ward didn't ask him to, and eventually that was the whole conversation, two men standing in a cold practice yard because leaving first felt like admitting something neither of them wanted to admit yet. It wasn't fixed. Emeric was starting to understand that some things didn't get fixed so much as carried differently by more than one person at once.

Across town, Ferra had the pages spread across the whole table by the time he made it to Bram's, corners weighted down with whatever had been in reach, a hammer, a cup, a folded rag. She didn't look up right away.

"Three more pages touched, not just the one," she said. "Same hand doing the covering, different hand underneath. Whoever wrote the original entries wasn't trying to hide anything. Whoever came back later was."

"The merchant house name. What was actually there."

"Wasn't a name at all originally. A route." She turned the page toward him, angled it into the light. Beneath the scraped ink, fainter, the ghost of a different hand: a waypoint marker, the kind used for dispatch riders, not merchants. "This wasn't a debt owed to a house. It was a debt owed to whoever ran that route." She let that sit a second. "Cobb runs that route."

It wasn't the baron's name. It still wasn't the baron's name. But it was Petram's word and Ferra's ledger agreeing with each other without either of them knowing the other existed, and that was worth more than either piece alone.

"Not enough yet," Ferra said, reading his face before he said it himself. "Enough to know where to point next."

"That's more than I had walking in."

She almost smiled at that, the same way Bram did when a piece came out of the forge closer to right than he'd expected. "Everything's more than you had walking in, lately."

Word from the capital road reached Kaldo two days later, unrelated on its face: Baron Wexler had petitioned for an audience with the crown's judiciary, unprompted, over a matter he described only as "irregularities requiring clarification, before misunderstanding hardens into accusation."

Nobody had accused him of anything yet. Not on paper. Not anywhere he could point to.

Emeric read the report twice, the way he'd read the discharged debt's date twice, hoping it would resolve into something less certain the second time. It didn't.

He knows. Not the specifics, not Cobb's name, not the route, not Ferra's careful work under the scraped ink. Just enough to know something was moving toward him, close enough now to feel the draft off it. And instead of striking again, instead of reaching for whatever had reached Tam, he'd gone the other direction entirely, toward the one body in the kingdom that could still, on paper, decide he'd done nothing wrong at all.

A man that calculating didn't run toward the crown because he was innocent.

He ran toward it because he'd already started deciding how to survive being guilty.
