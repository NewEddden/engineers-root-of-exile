Chapter 13 - A Way to See It

The walk to Bram's took longer than it had to, mostly because Emeric kept stopping to look at things that a week ago wouldn't have looked like anything. A cart stacked with lumber for the new grain store. Two farmhands arguing without heat over whose row had broken easier. The barony had the feel of a place waking up rather than a place being managed, and he let himself notice it instead of hurrying past the way he might have a month back.

Ferra was already at the table when he got there, one ledger open in front of her and a second stacked beside it, waiting its turn. She didn't look up right off. She had the settled focus of someone who'd learned that finishing a column mattered more than acknowledging whoever had come in mid-count.

"Wages for the farm crews," Emeric said, greeting and business in the one breath. "I want it running through you, starting this week. Same terms as everything else."

That got her. She closed the ledger with two fingers marking her place. "The whole payroll, or just the tracking?"

"Whole thing. You already know what everyone's owed better than I do. I ran your numbers against my own last week and gave up trying to catch you out."

Something crossed her face that stopped short of a smile. "Nobody's ever said that part out loud before." She reached for a blank page and started a fresh column without another word, which he'd learned by now sat closer to agreement than any actual yes ever got from her.

"There's one more thing," he added, before she could vanish back into the figures. "The head count from the farm meeting doesn't match the official ledger. Not by a little."

She looked up. It was the look she got when she was already running the possibilities before the sentence finished. "Old rot, or new."

"That's what I'm asking you to find out."

"I'll pull the last five years of tithe records against it." She was already reaching for a clean sheet. "Give me a few days."

Bram came through from the back a few minutes later, sleeves still shoved up, forearms marked with the sort of soot that had stopped washing out entirely somewhere back. He dropped into the chair across from Emeric with the whole weight of a man who'd been at the forge since before the sun. "Furnace plans," he said. "You said you'd have something drawn."

Emeric had the sketches on him, rough and workable, done in the shorthand he fell into when he was thinking faster than his hand could keep up. He talked Bram through it. Coal for now. The Fire Crystal there to supplement the heat, not carry it. The Air Crystal doing a thing Bram had never once heard of, its blast running straight through the molten metal itself instead of only into the firebox. Bram asked good questions, the kind that came from a man who understood metal and wasn't just humoring a prince's hobby. How hot. How long. What happened if the blast ran uneven across the melt.

"And the lake site," Emeric said, once the furnace had settled into a shape Bram looked easy with. "Water wheel to drive the bellows and the hammers both. No crystal for the motion, just the drop and the current doing the work they always have. The magic's only there to hold the heat where it needs to be."

"You want to move the whole operation out there." Bram didn't phrase it as a question.

"Eventually. Not overnight. This place builds what that place needs first. Tools, fittings, the parts small enough to carry out by hand. Once it's standing, everything moves. No shop in town after that."

Bram nodded, slow, turning over what that meant for a life he'd spent entirely inside walking distance of this one forge. "Molds too, you said. For the plow heads."

"One master shape, cast instead of hand-forged every time. Every plow comes out close enough to identical that a broken part off one fits the next, no re-fitting by hand."

"That alone would save me a season." There was real want in it, not just a craftsman's interest.

He studied the furnace drawing longer than Emeric had thought he would, turning the page in his hands as if it might read clearer from another angle. "I believe you," he said at last. "That's not the trouble. The trouble is I'm building half of what you're describing off a page that looks like chicken scratch, and the other half off you talking with your hands. I've built off worse, mind. But I keep thinking there's got to be an easier way to see what's actually in your head before I put three days into it and find out I made the wrong piece."

There wasn't much complaint in it. Under it was the real thing, the frustration of a man who loved the work enough to want it right the first time. "I like the work," he added, like he wanted that part heard clearly. "All of it. The new stuff most of all. I just wish I could see it the way you're seeing it, instead of guessing at your drawing."

Emeric started to answer. Something reasonable. Better sketches, more detail, a second pass over the plans. The words didn't get out.

He was thinking about the delving space, and the way it built the office back around him almost exactly as it stood, solid and walkable, real enough to lay a hand on. He'd never once tried to make something new in there. Only ever read what was already present. But if the space could hold a room that existed, there was no clear reason it couldn't hold one that didn't yet, a thing drawn instead of a thing remembered.

If he could stand the furnace up inside that space, full size, walkable, every crystal and rune sitting exactly where it would sit in the real build, then Bram wouldn't be guessing at a drawing anymore. He'd just look at it.

He didn't notice he'd gone quiet. Didn't notice the room around him pull back and lose its edges the way it did in the half-second before a delve took hold, except he wasn't reaching for the Hollow Crystal at all this time. He was only thinking about it, hard enough that the thinking seemed to be doing the work a rune usually did.

"Emeric."

Bram's voice, from somewhere further off than the far side of the table. Emeric didn't answer. Didn't move.

"Emeric." Sharper now. A chair scraped back. Footsteps.

Ferra glanced up at the shift in Bram's tone, pen still in her hand, not alarmed yet, only gone alert the way she always was.

A hand closed on Emeric's shoulder, meant to shake him, to shake some kind of answer out of a man who'd gone still and glass-eyed at his own kitchen table for reasons Bram had no way at all to make sense of.

The hand didn't stay a hand for long.
