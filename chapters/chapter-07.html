Book 2, Batch 7: Five Knocks

Chapters 50–54 | Wednesday 29 July – Sunday 9 August, Year 17

Ferra's road party has reached the shut gate of the first dwarf hold on the Sea Reach coast, unarmed, out of touch with the fleet, and vouched for by Gadd, the orc guide who was coerced into nearly walking them into an ambush. The fleet waits over the sea below the range, out of the hold's sky, because the war came to the dwarf holds from the air and a hold that sees strange hulls overhead shuts its gate. Runa lies cuffed on the K Endeavor's hospital deck while her vaccine series dries. The hold's mine is in trouble, and its grain debt falls due at the autumn reckoning.

A hold that shuts its gate on humans keeps a ledger that says why, and the ledger is right about the war. Then the mine falls on six of the hold's own people, and the humans the ledger warned about are the only ones at the gate with fans that need no mana and a hull that can bring them before morning. The hold asks for help and hates asking. This batch is about what it costs a proud people to be helped, and what it costs the helpers to work on someone else's terms.

The Murr, the deep people living beneath the hold, stop the dwarves' fast rescue, and they are right to; a young miner dies waiting for their slower way, and the dwarves are not wrong to grieve the lost hours. Imani begins a room heal alone and is saved by something only the three of them can do. Emeric is wrong about how the mine's gas behaves, in front of the people who were right, and says so. And deep beneath the hold, a dozen still pieces of one living thing wait for a body that has not moved in a year.

Chapter 50: The Wicket

At dusk on Wednesday 29 July the wicket opens on a gate warden with a ledger-keeper behind him. Gadd speaks first again. The ledger-keeper wants the party held under the hold's law until the keepers of the Anvil, the greatest of the holds, have been asked about humans. It is the first time that name is spoken. Steinar, a young surveyor off shift who has come up to stare at the strangers, argues that they should be heard: the orc house's letter asks it, and the hold owes the house. The warden splits the difference, the gate hall tonight and the council tomorrow. The hold is called the Spur, Ledra's rendering of its own name, after the spur of the range it is cut into, and Kaldo uses it as given. The gate hall is low, warm cut stone with a heating duct in the wall, and the dwarves bring goats' cheese and a thin ferment. Arthur and the knights sleep in their coats. Ledra tells Ferra what the warden said on his way out: he had never seen a human and thought they would be taller. Ferra rewrites the list of gifts by lamplight.

On Thursday the council sits. There is no Thane here; the Spur has a council with a speaker chosen from its seniors, a contract office for the ore contract and grain debt, inspectors who can stop a shaft for incomplete records, a production guild of foremen and crews paid against output, and ledger-keepers who hold the hold's records. Around the table are the speaker; Ingrith the inspector, whose full audit was refused only last week; Magnar for the production guild; Egil for the house that owns the deep seam; and the ledger-keepers. Ferra presents the house's letter and the Crown's seal through Ledra. The gifts follow. Kaldo's beer is poured and tasted and accepted with faces Ferra will later describe to Emeric, since the dwarves drink their own thin ferment and are quietly horrified at what Kaldo did with the grain. The iron bar is weighed, the glass handled, the lamps lit and examined.

The ledger-keeper reads the Spur's warning entry aloud, the one every hold keeps: humans will come back. Ferra listens to all of it and does not argue, because it is true about the war. Then the ledger-keeper asks whether her king's eyes are gold. Ferra says yes, and nobody speaks for a while. The hold's troubles are laid out: the ore contract, the grain debt at the autumn reckoning, small falls and bad air, Ingrith's refused audit, and Magnar's crews following an approved plan. Ferra offers fans that need no mana. The council's answer is trade at the gate market, nothing human in the galleries, and the sky stays shut. Ferra thanks them and means it.

At the change of shift, about six in the evening, the hall's floor shivers and a bell starts below. A runner comes up. The braced galleries of the deep seam cross from one kind of rock to another, and a length of roof has come down at the change. Harn's crew of six is behind the fall, and a broken drainage gallery above them is letting water in slowly. The council empties down the stair. Ferra stays in the hall with Ledra and Arthur, because guests do not follow. At dusk the speaker returns with Ingrith and asks Kaldo's envoy what her king can bring, and how fast. Ferra answers in a list: fans that need no mana, lamps that will not fire the damp, breathing packs for water, a surgeon, a healer, a man who lifts stone, and a hull to carry them before morning, if the Spur opens its sky once. The ledger-keepers object. The council grants it: one hull, by daylight, on the slag apron below the gate, ports shut and empty. The ledger-keepers enter their objection by name. Rule one of the Charter is unconditional; the sky is not. Ferra writes both down.

Chapter 51: Borehole

That night Arthur runs the hold road down to the sea, about thirty miles in the dark, with no suit and no kit, the council's leave in Ledra's hand and the speaker's seal in his coat. He is angry the whole way about being unarmed and, by the bottom, is not angry about anything. At the road-town's quay he reaches the fleet's boat and its field set, then Ward's voice, then Emeric's.

The K Discovery loads in the dark: six battery fans with their banks charged, hand cranks, dimmed lamps and safety lamps, gill packs, rope, Imani's ward kit and broth. Aboard are Emeric, Imani, Bettany, Bram, Dagny, Finn and two divers, Mira, Neris, two galley hands, and Linnet at the set. Ward holds the K Amara over the sea below the range, in sight and on the set, so the K Discovery is not alone. Grau, Runa and the rest stay with the fleet; werewolves are never let into the holds. The K Discovery lifts before dawn, climbs the range in the grey, and sets down at first light on the slag apron with her ports shut. Imani mends Bren's knee on the ramp before anyone else is off it. Dwarves on the gate stair watch a hull come into their sky. Then they see Bram come down the ramp, a human who looks like one of them, and go quiet.

Ingrith brings the night's news at the gate. Magnar's crew started a rescue shaft from the level above at once, fast and by the book, about two days to the pocket. At midnight the Murr came up to the deep exchange hall where they trade: Durn, an air-keeper, and Uwen, a guide, pale and huge-eared and veiled against the lamps. Uwen's ears turn toward Bram's boots, not toward whoever is speaking. The Murr heard the fall through the rock within minutes. Their warning was that the shaft would cut into the old air route their lower farm draws on, reverse the draught through the farm and carry the fall's bad air down onto people who already faint at their own air's edge. Magnar refused, and the shaft went on through the night, until at dawn a second, smaller fall in the shaft hurt four of his crew. Then Ingrith read the draught at the shaft head and found it already turning, as the Murr had said. Her reading stopped the shaft, not Kaldo and not the Murr, because an inspector may stop a shaft. The council confirms it as the K Discovery lands. Magnar says the Murr's way will take three days and asks who will tell Harn's wife. Nobody answers.

Emeric searches the deep with his sense. He finds six in the pocket, close together, one fading, with water rising on them slowly. Deeper, and off to one side beneath the lower levels, he finds something else: a dozen small notes, all the same note, all still, and beside them a hole his sense cannot read, a dead zone. He files it and gets on with the six. He says aloud what he knows: no flame and no spark near the fall, and the hold's own firedamp checks at the roof, which Ingrith's crews already carry and nod at; and the broken drain above the pocket could let go all at once. Durn opens a flood bypass below the pocket to draw the water down, which the Murr do for their own lower districts every spring. Emeric does not choose the route, the shaft or the bore.

An old survey bore from the expansion forty years ago appears on the Spur's mine plans and runs within a few yards of the pocket. The Spur's drillers clear it through Friday night and extend the last yards by Saturday morning. Bram and Dagny set the battery fans at the bore head, with the hand cranks beside them as the floor, to push air down, and Bram explains to Durn through Ledra that there is no stone anywhere in the loop. The Murr have asked for no mana in it, and nobody argues, since their law says air must never depend on mana alone. Durn listens to the fans for a long time before he believes it. Neris points out that six men in a closed pocket will soon be breathing their own heavy air. She sends down slaked lime in a cloth sock on a cord, with Ledra calling instructions down the pipe to spread it on the floor, and tells Bram that it is what the gill packs' canisters do. Bram did not know she had taken one apart. Dorrit's broth goes down a tube after it.

At noon on Saturday the first air goes down the bore. Up the bore come six knocks, then six again. The Spur's drillers sit down on the rock where they stand.

Chapter 52: Five Knocks

In the pocket, Harn lies in the dark with the water at their shins, then rising more slowly as the Murr's bypass draws it down, though he does not know why. It is cold, and his chest carries the cough the deep gave him years ago. The knocks on the bore pipe come in the hold's old rescue code. The air coming down smells of nothing: no lamp, no forge, no hearth. It keeps them alive, and he hates it a little.

Nils, the youngest of the crew, is pinned by the legs in the first fall, too far under the stone to move. He talks about his daughter and about the money he owes the mine for his tools and his mother's rooms, which is why he took the deep shift. Harn tells him the rescue will come from above. Then the knocks from above say: from below, three days. Harn does not tell Nils what that means. Nils dies on Sunday night, quietly, while the others sleep in the lime dust. Harn knocks five.

From Friday afternoon, Mira follows the Murr's route. Uwen goes first, ears up, Mira's lamp shaded to a coal, the Murr behind touching the walls as they pass, then the Spur's rescue crew, Finn's divers, Bren and Emeric. At a junction Bren unshades his lamp to see a step, and Uwen cries out and covers her eyes. Emeric warned them all, the lamps came dimmed, and it happened anyway. Bren shades it and says sorry with the only orc word he has. Uwen does not accept it and goes on. Where the Murr say the rock will allow it, the Spur's crew cuts a short way through. Then comes a flooded sump. The Murr pass it on a held breath. Kaldo's people go through on gill packs, Finn checking every clip twice.

Before dawn on Monday 3 August they reach the last fall between the route and the pocket. Uwen lays her palm and her ear on each slab in turn and says which one carries the roof. Emeric lifts that slab with gravity and holds it, his face going grey, while Mira and Bren pass the five living men through to Finn, who fits each with a pack for the sump. Then Emeric lowers it slowly, as Uwen tells him, and the roof holds. Others chose the slab and the moment. The Spur's crew goes back in for Nils.

At dawn in the exchange hall the five are carried up, with Nils carried up behind them. Magnar stands at the head of the stair and says to Durn's face that three hours would have done it. Durn does not answer. Nils's death had a clear cause, and his crew mourn him by name. One of the rescued miners says the fans helped and that the foreigners' presence added to the risk, and both are true. Mira, who heard the knocks go from six to five on the set at the bore head, does not think anyone in the hall is wrong.

Chapter 53: The Mist

On Monday mid-morning, the Spur's infirmary hall off the second level holds eleven on cots and mats: the five from the pocket, with Harn's lungs, crushed hands and one man with a stove-in chest who is dying; the four from the shaft fall, with broken legs and a crushed pelvis; and two of the rescue crew cut on the route. The Spur's ward healers splint and wash, and dust is in everything. It is more than Imani can reach one at a time. She starts with the worst. The stove-in chest is failing. She rebuilds the first man's chest and the second man's legs one by one, feels how many are left, and goes on to the third past the point of fainting, because the chest is still failing. Started alone, it would have killed her.

Emeric arrives in the doorway with dust to the eyes from the last fall and sees what she is doing. He asks the air-keepers first. Durn sets the terms: the hall sealed from the rescue's air loop, the doors shut, and a hand-cranked fan at the door to clear the hall if anything goes wrong. An older air-keeper says no. Blue air in the deep is how the passages died, she says, and she leaves. Nobody stops her; it is their law, learned in blood.

Emeric opens his hands over the cots and turns his own mana into liquid, with no jar, so it rises as a cool blue mist over the hall. Imani takes it as though it were her own breath. She can take it because of the sharing between the three of them: it carries his note, and she carries a piece of him. To anyone else it is only thick air. He gives it; she cannot pull it from him. The dwarf healers back away from the cots, and then one comes back, because the man under her hands has stopped bleeding. Ferra kneels at the crushed hands and the crushed pelvis and draws grit, rock chips and iron out of the wounds ahead of Imani, so nothing heals closed over dirt. It is her earth precision's first real work past the wall, and the first time the three of them have worked a room together. Neither woman speaks.

Bettany watches for nosebleeds. At about forty minutes the first one comes, from a patient, and she calls time. Emeric stops, Durn cranks the fan, and the hall clears. Eleven are living. Then Imani goes down where she stands, and Bettany has the ward before she lands. Emeric sits on the floor beside her, grey and drained far beyond anything surgery takes out of him. Half conscious, Imani tells him to sit down. He says he is sitting. She says properly. That night Ferra writes Imani's name for it in Imani's book, because Imani's hand is shaking: the given mist. The crew calls it simply the mist, and nobody outside the three says more than "the blue air."

By evening Harn is awake and asks Bettany what the blue was. She says it was the king. He asks whether that is a joke. She says she is not sure either. On Tuesday morning in the K Discovery's wardroom, Imani is up, angry about being carried, and eating. Emeric is asleep in a chair. Ferra counts what the hold now owes and finds, when she is done, that she has written nothing in that column.

Chapter 54: Braced Posts

On Tuesday 4 August, Nils's name is cut into his clan's ledger-line on the basalt of the first ring, as every dwarf's is. Kaldo stands at the back, as asked. Magnar grieves at the Murr, and Durn stands at the edge of it without answering.

On Wednesday the council approves Ingrith's full ventilation and support audit, and she stops three shafts on her own authority. Asked for Kaldo's layout, Emeric draws the draw-offs by ordinary gas behaviour: heavy damps sink along the floor to the low galleries, so draw them off there. His corner mark is on the sheet, as on every drawing he makes. Ingrith takes it for her survey, which she runs under real operating conditions, with carts running, shifts changing and doors used the way crews use them. On Wednesday and Thursday Emeric goes down with her crews, and the readings come back wrong for his drawing. The heavy damp pools at every braced post, not in the low galleries. Steinar, who knows the bracing, sees why: the deep galleries are braced with gravity stones fixed to the supports, and each planted stone pulls the air around it down a little, so the gas pools at every post. Firedamp is lighter than air and still gathers at the roof, so the old checks stay. Ingrith works it out with Steinar in chalk on the rock, and Emeric reaches with his sense and feels the pull, after the fact. The fix is hers: gas checks at every braced post and a draw-off pipe at the low point of each. In the council hall that evening, Emeric says plainly that he was wrong. When Bram proposes more fans for the east galleries, Magnar says fans do not hold up a roof that crosses two rocks. He is right, and Bram says so.

On Thursday Kettrin's letter arrives by coaster and runner. The orc house's market court has struck Isbrand from every roll that keeps the house's roll, barred him from every door and every road under orc escort law, and given his remaining goods in restitution to Yaro and the escort. The clerk who copied Ferra's plan is identified by his own signature on the wardstone and dismissed. Gadd is cleared, with Ferra's word entered in the finding. The ore contract's buyer has accepted a revised delivery schedule at a penalty, since orcs would rather be paid late than see a hold close. And the lenders' guild, without Isbrand's plan and with the house watching, has rolled the Spur's grain debt to the spring reckoning, at interest. The mine misses its target and the hold does not fall. Ledra reads that line twice and says she will still go home for the autumn reckoning. There is also a line from Brega: her grandson asks whether the king has been let in yet. Ferra does not answer it in writing.

On Friday the council acts. It closes the galleries Ingrith's survey shows short-circuiting the air, and they stay closed until the Murr sign them off. Safety compliance will now be paid separately from tonnage, and the production guild splits over it, Magnar's half walking out of the hall. Ingrith goes home hated by both halves, and Steinar walks her there. The ledger-keepers enter the rescue in the Spur's ledger beside their warning about humans, word for word as the council gives it, and pin Emeric's draw-off drawing beside it with Ingrith's correction across it in her hand. It is the honest record: humans came, they were wrong about the gas, and they pulled five out. A fair copy goes west to the Anvil, along with the council's letter asking the Anvil to receive Kaldo's king by its mountain gate. The sky's leave becomes a standing one: one hull at a time while Kaldo's work lasts. Kaldo leaves two hand-crank fans and their drawings with the Spur's workshops, which can copy a fan from a master pattern, along with four safety lamps, gas checks and the rescue drill. The battery fans go back aboard, because the Spur cannot charge them, and a machine is only useful if someone local can keep it running.

Imani offers to rebuild what the deep cough has taken from Harn's lungs, and he says yes. She does what she can in two sessions. He is going back down anyway, because his household's debt is tied to his wages. That evening Ferra reads the Spur's debt ledger and writes one line in her own: a fan does not make a safe mine. At the gate hall, Durn speaks through Ledra. The Murr give no guide unless you recover someone from a collapsed passage. Kaldo has. He gives them Uwen as their guide in the deep and asks for a survey of the lower farm's air with Kaldo's fans next week. At the gate market Dorrit buys dried deepcap with the face of a cook meeting a rival.

On Friday, on the K Endeavor, Runa receives her first dose of the vaccine, made from the sick wolf's tissue Grau took and raised and dried by Bettany, tested on animals first. Imani, back aboard, explains every step before she does it. Runa will not be held. She holds herself still, staring at the corner where Emeric sits, grey, because he came back aboard to be there. Grau writes in his column in Imani's book that he would have waited a day, and she writes his reason under hers. Runa smells the hold's dust on Emeric, and a dead man on it, and asks through Grau whose. He tells her: a miner, young, who waited.

On Saturday Emeric asks the council what lies under the lower levels. Egil, master of the house that owns the deep seam, answers: breeding stock, house business, lawful. Durn, in the hall for the Murr's survey, goes still. The Murr have heard those pieces' pulses through the rock for a year. They belong to a king slime taken from the Murr's pact ground in the deep by core poachers, and among most peoples with a slime pact, taking a slime's core without leave is theft. Durn will bring the Murr's claim before the council once the mine's business is done. Emeric says nothing more. At Egil's house door, Ferra watches an orc woman in the house's colours check herself from boots to crown, and again an hour later at the same door, in the same order. Ferra does not know why, and writes it down.

On Sunday 9 August, the call day, Emeric sits in the K Discovery's radio room on the apron, still drained, at the far edge of the relays' reach. Harrold reports that registration of the private watches closes tomorrow; Cinderhale's watch registered on the first morning, forty men paid from Tullio's book, lawful on paper; Keller has begun auditing the three biggest; and the magistrate's warrant is still unserved. Emeric asks, "How's he holding up?" Then Wren comes on Tess's line uninvited to report that the Motion Shop's new spanner rack holds forty-one spanners, and he names them. Two days after Nils's rites, Emeric listens to all forty-one.

The collapse fills the hospital deck with the right patients for the field mender's first trials: many small injuries, washed, set and closed first, and a register to count them. Bettany sprays a dirty cut in a hurry and Imani opens it again in front of the deck. A big knight wants some for a torn shoulder and it does nothing at all, because dense bodies resist outside mana. A deckhand who is spared a long healing is the first to call it a mender, and the name sticks. Imani's ruling, said once: washed, set and closed first, or not at all. Bettany's column has three months of the mist and a week of the mender, and she says both are too few.

Where the Story Stands After Chapter 54

The Spur has opened its sky to one hull at a time and written to the Anvil asking it to receive Kaldo's king. Five miners came out alive through the Murr's route, with air pushed down an old bore by fans that need no mana and a roof slab held up by Emeric's gravity, and eleven were healed in the first room heal, the given mist, which cost Imani a collapse and Emeric several drained days. Nils died waiting for the slower, safer way, and Magnar's grief at the Murr remains unanswered. Harn is going back down to pay his debt.

Emeric was wrong about the gas in front of the people who were right, and he said so. The Spur's ledger now carries the rescue beside its warning about humans, with his drawing pinned beside it and Ingrith's correction across it, and a copy of that record has gone west to the Anvil. The audit closed three galleries, separated safety pay from tonnage and split the production guild, leaving Ingrith hated by both sides with Steinar beside her. Kettrin's letter brought justice from the orc house: Isbrand barred, Gadd cleared, and the hold's grain debt rolled to spring, so the mine misses its target without the hold falling.

Below the lower levels lies the next problem. Egil's house holds a dozen still pieces of a king slime in pens beside a dead-zone vault, taken from the Murr's pact ground by core poachers, and the Murr will bring their claim before the council. An orc woman guards Egil's door and checks herself from boots to crown in the same order every hour. The Murr have given Kaldo a guide and asked for a survey of their lower farm. Runa's vaccine series has begun and her cuff should come off around 21 August; Maeve still has not told anyone she saw the girl cast. Emeric's promise to find out whether his family's gold-eyed king knew of the pelt bounty has not yet been started, the coal question waits for the Anvil, and at home Keller's audit of the armed watches is under way.

This batch shows Kaldo earning a small, concrete turn rather than trust: help given on the host's terms, a mistake admitted on the record, and a proud hold that hates asking deciding that the record should say both.
