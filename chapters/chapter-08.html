Book 2, Batch 8: The Mark

Chapters 55–60 | Monday 10 August – Tuesday 1 September, Year 17

The Spur, the first dwarf hold of the Sea Reach, has opened its sky to one Kaldo hull at a time after the mine rescue, and its ledger now carries Emeric's corrected drawing beside its old warning about humans, with a copy gone west to the Anvil, the great Sea Hold. The Murr, the pale, huge-eared people who live in the deep beneath the Spur, have given Kaldo a guide, Uwen, and asked for a survey of their lower farm's air. Beneath the lower levels, a dozen still pieces of a king slime lie in Egil's house's pens, taken from the Murr's pact ground, and an orc woman guards his door. Runa is still cuffed through her vaccine series, and Emeric's promise to find out whether his family's gold-eyed king knew of the pelt bounty has not yet begun.

The Murr have lived for two thousand years by a law the war wrote in their dead: air must never depend on mana alone. Kaldo arrives already agreeing with them, which is not the same as understanding them. Emeric measures their air the right way and gets the wrong sum, and a guide who has never seen a vane hears what his instruments cannot. Then something in the deep starts answering every sound the visitors make, and the people who know how to listen decide what dies.

A desperate house stole a living king slime and kept it silent in a dead zone for a year. Everyone who sees it wants it, and the one man who does not carries it home. Then, at the Anvil, a keeper lays one of Emeric's drawings beside a war order for the engines that broke her people's golems. The marks match. The method in the margin is his. Who drew the order, nobody can prove. Kaldo also has coal that the merfolk smiths want and does not sell it, and a king with a mark he cannot explain writes an order against digging at old sites, with an emergency exception he writes himself.

Chapter 55: The Listening Stone

On Monday 10 August, Neris goes down the sealed route from the Spur's deep exchange hall. Surface visitors are licensed and recorded at its head, lamps are shaded, and no flame is allowed past the farms. The Murr's halls open out beneath the Spur, warm and damp, smelling of earth and mushrooms, with lamp-worm racks in the ceilings like slow green rain, fungus beds, and people who turn their ears toward Bram's boots rather than toward whoever is speaking. At the farm partition stands Tovan, very polite and grey about the mouth.

The Murr keep damp slimes everywhere as part of their pact, and slimes breathe out free mana as an even haze, so to Emeric's sense the deep is a fog. He can find a body at arm's length and not much farther. He says so, and Uwen looks almost pleased. Durn wants the airflow measured with doors and workshops in normal operation, and the deep's council permits closing only the lower farm, and only for a fixed term. For two days Emeric runs vanes and smoke threads under load, passage by passage, correctly. The totals do not balance. He checks the sums twice, and Bram checks them a third time.

Uwen hears the answer. She lays her ear to one wall, then another, and says where the air is going, through cracks no vane can enter. She listens to a damp colony's pulse and says how much of the bad gas it is eating. He was measuring the right things in the wrong world. He says plainly that he was wrong, for the second time in a week. Bram writes it down for Tess: air in the deep is heard. Durn decides. The lower farm extension closes for a fixed term, which is the air-keepers' power. Inspection ports will go into the air law for every new chamber, by an amendment the deep's council will vote on after the fleet has gone. The blocked natural vent is partly recovered by a Murr bypass, because clearing the old collapse would risk the farm above it. Tovan hears the term and says nothing about the faints, which is how Durn knows he was right. Kaldo leaves two battery fans and a hand-crank charger, a small dynamo with no stone anywhere in it, placed where Uwen says the air wants to go. Bram leaves spare bearings and the drawings and says when the bearings will fail in the damp. In payment Durn gives Ferra copies of the deep's air maps. She enters the fans in the gift ledger as a gift given and the maps as a gift received. Nobody corrects her.

Steinar has come down as the Spur's observer. In a side passage, in the dark, where he thinks nobody else can hear, he tells Uwen how many times a Murr warning has saved a gallery at the Spur. Uwen can hear everyone, and so can Neris.

On Tuesday Neris, Dagny, Steinar and two Murr surveyors go below the farm into new, unmapped chambers to find a safe way back and learn where the air goes. The sound cues stop making sense. Steinar drops a hammer, and the tunnel answers with the hammer's ring from below and behind them. One Murr surveyor slips into a fissure, and someone has to decide whether to give away their position to pull him out. Neris does, with Dagny holding her belt. A lamp goes down the fissure and does not come up, and a gill pack's bottle goes with it. Then they are cut off in a dry chamber with whatever is hunting them between them and the way out.

Neris's people sound best in water, and the flooded passages carry the hunters' movements to her before the rock does. She puts her palm in the water at the chamber's edge and hears them. She makes the party sit still and silent and tells them by touch where the thing is. One of the Murr surveyors, bleeding, finds her hand in the dark and holds it.

Chapter 56: The Mimic

On Wednesday 12 August the rescue team assembles: Uwen and two Murr hunters, with Bram, Arthur, Mira, Bren, Finn with gill packs, and Emeric. The Spur's council allows Kaldo's knights their arms for a beast in the deep, and rifles are fetched up from the K Discovery on the apron, but the Murr ask them not to fire. Uwen names the thing for them in a word that comes into Kaldo's tongue as the mimic: a blind, pale, amphibious hunter of the flooded deep passages, about a man's length, with many small teeth, hunting by hearing through water and rock. It copies its prey's sounds back to them from somewhere else, a dropped tool, a footstep, the rhythm of a voice, so the prey turns toward the wrong place. A mated pair hunts together, one calling and one waiting. This pair has taken two of the Murr's farm hands from the new chambers this year, which is why those chambers went unmapped and why the Murr decide it dies.

The kit reaches its limits. The gill packs will not fit through the narrowest flooded crawl on a diver's back, so they are pushed ahead. The safety lamp's flame shrinks in the lower chambers' bad air until it is only a warning, not a light. Emeric's sense is useless beyond arm's length. Bram finds a crack into the survivors' chamber and sets a battery fan to push fresh air through it, which is exactly right. Then he takes a shorter crawl that Uwen says rings hollow, because the survivors' air is failing. It slumps behind him. Arthur hauls him back by the harness, and the battery bank Bram was carrying is gone. A week ago Magnar told him fans do not hold up a roof that crosses two rocks, and Bram thinks about that the whole way along the long crawl.

When one of the pair comes up out of the water at a Murr hunter, Bren fires once. The round hits rock. A breath later the deep gives the shot back from three directions, and the second hunter is somewhere none of them can place. Uwen turns her face to Bren in the dark and does not need to say anything. Nobody fires again.

In the last crawl, with no room to swing, the first of the pair comes at Emeric. Uwen says, "There." He strikes with the Crumple, his padded blow, so his hand stays whole, and knocks it back into the hunters. The Murr kill it where Uwen's sounding says its head is. The second flees deeper, and the Murr let it go and seal the farm off from its passage. Arthur, who could not swing at anything and had to watch, is angrier with himself than with anyone, though he finds his own way to be useful getting the survivors out.

The survey party comes out alive. The badly bitten surveyor goes to the Murr's own healers, and Imani's offer is declined politely and left open. Neris is trusted now, for listening, and Uwen gives her an echo-stone to keep. The knights are not trusted, because of the shot. Bren's hand, torn as the second creature passed him, is mended by Imani on the K Discovery that evening. The next morning he apologises to Uwen at the exchange hall, with Ledra interpreting this time, and she accepts it, a little.

Chapter 57: The Pens

On Monday 17 August, in the Spur's council hall, Durn brings the Murr's claim under their slime pact: a king slime taken from the Murr's pact ground by core poachers a year ago, bought by Egil's house, and held. Taking a core-bearer without leave from a neighbour's pact ground is theft. Egil's answer is real. His miners were dying of the deep's air. One king could bud damp slimes for every gallery. He paid the poachers and kept the king drained in a dead-zone vault so it could not eat its cage, and cut pieces from it to grow kings of his own, and none woke. The council orders the vault opened and the king returned. The house's pens are forfeit, and it owes the Murr restitution in metal and worked tools. Egil is answerable to his own people's law, not to Kaldo. He looks at Emeric, not at Durn, as the speaker reads it out, and he stays bitter.

At the vault door stands the orc guard in the house's colours, halfway through her hourly check. She finishes it before stepping aside, because the order is lawful, and then asks who is paying her for last month. Ferra writes her name down for the first time: Siv. The pens are a sad room: a dozen grey lumps in glass, perfectly still. Inside the vault, the dead zone feels like cold water, and the king lies on a slab, dull, cold and colourless, with drain cuffs on it. Emeric carries it out of the zone and takes the cuffs off. It wakes and calls its pieces. The pens empty as the grey lumps slide across the stone floor one after another and go into it. Egil watches every one.

Outside, Arthur, who once shot the handler of one of the Church's engineered knights, checks Siv aloud for the juggernauts' tells, because she is a big woman who feels nothing. She is furious, then interested. The juggernauts had uncanny faces without scars, and Siv is scarred all over by things she never felt. She was born unable to feel pain. As a child, breaks, burns and an infection went unnoticed, everyone called it a blessing, and she became a fighter to learn her body's limits by other means. Imani, sent for, examines her with her consent: no reservoirs along the spine, no womb-cut scar. The pain pathway simply never grew, and nobody can build what was never there. Siv asks whether Imani will tell her what is in her body anyway. Imani says yes. Emeric privately recognises Siv's hourly check from boots to crown as a pilot's pre-flight walkaround.

From Tuesday Uwen guides them down through the deep to the pact ground, the king riding on Emeric's shoulder, pearl-coloured now and the size of a cat. Everyone who sees it wants it. At the vault door Egil offers a share of the deep seam for it. At the under-door treaty-house an orc trader offers a price that would buy a hull. Siv makes no offer but tells him the poachers who came for it in the spring offered her more than either, and she turned them away because she had signed. Emeric says no to each without weighing it.

On the way the Murr put out the lamps and make the party walk a passage lined with echo-stones, stones that hold a sounding and give it back when tapped. Each one, tapped, gives back the shape of a passage in the Still Halls under Duvrenne, the Murr's old home on Ostrellin, where whole passages suffocated when the dead zones came. The Murr's councils will not discuss it. The stones do. Nobody in Kaldo's party speaks until they are out, and Emeric thinks Armand should have been there.

In the year the king was silent, the old slimes of the pact ground had begun to flow together into a Gathering, the thing the king's pulse used to prevent, and the Murr had been listening to it with dread, since a Gathering that fails tears itself apart. When the king comes home, its pulse breaks the Gathering. In the dark the old slimes part again, and the surplus goes into the air in faint blue flashes. Uwen breathes out for what feels like the first time all day. Then the king rolls off Emeric's shoulder, goes three feet, and comes back. He puts it down again. It comes back again. The third time it stays and watches him go. He thinks that is the end of it. While it rode on his shoulder, though, it pressed a bead of itself into his coat's lining, and nobody knows that yet.

That evening on the K Amara, Ferra reads Siv's terms aloud: wages, shore guard, freedom to refuse any mission, freedom to leave at any port, her own people's law kept, passage home. Her reason is her own: Imani. Ward reads the terms twice.

Inside the vault's dead zone the king's slab is strapped as well as cuffed. Emeric reaches for the mana scalpel to cut a strap and nothing forms: held mana, in a place with nothing to hold it. He carries the whole load out and cuts the strap on the threshold with a dwarf's knife, and later that night he admits to himself that the scalpel on the hill had been luck.

Chapter 58: The Mountain Gate

On Friday 21 August, Imani and Grau agree the danger window has passed, and Runa's cuff comes off. She is well and unmarked. Her ordinary nose has been there all along; then the magic in it returns all at once and the whole sea comes in. Mira is at the rail, and Runa sees the scar Mira kept and asks through Grau why the healer did not take it. Mira says so she sees it coming next time. Runa looks at the crescent scar she kept on her own forearm and says nothing. Mira is the first knight she wants to be like. Maeve, watching the air lean toward the girl's face again, finally tells Emeric and Imani what she saw at the moot. Emeric says he knows; he felt it on the deck. Maeve is annoyed to have kept a secret he already had.

On Saturday, with the Spur's business done, the K Discovery lifts off the apron for the last time, the speaker watching from the gate stair, and the fleet flies about 210 miles west, low along the range. Before the lift, Ward comes across to the K Endeavor's rail to see the girl his knights are guarding, and his size and smell begin a week of Runa being afraid of him. On the deck, out of the cuff, she feels wind for the first time in weeks and does not speak for an hour. That evening the fleet sets down off the Anvil's gorge-town, a few miles from where the Anvil's sea end lies under the trench water, and Zale's ship feels a hum come up through her hull, which Zale describes on the set as the sea purring. At dusk a relay is launched from the K Discovery's deck over water, with Sorrel and a dragon watch at the rails, so the chain is whole to the Anvil. Nine relays remain.

The Anvil's answer waits at the gorge-town's warden hut. The Thane will receive the king by the mountain gate on Monday, but the Anvil's sky stays shut, because the Anvil was not the one rescued. On Sunday the party climbs the road unarmed under orc escort: Emeric, Ferra, Armand, Bram, Ledra, Arthur and four knights, with mules carrying the gifts. Werewolves never enter a hold, so Grau and Runa stay aboard. At the mountain gate on Monday they meet the Thane, chosen by the guild seniors and presiding over the council without outranking it. In the Great Hall under the Anvil-Spire, brass reliefs show the mountains of the old dwarf home on Ostrellin, which no living dwarf has seen. Armand stands in front of them a long time. Warmth is rank here, with the old clans near the heat ducts and the outer rings cold, nearest the sea, where the pumps can be felt through the stone. Every dwarf in the hall looks again at Bram, and Bram looks at the brass instead.

On Tuesday at the foundries, Bram watches Deepforge being cast and rememberer being made, the dwarves' metal that returns to its shape: the furnace, the cooling, the reset, the signs of failure. His first notebook page is wrong; he drew the bend and missed the reset spring. A dwarf smith is mildly insulted that Bram asks "How much does it pull?" instead of "How many times?" His second page is right, with the reset spring, the temperature range and a broken test bar. Emeric calls it memory metal and the smiths call it rememberer, and both names stand. Bram sees a route to it in Kaldo's own materials, a bronze with a little titanium, pressure-forged, for seal rings, but the dwarves keep their lead. Bram also sees in the first hour what the Anvil does with its vents, warming the halls, drafting the air, soaking the steel, and what it does not do, which is turn anything. He draws a turbine on the back of his second page for the smiths' council, knowledge only. At the loading compartments, where goods cross between the wet side and the dry, Emeric warns that crews moving in and out of pressure will get the bends. The lock-keepers already stage their crews out by a rule the hold bought from the Rim generations ago, and flood doors on every ring already answer the danger of inrush. Others got there first; he learns it and says so.

At Deepforge, beside the rememberer, a smith shows Bram an unfinished golem body with an empty stone seat. Bodies they can make; minds they cannot, and the ones they had died in the war. Privately, Emeric works out why Kaldo can make golems and the dwarves cannot. At home a golem's mind is a control stone, and Kaldo has the dome's tree to grow them and delvers who can etch a script into one, which he will not teach and could not sell. The stone keeps a body upright with a hundred small corrections a second, so the rider only has to say where to go. Without a stone, a person would have to balance the machine himself with levers and pedals, and no hands are fast enough. On his other world a reckoning engine did those corrections, and Kaldo has no reckoning engines yet. He thinks: not yet, and leaves it.

In the reception hall, Ferra sits in a ball-shaped cell on the dry side, with thick quartz and a speaking tube between her and the dark water where the crown of Aumere's iron buyer waits, her fins slow in the cold. Since the judgment, Aumere's crown holds the Rim's iron trade. Through the water-lock the buyer passes a sealed letter from Ilenne for Zale. Then she asks Kaldo, on behalf of the Rim's smiths, for coal, so the Rim can smelt its own deep-floor nodules. The Thane's council knows Kaldo carries coal to trade and watches, because, as the dwarves say, the day Kaldo sells coal is the day they decide what Kaldo is. The answer: Kaldo sells no coal past the wall for a term, until the next voyage's terms are made, and instead brokers dwarf iron to the crown's buyers at a fair rate, a conditional contract with the Thane's council and the crown's buyer, the rate set in Ferra's book with both sides' marks. The nodules go into the same contract, paid for by their metal on the assay of Aumere's certifying bench. The Thane says nothing about it, and Ferra understands that the silence is the answer she came for. The smiths' council buys the second cask of beer. At the Anvil's market, Kaldo buys a few hundredweight of lodestone sand, heavy black waste from the tin and iron works, for smaller, stronger motors at home, paid for in Kaldo's jarred food, which the dwarves want more than anything else Kaldo carries. Both sides think they have robbed the other.

The night before the foundries, Emeric drew Bram a plain wide cuff on the back of a ration list, as heavy as it could be for its size, something he could reshape. At Deepforge Bram casts it in a tungsten alloy, about three kilos, dark and plain. The smith asks what it is for. Bram says a bracelet, and the smith looks at Emeric's wrist and does not ask again.

Chapter 59: The Mark

On Wednesday 26 August Emeric goes down to the Anvil's sea end by trench-tunnel, along ridged iron decks and ore trams, to a sealed record hall at one atmosphere. A port of graded quartz in its wall looks out on black water, with the spire's heat-rings glowing far off and the hum in the floor. The keeper is Jofrid, old and precise. The war ledgers hold every hold that was taken, every golem broken by a dead zone, the seals on the orders, and the records that proved rationing mana stones to humans was right. Her stance is simple: we warned you.

Then she reads from the ledgers' last war decades: human conscripts given to the rite by the thousand, counted by dwarven spies and prisoners' tallies. She says flatly that the humans fed their own sons to it. It is the first time anyone past the wall has said it aloud on the page, and it comes from the people with the least reason to. The keepers always knew and never said it to a human. Armand asks for the years, and she gives them without looking up. One conscript's line in the record lands harder than any total.

Jofrid has the Spur's fair copy, with Emeric's draw-off drawing and his corner mark. She lays it beside a war order, under the royal seal, for the stone-throwers that carried overloading stones to the golem lines. The comparison works on three levels. First, the marks match: a ring with one stroke through it that stops short of the far side, in the order's corner and in his. Second, the margin holds a tolerance stack worked exactly his way, the same order of terms, the same habit of writing the worst case first. Third, authorship is unproven, and Jofrid says so herself before anyone else can: a mark can be copied and a method can be taught. She keeps it as evidence, not a verdict.

Emeric asks to take the document away to study. She refuses. She lets Ferra copy it by hand, order and margin, into Ferra's book, and watches every stroke. He leaves the original with her. In his own thoughts only, the mark is the one he has drawn in margins since he was a boy, on the other world, before he ever came here. He does not say it aloud to anyone that day, not even Ferra, and the narration does not explain it.

That night in the gate hall Ferra opens her book. Beside the copy she lays a page from the day he was crowned, a page on which she once drew the worn mark at the foot of the founder's seal matrix, a mark she knew from every drawing he had ever made. She looks from one to the other and closes the book. She never asks him. Two people each hold a piece of it, and neither puts it on the table. Late, he says to her, "I used to know it wasn't me. Now I have to find out." She notices that he has stopped saying "that wasn't me," and has not started saying "it was."

The inquiry begins with its first page: the mark's dating, the document's provenance, whether the mark was reused or copied, and any record that contradicts it. Jofrid agrees to answer written questions through the orc house's roll. Armand takes the ledgers' chronology and insists on separating planners, volunteers, conscripts and refusers wherever a record allows. It is the same inquiry Runa asked for. On Friday morning the Thane sees them off at the mountain gate. The Anvil keeps its warning, and Emeric's drawing now sits in two holds' ledgers. On the way down, from the gorge-town's warden hut, Ferra sends the rememberer's numbers home to Tess by relay, in cipher.

Chapter 60: Provenance

On Friday 28 August, back aboard, the crew argues in the K Discovery's wardroom. Ward says, "I'll not apologize for men I never met," and that somebody has to say when a concession costs more than it is worth. Armand says records lie, the dwarves' too; a mark can be copied; and the conscripts in Jofrid's ledger were not the men who drew the order. Bram takes it hardest. The dwarves at both holds went quiet at his face, and he has carried that for a month. He says almost nothing, and he says it to Emeric. Imani wants present harm first: the bites, the doses, the Murr surveyor whose healing they declined, because what Kaldo does next is the only part anyone can act on. Zale, married into the wronged, has the most standing to speak and uses it least, saying one thing about the unopened letter in his pocket. A K Amara gunner whose grandfather died in the rite asks, "Who owes him?" Emeric answers: "The Church owes him. It owes your family the truth, too. We won't let them hide that debt behind what our ancestors did to someone else." Ferra closes it by writing down not the argument but the list of what was promised at this stop and what it cost. The question in the room is what Emeric does with incomplete evidence, and the objections change the terms he will set.

On the K Endeavor's deck, Neris tells Emeric to his face what the coal decision costs the Rim's smiths, her people. She is right about the cost, and he does not argue. She has Ferra enter her objection as an outside entry under her own name. That dusk Zale opens Ilenne's letter on his own deck, reads it, and goes over the side into the sea for an hour. Nobody asks.

On Saturday, through Grau at the hospital deck's corner, Emeric gives Runa the first piece of the truth he promised: an old order for engines that broke the dwarves' golems, bearing the mark he puts on his own drawings, with nothing in it about pelts. He does not know yet who drew it. He is looking, and he will tell her what he finds. She says, "Then look." Grau does not stop halfway this time. Runa watches Ferra write the inquiry's first page and asks, through Grau, for her own list to be written down. On the facing page Ferra writes every scent, every name Runa has and who did what, so Runa no longer has to carry it all in her body. Runa makes Grau read every line back.

That day is also the late-August call day. Keller's audit has found the Cinderhale payroll lawful, though not the watch innocent: coordination and risk are shown, but there is no lawful ground to confiscate anything. Corbin lays the unserved warrant beside the clean audit and reads both out on the line. He asks for observers with a half-company escort. There is one half-company spare, and a riot has been threatened in the Wayford line towns. Emeric sends it to Wayford. The clerk reads back the minute: Cinderhale observers declined; Wayford preferred. Corbin is right, and overruled again. Emeric does not sleep. That night he writes an order in his own hand, Ferra enciphers it, and it goes up the relay to Harrold as regent: no Kaldo extraction or excavation at any old peoples' site without recording provenance and claims and holding a review, with an exception, written by Emeric himself, for fuel and defence in an emergency. Harrold acknowledges it in one line.

At dawn on Sunday the king slime is on the K Discovery's rail, slightly smaller and very pleased with itself. It tracked the bead in his coat lining across about 210 miles in ten days. Emeric finds the bead and understands. He sits down on a coil of line, and it climbs into his lap and makes the purring pulse slimes make when they have eaten. You keep me safe, and I will follow you: that is the whole bond, and it is not a bond yet. He calls it Mochi. Only Ferra and Imani know it is a word from his other world, and they look at each other and do not explain. Everyone else thinks it is a nonsense word, and it answers to it. Runa, brought over by Grau to see why the deck is laughing, finds that it has no smell at all, which offends her deeply, and growls at it all morning.

The fourth gift set goes to Jofrid for the inquiry, kept in the record hall under the hold's seal so she can ring him when her dating finds anything. The fifth goes to Uwen for the Murr, because Bram told Durn when the fans' bearings will fail and somebody should be able to say so before the air does. Uwen comes up to the gorge-town's quay to see them off, something a Murr guide does for nobody. Radio does not pass through rock, so Bram strings the set's wire mast at the Murr's surface door, and the air-keepers agree to sit by it at the hours on Ferra's card. Uwen listens to the hiss with her ear to the brass, the way she listens to a wall, and says it is the sky breathing. Fifteen sets remain. Ferra asks the Thane for flat ground at the gorge-town for winged machines, and the answer is no. The war came to the holds from the air, and a field is a door in the sky. Ask again when the dragons' terms are answered. Ferra writes it down as an answer, not a loss. The seed hand's crates gain alpine barley and hops from the Anvil's terrace farms, and the Murr's damp-farm fungus starter goes into Imani's quarantine bay.

On Monday, Mochi buds one small damp colony into a sealed glass jar, the gift owed to Egil's house now that the Spur's air is fixed: an apology from one side and the price of peace from the other. Ledra's contract ends at the Anvil and is paid. She leaves at the gorge-town's quay to go home east for the autumn reckoning, carrying the jar for Egil's house and letters for Kettrin. At the rail she reads Ferra's last outside entry back to her in orc to check it and finds nothing wrong. There is now no interpreter aboard until the desert's treaty-house hires one.

On Tuesday 1 September the fleet lifts south toward the desert, about six hundred miles away. Emeric crosses to the K Endeavor for the lift with Mochi, and the Sea Reach goes grey behind them. Mochi sits on a crate in Runa's path, and she growls at it. When nobody is looking, she puts her hand on it. It is warm, and she takes her hand back fast.

At dawn on Monday 31 August, on the gorge-town's shingle, Emeric delves into the cuff, bonds it to himself and seals it. He asks Armand whether anyone has done this before. People have always reshaped metal, Armand says, but nobody he knows of has bonded a thing and sealed it into one keeping; that is delving, not shaping. Armand tries to reshape it and fails, because it pushes back the way a body does. Emeric gives him access and it gives under his hand; Emeric closes it again and it will not move. Then, with Ward clearing the beach, they force a small stone past capacity well away from the hulls, and in the dead zone the cuff locks: nobody can change it, Emeric included, until the zone clears. Armand calls it the first new thing he has seen made with mana in sixty years that still works when the mana stops. Emeric says it does not work; it just stays what it was. Armand says that is what he meant.

Where the Story Stands After Chapter 60

The inquiry into the mark has begun. At the Anvil, a war order for the stone-throwers that broke the dwarves' golems carries Emeric's own corner mark and a tolerance stack worked exactly his way, though authorship remains unproven. The original stays with Jofrid, a hand copy is in Ferra's book beside her page from his crowning, and neither of them has put what they hold on the table. Emeric has stopped saying "that wasn't me." Runa has been given the first piece of the truth he promised, and her list of seven now lives in Ferra's book. Jofrid has a gift set to ring him if her dating finds anything.

The Murr taught Kaldo that air in the deep is heard, not seen. The lower farm is closed for its term, inspection ports go to the Murr's own vote, the fans will fail when Bram said they would, and Ferra has recorded the Murr's maps as a gift received, an entry that may not be right. The Murr hunters killed one mimic and let its mate go, and they trust Neris but not Kaldo's guns. The king slime went home, broke a forming Gathering, and then followed Emeric 210 miles to the fleet. It is now Mochi, aboard and not yet bonded, and Runa growls at it but has found it warm. Siv has joined on her own terms because of Imani, and the knights are adopting her hourly check.

Kaldo will sell no coal past the wall for a term and will broker dwarf iron to Aumere's crown instead, and Neris's objection on behalf of the Rim's smiths stands on the record under her name. Bram brings home the memory-metal numbers, a turbine drawing and lodestone sand for motors. Zale has read Ilenne's letter and keeps what it says to himself. Ledra has gone home, so the fleet is without an interpreter. At home, Keller's audit found Cinderhale's payroll lawful, Corbin was overruled again, and the midpoint order now forbids digging at old peoples' sites without review, except in emergencies for fuel and defence, an exception Emeric wrote himself. The fleet has about 84 tonnes of food, nine relays, and a relief rendezvous off the Sabir coast in October.

This batch turns Emeric's competence on himself. He is wrong about the Murr's air, wrong to trust a gun in the deep, and suddenly unsure of his own hand on a two-thousand-year-old order, and he responds by leaving the evidence where it belongs and starting to ask questions instead of answering them.
