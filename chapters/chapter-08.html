Book 3, Batch 6: The Courts (ch 48-57)

Synopsis v1, clean, awaiting your approval, not drafted. Friday 9 June to Thursday 31 August, year 19. Rules CX to DS. Movement V's courts and the Hearth's Scenes 29-35 are placed and dated. No verdict, number or name was changed.

The spine

The realm judges the men who made war on it. The king's own law touches him twice: a rule that will bind every king after him, and a pardon he won't give.

Ch 48. The Report

Quarrie's full report on the gate is printed with the Moss report beside it. It recommends that the Rule on Royal Force outlive the Authorisation. On 23 June the Assembly makes it standing law, so every future authorisation carries it. The king says it may cost some king a day he doesn't have. Ferra says that king may be worse than him, which is the point. He signs. Davey Corve is pardoned and Hal Belk is paid.

Ch 49. Hers

Maudie wins the dues case with the register, the slips and the receipts. The court refuses to endorse the burning of the rolls. On the quarter day Tench pays in notes at par and keeps her receipt.

Ch 50. Grievance Isn't a Conspiracy

Judge Agnes Fairlie presides at Kestle, with Keller stepped aside. The conspiracy count against the League fails. The court finds no innocent loss, and says that isn't authority. Five members never come forward, and Kezia tells Corrick he decided that for them too. In the corridor Ward says a day sooner and he'd have had Fulk. Corrick says there was no warrant until they broke in to get one. Sir Edwin's killer is sentenced to hang.

Ch 51. A Year

Davey testifies, and his father gets twenty-five years. In their last scene his father asks only whether Davey's mother is eating. Corrick and Kezia get a year each and Gethin six months, with no credit for time served. Kezia's map is kept out of the sentencing reasons, as she asked. Maudie pins the real months beside the nominal ones on the League's wall.

Ch 52. Neither

The morbidity review on Cal Pascoe can neither absolve nor condemn Imani. Inga adds a coloured-thread mark to every blind tie. The Medical Pass Office opens with Ward, Imani and Mother Brannagh: both sides search together, and both sides pay penalties.

Ch 53. Never Your Years

Pryor's charter brief is read whole. The judgment admits the Assembly is unequal, then sentences him to hang. Sayer's courier produces the signed original. The murder count fails, treason is proven, and the bench sentences her to death two to one. Ruth Penhale speaks against it.

Ch 54. No Royal Favour

Pim brings forty names asking for clemency. The king answers: "No. I could. I'd like to." At Turnstone he tells Corrick he's in the funnel. Corrick answers: "Then don't call knowing enough." In the Motion Shop, the bridge machine runs its first stored program and works out the Board's weekly redemption round before lunch. Cuthbert gets fifteen years, and the Church inquiry closes with nothing proven.

Ch 55. Nine to Three

Tullio is guilty on the count his own receipts prove. The jury splits nine to three on intent, so the capital count ends in a mistrial and is not retried. He gets thirty years. Vantic feels relief first and can't say so. She visits him, and he's alive to hate her.

Ch 56. The Engine

Treloar gets twenty years, with Kezia's old timber warning weighed on its merits. Wystan is acquitted because nobody can prove he knew. The Mark 1's engine runs four minutes on its bench and stops every hammer in the Sky Yard, and Sorrel won't leave. Mabel writes Corrick one line, the first since April: neither of them knows why Fulk ran.

Ch 57. The Hanging Morning

The full bench dismisses the three appeals. Imani argues that the rope protects nobody, Sir Edwin's sister answers her, and neither wins. On 31 August Pryor, Sayer and Sir Edwin's killer are hanged in the yard at Kestle. Imani spends the morning at the Pass Office signing passes with Brannagh.

Closed in v1: thirteen

The Rule on Royal Force as standing law; Sayer's sentence (the lock kept, the "Sayer lives" lean withdrawn); Ward's counterfactual; the Hearth's court and judge; Mabel's scene done by letter; the bridge machine's first run; the Mark 1's bench run; where the three hang; Davey, Hal Belk, the courier, the morbidity review and the Pass Office; Tench's payment date; Davey's father's ward; the night glass; mysteries kept.
