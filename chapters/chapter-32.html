Book 3, Batch 13: The Gathering

Chapters 115-129 | Monday 27 October - Sunday 30 November, Year 22

By the end of October in Year 22 the king had been censured by his own review, his daughter was home, and the blood of the gold-eyed had become a licensed medicine. The guest towers stood finished on the civic ring. Every crown and every people Kaldo knew had been asked to come on Hollow Night and build with it, and most had said yes. The K Sorrel sat fuelled on the Field with the crater-lake charts on her captain's seat.

This batch is not a skip. It follows the Gathering a day at a time, from the morning Kaldo fetches an emperor off a lake to the morning the last guests sail home, and then one more chapter for the bill. It is the week Kaldo's institutions meet people who never agreed to Kaldo, and hold.

Chapter 115: Above the Weather

On Monday 27 October the K Sorrel comes down out of a clear sky onto the crater lake at Ostrel on her gravity stones. There is no wind under her and the banners on the quay don't stir. Emeric and Harrold stand in her door while the imperial barge brings the family out, and nobody from Kaldo sets foot on the island. Emperor Severin last saw Harrold as a grey-bearded man of sixty. The man in the door looks thirty-three. Severin nearly shows it, and the Empress Isaure sees the nearly.

Once the liner is over the crater's mountain ring, Sorrel switches the stones to idle and flies on the wing. The violet ports along the belly go dark. Severin asks where the mage is, and there isn't one. A road that takes a courier ten days passes under the window in twenty minutes. Two Outriders hold off the wingtips, and when Marius asks what they are, Emeric tells him they are protection, and that they carry enough. Severin drinks one glass of wine and notices that the famous drunk prince orders coffee. When Emeric explains that the only magic on the trip was the part over the lake, because the Core has no runway, Severin asks how long a runway is and writes "3,000 metres" in his own hand.

The liner lands at the Field on the runway with the stones still off, and Severin finds that stranger than the lake. That afternoon, at the top of the needle tower, he asks how many men turn the lift. None. He asks what happens if the power fails, and they drop a car a metre on purpose to show him the brake gripping the rails.

In her cabin the wine in Isaure's glass does not move through the climb over the Core, and when she asks why, the steward shows her a flywheel the size of a cartwheel turning in a sealed case under the floor, the liner's gyro stabilizer. She writes it in her letter home before she writes about the view. At the landing Severin's guard wear the Core's finest plate, held light and hard by its stones, and Ward looks at it a moment too long. Severin notices. Nobody says that in a dead zone it would be a cage.

Chapter 116: Dark on the Bench

On Tuesday 28 October the emperor's youngest, Mirela, slips her steward and finds the king of Randillia drinking coffee at a street table by the South Gate. She asks him where his crown is. Then she asks for Mochi, and is told no for the first time.

The peoples come in on the morning tide under a written passage schedule: the merfolk swimming in behind Ilenne and Zale, and Kaldo hulls carrying the dwarves of the Sea Hold, the borderland werewolf packs with Grau, the Sabir, the Murr and the winged ones, with Liora walking down the gangway. In the harbour's inspection hall each people lights its own stones on the bench, by consent and under watch. The dwarves' stones go dark one after another in front of their own engineer, Hulda of the Sea Hold. Their wardens run on beast cores, so it costs them nothing they need, but it costs Hulda her temper. Emeric touches one and finds it full and shut, refusing, the way a stone under a single standing order refuses. He says it is a guess. Hulda demands a sealed copy of the evidence and a seat at the inquiry, and Ferra grants both in writing before midnight. Hulda also mentions, in passing, that the Sea Hold's wardens killed a beast at the Sea Reach in September and never called it in. Ferra's chart was a pin short.

That evening the polar station's radio reports that the First Glass has seen its first light, in one line from Junia Bask's logbook. Emeric writes his own one line for Saban's file about where such orders would be kept. It says "the drafter". Nobody says Ostrel.

The guest hulls' crews and stewards go from the quays straight to the shops: jeans and jersey by the armful, rubber boots for the merfolk, a dozen jackets in one size for an imperial steward. Ferra opens an export column the next morning, and Vantic asks for the mill's capacity before lunch.

Chapter 117: A Wall That Asks

On Wednesday 29 October three dragons come up the bay: Saldis, who signed the Leaving Terms for the dragons; Sohra, her heir; and Corvan, her son, who came because his mother would not leave him at home with the house's grievance. The City Shell holds them at its edge, the first thing in Saldis's life that has stopped her without fighting her, and the watch opens it by hand, one note at a time. "You built a wall that asks," she says. Corvan changes to his smaller form only when his mother tells him twice. The river wall is full of people in dragon costumes, roaring.

They lodge at the castle as Harrold's house's guests. Ward objects to Corvan sleeping under the same roof as Ferra and Imani, and says why. Saldis answers that then she is under it too. Ward puts a rifle team on the north tower anyway, and Saldis knows it is there and says nothing. All week, whenever Ferra or Imani is in the room, Saldis is between them and her son. Only Imani notices, once.

That night Grau asks Imani, doctor to doctor, why a market stall is selling a child's wolf costume trimmed with real pelt. Imani walks the question to the school in the dark. The next day the school changes its rule, and the wolf masks go to felt.

After the pelt, Grau reads Bettany's mender columns from the voyage on and tells Imani what the universal form will get wrong in his people, and why. She writes it down and does not agree with all of it. They settle on a trial on his terms: his patients, his numbers.

That afternoon Runa's guard reports that she went still at a door on the werewolves' floor of the guest tower and wouldn't say why. At two in the morning, from the north tower, Ward sees the king sitting on the kitchen step in the dark, and a minute later the girl coming down the back stair dressed for the road with her boots in her hand. He's there because he knows her. The musk on that floor is kin to the second name in her ledger, and she means to go and look at him alone. You're not going alone at fifteen. She says she'll hate him for it. He says she's allowed to. She sits down on the step beside him.

Chapter 118: Escort

On Thursday 30 October the liner flies the guests who want to go over the states: Milford's farms, the dam, the highway, the maglev test track, the Capital Line's piers and the old capital. Severin asks if the liner is armed. "She isn't. The two beside her are." Liane, thirteen, asks Sorrel how something so heavy stays up, and Sorrel tells her the wing does it and the stones only make it easier. A Velmoor lord who called the lifts unsafe sits in the aisle seat the whole way. The winged ones were invited aboard and said no, and nobody asked twice.

On the ground, Saban wants the mechs where every guest can see them. Ferra moves the Axion back inside the stadium gate's inner yard, where the stand can see it and the queue can't, so that the machine meant to keep the Church polite doesn't frighten a guest. Ward dislikes it and agrees. At dusk Cardinal Benedikt arrives by road, because a Church hull over Kaldo would meet the Shell, and Doran takes him home to Saint Edwy's. Saban notes that the cardinal carries one sealed letter he hasn't opened.

In the morning Saban checks his guest list and Grau smells the man across a breakfast table: he is the cousin who wasn't there, the same musk, two hundred miles away the night Coldspring burned. Runa nearly went for an innocent man. Ferra writes it in the ledger in Runa's words: her nose was right and she was wrong. The same day Saban's plainclothes watchers wear the first twenty pocket sets, small radios with an earpiece on a thin wire under the collar and a throat microphone under a scarf. Saban says it's the first week of his life he's known where all his people are without seeing them.

Chapter 119: Hollow Night

On the morning of Friday 31 October the winged ones refuse the Grand Museum's common catalogue. Kaldo's curators would keep the last word on the labels, and Tiel will not let a human hand edit how his people lost the sky. Kaldo gives them their labels by noon, in writing. Tiel also asks what wind the needle tower was built for, and tells Emeric their rigging would have saved a third of the steel. Emeric says he is right. At three the peoples walk into the stadium before the crowd, and the crowd stands. The envoys of Velmoor and Solreth, who were taught that knights beat monsters, watch a city cheer them.

At five the Grand Library opens its doors on paper and film. Odile Ashdown runs it. The Asking Desk's first call comes from a school in Brask, asking how big a dragon is. The first deposit from abroad is the winged ones' own account of the leaving, copied by their keepers and handed over on their conditions, and Odile shelves it exactly as given. Princess Celestine stands at the catalogue cases with a pencil and asks how the Holding Board is chosen.

At dusk the pumpkins are lit on every step. A werewolf fixes a child's felt mask, because the ears go the other way. Children run past Corvan dressed as dragons, and he stands at the stone for the people with no graves a long time and says nothing. Sohra sees, and doesn't go to him. Across Randillia the ration halls serve pumpkin pie and fried chicken free, and the radio reads out the menu.

Chapter 120: The Dome Hall

The grand dinner is in the Grand Museum's dome hall, at round tables with crowns, peoples and citizens mixed and no high table. Maren runs the kitchen with Dorrit Hake and Tahl, the merfolk cook their lantern prawns at their own station, and the king stands at a stove at his own dinner, carving the beef with his sauce. Hulda tells him he boiled the dwarves' mushroom correctly. Imani's cards sit on every table in every language, with a plain dish beside every mana dish.

A Velmoor lord's son strikes a waiter, Wim Huckle, for not bowing. A knight in a stealth suit who has been standing three tables away unseen has the young man's wrist before the plate hits the floor, and walks him out quietly. Wim also sees a Velmoor lord call the tiramisu a pudding of coffee grounds and then take a second helping when he thinks nobody is looking, and a Duvrenne count who bragged of a strong stomach go quiet after one helping of the mana food and very politely refuse another.

Severin bows to Saldis first, in public, before anyone, and every envoy writes it down. Isaure spends the evening with Amara, back in her own body, and Harrold doesn't let go of Amara's hand while old lords whisper about necromancy. Saldis says the pumpkin soup reminds her of something from before the leaving. Corvan eats nothing. Severin eats the tiramisu slowly, asks its name, gets one word, and asks for a second. Isaure asks Maren for the method, and before midnight Maren's handwritten recipe is the first recipe in the Grand Library. The toast is short, because Ferra cut it.

Chapter 121: I Never Started

After the dinner, in Severin's long room at the top of the needle, with the two families at two tables and nothing recorded, his steward pours, and Emeric takes the glass. "I thought you swore off drinking." "I never started." Emeric tells him the drunk prince was a mask and doesn't say whose idea it was. He drinks one glass, slowly.

Severin says that on paper Emeric is his subject, that they both know what paper is worth here, and that he would like it to keep being worth something. He has read the review that censured the king, and he says that some of his court took it for weakness. He doesn't. A king bound by his own paper is the only kind worth signing with. Then he offers his eldest daughter. Emeric says he has two wives and is capped out on wives, and Ferra, across the room, says "He is" without looking up. Emeric adds that he is forty-four, which surprises the emperor for the second time that night. They go down the family looking for another husband and find everybody taken, until Corbin, at the next table, says "No" without turning round. Then Severin tells him what he will do if the pope calls the Gathering a breach, and Kaldo agrees to route its Kesrath compact through imperial ratification, so that he has a public win to stand beside the risk.

Before she sleeps, Celestine tells her father she would have said no herself. She wants the Library. He believes her.

Chapter 122: Seven

On Saturday 1 November, on the knights' parade ground, the families sit together on the stand. Prince Marius, nineteen, who has never lost a fight because nobody dares beat the heir, gets his duel. Emeric uses no magic of his own, only cancelling each of Marius's fire spells with its exact reverse. He disarms him, hands the sword back and taps him where a cut would land, until the boy stops trying to win and starts trying to learn.

Rowena asks for the dragon form and goes for his life, every strike at a mortal place. Corvan leaves the stand when the dragon stands up. Ward is on his feet by the second exchange but never stops it, because control never slips. She ends on her back, alive and laughing, and when Emeric changes back his hands won't stop shaking until he has eaten twice. Severin asks Harrold when he raised such a monster. "I didn't. She had him till seven. Kaldo had him after." Maeve fights him last, both in partial dragon form, and it is the hardest of the three. He wins it too. Corbin sits it out. Afterwards Sohra tells Maeve the partial forms were better than she expected. What Severin takes home is the parade ground: one man holding back exactly as far as each opponent asked.

Maeve's bout is fought under a clear morning sky, her storm's partial form called and not brought by weather, which until last year she could not do. That is why Sohra tells her afterwards the partial forms were better than she expected: she knows what a weather-door changer usually looks like without weather.

Chapter 123: Powder

On Sunday 2 November Cardinal Benedikt opens his sealed letter at the altar of Saint Edwy's and reads it aloud: the Holy See declares the old peoples' entry into Ostrellin a breach of the Leaving Terms. The same words are read that morning in every parish of the Core, Velmoor and Solreth. Doran stands beside him and does not stop him. Then Benedikt sits down in the front pew for the rest of the week, because the letter's second instruction was to watch.

Countess Henrike Marrant of Velmoor hears it from the third pew and stays. That afternoon she takes tea in the harbour flat of a Velmoor-born nurse she once trained under, with a bath, a refrigerator, a telephone and a window on the bay, and works out that a Kaldo dockworker lives better than her duke. She writes it down. Runa tells Emeric that Marius's staring at her tail doesn't bother her, because she has no time for boys and her revenge comes first, and he pats her head. In the crowded halls of the peoples she smells powder on a servant, tells the nearest stealth knight, and stands still while the pistol is taken from his coat without a scene. The man was afraid because his master's household had been told the peoples eat men. Saban finds no one behind him.

The stealth knight Runa tells about the powder speaks into his collar before his hand moves.

Chapter 124: The Guest Edict

An imperial minor noble who would not leave a Kaldo woman alone at the river market was arrested on Saturday night. On Sunday his chamberlain asks Severin to intervene, and Severin declines. He is in their country. On Monday 3 November, in the hall of the peoples and before every delegation, Severin reads the Guest Edict in his own voice and signs it: any member of the peoples in his lands by invitation is under his protection, and no imperial soldier, magistrate or coin may be used to harm one, whatever the pope proclaims. It doesn't undo the Terms. It takes away the Church's permission wherever his law runs. Kaldo announces that its Kesrath compact will go through imperial ratification. At the Polytechnic that afternoon Marius asks to stay and study, and his father says no, this year.

A Solreth courtier on his crown's accredited list offers the ferry guard at the South Sentinel landing a purse to take him across to the Standing Hall. Because he is accredited he is not arrested. He is declared unwelcome and put on Tuesday's ship, and Ferra writes to Solreth herself to say why.

Severin asks why every clock in Kaldo agrees. The answer is a single time: the master clock in the exchange sends a signal down the wire and out by relay every hour, and every tower, station and clinic sets itself by it. Ottoline keeps it. He writes that down too.

Chapter 125: On a Tuesday

On Tuesday 4 November a dock worker, Osk Tallam, comes into the hospital with his hand crushed under a cargo net, and the guests who opted into the hospital tour watch from the theatre's gallery. Imani doesn't stop for them. She and a mender close the torn vessels and knit the broken bones within the hour, without a scar, and she tells Osk himself, before anyone else, that the tendons will take weeks of work. Nothing grows back that wasn't there. In the gallery Cardinal Benedikt writes down exactly what he saw: no ceremony, no reverence and no price, on a Tuesday. He adds nothing to it.

The ward compound is in the line while Imani works on Osk Tallam's hand, its first use before strangers, and she spends less of him than she would have. Bettany says so for the gallery, because nobody else would think to ask.

Chapter 126: Dead Ground

On Wednesday 5 November the guests ride the electric buses on the highway and the maglev test track. Then, in the Works Town and an emptied test block, under a published map with Ward holding the abort and Hulda and an imperial engineer as witnesses, Kaldo blows a stone on purpose. The dragons, the merfolk and every guest whose body leans on mana stay outside the line by their own choice. The wind goes through the street and the stone lamps die. The electric lamps, the pumps, the radio and the trains don't. Marius reaches for his fire and there is nothing there for the first time in his life.

"Magic may leave this street," Emeric says. "The pumps and lamps you're watching run on tested mundane equipment." Then everyone walks back into Kaldo's dense air and the Mage Corps lifts a bridge span in front of them. Later, in a west-facing glass flat, the Sabir point out that their houses stay cool without a machine, and Emeric agrees out loud.

After the cool houses Tahl asks, as he said he would, what Kaldo's charge would cost as the Lift's backup. Emeric's terms, said once: the rota calls it, not Kaldo; the Sabir can end it on a season's notice; and the method is taught, so the backup can become theirs. Tahl takes the terms home to the rota.

Chapter 127: The Joint Note

On Thursday 6 November the merfolk's engineer, Ysmay, shows the hall pitting on the bronze impeller of the pump that lifts seawater to her people's baths, where two metals in seawater are eating the weaker one. Kaldo's engineers admit the flaw. Hulda offers glass-iron, the metal the sea can't eat, at the Sea Hold's price, which Ysmay resents out loud. Solreth's engineer, Benno Sarrat, won't sign a fix that needs either Kaldo's yard or the Sea Hold's forges, so the joint note uses glass-iron where it can be bought and a cast zinc collar that any foundry can pour where it can't. It carries four signatures, and Kaldo's goes last. Ferra stops Emeric's lecture with a hand on his arm, and he praises glass-iron in public instead. That afternoon Hulda takes her seat at the inquiry into the dark stones, which records Emeric's guess as a guess and adjourns.

In the district court, in public, the Velmoor lord's son is convicted of assault, fined, and ordered to pay Wim Huckle damages, and an apology is read in open court because Wim said he wanted one. The imperial noble is convicted of harassment, and the frightened servant of carrying a weapon where weapons were barred. Each one's envoy sits in the gallery. Severin sends nobody.

Chapter 128: A Week Late

On Friday 7 November the orcs arrive, a week after everyone else, because on Hollow Night they kept their own vigil for their dead at the Isle's doors. They bring a cart of pumpkin seed from their river farms. Their rooms were kept empty for a week and nobody billed them for it. Then they go to look at the ring road and kindly point out that their road-binder sets without a kiln. Bram asks for the recipe and they make him wait. At noon they greet Saldis on the castle roof in the words the old passage treaty prescribes, and she answers them in kind. That night the kitchen cooks Hollow Night again for them, with their own ox-roaster and clay-pot cook at the stations, and Maren's tiramisu goes round again. The orcs ask for the recipe and get it, and Bram gets his binder recipe on a napkin in exchange.

The night before, on the castle roof, Harrold asked Saldis through Grau for the one thing four years of lessons had not given him, anything smaller than a wing. Dragons' eldest do not explain. She held out her own human hand, put one claw out of it, and waited. He got it on the fourth try and looked at it for a long time. That is how the hatchlings look at their first, she said, and then: it still is not an answer. He said he had not asked one. Corvan watched from the roof stair and went down without a word.

At dawn on Saturday Saldis tells Emeric, once, that this was a visit, not an answer. Corvan is exactly as cold as he was over the bay. The three dragons go down the sea road, and the Outriders turn back at the Shell's edge.

Chapter 129: Returned with Reasons

The guests go home over the week. On Monday 10 November Celestine asks Odile Ashdown what it would take to deposit a copy of the imperial archive, and takes the form: a rights line, a catalogue first, the film in checked batches, her father's seal on each. Mirela asks for Mochi a third time and is told Mochi chooses where it sleeps. The K Sorrel takes the family home and sets down on the crater lake as she came, and Marius looks back at the towers until the wing hides them.

On Monday 17 November a Library panel that includes a Duvrenne surveyor returns the Polytechnic's first-year mechanics text with written reasons: a worked example puts the load in the wrong place and gets the answer wrong by a factor of four. The same morning the panel shelves Countess Marrant's account, The Hollowing of Velmoor, which calls Kaldo's border towns a pump draining her country of nurses and machinists, with no note but its receipt number. Duvrenne takes the Holding Board's foreign seat, without a vote. At breakfast the next day Emeric reads the return letter, finds that the chapter's author was himself, and laughs until Ferra takes it off him to read.

The post-visit report is printed in full: one assault, one harassment, one bribe and one courtier sent home, one pistol, one mild reaction to the mana food and the warning card rewritten, the dark stones and an inquiry with a foreign seat, no guest hurt, no private room entered. Keswick's legation reports that the Church's parish clerks in the Core have slowed every imperial filing since the edict, and expect to for a season. Keswick adds one sentence of his own: his Majesty signed it anyway. On Saturday 22 November something comes up the Rim's outer fetch line and is driven off, the eighth pin on the board, seventy-two days after the seventh. On Friday 28 November Wystan's exemption for the eastern hills' ironworks runs out on its date, and he doesn't try to extend it. On Sunday 30 November the Year 23 budget's papers are on Ferra's desk, with the sheet on slowing the groves on top.

Where the Story Stands After Chapter 129

The Gathering happened, and it held. Eight peoples and every crown on the continent spent a week in Kaldo. A dwarven engineer sits on an inquiry into why her people's stones went dark, and the king's only answer so far is a guess he wrote down as a guess. The pope has called the visit a breach. The emperor answered with his own law and is already paying for it in parish clerks, and he went home with a runway's length in his own hand and the parade ground in his head. His daughter wants the Library, and his son wants the Polytechnic and has been told to wait.

The institutions Kaldo built met people who owe Kaldo nothing. A court tried an emperor's man and the emperor let it. A library panel shelved the sharpest attack on Kaldo yet written and sent back the king's own textbook. A people that won't let humans tell its story took its labels back. Emeric could order none of it, and he laughed at one of them.

Past the wall the attacks keep coming closer together, and the dragons have gone home with nothing promised. Corvan is unchanged. The Year 23 budget is waiting, with the vote on slowing the groves at the top of the pile.
