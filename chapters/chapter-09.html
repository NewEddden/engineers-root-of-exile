Chapter 9 - The Summons

The old floorboards creaked under Ward's hurried steps, fast enough that Emeric knew before the door opened.

"We might have a problem." Ward held out a folded letter, still sealed. "Came direct from the capital. I've seen this packaging before. It's the royal court's official summons stock."

Emeric took it and turned it over once. Royal seal, unbroken. Wexler's timing exactly as quick as the report had made it sound.

"Guess the baron's feeling froggy," he said, and knew before the sentence finished that it hadn't landed.

Ward didn't ask. He'd stopped asking about most of what came out of the prince's mouth these days.

Emeric opened the letter.

The seal was intact. Randillia crest, pressed deep, no wax cracks from handling. This had come by rider, not relay.

He broke it and read fast, the way he read everything now. Structure first, content second.

By order of the crown's judiciary, on petition of Baron Wexler of Kaldo, Prince Emeric Stelan Randillia is called to answer questions regarding the administration of Kaldo barony, specifically financial irregularities alleging misuse of discharged debt records. Advocate Keller has been assigned to receive testimony and evidence from all relevant parties prior to a formal hearing.

He read it again. It didn't change.

"He petitioned first," Emeric said. "Before I had anything to bring Keller myself."

Ward's jaw moved, once. "Meaning?"

"Meaning I know exactly how this process works. I've used it before." Emeric set the letter down flat on the desk and smoothed a crease that wasn't there. "I just don't get to run it. He does. Right now, today, I'm not the one bringing evidence to Keller. I'm the one Keller's been told to investigate."

Ward read the letter twice himself before handing it back. "How long do we have?"

"Petition's dated six days ago. Travel time from the capital to here, plus processing." Emeric turned the page over, checking the back for a return date he already knew wasn't there. "Which means whatever Wexler told them, they've been sitting with it the better part of a week. We're not early to this. We're catching up."

"You said Keller before. You know him."

"Used him once. Different matter, different arc of my life, so to speak." He caught himself before that sentence went any further sideways. "He's fair. That's the problem. He won't take my word for anything just because I'm the one holding a crown mandate. He'll want what Ferra's got, and Ferra's got a route, not a name."

"Is that enough to walk in with?"

"It's enough to walk in with something instead of nothing. It's not enough to walk in and win."

Ward set the practice blade he'd carried up with him, out of habit more than intent, against the doorframe. "You're going yourself."

It wasn't a question. Emeric answered it like one anyway. "I'm not sending a courier to answer a summons with my name on it. That's not how this works, and even if it were, I wouldn't do it that way."

"That's not an engineering answer."

"No." Emeric almost smiled. "It's not."

He found Ferra before the hour was out, the letter still folded in his coat where he could feel its edge against his ribs like a splinter he hadn't pulled yet.

She read it once, faster than Ward had, ledger-speed, the way she read everything.

"He's not accusing you of anything specific. Look at the wording. Irregularities. Financial administration. He hasn't named Cobb, hasn't named the debt, hasn't named a figure."

"Because he doesn't know what I have."

"He doesn't know what you have," Ferra agreed. "But he knows something's moving. That's why he went first. Not because he's confident. Because he isn't."

Emeric turned that over the way he turned the crystal over, without quite meaning to. A man sure of his footing doesn't need to define the ground before anyone else gets a vote on it. Whatever Wexler's petition technically was by now, it wasn't a man closing a door. It was a man choosing which room the fight happened in.

"What do you need," Emeric asked, "to get the route findings to something Keller can't wave off as speculation?"

Ferra didn't answer right away, which from her was its own kind of answer. "More time than you're going to give me. But I can get you closer. Not proof. Closer."

"Closer's what I've got room for."

She almost smiled at that, tired around the edges of it. "Everything's what you've got room for, lately."

He left before dark, the letter still in his coat, Ferra's half-finished work promised to follow by the fastest rider Bram trusted with something this quiet. Ward didn't argue about coming along, which meant he'd already decided he was, and Emeric didn't waste breath pretending otherwise.

The last thing he did before the horses were saddled was find the crystal in his pocket again, turning it once between two fingers, the same reflex it had become without his permission.

Whatever he'd found underneath the loop three nights ago was still there. Unnamed. Unfiled. Waiting the way it had waited before he'd ever noticed it. He didn't have time to go back and look at it properly, so he filed that too, next to Tam's name and the discharged debt's date. One more thing that would have to wait its turn to be understood.

He rode toward the capital not knowing which of the things he carried would matter more before this was over.
