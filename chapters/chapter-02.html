Book 2 Synopsis 124–126: The Answer

This is the final batch of Book 2, covering May 8 through June 14, Year 18. These three chapters conclude the novel's central question: When Emeric has the power to act, who has the authority to tell him no?

The chapters remain planned synopses, not drafted manuscript.

Chapter 124 - The Gate

May 8–13, Year 18

On Saturday morning, Dacey, an Islander net-mender whose brother Bryn died in the Isle disaster, stands at Kaldo's castle gate. She carries handwritten copies of the two warnings her brother gave before his death.

Emeric comes down to meet her alone, without Ward or any ceremonial escort. She doesn't curtsey. Instead, she hands him the warnings.

Although Emeric has already read the originals three times, he reads both copies completely. Then, before Dacey can accuse him, he acknowledges what happened.

Her brother warned the works twice. His first warning was filed as insufficient to stop the excavation. The shaft wasn't stopped until three weeks after the second warning, by which time the damage had already been done. The exception that permitted the work to continue was authorized under Emeric's own clause, and Harrold signed it.

Dacey already knows. She has read the record.

What she wants to know is what the king intends to do about it.

Emeric explains that he will not decide the consequences himself. Even if he tries to be fair, he knows he could unconsciously decide in his own favour. Instead, he intends to establish an independent Isle Works Review, where the families of the dead will have the right to question everyone involved, including him.

Dacey asks whether he will answer those questions and whether he will obey the review's findings. He promises both, within the crown's authority, and commits to putting that promise in writing.

She offers no gratitude and asks for nothing personally. She intends to stay until the review sits.

Later, Harrold tells Emeric that he would sign the exception again, given the information he possessed in September. He also recognizes that the information available now changes the judgment of what happened. He volunteers to testify first, but Emeric refuses to make that decision for the review.

On Sunday, in Magistrate Keller's working room, the legal terms are established.

The review will include Keller, three Assembly delegates, Mother Sabbe representing the Island families, and a place reserved for a representative chosen by the orc families whose dead lie beneath the buried city.

The review will examine the decisions that permitted the work to continue, determine the crown's responsibility, consider compensation, and decide the crown's position on the excavation and the chamber beneath the Isle. Its authority cannot extend unilaterally over Duvrenne or the Islanders, whose rights remain protected under the Compact.

Emeric signs a binding commitment to answer questions, obey the findings on the crown's share, pay compensation from the crown's purse, and publish the findings. Harrold signs alongside him without being asked.

On Monday, the two men repeat their signatures publicly on the records hall steps, where Emeric previously delivered the King's Word.

Some citizens accuse the king of buying himself a favourable jury. Others defend the process. Ferra, recused from the review, stands among the crowd and records both reactions.

Meanwhile, the first communication from Kaldo's board to Kettrin's gift set reaches the orc treaty-house. Ferra informs the families about the disaster, confirms that their protective zone was not disturbed, and offers them a place on the review. Hull five departs carrying the full record and four communication sets, three sold to the treaty-house and one given to the families.

On Wednesday, the Assembly elects its three representatives. Among them is the mill lord who routinely opposes Emeric. He openly declares that he will find against the king if the evidence supports it. The crews' representative promises to follow the evidence wherever it leads.

Finally, a letter arrives from Kesrath granting Emeric permission to visit Duvrenne's half of the Isle. Emeric accepts the permission but places the letter in a drawer. He will not visit the site in his capacity as king while the review is examining his decisions.

The chapter closes with the review established, an opponent elected to judge the evidence, and an empty chair waiting for people who have not yet agreed to participate.

Chapter 125 - Take Your Beast Back

May 11–23, Year 18

While the review is being assembled, Emeric gathers Ferra, Imani, Armand, and Saban around the long table.

He has decided to send the pope something recovered from the dead Drifter: one of its gold eyes.

Alongside it, he intends to send a short handwritten message questioning whether the creature is the Church's creation.

The others object for different reasons.

Ferra warns that the message threatens the delicate balance maintained by the Letters. Their arrangement with the pope works because she has more to lose from exposing certain secrets than from maintaining the peace. Sending an unidentified creature's eye into her private correspondence could disturb that balance.

Armand points out that Emeric is asking a known liar a question whose answer he cannot verify.

Saban worries that using the Church's private communication route to transport a sealed specimen will attract attention and possibly compromise the route permanently.

Imani offers a more scientific objection.

She has examined the Church's juggernaut, a constructed being whose biological blood typing resembles that of a human. The creature from the Isle is different. Its blood does not fit the known classifications, although it contains a biological strand that Imani has encountered in other peoples.

Whatever produced the Drifter, she is confident it wasn't the Church's usual manufacturing process.

Emeric agrees.

He doesn't truly believe the pope created it. He wants to find out how someone who has always seemed to know more than he does will react when accused of creating something she fears or doesn't recognize.

He is looking for the reaction, not necessarily the answer.

But the specimen isn't his property alone. The eyes belong to the Isle Compact, with Kaldo acting as custodian. Ferra insists on authorization from the owners.

Oskel signs his consent through Waylon's communication set. Mother Sabbe gives hers from the Isle, declaring that the eye should be returned to wherever it came from. House Ducatis releases the specimen from escrow.

In the Motion Shop, Tess prepares the eye.

The golden crystalline organ is placed inside a sealed glass jar of spirit. The glass preserves its contained mana but does not conceal its presence. The jar is therefore enclosed inside a specially gasketed iron box that can hide its magical signature during transport.

The other eye and the creature's core are transferred to their new steel-and-glass case of record.

Emeric personally writes the message, without a draft or any sheet underneath it:

Is this one your doing? Take your beast back.

He does not sign it.

On May 14, a House Ducatis rider departs with the box, carrying instructions to deliver it through the pope's private chamberlain.

Nine days later, the package reaches Ostrel.

The chamberlain examines the message under a lamp, checking for hidden impressions or marks. Finding none, he brings the container to the pope.

She opens the iron case and lifts the glass jar toward the light.

The chamberlain observes her turning her hands over, an old, familiar habit whose significance he has never understood.

Then she orders him out.

An hour passes without a sound from behind the closed door.

Finally, she summons him and requests paper, no official seal, and the rider who delivered the package.

The chamberlain follows her instructions.

For the first time in his service, he deliberately leaves something out of his records.

Chapter 126 - Paper Thirteen

May 28 – June 14, Year 18

The final chapter opens with the orc families' response arriving through Kettrin's communication set.

They will not appoint their representative until they have read the full record themselves. They insist that no finding concerning the buried chamber, their protective zone, or their dead may be made before their representative takes a seat. They also intend to select their own visitors to the Isle once the protective zone has survived a full winter.

The review will respect those conditions.

Mother Sabbe arrives in Kaldo aboard the K Endeavor, carrying dried fish for the Islander families. She has not left the Isle in forty years. Standing before the city's great structures, she remarks on the breathing of its mana field.

She also reports that the dead creature's carcass is turning grey as its mana drains into the condenser.

Then, on June 1, the Ducatis rider returns with an extraordinary letter.

There is no official seal.

Ferra recognizes something unusual before she reads a single word: the handwriting uses letter forms that have been obsolete for approximately two thousand years. The writer uses them naturally, not as someone copying an ancient script.

At the top of the page, someone began writing a name and violently crossed it out.

Ferra can make out three letters.

Ava.

She enters them into her uncertainty notes without drawing a conclusion.

The body of the letter is nothing like anything she has ever received from the Holy See.

The writer furiously accuses Emeric of summoning something dangerous, destroying two thousand years of planning, and doing it because he wanted to be kind.

Below the angry accusations, in smaller handwriting, is practical information.

The thing beneath the island remains dormant until air enters its chamber. Approximately seven weeks later, it awakens.

The letter states that the creature came from beneath Emeric's island, that similar chambers exist elsewhere in the world, and that he should burn the chamber before it awakens again.

The writer never identifies the mysterious them supposedly called by Emeric.

Ferra, Emeric, and Imani examine the letter privately.

For Emeric, the most important revelation isn't the insult or even the warning.

For the first time, the enemy is afraid.

The pope, who has demonstrated knowledge and confidence beyond anything he understands, is terrified of what emerged from the Isle. If the Drifters were not created by the Church, then the pope knows something about their origins and fears whoever made them.

That possibility is more troubling than discovering the Church had made the beast herself.

The following day, Ferra compares the letter's technical claim with the works records.

The Drifter emerged on April 19.

The last explosive charges were fired at the bottom of the shaft on March 2.

Forty-eight days separate the two events, almost exactly seven weeks.

The correspondence is too close to dismiss.

The breach occurred before the March 24 stop order. The later attempts to close the shaft therefore could not reverse the original damage.

The decisive action was the work permitted under Harrold's emergency exception, which depended on Emeric's clause.

Emeric recognizes the responsibility immediately.

Yet the letter also presents an urgent problem: whether to burn the buried chamber.

He refuses.

He has already surrendered the decision concerning the hollow to the Isle Works Review. He cannot promise to surrender authority and then reclaim it whenever an emergency makes his own judgment more convenient.

The site also belongs to other parties under the Compact and lies beside the dead of the orc families, who have explicitly requested that nothing be done without their participation.

And the advice to burn it comes from an enemy whose motives are unknown.

Emeric acknowledges that refusing to burn it could create another catastrophe. If that happens, he will have to account for this decision as well.

Ferra copies the letter's verifiable technical claim into the official works record as Paper Thirteen, preserving its source. The complete letter is delivered under seal to Keller.

The king does not conceal the evidence, but neither does he expose information that the review has not yet decided how to handle.

Meanwhile, the Church begins spreading its own interpretation of events.

Across the northern chapels, priests deliver nearly identical sermons connecting the Isle creature to the gold-eyed King of Randillia. The phrase beasts of the crown spreads, soon appearing in broadsheets associated with the more aggressive members of the Heirs.

On June 10, a second letter arrives from Ostrel.

This one carries the official seal of the Holy See.

Its language is formal, restrained, and cold. The Church denies sending any earlier correspondence, states that the specimen was burned as unclean, offers prayers for those killed, and suggests that the king must answer for whatever emerged from his land.

Ferra places the letters beside each other.

Two completely different hands, from the same seat of power.

Emeric concludes that the Letters' arrangement still restrains the Church from direct confrontation. But he also recognizes that this protection is temporary. Once independent hunters begin discovering and trading the magical stones inside beasts, the Church's control of those secrets will weaken.

He cannot predict how long that will take.

On Saturday, June 12, the Isle Works Review formally opens.

Before administering any oath, Magistrate Keller reads the names of all fourteen people killed in the disaster.

The Assembly's three delegates are present. Mother Sabbe sits with her stick across her knees. Duvrenne's observer occupies a side table.

The orc families' chair remains empty, with their letter placed upon it.

The families of the dead are given their own bench.

Keller confirms the review's authority, accepts the condition placed by the orc families, and enters Paper Thirteen into the proceedings. Honor Linden is assigned to produce the official copy.

Harrold asks to testify first.

Keller refuses.

The exception was made possible by the king's clause. The king will therefore be examined first.

That afternoon, fourteen names are placed on the wall of the First Room.

Neris personally checks every name. She finds one Islander's name spelled incorrectly in the Randillia fashion and insists that it be corrected before the room closes.

Nothing else about the Isle is displayed. The dead come first.

Finally, on Monday, June 14, the review begins taking evidence.

The hearing room is full.

The families occupy their bench. The mill lord has his pen ready. Ferra sits among the public, recused from the proceeding, with nothing in her hands.

Keller calls Emeric.

The King of Randillia walks to a plain witness chair positioned below the magistrate's bench.

He sits.

For once, he is not the person responsible for determining what should happen next. He is not deciding which facts matter or whose judgment should prevail.

He is there to answer.

Keller begins with a straightforward question.

When did Emeric write the clause that authorized the exception?

Emeric answers:

August 29, last year.

And Book 2 ends.

The final image is deliberately different from the opening of the story: Emeric still has the power of a king, the strength of a dragon, and the ability to reshape the world. But he is sitting below someone who can question his decisions—and he has agreed to be bound by a judgment he does not control.

Book 2 concludes at Chapter 126.
