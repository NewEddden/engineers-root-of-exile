Book 2 Synopsis 115–123: The Isle Batch 18 — The Isle | Year 18, March 20–May 8 This batch covers the awakening of the first Drifter, the battle on the Isle of Shards, Emeric's first full dragon transformation, and the political consequences of the disaster. The following is a chapter-by-chapter prose synopsis of the proposed story, not the drafted manuscript. Chapter 115 — The Datum On March 20, a second warning arrives from the Isle of Shards. Bryn, an Islander shard cutter who raised concerns months earlier, reports that the heartbeat beneath the island has grown faster and the warm rock has moved closer to the excavation shaft. Waylon forwards the warning to Kaldo and adds that he believes Bryn. Tess investigates the old survey records and discovers a serious mapping error. When Kaldo and Duvrenne combined their survey systems after the armistice, the reference point was shifted approximately forty yards. The result is that the excavation shaft is forty yards closer to the mysterious underground hollow than anyone realized. Emeric immediately orders the drilling stopped. Waylon obeys within the hour, and Harrold accepts responsibility for having authorized the original exception that allowed the work to continue. What nobody knows is that the decisive damage was already done on March 2, when blasting charges cracked the hollow's seal. Emeric decides against personally visiting the Isle. His mana sense would be overwhelmed by the thousands of charged stones, and his presence as king would create complications under the treaty governing the island. Instead, Ward assembles an inspection party consisting of Mira, two Crestless, Neris, Tess and Imani. Neris volunteers because she possesses an unusual ability to listen through rock and water. The party boards the K Endeavor on April 10. Emeric watches the ship leave, uneasy about sending others into danger but convinced that their abilities make them better suited to the investigation. Chapter 116 — Two Beats The inspection party reaches the Isle on April 11. Waylon receives them at the jetty, while Mother Sabbe welcomes them among the Islander families. Neris begins investigating the strange heartbeat. She listens through the rock beneath the shaft and even submerges her head in the cove at slack water. She hears something enormous beating underground, accompanied by a second, slightly delayed rhythm that sounds like an echo. The heartbeat continues accelerating. Tess lowers measuring instruments into the excavation shaft and discovers a steady current of air flowing downward. The pressure readings confirm that the hollow's outer chamber has been breached and is drawing in the island's atmosphere. Ward orders the shaft head cleared and nearby workers evacuated. Mother Sabbe moves the families without argument. Not everyone accepts the danger. Oskel, Duvrenne's commander, believes Kaldo is exaggerating the problem, while the works foreman protests the loss of valuable oil. Waylon sides with Tess and Ward. The decision is made to seal the shaft using a massive dustcrete plug supported by sandbags and timber. Six volunteers undertake the work, including Bryn, who insists on helping close the hole he warned everyone about. By Sunday night, April 18, the plug has been poured but remains wet. Meanwhile, Ward visits the six ancient statues standing guard at the buried city's doors. They face outward, as though protecting something behind them. He remains there silently before returning to camp. Chapter 117 — Up the Shaft At approximately 5:40 on the morning of April 19, something emerges through the wet dustcrete. It is initially no larger than a dog, with dark mineral armour resembling wet basalt and two unnaturally large golden eyes. Three men standing watch approach it with a lamp, believing it to be an animal. Within seconds, all three are dead. The creature moves toward the works' charged-stone storage shed and begins consuming the stones. Its body grows as it feeds, and the crystal beneath its feet turns grey and lifeless. Waylon raises the alarm and manages to send a message to Kaldo before the creature destroys the relay mast. He reports that something came up the shaft, killed three men, is consuming the stones, and is growing. In Kaldo, Amara uses the relay network to observe the island directly. She immediately informs Emeric. The K Amara launches at full speed, carrying Emeric, Ferra, Armand, Arthur, Bren, the flight knights and the dragon rifles. Back on the Isle, Kaldo's and Duvrenne's defenders are discovering how dangerous the creature is. It extinguishes magical attacks before they can land, but ordinary weapons can injure it. The defenders employ cannons, 20 mm guns, steel weapons and the inward-enhanced strength of the knights. At approximately 9:30, Waylon orders the first firing of the AM-T2 dead-zone breaker. The round creates a zone approximately one hundred yards across, extinguishing nearby magic and shutting down the creature's crystal vanes. Its growth stops. Its spell-cancelling ability disappears. But the creature does not collapse. It continues moving under its own physical strength. Watching it, Ward recognizes something horrifyingly familiar. "It's doing what we do." The creature is employing inward strength, just as the knights do. At 9:41, a sailor aboard the K Endeavor makes an apparently unimportant entry in the ship's log: a small seal has entered the water near the cove rocks. Unknown to everyone, this is the second creature—the Broodmother—escaping the island while the defenders are occupied. Chapter 118 — The Last Lamp Five surviving members of the plug team are trapped beneath fallen timbers in a side gallery. The main shaft is inaccessible without risking another encounter with the beast. Ward leads Mira, Neris and Imani through an alternative route beneath the island. They enter an ancient cave, passing Vey's chamber, inactive golems and the six statues standing guard at the old doors. As they move through the passage, the charged stones around them begin losing their power. Their instruments fail, their magical lighting dies, and the darkness closes in. Only Ward's ordinary oil lamp continues burning. Neris guides them by listening through the rock. At one point, she orders everyone to stop because the creature is moving directly above them. Eventually, they reach the collapsed gallery and break through the rock. In the lamplight, Ward sees the eyes of a survivor trapped beneath the timbers. Beyond the injured man, something small moves away through the darkness toward the cove. Ward does not pursue it. He has people to rescue. Then the main beast returns toward the shaft. Ward realizes that a carefully placed explosive charge could collapse the roof onto its head and neck. For a moment, he has an excellent opportunity to inflict devastating damage. But detonating the charge would kill the five trapped workers. He abandons the attack. The rescue begins. Four workers are successfully extracted, but Bryn remains pinned beneath the main timber. Imani tries to save him, taking precautions against crush syndrome before releasing his legs. Despite her efforts, Bryn's heart stops during the evacuation. Ward carries his body out himself. Elsewhere, Waylon is defending the works camp's gate alongside Kaldo's guards and Oskel's soldiers. The last families are being evacuated toward the cove. During the third hour of fighting, with the creature looming over the gate and Duvrenne's second cannon destroyed, Waylon's eyes suddenly turn gold. Mother Sabbe witnesses the change and speaks a word in the Islanders' language that Waylon understands. He does not transform, and he does not understand what is happening to him. Chapter 119 — Gold After approximately three hours at maximum speed, the K Amara reaches the Isle of Shards. From above, Ferra sees devastation: dead crystal fields, burning buildings, ruined defensive positions and an enormous creature approximately thirty yards long. At the works gate, Waylon stands with a pike, holding the road between the beast and the remaining people. The K Amara opens fire with two dragon rifles, striking the beast through its shoulder and neck armour. The creature turns toward the attacking ship. Arthur orders the weapons to cease firing. Emeric has already jumped from the apron. He descends toward the gate using his gravity envelope, intending to save his brother. As the creature's foot comes down toward Waylon, Emeric's body reacts before he consciously decides what to do. He transforms completely. For the first time, Emeric becomes a full dragon. His entire body changes in midair—scales, horns, wings and massive physical strength. He lands directly on the creature. The resulting battle is fought primarily through physical force. His magical gravity attacks fail because the beast can cancel them, forcing Emeric to rely on his dragon body. He attacks with claws, mass and leverage, then directs gravity-pressed dragon fire into the wounds opened by the rifles. Because this fire originates from his own body rather than a conventional outward spell, the creature cannot cancel it in the same way. Arthur, Bren and the flight knights attack its legs, while Mira emerges from the cave and joins the fighting. Together, they bring the creature down. Emeric pins it against the grey crystal field and burns through its exposed neck until it finally stops moving. Its golden eyes fade last. Emeric tries to speak Waylon's name, but his new throat produces only a roar containing the rough shape of the word. Waylon understands. The transformation has consumed so much mana that Emeric cannot return to human form. He remains a dragon. Chapter 120 — Seven Hours For the next seven and a half hours, Emeric lies on the crystal field under the open sky, unable to transform back. The battle has ended, but the human cost is only beginning to become clear. Ferra sits against his foreleg with her notebook, recording the dead and reading their names aloud. Imani moves between the wounded and Emeric, examining him and confirming that his heart is sound despite his extreme mana depletion. Waylon stays beside his brother. After a long silence, he admits that he does not believe he was worth flying a thousand miles to save. Emeric answers with a sound Ferra records simply as no. Around them, the Islanders and the works crews struggle to understand what they have witnessed. Mother Sabbe approaches the dragon, studies his eye, and walks away. A six-year-old Islander girl screams when Emeric turns toward her. Even after he returns to human form, she refuses to approach him for the remainder of the week. Emeric does not force the issue. Meanwhile, Oskel prepares an official report to Duvrenne. Although he has never liked Emeric, he records what he witnessed accurately: a creature emerged from Kaldo's excavation, and the King of Randillia transformed into a dragon and destroyed it. At Kesrath's tower, a Church priest has watched the battle through a long glass. He prepares a coded account for Ostrel, connecting the golden eyes of the creature with those of the king. In Kaldo, Saban receives the verified news and recognizes the danger of allowing the Church to control the story. He persuades Corbin to announce the truth immediately, including Emeric's transformation, without publishing casualty names before the families are notified. By nine that evening, Kaldo News has published its announcement. At dusk, Emeric finally returns to human form. Ferra covers him with her coat. His first question concerns the dead. There are fourteen. He asks for their names, and Ferra reads them. Chapter 121 — Shut With the creature dead, Imani, Tess and the others begin examining its remains. The discoveries are deeply unsettling. Its blood does not match any known blood type. Imani also finds the same mysterious third strand she previously detected in samples collected from the different peoples aboard the fleet. The creature's anatomy provides further evidence that it did not develop like a natural animal. Its claws show no wear, its body has no ordinary scars or parasites, and its internal structures lack the expected signs of conventional growth. Armand and Emeric examine its magical signature and find no familiar biological lineage. The creature appears to have been manufactured rather than born naturally. Meanwhile, its remains release mana into the surrounding atmosphere. Tess and the works crews construct a condenser to recover the escaping mana before the carcass eventually crumbles into grey dust. Inside the creature's chest, they discover a large crystalline gait core, still active. Behind its eyes is a tiny crystalline marking that nobody can interpret. Tess constructs a sealed iron container with copper crush gaskets to preserve and isolate the core. She summarizes its condition: "It isn't dead. It's shut." Ferra then negotiates how the creature's remains will be divided under the Isle Compact. Kaldo retains the core and both eyes, Duvrenne receives selected plates and tendons, and the Islanders receive their agreed share of recovered materials. When the Church priest requests one of the eyes, Ferra refuses. The Church is not a party to the Compact. A letter is also prepared for the families connected to the ancient city's sealed doors, confirming that their protected zone was not disturbed. That evening, Emeric considers what he knows from Earth: in stories, the first monster is rarely the last. He does not explain that thought to anyone. Instead, he gives his people a practical instruction: "We don't know how many. We build as if it's a lot." Ward considers it the first sensible statement anyone has made all week. Chapter 122 — Fourteen News of the battle spreads throughout Kaldo. The public response is divided between pride, fear and suspicion. Many celebrate their king for destroying the creature, while others are disturbed by the revelation that he can become a dragon. The Heirs begin circulating a more dangerous interpretation: that a gold-eyed monster emerged from land controlled by a gold-eyed king, who then revealed himself as another extraordinary creature. Corbin refuses to engage in an immediate public argument. Instead, he ensures that the victims' names are read publicly as they are confirmed. Honor Linden is among those responsible for copying the casualty lists. When she sees the Isle of Shards listed as the place of death, she remembers her own son, who died there during the earlier war. At the next gathering of the Heirs, she remains silent. Her supporters wait for her to speak, while the more radical members continue without her. Emeric returns to Kaldo aboard the K Amara on April 23, exhausted from the transformation. Imani temporarily forbids him from flying through his core. Runa recognizes the Isle's scent on him and dislikes it, but she stays close, sitting on his feet in the castle kitchen. A further unsettling discovery occurs when Tess brings home a sealed sample of the creature's armour. Mochi, the king slime, refuses to consume it—the first thing it has ever rejected. Runa instinctively backs away as well. Ferra records Emeric's complete transformation in her case book, along with Waylon's first golden-eyed awakening. On April 24, the K Endeavor returns with the Kaldo dead. The Islanders have buried their own on the Isle, while Duvrenne's soldiers are returned to their homeland. Bryn's sister, Dacey, accompanies the ship to Kaldo. Doran conducts the funeral service for the dead brought home and enters their names into the parish death book. Neris asks whether the First Room will record what happened on the Isle. Ferra says the room will remain unchanged until an independent review has been completed. Only afterward will the names of the dead be added. Chapter 123 — The Record The immediate crisis has passed, but the question of responsibility remains. Over the following days, Ferra and Tess assemble the complete history of the excavation. Their record includes the first mysterious heartbeat beneath the island, the inaccurate survey line, the discovery of unusually warm rock, Tess's earlier request to halt drilling, Harrold's authorization to continue, Bryn's two signed warnings, the Assembly's decision to defer further surveying, the blasting charges fired in March, Emeric's eventual stop order, and finally the breach and the deaths of fourteen people. Everything is arranged chronologically, with the original signatures and dates preserved. There is no attempt to conceal the decisions that contributed to the disaster. On May 6, Emeric reads the entire record alone. Harrold then reads it, including the page carrying his own signature authorizing the emergency drilling exception. Neither man offers an explanation that evening. The following day, Ferra deliberately places Bryn's two warnings on top of the complete record, despite their chronological positions. They were the warnings that proved correct, and she wants that fact to be impossible to overlook. On the morning of May 8, Dacey arrives at the castle gate. She is a net-mender from the Isle, and she carries handwritten copies of her dead brother's warnings. She has travelled all the way to Kaldo because she wants an answer from the king whose government allowed the excavation to continue. The batch ends with Emeric walking down toward the gate to meet her. His answer—and the question of who will independently investigate the disaster—belongs to the next batch. End of Batch 18. Chapters 115–123 conclude the Isle battle and establish the consequences of Emeric's public transformation. The final two proposed chapters, 124–125, are reserved for the box sent to Ostrel, the pope's response, Emeric's decision about the investigation, and the ending of Book 2.
