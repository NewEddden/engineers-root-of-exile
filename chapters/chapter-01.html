Book 2, Batch 1: Guests

Chapters 1–9 | About 19 April – 7 May, Year 17

Book 2 opens a few days after the fleet's departure. Eight ships carrying about 830 people left Kaldo on Tuesday 14 April, Year 17, flying low and west beneath the wind. On the third night they climbed into the Band to charge their stones, and the K Discovery laid the first of the relays that let Emeric's mind travel home. Emeric sails on the K Discovery's forward deck with Sorrel at the wheel. Ferra, Ward, Armand, Maeve, Mira, Arthur and Bren ride the K Amara. Imani runs the hospital deck of the K Endeavor with Bettany beside her, and Bram and Dagny are aboard hull four. Zale and Ilenne sail on Zale's own ship. At home, Harrold holds the kingdom as regent under a sealed and written law, which means his signature binds and Emeric's wishes from a thousand miles away do not.

The purpose of the voyage is to bring Ilenne home. Her mother, the merfolk queen, answered at the wall water that she would meet them at the door herself and that the people of the dry land could come in with her daughter, in her house, by her word. She gave no date. She also gave one name in the warning colour and left it untranslated. Ilenne has kept the subject of her binding shut, and Emeric has not pushed. The fleet knows its ancestors left few friends past the wall, and its one hope of slipping in quietly is to be taken for the humans who long ago went with the old peoples willingly.

This first batch is about being a guest. Kaldo arrives with better tools than anyone it meets, and with no right to use them on anyone. Every chapter places the convoy under someone else's rule: air its bodies must learn to bear, a merfolk law Ferra can read but not rewrite, a dragon law the fleet breaks without knowing it exists, the caldera watch's terms at the ring, and a queen whose word opens one door and silences nobody. Emeric's competence stays intact. What changes is that being right no longer gives him authority.

Chapter 1: The Wall Water

About 19 to 21 April, roughly a thousand miles out, the convoy crosses into the thick water, and it does not happen gradually. There is a line on the sea, crossed in a few hundred yards, and every body aboard feels it at once.

For Emeric the sea goes loud with mana, far louder than anything he felt at home, a pressure in the sense he never turns off. For someone who has eaten the stones it is pleasant, like warmth after cold, and that is the first thing he notices and does not trust. The stones in his coat pockets warm against his ribs. The air is heavier to breathe and faintly sweet, sound carries farther across the water, and colours look a shade deeper, the way the world looks after rain. A bucket hauled up stings the hands of the unchanged like nettles. The swell runs longer and heavier, and fish rise to look at the hulls, bigger than fish should be. On the K Endeavor, Noll's test-rack dials creep upward at sea level, something they never did at home. The lift stones run sweeter. Sorrel reports that the compass is fine, and is obviously relieved.

The bodies aboard react just as quickly. The knights' enhancement starts rising on its own, so the bleed-off drills have to begin within the first hour. The unchanged grow heavy and yawn by noon. Ilenne brightens by the hour and visibly tries not to. Mira watches her own breath catch and laughs at it. The water-walking boots grip twice as hard as they did in Kaldo's bay, exactly as Emeric warned, and somebody gets thrown. It is Bren, just as Mira said it would be.

Where the thick water begins on their bearing stands a pilotage mark: a tall reef-stone pillar on an old shoal, its top set with coloured glass that catches the morning light. Ilenne explains that this is where ships wait to be fetched. Nobody has waited there in a very long time, and yet somebody still keeps the glass clean.

The routine the fleet drilled at home now becomes real. Knights hold their enhancement and burn it off on purpose. Civilians are at the cabin mark by dusk, and the hulls set down on the sea at night with sea anchors out. It lands differently by trade. Helmsmen grow slow and heavy-handed by the second watch. Ruan's gun crews carry a dull headache and can still lay a gun. The galley fires behave oddly in the thick air, and the cook is furious about it. Imani and Bettany are fine and run the dose cards. Linnet Shaw, the radio hand on Zale's ship, hides a nosebleed in the radio cabin until she drips on the log. The cost is real: a scheduled report home goes out four hours late, Amara answers the late report with a question, and Imani sets a new rule for every hull. Report the nose, or you are off watch for two days anyway. Linnet loses the night watch for a week, which she minds more than the blood. It is the first stage of the bleeding sickness, the one Kaldo already understands. On a charging climb into the Band, another relay goes out of the K Discovery's hold.

At first light Ilenne goes down Zale's ladder and calls. What answers is the creature that guarded her mother's house, the old escort that swam with the soldiers on long runs: a back the length of two hulls beneath the swell, seen only in pieces. It obeys the queen and does not like them. Through Ilenne it makes its terms known. The ships smell of the dry. It will not swim under them. It will not take the straight line either, past a stretch of water where it turns its whole body away, so the fleet goes round. It takes station a mile ahead and sets the pace, not Sorrel.

The escort does not make the sea safe. The ships that sailed west never came back, and the old saying on the islands names both killers: past the last island the water gets thick, and then it gets hungry. Imani's cards answer the thickness. The escort answers most of the hunger, because it can talk off anything that will listen, and most deep things give a queen's creature room. On the first night, at Zale's rail, Ilenne says it plainly. Most things. Nobody asks what the rest do.

Ordinary life carries on around all of it. Fletcher tends Ilenne's tank. Armand insists he is not tired. Maeve stands at the rail with Mira and the spear, bad at it and not minding. The voyage diaries come out on every deck the morning the wall is crossed, and Arthur draws the pilotage mark and gets the colours of the glass wrong, because the light is fuller than at home. The fleet now follows something immensely powerful that it does not command.

Chapter 2: Two Places at Once

About 22 to 23 April, Amara is watching through the eye from the Well, high above Kaldo, when a Church hull crosses the city low and slow while signalling that it is on a routine flight. She calls the fleet.

The decision belongs to Harrold, and Emeric asks rather than orders. Harrold wants the knights in the air, and the Answer Towers already have the hull in their sights. Emeric asks him to hold the knights and let him go himself. Harrold objects to the question as much as to the plan. Under the seal this is his decision, and a king who can reach home within the hour from a thousand miles away turns the seal into a courtesy. He decides anyway, on his own terms. The towers keep the hull covered. If it drops below two thousand feet over the town, or is not gone within the hour, the knights go up, king or no king. He says it in front of Amara, who does not take sides.

On the convoy, Ward counts the cost before they climb. A ride needs Emeric's body at Band height, and past the wall Ward's standing order is that no hull goes anywhere alone, so the whole convoy must rise into the Band with its racks open. While parked it cannot run or drop quickly to the sea, the unchanged spend longer at height than their cards allow, and the king is an empty body lying on a deck. If the ride breaks, nobody knows where Emeric ends up. Ward is right, Emeric says he is right, and they climb anyway, the stop doubling as a charge. Another relay is laid while they wait.

Emeric rides relay to relay into sentry five and goes out over Kaldo in his own face. The hull's people tell him they just happened to be passing. He knows it is a lie and tells them to leave. They leave, because of what he holds over the pope in the letter he wrote before the voyage. Emeric's misjudgement is that he expects them to put his appearance down to a fast hull or a hidden route.

Back in his own body after nearly two hours parked, cold on the K Discovery's deck, he lays out the questions to Ferra and Imani over the set. Why send a ship at all? Why that day? Why so close to the Well? Nobody has an answer. Emeric has won the exchange, but he has shown the Church a capability it did not know he had, and left a weak point behind him.

Chapter 3: The Regent

The same days, about 22 to 27 April, seen from home. Harrold governs. He sits the sittings, receives the envoys, and listens to the pulpits preach about the king who sailed off to the cursed sea. He is regent for Amara's sake, not for Emeric's, and he resents spending a week a month away from her.

He makes one decision entirely his own. A Velmoor merchant house wants lamps, bulbs and glass at a premium, paid through Vantic's bank, around the imperial credit block. The block has shut Randillia out of the imperial houses' credit, and the kingdom programme runs on cash. The goods are already on the release ladder and have been sold to strangers for years. The premium is real coin, paid through Vantic's bank before anything leaves the quay, and the alternative Vantic lays in front of him is borrowing on worse terms from someone worse. So Harrold signs, with safeguards: one year, with a break clause he can use without cause; release-ladder goods only; every sale made on Kaldo's quay inside the customs house, with no Velmoor factor allowed past it; every factor named in advance and vetted by Saban's people; payment before loading; and no substitute without the regent's written exception for cause. Saban says that strangers on the quay in the year the fleet is away is a door he would rather not open. Harrold answers that a door with a lock and a man on it beats having no money, and writes Saban's objection into the minute. He does not ask Emeric. He tells him afterward. If it goes wrong, an enemy will have to beat his safeguards, not walk through a gap he left open.

A lord down from the capital on business sees Harrold and Amara together and goes white, though he says nothing to the room. Later he privately asks Saban who the lady is. Saban only smiles. "People tell me I look like her" is Amara's line to say, not his. Rowena adds a dry comment over the field set.

At his first sitting Harrold passes Weekes's gangs cutting the water main up the south road of the old capital, and a lord complains to the regent about the trench at his door. Harrold has nothing to say on the king's behalf and says so. It is the price of the regency.

Corbin works beside him over the stewards' weekly reports at the long table, and Corbin is good at it. Harrold has a promise to keep, and keeps it. Nothing between them resolves. Tess walks the Motion Shop on her own feet while Wren keeps the books.

At Ostrell, a Church officer delivers his report on the flyover. The others in the room argue the ordinary explanations: a fast hull, a lookalike, a king who never really sailed. The pope listens to all of them and asks a single question. How high does the Well sit? Then he dismisses them. Home keeps working and changing without Emeric, and the Church has started to look.

Chapter 4: Thick Water

From about 24 April to 1 May, the convoy moves in stages behind the creature. Every few nights Imani raises the mark a notch, and by the end most knights are sleeping on deck while the unchanged still sleep at the mark. A dense-water animal turns up, ordinary to Ilenne and alarming to everyone else, and the escort sends it away. Later they pass an old current marker on a reef, and then a distant shape on the surface that Ilenne calls a fishing camp of the outer waters. She will not go near it, and the creature takes them wide. People live out here, and they are not hers to command.

Around the 28th, the escort makes the limits of its protection plain. It is night, the hulls are set down on sea anchors, and the creature has gone deep to feed as it does after dark. Emeric feels something a full minute before it arrives: something big rising slowly under the hulls. Its own density swamps his sense, so all he can read is big and under us. He calls it on the set, and the watch lifts every airship off the water on its stones, burning charge the fleet cannot spare, which Ferra logs. Zale's ship cannot lift. The thing comes for the one hull left in the water. It takes the sea anchor's line, then the rudder, then a deckhand off the low waist.

Ilenne goes in after the man. She speaks to the thing, as she can with sea life, and it does not answer. It is not listening. It is hungry. She reaches the deckhand and holds him up while it circles, and in the dark she can do nothing more. Zale lights the water with every lamp on his deck. The knights can do nothing at all, because water stops a round within a few feet, and their night glass shows only the swirl on the surface, since light reaches only a few yards into night water. Then the escort comes up out of the deep. The two great things meet beneath Zale's keel, it is over in a minute, and the hungry one goes down and does not come back. The deckhand's leg will keep him on Imani's deck for about two weeks. The rudder and sea anchor wait for Bram. From that night on, Zale's ship sets down inside the ring of hulls with the escort lying beneath her after dark, and Ilenne names which fish are safe to eat while the heavy ones go onto Imani's dose cards.

Ilenne holds her colour steady all day, with everyone, except with Zale. Ferra notices. Zale does not. On a charging climb Ilenne says it is cold up there, and Zale's coat ends up around her. She keeps it.

One night on his deck she tells him one piece of what she has been keeping: she ran from a binding. She explains how a binding works. It is done in the water, before witnesses, both parties say it, once, and it lasts until one of them dies. Zale asks a question she did not expect. Not whether she agreed, but whether anyone asked her. Then he tells her about the duchy he would not take, his mother's seat at Emberfell, and what it is like to be the door a family wants to walk through. It is the first thing anyone from the dry land has said to her that she did not have to translate in her head first.

She still will not give the name of the house. Her colour and a single line give her reason: if she says it on the sea, she has made it real out here too, and out here was the only place it was not. Zale lets it go. He should not have, and both of them know it. In a brief, lighter moment between Maeve and Mira, Emeric's glare lands on Bren. The romance now has a deadline, and between Zale and Ilenne a question of trust stays open.

Chapter 5: Once Until One Dies

About 1 to 2 May, with the convoy set down on the sea, Zale brings the problem to the K Amara's council table: Emeric, Ferra, Imani, Armand, Ward and Ilenne. Ilenne explains the binding herself. It is done in the water because there an "I will" lasts forever. The courtesy she taught them at home is not a ban on promises in the water but the reason such a promise is dangerous, which is why guests never say "I will" there by accident and only a king may speak for his country there. A binding is the same act done on purpose.

Ferra asks the questions a clerk would ask, and two of the answers matter. The custom does not say who may be bound, and it does not say from where. Nobody from the dry land has ever stood in the water at Aumere's door, so the question never arose. From that gap comes the idea: marry her to somebody first, creating a binding the court would have to honour unless it was willing to say out loud what its custom really is. Ferra writes it down as a risk, not a ruling. In her book it reads: holds if the water, witnesses, both say it; does not say who may; they will say it for us. She tells the table plainly that she is guessing.

Before anyone else can be suggested, Ilenne names Zale. Zale translates his own proposal: "She's asking if I'll marry her. I think. Or she wants my boat."

Everyone lays out what could go wrong. Emeric points out that if a house wants the throne through her, whoever is married to her stands in its way. Ward says they would be arming a court against a land man in the middle of a foreign city. Armand notes that a binding the court cannot undo is also one Zale cannot undo. Zale's answer is: "I've got a boat."

He does not answer at the table. He takes a night, returns to his own ship, decides there, and tells Ilenne before he tells his brother. Imani separately asks Ilenne, in words, whether this is what she wants and whether she understands what it costs him. Ilenne does understand, says so, and says yes anyway.

The plan is set: the binding will happen in the water at the door, before two witnesses, before they go in. Under the custom, two recognised witnesses of the water are needed, at least one of them independent of both households. What began as a clerk's question has become a legally informed gamble, and it is freely chosen.

Chapter 6: Sohra

About 3 May, a day or two short of the ring, the convoy is parked in the Band when a dragon arrives. Her name is Sohra, and from her side she is enforcing a law.

The dragons have one law from their makers: keep the Band unbroken. Drawing on it is the crime Kaldo has been committing for years without knowing any law existed. The fleet has also crossed the line set by the Leaving Terms, an old agreement that the people of Ostrellin stay in and others stay out, which the Church kept from the kingdom. Sohra found them through the draw itself. She felt hours of it during the parked ride, eight hulls with their racks open, and tracked it. She breaks the newest relay on her way in, since disabling an unattended thing that draws is routine enforcement and needs no warning. Then she makes one pass down the line and lays fire across the open necks of the K Amara's port charging racks. Not the decks, only the racks. Among the peoples who live with dragons, fire laid on the thing doing the taking, and nothing else, is how a dragon says stop. Everyone past the wall knows this. Nobody from Ostrellin could.

The K Amara's stasis shell is up, and it takes the whole of that warning. The shell gauge drops on the bridge, the shard stone below crazes across two facets, and the shell holds, weaker on that quarter. Ferra logs it. To Sohra, a shield raised against the law reads as a refusal, and she begins sweeping runs down the convoy. A mile down the line on Zale's ship, Ilenne recognises the warning and shouts it in her own language to Zale, who has only half the words, while Linnet is still reaching for the set. Ilenne knows what the first fire meant. She cannot know what Sohra intends next and does not pretend to.

Ward holds the fleet's fire while she sweeps: a lone contact, a shell holding, no lives yet in danger. Her second pass comes in on the K Amara's weakened quarter, and this time the fire goes through. The port rack bank burns, and the fire runs down the feed trunk into the after dry store below it. Opening the store into the Band's wind would feed the fire and risk the hull, so Ward, with Ferra's agreement, has the vents and hatches shut to starve it. A rigger goes in to close the last vent and a knight pulls him out, and they are the two worst burned. A loader carrying the second dragon rifle up from the port rack goes over the rail to escape the fire. A flight knight catches the man but not the rifle, which falls from Band height into the thick water. Ferra logs it in the middle of everything else. The K Amara holds: Ferra on the gravity banks keeping her level with the port bank dead, knights isolating the burning racks and cutting their necks shut by hand, the planted deck keeping people aboard, fire crews sealing the after store, civilians moved forward, and Ward running all of it.

When Sohra turns toward the K Endeavor's hospital deck, lives are at stake, and Ward gives the order. Arthur fires the K Amara's dragon rifle. Sohra cannot feel the round coming. She sees the flash and twists on instinct, and it takes her left horn off at the root and opens her neck a hand's width from the throat. She drops three hundred feet. Then she changes into her humanoid dragon form, a target too small for the sight to hold, and comes for the K Amara, because that is where the gun is. On the apron Arthur's second round misses, Mira's spear and Bren go down without being killed, Ward calls check fire on the deck, and Ferra keeps the hull level.

Emeric comes off the K Discovery and knocks her off the deck, and the fight goes out over the water. Then Sohra smells him, and everything lands on her at once: the scent of a dragon house in a human body, the thief of the Band, and a face her elders describe. She knows the draw, the scent and the face. She assumes that a gold-eyed human in the Band is the old enemy come back, and like any dragon she does not ask. She tests. Emeric orders the fleet to stay with the K Amara and pulls the fight away from it.

He is outclassed in power but not in skill. She has gravity and fire at a dragon's capacity. What keeps him alive is a boxer's footwork, being small and fast against something huge, the Crumple padding his insides against the blows that land, and reading where her power gathers a heartbeat before it arrives. From the ships it looks as though he is dead. Then the fire skin comes back without his choosing it, full cover, face included, with no breath, and he reverses her fire and throws it back at her. It is a sprint with a hard end. His gold eyes flare, and for a moment his forearms show plating. When she yields he stops, the blow ready, because he sees that she has yielded. To her, being spared is an insult she did not ask for. A test that is finished is finished.

The fight has drifted a few miles from the convoy, and Emeric comes down on instinct onto the K Endeavor, where the wounded are going. Ward has already brought the burned K Amara alongside, deck to deck, to pass her wounded across, so Ferra is on the K Endeavor's deck with the burned and Imani is there because it is her deck. Sohra follows him down and changes on the deck. In her humanoid form she tells him that she has never seen him and yet knows him on sight, because her elders tell of a man with his build and his face. Imani is the only one who walks toward Sohra's bleeding neck. Sohra lets her look but not touch.

Mid-sentence, Sohra catches something on the air, turns her head into the wind, and moves toward Ferra and Imani at a dragon's speed in a humanoid body, with a dragon's complete indifference to how that looks. It is recognition, involuntary, not an attack. Emeric reads it as an attack, as anyone would, and is between her and them before he has decided anything, wings and horns out, the partial form going further than it went for his own defence. She stops a stride short, because she was always going to stop. She breathes them in. Those two feel like something she has not felt since her mother. She cannot say what it is and cannot tell the two of them apart, and she does not pretend to. To her, she walked toward something. To him, she charged his wives.

The second flare spends the last of him. Emeric goes down on the K Endeavor's deck, and Imani catches him.

Chapter 7: The Bill

About 3 to 6 May, on the K Endeavor's hospital deck and then the K Amara, Imani keeps Emeric alive. For a long stretch she and Ferra are simply frightened, before anything is funny.

When he wakes, he wants to try calling the form again, carefully, with a plan. As his doctor, Imani forbids it, means it, and he does not get around her. Once he is stable, Ferra puts a question to him in her accounts voice. He is three sentences into his test plan before he sees her face, explains that women like her exist where he comes from, makes a joke he then has to explain, and makes it worse. Imani adds a clinical follow-up without looking up. Work first, fun later, turned back on him.

He lists aloud for the two of them what he now knows he does not know: Ilenne's word for his eyes, the peoples she says react to them, and a dragon who knows his face. In a delve he finds his sealed shelf, and one spine there carries a title it did not have before. He cannot open it, and nothing comes of it but unease. He calls his mother. Amara has nothing either.

Ilenne explains what happened from the dragon's side. The first fire on the racks was the telling, the warning everyone past the wall knows. "To her, you are here. That is the wrong." Ilenne knows there was an old promise but not its words. Because nobody fired on the telling, Ruan's crews have nothing to blame themselves for. Arthur, who took the horn off a creature that had the right of it, takes that hard and tells only Mira.

The repairs take two days. Bram and Armand mend the K Amara's crazed shard stone with stone resin, running the grown-stone recipe inside the cracks: shard dust ground from the canister flakes, liquid mana from hull four, and the cracked stone itself as host, so new grain grows into the cracks and closes them. It is a true mend rather than a filler. Armand re-keys the mended band. It takes two days to set, with one quarter weak meanwhile, and it will not mend a stone broken right through. Emeric sums it up in one line: patch it at sea, or recut it at home. Dagny grinds the dust, and Ferra adds shard dust to the stores by weight. Over the same two days Bram and Dagny refit the port racks from hull four's spares, and the K Amara is topped up from hull four's liquid mana tanks.

Ward writes down standing orders as a record of what the fleet just did. Escorts do not fire automatically on a lone contact in the air or on a limited, unfamiliar act. They track it, cover the vulnerable systems, meaning the racks, the lift and the hospital deck, and look for a signal, with Ilenne on the set if possible. They fire at once when a life or a ship is in immediate danger. No rifle fires at anything that has landed on a Kaldo deck, and check fire is called the moment anything lands. Ward says aloud that this leaves a grey area that will become somebody's call in a hurry. One crew member objects on survival grounds and is not wrong. Ward keeps the rule anyway, and the objection is remembered. Ward also keeps his own objection open: twice in ten days the fleet has hung on a risk only Emeric believed he could manage. Emeric does not change, and the argument stays alive.

At the K Amara's table, Emeric, Ward and Ferra make a short, deliberate decision to keep drawing. They have hull four's liquid mana, what the hulls hold, and the slow fill from the thick air. There is no way to reach the ring without drawing at all. They do not know whether Sohra's law covers every draw or only the Band, and they say so. Ward thinks the next dragon might do worse. They switch to charging low from the thick air at sea level, slower, with longer set-down stops, and will climb only when Ferra's reserve mark is crossed or a relay must be laid, each climb short and with a dragon watch posted. Ferra sets the mark. Nobody calls it safe.

The fleet's reaction is not one voice. The K Amara's burned are grateful, the knights are awed, and some sailors are afraid; one asks to be moved off the K Discovery. The cook is offended that nobody ate his stew. On Imani's deck, Bren writes down the first dragon anyone has ever written down, and spells it with two g's. Maeve, who taught Emeric lightning, wants to know what else he has been keeping from her.

Ferra reads out the bill: the port racks refitted and the shell mended; one dragon rifle lost, five left; a new count of days of food after about ten tonnes of flour, dried peas and biscuit were ruined in the after store, roughly eight days of the whole fleet's food, so the fleet moves to fish-heavy days sooner than planned; fourteen burned, two of them badly; no one killed; three days lost. Sohra leaves for her elders. Her report will be what she saw: a Band-thief with the old face, who beat her and then stopped. The dragon has cost more than a day's rest, and Emeric's private mystery is now a public fact in the fleet.

Chapter 8: The Ring

About 6 May, the fleet sights Aumere: a broken black ring about eight miles across, its rim breaking the surface as islands and reef. The Rim smokes and Below glows. There are furnaces, kilns, cranes and causeways, and a breakwater ring set with water-gates. Winged watchers on the outer cliffs see eight hulls come in, one of them scorched. Sorrel reports birds that are too big.

At the outer markers the escort stops and will go no farther. A boat of the caldera watch comes out. The watch serves the crown, is drawn from both the Rim and Below, and answers to the crown as an office rather than to any house. Its officer, Taurre, logs eight hulls, one burned, and gives his port orders. The hulls will stand off outside the ring, on the sea and not in the air. The Challenger and Zale's ship may come in to the gate under escort. Land guests may enter only hard domes and bells, with Keepers and pilots beside them. The Breath is closed to them.

Then Taurre asks what burned, and whether anything of theirs is in his water. With Ilenne translating, Ferra declares it plainly: a dragon's fire on the K Amara, fourteen burned, nobody dead, and one weapon lost over the side in the outer water, at the position in her log, which she reads out with its mark, unrecoverable. She declares it because a guest declares what she has dropped in her host's sea, and because it lies miles down where it can hurt no one. Taurre writes it in the watch's hazard log.

A Rim pilot boat follows the watch and claims its right: no land people's ship comes to the docks without a Rim pilot, and the fee is the fee. The queen's escort does not cancel it, because the Rim's institutions do not answer to her escort. Ferra pays in iron bars, having read that iron is what they lack. The pilot's colour shifts at the sight of it, and by nightfall the Rim's house knows the strangers carry iron worth asking about.

At the K Amara's table they argue about the stone she carries. In her iron-skinned hold an ordinary sense will not read it, which is exactly why it matters that they say so. Emeric holds to the promise he made Ilenne at the wall water: the queen hears of it from him, first thing at the door, and decides where the K Amara may lie. Until then Ward takes the K Amara out past the outer markers and keeps her there, the only hull barred. Emeric tells Taurre only that she carries something the queen must hear of before she comes closer, which is true and all the watch needs to set the line. Ferra writes nothing down about the stone and explains why: a written line can be read, copied or taken, so the only safe record is none. The rifle she declared an hour earlier is different to her. It is lost, miles down and harmless.

Close up, steam rises off the vents on the Rim's islands and a geyser goes up white beside a kiln yard, its heat piped into the furnaces. Through the water inside the ring, domes descend the caldera wall in tiers, most of them full of sea, a few of them silver with air. Ferra asks who those are for. Taurre answers: the dry ones are bells, for records and dried food, and for guests who breathe air when they host people of the land. Mostly the dwarves, and others when they come. He tells the story of the First City in three sentences, the way people tell something everyone knows. They once floated their cities. The war's dead ground came over the water and the city went down. Now the rock holds them.

Emeric asks whether there are humans out here. Taurre says yes, as if it were obvious: a few, scattered among other peoples, and one people of their own far to the west who keep to themselves. To the fleet this is the hope it sailed on, now with a shape. Taurre's ease also suggests the merfolk, at least, will never be fooled, because they know exactly where the fleet came from. Emeric files it away.

Asked what the court will make of eight armed hulls, Ilenne says the old men will talk about an old promise between her people and the dry land, one nobody alive has read. Armand files that away.

Ned proposes the quiet entry they rehearsed: the Challenger diving outside the ring and surfacing at the door. Ilenne stops it at once. Coming up from below is how a hunter comes. She goes down outside the ring and comes in on the surface, level. Aumere is a sovereign state with its own procedure, and it governs how Kaldo arrives.

Chapter 9: The Door

About 7 May, the Challenger comes in on the surface to a water-gate in the Rim's breakwater, the door, with a Rim pilot ahead of her because the rule is the rule. Noll is at the stones and Ned at the helm, with Zale's ship on the water beside the gate. The K Amara waits beyond the outer markers and the other hulls stand off outside the ring. Emeric wears the king's coat with the gold dragon on its back. His sword stays locked in the K Amara's strong box, because Ilenne told him: not at my mother's door. The knights remain aboard their hulls.

Ilenne comes out of the hatch on her own legs and walks up the gate's steps toward her mother. The queen is there in the water herself, as she promised, with the captain of the twelve, her guards, and Taurre, because a land ship is at the gate. When she sees her daughter, her colour slips into joy for one breath in front of her guards, then she holds it still again.

She does not go to Ilenne. A land ship is at her gate, and the forms come first, for her as for anyone. Taurre declares the ship and its terms, where the hulls lie and the pilot's fee recorded, and the queen hears it as the crown, not as a mother. She greets the foreign king level, rank to rank. She looks at Emeric's eyes and says the same word the captain once used for them, explaining nothing. Ilenne stands on the step and waits through all of it, because she knows the forms better than anyone there. Two rulers are held by the same kind of rule.

Before the queen receives her daughter, and before anything is said in the water, Emeric asks for her ear apart on the steps, with Ilenne translating. He tells her what the K Amara carries, where she lies, and that Ward keeps her. The queen affirms the watch's line and tightens it: the K Amara stays beyond the outer markers and does not move without her word. It is a port order given through the watch officer, not a promise. She looks at him for a long time. Only then does she go up the steps to Ilenne, and whatever her colour does, she lets it.

Ilenne and Zale stand on the lowest steps in the thick water. The captain of the twelve and Taurre serve as the two witnesses, Taurre as the independent one, since the watch answers to neither household. Both say it. Then, by custom, each names their house. Ilenne names hers. Zale names his in her language, badly, with Ilenne correcting one sound: his father, who was king of the land, and his brother, who is king now. The captain goes pale with the colour of deference, and most of the guards follow, though it is deference to rank, not consent. One guard goes slate, the warning colour, and holds it. Taurre's colour does not move at all, which is its own answer. On the threshold Maeve says, "Well. That's out, then."

Ushar arrives too late to stop it, because nobody can stop a binding said in the water, and he knew that. He is there by right: the door is a gate in his house's breakwater. Emeric feels him a full minute before he surfaces, a strong presence rising slowly from below, straight at the steps. He says nothing and does not move, because moving first is the point of this. Ushar surfaces under Zale, close enough to touch. Coming up from below at the queen's own door is how a hunter comes, and every merfolk on the steps reads it.

Then the insult, in the open custom. To Zale, Ushar makes the low sideways sign across the body, like wiping a table, which means a man whose word is water, the worst thing a person can be called. Aimed at a man who has just said his one "I will" in the water before witnesses, it calls the binding worthless. To Emeric he uses the court's old word for his eyes, said the old way: the gold eyes in the sky, come back with ships. He does this in front of the watch. His glance goes once to the dragon on Emeric's coat and comes away, and he says nothing about it. Emeric sees what it is for. A land man's raised hand at the queen's door is the breach the Rim's house needs in order to invoke the old promise. Ushar wants a blow.

Zale's hand goes to his knife and stops there, because Emeric's hand is on his wrist. Then Zale does the one thing that hurts Ushar in his own custom. He gives him the full guest's bow, level, perfect and slow, the way Ilenne taught it, in the water, to the man who just came up under him. A guest's correctness at the host's door against a merfolk lord's rudeness, in front of the watch. Ushar's colour shows it landed. Zale's face does not. Emeric steps down into the water beside his brother, level, coat and all, and says nothing. The wool soaks to the shoulders and he does not look at it. He is the one man there whose word in the water would be his country's, and everyone on the steps knows what it would mean if he spoke. He simply looks at Ushar with the eyes Ushar just named, and holds it until Ushar looks away. Ilenne turns colourless at Ushar in front of her mother, the flat skin of courtesy that is what a thing in the deep shows before it eats. Every merfolk present reads it, and the queen sees her daughter do it. Ilenne still does not speak the house's name.

The queen says one sentence to Ushar in the water: he came up under, at her door. Taurre logs it. She can do no more to the Rim's heir at the Rim's own gate, and both of them know it. Ushar goes down the way he came, slowly. Nothing is settled. Zale has been named a man whose word is water before witnesses, on his wedding day, and a name like that must be answered before witnesses or it sticks. The queen has noticed, twice now, that Emeric knows not to say anything binding.

With the water still settling where Ushar went down, the queen bows and then turns her hand palm up: come in, my house, my word. Only a host does it, and it covers her house and nothing more. Emeric bows back, dripping, and does not look up.

Where the Story Stands After Chapter 9

The fleet has crossed the wall, learned that the thick water carries both the bleeding sickness and things that hunt, and reached Aumere battered but intact. Sohra's attack left the K Amara scorched down her port side, fourteen burned, a dragon rifle lost at sea and declared in the watch's hazard log, food stores reduced, and the convoy committed to slower charging from the thick air. It also revealed that Emeric is not wholly human. The fire skin came without his choosing, a dragon knew his face on sight, and the whole fleet saw it. Sohra has carried her report to her elders, and the watchers on the cliffs saw fire in the Band.

Zale and Ilenne are bound, legally if the court accepts that a land man can be bound, and Ushar has already shown how the Rim's house will fight it: in public, through the custom, by naming Zale a man whose word is water. Zale must answer that before witnesses. Ilenne's old men have hinted at an ancient promise between the merfolk and the dry land that nobody alive has read, and Armand has filed it away. The stone aboard the K Amara keeps her beyond the outer markers on the queen's order, and one guard heard about it on the steps. Emeric's coat is soaked from the door.

At home, Harrold's regency works and makes its own choices. Velmoor's factors are coming onto Kaldo's quay under his safeguards, over Saban's recorded objection. The Church has watched Emeric appear over Kaldo while his fleet was a thousand miles away, and the pope has asked how high the Well sits. Saban's reply to the wedding is already on its way by relay.

This batch establishes the terms for the rest of the voyage: the people of Kaldo are guests. They can be right, strong and well equipped, and still have to wait at someone else's door for a turned-up hand.
