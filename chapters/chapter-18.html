Book 2, Batch 18: The Isle

Chapters 115–123 | Saturday 20 March – Saturday 8 May, Year 18

The First Room has opened, with nothing in it about the Isle of Shards. On the Isle, under the exception Harrold signed as regent and defended at the Assembly without apology, the works shaft is still going down for oil. The Assembly deferred the survey of the hollow in February; an engineer's warning and Tess's vote were both outvoted; and a shard cutter's signed warning from November sits filed as insufficient to halt. Waylon runs the works. The condominium with Duvrenne means nobody lands on the shared island without both crowns' leave.

This is the batch where the Isle answers. A second warning from the same shard cutter sends Tess back to the works record, where she finds that two honest maps were joined at a wrong stone and the shaft is forty yards nearer the hollow than anyone has drawn it. The king stops the shaft that afternoon, three weeks too late. He sends the people who can hear what he cannot, they find the hollow breathing, and they pour a plug. Then something comes up through the wet plug.

What follows is one long morning: a beast that eats the island's stones and grows, a dead zone that fails to drop it, a rescue through the old cave in failing lamplight, a brother holding a gate whose eyes go gold, and a ship at full burn for three hours. Then the king comes down out of the sky as something nobody has ever seen, in front of everyone, including a Church priest with a long glass. Afterward come the dead, the tells that the beast never lived, a core that is not dead but shut, and a record laid out in order with the warnings that were right placed on top.

Chapter 115: The Datum

On Saturday 20 March, Waylon's set brings a second signed warning to the line room. Bryn, an Islander shard cutter of the west cliffs, the man who signed in November, signs again: the hollow's beat is faster, about one in eight seconds now against one in eleven when the exception was signed, and the warm rock has moved toward the shaft's foot. Waylon sends it whole and adds one line of his own: he believes him. From Monday to Wednesday Tess lays the works record over the bore map and the June survey line again on her bench at the Motion Shop, and finds what nobody explained in June. The surveyor's old line was right. The bore map was redrawn after the armistice, when Kaldo's and Duvrenne's survey marks were merged for the shared island, off a datum moved about forty yards to agree with the stone at Kesrath's tower. The hollow's drawn edge moved with it, and the shaft's did not. The shaft's foot is about forty yards nearer the hollow's outer chamber than the map shows. Nobody lied; two honest maps were joined at a wrong stone. Linus, the surveyor of the Claim, confirms it from his field book by Wednesday noon.

That week, at the hospital, a Grayrock girl of four born with both lenses clouded is brought in by her father. Emeric takes the cloudy lenses out, Earth surgery from the 1700s, and Imani builds two clear lenses in their place. Both eyes are done in a day. Imani tells the father before anything else that the eyes are fixed today but seeing is not: a brain that has never seen has to learn it, and four is young enough to learn well. A week later, in the hospital's yard, the girl follows a pigeon with her whole head, then her father's hand, without knowing yet that it is a hand. Emeric watches from the door, then goes back to the stop order.

On Wednesday 24 March, in his study, Emeric says it aloud before anyone else can: if the map is wrong, they have been digging toward it for six months. He orders the shaft stopped by relay that afternoon, under the other half of his own clause, since the clause that let an exception be signed lets the king end one, with the date and the reason in his hand beside Harrold's. Waylon stops it within the hour. Harrold, rung at the capital, says he would have signed the stop too and will answer for the start. The decisive charges were fired three weeks before, on Tuesday 2 March, the night shift's last round at the shaft's foot, and nobody can know that yet.

That night he tells Ferra why he will not go himself. His sense is deaf there: the shard field and the lightning outcrops are wild stones by the thousand, each a bright note, the drift at the Band's foot again, and he would be standing on the thing he is trying to hear. Neris listens through rock and water with her ears and her own sense, the way the Murr trusted her in their tunnels, and Tess's instruments read pressure and heat, which a stone field cannot drown. A king on the Isle is also an event under a treaty: a works inspection is the Compact's business and needs no leave, but the King of Randillia on Duvrenne's half of a shared island needs Duvrenne's. He asks for it that week, with the answer due in May. And he would rather the people who can hear look first. Ferra says that is the right order and she does not like it either.

On Monday 22 March the roots group meets at the long table. The year's stone drop is in, a hundred and two, forty-eight from the dome and fifty-four from Grayrock, and with it the vault holds about two hundred and eighty blanks Ferra has kept against the Church. Emeric asks to put a hundred of them into the ground this spring, fifty pairs soaked in the Band first like Grayrock's, so that every holding of Kaldo's states gets a grove. The groves were the land's mana before they were burned, a grove makes a grove, and in six years there would be thousands; he gives the sum, and Ferra checks it and writes "estimate" beside it. Ward's condition wins: no grove goes in until its guard does, a walled close, a drop cage, a post, a dog run, a bell line and a sworn watch. That moves the planting from April to May and costs the first fifty pairs a month of growing, and Emeric says Ward is right. Armand says the soak is circulation, the dragons' own old job and not a draw, and it goes in Kaldo's own sky as soak, for the observers. Saban says the Church will want every seed and somebody will sell one. Ferra funds the walls before the trees and keeps a reserve she does not name aloud. The first tins go up to the K Meridian's racks that week, counted in by two.

On Thursday 25 March Emeric goes to Milford, because he has decided not to go to the Isle, and Ferra says so. The crates from the voyage are showing their first green in the glass houses: the orc pumpkins, the paddy rice in its trays, the desert beans. Hollis's labels are on the seed vault in the cold room, and Hollis reads back to him the standing order that every seed goes to Milford first, since Hollis wrote it. On the shed's board he draws a grow tower with Hollis and Paxton: trays of greens on a slow carousel past a glass face, roots in water, carp at the foot, light stones under the trays. Hollis is right that towers will not feed a kingdom bread, so it will be fields for grain and towers for greens, fruit, seedlings and fish. They say the failure points as they draw: if the pump stops the roots dry in hours, so a header tank; one sick root spreads through shared water, so four loops. Meat goes on every table next: Hester's hatchery and broiler houses first, pigs on the dairy's whey, fish, at Ferra's price for a weekly measure in the ration for everyone in Kaldo's states from next spring. Emeric names the two risks before they bite, that dense air changes animals and that feed from the grove belts drifts toward mana food, so the herds stay off the belts. In the pump house, Linus has installed the first water board: a gypsum block at root depth, a relay and a valve on the drip line. One block's lamp goes green while they watch, then white as the water runs. Emeric touches nothing.

On Thursday 1 April, after Ferra's read, comes the first lesson. Sohra on the set, Harrold at the table with his hands flat on it, Emeric interpreting. It is talk, not demonstration: you do not make a piece, you hold the whole and put down what you do not need, the wings first, from the outside in. Emeric translates it as fold. Sohra corrects him: put down. Harrold refuses to say either. That night the light dragon lies on the bay shore past the wall with the water lit for a mile, and nothing comes off him. By the end of the month Kaldo calls it the king's lamp and stops asking.

On Monday 29 March the party is chosen. Ward goes, because he asked, since the Isle is where he stood beside the old peoples' statues. Mira goes with two of the Crestless, for the stealth plate and the night glass. Neris goes by her own asking and her contract's right to go where she likes, because she is the only one in Kaldo who has listened to a rock and been right about it. Tess goes with her instruments, a mercury barometer, a pressure gauge on a hose, thermometers on long cables and her decider's listening coil. Imani goes for the warm ground and the men who have worked beside it, since the works' hospital ward is hers under the Compact. Emeric stays, and so does Ferra, who has the Assembly's committee. Ward writes the party's rule in his own hand: nobody goes down the shaft, and nobody goes into the old cave without a word to him first. Four rounds of the AM-T2, built on Bram's floor in February with a raw breaker stone, a mundane charge and a heavy body, already sit at the Claim's battery as its answer to Church hulls, under Tess's rule never to be fired within eighty miles of the towers; the Isle is a thousand. The K Endeavor lifts for the Isle, and the king watches it go from the castle roof, liking the order no better than anyone.

On Friday 2 April Runa turns eleven, the first birthday on her card, and she is suspicious of the cake. On Saturday, in the castle yard, Emeric gives her her first boxing lesson. Hands up. She bites the pad, and he has to stop himself laughing so she'll keep going. She loses the first exchange in seconds and says again, five times, and on the fifth her feet have gone and he says no. She says she can keep going till she falls. He says that's exactly what he's teaching her not to do. When Imani comes out to close the yard and Emeric, delighted, says one more round, Runa tells him he isn't allowed. He stops.

Chapter 116: Two Beats

On Sunday 11 April, at dusk, the K Endeavor comes down on the cove over the bell tank. Through Neris's eyes: Waylon on the jetty pretending to be unimpressed by the party; the Claim's long sheds; the Islanders' houses round the cook tent; Mother Sabbe at her fire; the shaft head under its frame on the shard field's edge; the field glittering in the last light; Kesrath's tower with its lamps at the far end; and the pitch lake between. About three hundred of Kaldo's and the Islanders' crews live on the island with about a hundred and twenty of their families in the camp, alongside a half-company of Kaldo's crown works guard with the battery, two 20 mm and the AM-T2's breech gun. About two hundred of Duvrenne's people are at the tower under Oskel. And at the tower's chapel there is now a Church priest, sent from Ostrel to Duvrenne's court after the trap and posted to the tower in March to pray for the works, with a long glass he takes up to the roof every morning.

From Monday to Wednesday Neris sits on the shaft frame's footing with her palms and her ear on the rock, the way she listened for the Murr, and at slack water she goes down to the cove and listens with her head under. She hears one heart, slow and huge, with an echo in it a breath behind the beat, as if the hollow rings. It is quickening: she counts it against Tess's watch, and by Wednesday it beats once every seven seconds.

On Thursday 15 April Tess lowers her hose to the foot of the stopped shaft. Nobody goes down, by Ward's rule. At the bottom of a stopped shaft air is moving, a faint, steady draught down into the rock, and the gauge reads the foot a little low against the head, and falling. She says it in one sentence: the outer chamber's seal is broken and it is breathing the island's air in. Ward clears the shaft head and moves the nearest sheds' crews back two hundred yards that afternoon. Mother Sabbe moves the families from the camp's near edge without being asked twice, because the Islanders have always said the field breathes and Kaldo has now measured it. That evening at the cook tent's fire Oskel says Kaldo is frightened of a hole it dug, and the works foreman says the bonus was for oil and the oil is forty feet down. Waylon backs Ward and Tess against his own foreman at the fire, with Mother Sabbe watching him do it.

Tess's fix is to plug the shaft above the crack and give the chamber its seal back: a dustcrete plug poured down the shaft onto a cone of sandbags and timber at about sixty feet, two days to set. A plug team of six volunteers for the double wage, working from a side gallery the shaft crossed in February. Bryn volunteers first, because he signed the warnings and wants to be the one who closes the hole. The pour starts on Saturday noon. By Sunday night the plug is poured and wet. Tess checks the protected zone along the buried city's landward edge, and it is untouched; the shaft and the hollow lie outside it, as Harrold's line drew. On Sunday evening Ward goes and stands for a while beside the six statues facing outward at the doors, comes back, and says nothing about it.

Chapter 117: Up the Shaft

On Monday 19 April, at about 05:40, the plug is still wet. Something comes up through it the way a hand comes through wet sand and climbs the shaft's ladder rungs and timbering in the grey before dawn. It is the size of a dog, compact, armoured in dark plates like wet basalt, with two eyes too big for its head, clouded grey-violet crystal with gold cracking out from the pupil and stopping short. The shaft-head watch, two Islanders and a Kaldo hand, think it is an animal out of the rock and go toward it with a lamp. It kills all three in seconds. It does not eat them.

It goes first for the works' stone store in its iron shed beside the shaft, the charged stones the works run on, fire stones for the stills, lamp stones and the pumpjack's gravity stones. It bends the iron door in, and the stones go dark one by one. Then it goes out onto the shard field, feeding as it walks, and the crystal goes grey behind it. It grows as it feeds, to about four metres by seven o'clock and ten by nine. Then it goes for the things that carry power and messages: the generator house, the Claim's relay mast, the pumpjack. At 06:20, from the works office, Waylon rings the line room on the Claim's set before the mast goes, in four sentences: something came up the shaft; it killed three; it is eating the stones; it is growing. Then the mast goes, and the Claim has only the K Endeavor's set.

At seven, in her dome in Kaldo, Amara takes the relay over the Isle's strait and turns its glass on the Claim, which she has never done for anything but weather, and watches the thing walk across the field. She tells her son in about four sentences. At 07:30 the K Amara lifts with Emeric, Ferra to count, Armand to hear its note, Arthur, Bren and every flight knight aboard, and the dragon rifles uncovered.

Others fight it first. The Claim's two works mages and Duvrenne's tower mage throw fire and lightning, and it cancels each glyph mid-cast, the way a lamp is pinched out. The battery's 20 mm and the K Endeavor's guns off the cove chip its plates with rounds it cannot read, since it reads nothing mundane, and it ignores the machines until one hurts it, then turns its head to the battery and studies it, and walks at it. Oskel brings Duvrenne's men out of the tower with their two old powder cannon on carts; he does not like Kaldo, and comes anyway because the thing is on the island. The Claim's watch, padded on inward strength, go at its legs with axes and pikes, which hurts it most and costs the most.

At about 09:30, through Ward's eyes on the Claim's wall, Waylon gives the order, since the works' command is his, and the breech gun fires one round of the AM-T2. The breaker stone bursts at the beast's shoulder and drops a dead zone about a hundred yards across onto it and the field around it. Every lamp stone in the circle goes out. A works mage at its edge sits down hard with nothing in his hands. The beast's vanes go dark, it stops growing, and it cannot cancel anything while the zone lasts. It does not drop. It keeps coming, slower, on its own strength, through the zone and out the other side. Ward, a knight who has fought with that kind of strength all his life, says it flatly to Waylon and the battery crew: "It's doing what we do." Nobody else has a word for it. Waylon writes it in the works log because Ward said it. Under the field, the five survivors of the plug team are tapping on a pipe.

At 09:41, while every glass is on the field, the K Endeavor's deck watch writes one line in the log: seal, small, into the water off the cove rocks. Nobody reads it.

Chapter 118: The Last Lamp

At about nine, through Ward's eyes, the plug team's side gallery, which crosses the shaft at about sixty feet, has come down at its shaft end where the beast went up through the timbering. Five of the six are alive behind the fall, Bryn among them, their tapping on the pipe heard at the Claim. The shaft is the way in, and the shaft is the way the thing goes, and a party down the shaft would bring it back down on them. Tess's survey shows the gallery's far end ran within a few yards of the old cave's back passage, the unwalked blank on Linus's map beyond Vey's chamber, and Neris, who has listened to that rock all week, says there is air on the other side. Ward leaves the two Crestless with Waylon and takes Mira, Neris and Imani in through the cave from the cove, past Vey's chamber and the old races' golems standing dead at their gates, and past six statues of a people who held their doors, still facing outward in the dark.

Through Mira's eyes, the passage runs under the shard field where the beast has been feeding. The stones in the rock around them go grey, and the party's lamp stones die one by one in their brackets as the field drains. Tess's listening coil on the set goes dead. The night glass's heat layer shows nothing in the cold rock. Ward's oil lamp keeps burning, because Ward always carries one. Neris leads by ear. Where the passage forks she listens and chooses, and once she stops them all for a full minute because something is moving above: the beast, crossing the field. They wait it out under it.

They break through into the gallery's far end with a bar and a pick. In the lamplight a man's eyes look out from under the fallen timbers, a survivor pinned. Beyond him something small and fast moves in the dark of the gallery, going away toward the cove. Ward has five people to get out, and he does not follow it. At the gallery's shaft end the beast's head and forelegs come back down the shaft after the people it can hear, close enough to see through the gap in the fall: its head in the shaft, its neck in the timbering, and the gallery roof's main beam above it. Ward has the works' blasting satchel, carried through for the fall, and one clean chance to bring the gallery roof and the shaft's timbering down on its head and neck while it is pinned. It would bury the five. He gives it up. He gets them out instead, one at a time, through the hole they came in by, while the beast's head works at the fall from the shaft side.

Through Imani's eyes, four come out walking or carried. Bryn's legs are under the main timber. Imani knows the crush rule Emeric taught her, that a crushed limb released without care floods the heart with what has built up in it, so she puts the tourniquets on before they lift and keeps her hands in him the whole time. It is not enough. His heart stops in the passage on the way out and does not start for her. The choice is not rewarded. Ward carries him the rest of the way himself. Four of the plug team are alive, Bryn is dead, and the beast's head pulls back out of the shaft and up onto the field, alive and bigger.

From about eight to past ten, through Waylon's eyes, the works camp's gate on the field side holds, with the families behind it in the sheds and the cook tent. Mother Sabbe refuses to leave her fire until the children are down at the cove boats. Waylon holds the gate with the Claim's watch, the battery's last 20 mm, Oskel's two cannon and his men, and the two Crestless Ward left him. It is a plain fight with pikes, axes and guns, men padded on inward strength; nothing Waylon casts can reach it, since he is not a caster yet. The Claim's watch loses four dead and a dozen hurt, and Duvrenne loses two under the second cannon. By 10:30 the families are all at the cove. Somewhere in the third hour, with the beast's head over the gate's timbers and Oskel's second cannon gone under its foot, Waylon's eyes go gold. The two Crestless see it. He feels his eyes burn and does not know why. Mother Sabbe sees it from her fire and says a word in the Islanders' speech that the Crestless do not understand and Waylon does. He does not change.

Chapter 119: Gold

At about 10:40, through Ferra's eyes, the K Amara comes down out of the Band after three hours at full burn, the last hour on the afterburner, and Ferra already has the diesel figure. The Claim is under them: the field grey in a broad track, the sheds smoking, the battery silent, and the beast, thirty yards long, its head down over the works camp's gate, where a man in a Kaldo coat stands in front of the families' road with a pike. Two dragon rifles fire from the K Amara's apron as she comes in, the first fired since the voyage, steel it cannot read: one round through a shoulder plate, one through the neck plates, about nine rounds between them. It turns its head up at the ship. Arthur calls check fire, because the king has gone off the apron on his envelope, straight down at the gate, without looking back at her.

Through Waylon's eyes at the gate, the beast's foot is coming down on the gate, and the man with the pike is his brother, and the king's body answers before the king decides. All of him changes at once. The man in the black coat coming down out of the sky is, before he lands, a dragon, the first full form anyone has ever seen: charred black scale with molten ember fissures, dark crimson wings that blot out the sun over the gate, a crest of swept spines and one pair of horns, gold eyes, about twenty metres long and smaller than the beast by about a third. He lands on it.

It is mass against mass, claws against plates, and the beast is denser than anything he has ever pulled, so it is weight and leverage rather than casting. When he tries gravity on it, it reads the cast and pinches it out like the mages' glyphs, so he stops casting at it. Then the breath, fire pressed by gravity out of his own body rather than cast, which it cannot read as a glyph and cannot cancel, because it is a body, goes into the holes the rifles made in its shoulder and neck. Arthur, Bren and the flight knights come down after him in their suits and go for its legs with the Claim's watch. Mira comes up out of the cave mouth at the cove with Ward and goes in with her spear at the tendon behind the knee. At about 11:30 the beast is down on the grey field with its legs cut and its neck open, and he holds it with his body and burns it through the neck from inside the wound until it stops. Its gold eyes go dull last.

Then he says Waylon's name, and it is a word, not a roar: his own voice, only enormous, low enough that the gate's boards hum and the knights at the beast's legs hear it through their helms. It is the first thing anyone on the Isle has heard a dragon say. Waylon answers it as if his brother had asked him something, because he had.

Ward's party comes up out of the cave mouth behind Mira, and Imani walks straight across the grey field to the dragon with her bag, because he is a patient and she has never let size decide that. She puts both hands flat on his snout to read him, and he licks her, once, from her boots to her hair, with a tongue the width of a door, before either of them has thought about it. She stands there dripping. "Emeric." "Sorry," says the dragon, in a voice that rattles the sheds' shutters, and does not sound it. Ferra, on the K Amara's ladder, laughs out loud for the first time since the burn began, and stops when she remembers what is under the sheds. Imani wipes her eyes clear with her sleeve and reads him anyway. "You're empty." He knows. The full change is the most expensive thing he does, he is past the floor of himself with nothing to draw in the island's thin, stone-drunk air, and he cannot change back.

Chapter 120: Seven Hours

From 11:30 until about ten past seven in the evening the dragon lies on the grey field beside the carcass, too big to be anywhere else, in the sun, unable to be a man, for seven and a half hours. Ferra sits against his foreleg with her book on her knees and counts the dead as they are brought in, because that is what she does with what she cannot fix, and reads the names to him. Imani comes and goes from the works' ward, puts her hand flat on his neck between patients, and says his heart is sound and empty. Waylon sits on his other side and says nothing for an hour, then says he was not worth flying a thousand miles for. The dragon says, "No." Ferra writes it down anyway.

The works crews and the Islanders saw the king fall out of the sky and become the thing that killed the thing, and some come and stand at a distance for a long time. Mother Sabbe walks up to his head, looks him in the eye, and goes back to her fire. An Islander girl of about six, from the gate's sheds, who saw him land on the beast from twenty yards, screams when he turns his head. For the rest of the week she will not let him near her, even when he is a man again and sits down in the cook tent, and he does not try. Her mother apologises. He tells her not to. The crews of the K Amara and the K Endeavor are sworn to nothing this time, because there is nothing left to swear.

That night by lamplight Oskel, a sapper of twenty years who never liked Emeric and has two of his men dead under a cannon, writes his report to Kesrath, and it is honest: the beast came out of Kaldo's shaft; the King of Randillia became a dragon and killed it; Kaldo's knights went in where his own would not. He does not know what to call the king in it, and leaves a blank. On the tower's roof the Church's priest watched it all through his long glass from the first cannon to the dusk. He writes to Ostrel in the Church's cipher by the next Duvrenne boat: a beast with gold in its eyes came out of the gold-eyed king's shaft, and then the king showed what he is. The Church gets its word for him the same week.

In Kaldo, on Monday afternoon, Amara's sight through the relay's glass puts Kaldo's verified word in Saban's hand before the king has changed back. Saban works it out, in his head and then to Corbin in the chair. Kaldo was told the truth in January and it held. The Church will have its version within the week and the courts within a month, distorted. So tell the city tonight, plainly, before any rumour does, the king's dragon included, because a city told first cannot be told otherwise later, and name no dead until the families have been told, from Ferra's list on the K Endeavor's set. Corbin agrees. Kaldo News's late sheet goes up on the boards at nine.

At about ten past seven he comes back to himself on the grey field with Ferra's coat over him and every bone aching. The first thing he says as a man is to ask how many, though he heard every name from her in the afternoon. Ferra tells him anyway: fourteen. He asks their names. She reads them again.

Chapter 121: Shut

From Tuesday 20 to Thursday 22 April the beast's tells come from different people, and nobody has to say what they add up to. Imani types its blood against every type on her register: no type, no near match. And in it is a third strand she has seen before and could not place, in her own register's samples from every people aboard the fleet; she writes "the same unknown" in the margin. Armand and Emeric listen to its note as it bleeds out, and there is no family in it, no likeness to anything that ever hatched or was born here. Imani opens it with the works' surgeon: no wear on its claws, no scars, no parasites, a gut that has only ever held stone, bones with no growth lines. It never lived. Every mage who threw at it says it read magic. Oskel's report says it studied the guns before anyone of Kaldo's does. Its eyes are gold. Ferra writes that down, and nobody says it aloud. That evening Waylon looks at his own reflection in a works shed's window for a long time.

The carcass does not rot. Its mana bleeds into the air, and the island's air thickens for weeks: the works mages feel it on Tuesday, the Islanders' goats get frisky, and nobody gets sick. It will be grey dust in one to three months. Tess and the works crews rig the K Endeavor's condensing plant on a frame over it, pressing what bleeds off toward mana ice in iron cells, enough to fill the giant golem's canisters for a season. Every man who handles the blood is written in Imani's book by name, date and hours and checked weekly. Nothing has been found yet.

On Wednesday Tess cuts out the gait core from the chest, the crystal brain, translucent grey-violet with folded lobes and icy-blue lines glowing inside, about seventy by forty-five centimetres in a beast this size, still lit. Behind the eyes is the cradle mark, a grain of written crystal nobody can read yet. Tess knows from Kaldo's own work what keeps a stone's charge: sealed iron. She builds the shut case on the spot from the works' stores, an iron box from the oil works' fittings, copper crush gaskets cut from the pipe joints between knife-edged flanges, no rubber because rubber leaks as it reverts, and a needle on the front from a works sensor stone. The needle does not fall. She says the line Kaldo already owns: "It isn't dead. It's shut."

On Thursday, in the works office, Ferra settles the parts. The beast died on the Claim's side of the field, but it came out of a shaft under the Compact and fought on both crowns' ground, so under the Compact's escrow rule, with House Ducatis holding it, the carcass belongs to the Compact's three owners. Kaldo keeps the core and both eyes, because it holds the only way to keep them, the shut case, and says so. Duvrenne takes plates and tendon sealed in Kaldo's iron, by Oskel's signature. The Islanders take a share of the dust and the condenser's ice, by Mother Sabbe's. The Church's priest asks for an eye, for Ostrel, and is refused politely: the Compact has three owners, and the Church is not one. Ferra writes to the doors' families by the next hull to the Sun Shore, with the first trade sets, before any rumour reaches them: the zone held, nothing inside it was touched, the beast came from the hollow outside it, the names of the dead, and that Kaldo does not yet know what the hollow is.

That night in the cook tent, with the six of them who are there and the beast's core in an iron box under the table, Emeric thinks what Earth taught him, that the first monster is never the only one, and does not say it. Aloud he says: "We don't know how many. We build as if it's a lot." Ward says it is the first sensible thing anyone has said all week.

Chapter 122: Fourteen

At nine on Monday night Kaldo News's late sheet goes up on the boards, written by Saban himself: a beast came up out of the ground under the Isle of Shards; it killed people of the works; the king fought it in his dragon's form and killed it; the dead will be named when their families have been told. No adjectives. From Tuesday to Friday, through Corbin's eyes in the chair with the king away, the fishwives on the quay, the carters and the school gate show fear and pride in the same breath. By Wednesday the Heirs' broadsheet carries the rumour's first form, brought in by a Duvrenne trader off the strait: the gold-eyed king's shaft bred a beast with his gold in its eyes, and then he showed them what he is. Corbin does not answer it in print. He has the casualty list read at the records hall at noon each day as the names come in.

Honor Linden copies the casualty list for the records hall's board, because copying is her work, and stops at the place: the Isle of Shards. Her son died on it. She finishes the list, pins it, and goes home. At the Heirs' meeting that week she says nothing either way, the first time they have seen her with nothing to say. The half of the movement that stayed with her waits for her. The angrier half does not.

On Friday 23 April the king comes home on the K Amara with Ward and the party, while Waylon stays with the works. He spends two days empty and is on his feet on the third, and Imani will not let him core-fly for a week. Runa smells the Isle on him, does not like it, says so, and sits on his feet in the castle kitchen for an hour. On Saturday Tess brings home a shard of the beast's plate in a sealed iron jar to show Imani something, and opens it near Mochi. The slime goes flat, cold-coloured and still, and will not touch it, the first thing it has ever refused. Runa, who has eaten everything Mochi has ever eaten, backs away from the jar too. Emeric reads Mochi with two fingers for a breath and no more, and takes his hand away fast: it is not food, it is not anything, the same cold as the vault. He tells Tess to put the lid back on and take the jar to the iron shed. Ferra's case book gets its first entry in a new column, under Maeve's: Emeric, full, the catalyst again, someone he could not lose about to die. Under it: Waylon, eyes gold, first signs, no change. She does not draw a line under it yet. On Saturday by set Waylon says he is staying on the Isle with the works stopped and the shaft to be filled; Alina has written from Kesrath, and Oskel shook his hand. He does not mention his eyes. Emeric does, and Waylon says he knows, he saw them in the window, and changes the subject to the plug. Ferra's book also records that this was no step on the ladder of violence: he killed a thing that never lived to save his brother, and nobody needs to push back on that. What the bill is, the world will decide.

On Saturday the K Endeavor brings the dead to the castle quay, fourteen in all: the shaft-head watch, two Islanders and a Kaldo hand; Bryn; four of the Claim's watch; two of Duvrenne's, sent home to Kesrath on Oskel's boat with Kaldo's honours; and four of the works crews killed at the shed and the generator house. The Islanders bury theirs on the Isle at Mother Sabbe's word, so only the Kaldo dead come home. Bryn is buried on the Isle, but his sister, Dacey, comes to Kaldo on the K Endeavor anyway and stands on the gangway. On Tuesday Neris asks whether the First Room will add the Isle. Ferra says not until the review has sat, and then the dead's names go on the wall first. Neris says that is right, and that she will check it herself. On Monday 26 April Doran says the words over Kaldo's dead at the parish church and writes their names in the death book he has kept for longer than the king has been alive. The First Room adds nothing to its walls, because the review has not sat.

Chapter 123: The Record

From Monday 26 April to Wednesday 5 May, in Ferra's office, Ferra and Tess build the record: every paper in order, nothing summarised, each with its date and signer. First, the hollow beating once, from the expedition's log. Second, the surveyor's old line that did not match the bore map, 23 June last year, unexplained. Third, the warm rock and a second beat, 15 September, the shaft foreman's report. Fourth, Tess's pause asked for the same day, and refused. Fifth, Harrold's exception under the king's own clause, 15 September, with the date and the reason in his hand: the relief pair's run home and the Answer Tower needed the oil after the raid burned the reserve. Sixth, Bryn's first signed warning in early November, filed as insufficient to halt. Seventh, the engineer's finding and the survey fund deferred by the Assembly on 9 February, with the vote. Eighth, the night shift's last charges at the shaft's foot on 2 March, from the works log. Ninth, Bryn's second warning, 20 March. Tenth, the datum, from Tess and Linus's field book, and the king's stop order in his own hand, both 24 March. Eleventh, the draught, the clearing and the plug, 15 to 18 April, Tess and Ward. Twelfth, the waking, 19 April at 05:40, and the dead, fourteen, by name.

On Thursday 6 May Emeric reads it first, alone in the long room, from the first page to the last. Then Harrold, who came down from the capital the day he heard, reads it, and comes to his own signature on page five and does not look away from it. Neither of them says anything about it that night. On Friday Ferra puts Bryn's two warnings on top of the record, out of date order, because they are the ones that were right. Nobody asks her to put them back.

On Saturday 8 May, in the morning, there is a woman at the castle's gate. Dacey, Bryn's sister, an Islander net-mender, buried her brother on the Isle at Mother Sabbe's word, came to Kaldo with Kaldo's dead anyway, and has stayed a fortnight in the Islanders' lodging by the Port Town. She has her brother's two warnings copied out in her own hand, and she is asking for the king. She waits for his answer. The king walks down to the gate.

Where the Story Stands After Chapter 123

The first beast came up the Isle's shaft on 19 April and is dead. Fourteen people died, about thirty were hurt, and the shaft, stopped on 24 March, will be filled under Waylon and Oskel. The beast never lived: no blood type, no growth lines, no family in its note, a gut that held only stone, and gold eyes that Ferra wrote down and nobody said aloud. Its core and both eyes are in Kaldo's shut cases, and behind the eyes is a cradle mark nobody can read. The carcass belongs to the Compact and is bleeding mana into the island's air under a condenser, and will be dust within months. Imani has found a third strand in its blood that matches an unknown in every people's samples, and is watching every man who handles it. Mochi will not go near it. A small seal went into the sea off the cove at 09:41, and something small went away through the gallery toward the cove, and nobody has connected them.

The world has seen the King of Randillia as a dragon in daylight. The works crews, the Islanders, Duvrenne's people under Oskel, whose honest report leaves a blank where the king's name should be, and a Church priest with a long glass whose cipher letter is on its way to Ostrel. Kaldo told its own city the truth the same night, dragon included, and the Heirs' broadsheet answered with the rumour that the gold-eyed king's shaft bred a beast with his gold in its eyes. Honor Linden, whose son died on the Isle, has said nothing either way. Emeric is recovered, not yet flying, the full form now his and known, its cost proven: seven hours unable to be a man. Waylon's eyes went gold at the gate, and he has not changed. An Islander girl of six will not let the king near her.

The record is laid out in order, with Harrold's signature on page five and Bryn's two warnings on top. Dacey waits at the castle gate with her brother's warnings in her own hand, and the king is walking down to her. Duvrenne's answer on a royal visit to its half of the Isle is due in May. Ferra's letter goes to the doors' families with the first trade sets. The First Room will add nothing about the Isle until a review has sat, and then the dead's names go on its wall first. The king has said aloud that they do not know how many, and that they will build as if it is a lot.

This batch is about warnings and who listened to them. Every paper that could have stopped the shaft existed, signed and filed, and the one that found the error came three weeks after the last charges. Kaldo's people did the hard things right on the day: Ward gave up a clean kill to bring five out of the dark, Waylon held a gate, and a king spent himself past the floor. And still it ends with a sister at the gate holding the warnings that were right.
