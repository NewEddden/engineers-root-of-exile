Book 2, Batch 15: Colder Than the Listening

Chapters 102–108 | Saturday 14 November – Monday 28 December, Year 17

The fleet has left Aumere for the dragon matriarch Saldis's cone, the youngest live volcano of the Caldera Chain, to answer her summons. Since the night Corvan struck the seed party, Emeric has kept his promise: no climb to the Band, no drum, nothing new placed there. He now carries a mana core, Imani's week of watching it is nearly done, and Duncan Hale of the Westgone march is aboard with his breath count, his staffs and his letters unopened. Zale has shared with Ilenne and stayed at Aumere with the pickup pair, to meet the fleet on its way home. Liora and Neris have joined the fleet, and Runa has been promised that Emeric will teach her himself.

The fleet comes to the dragons' house the way the orc house said a dragon's house is come to: on the water, under its own diesels, nothing in the air. At the cone a dragon with one horn walks two women up to her mother's grave and does not know why she wanted them there, and her grandmother breathes the same two women in and sends her grounded son up the mountain before she says a word. Kaldo owns its breach of a promise it cannot read, offers to let the dragons measure its conduct, and is granted one climb home. Halfway up the wind turns, and Emeric chooses restraint at a cost the whole fleet feels.

Then the old dragon says the north is the one place colder than the listening, and nobody aboard knows what she means. No people keeps that sky, so nobody can give leave to draw on it, and the fleet flies north drawing nothing, meets two relief hulls from home, and lies a month on open water under ice cliffs at the top of the world while one hull drills six hundred metres into the ice to find the year the world's mana fell. The clues pile up and every one is dismissed. On 25 December the whole fleet eats at the Long Table on the ice under the lights.

Chapter 102: Her House

At dusk on Saturday 14 November the fleet sets down on the sea at the line the orc house's first warden named, with the cone's glow on the water. Ferra writes the stop's reasons at the K Discovery's rail: the summons must be answered, the climb home must be asked for, and the fleet must come as a dragon's house is come to. Ward sets the night watch. The night glass's heat layer is useless against the warm rock of the ledges, so a dragon lying on them cannot be told from the stone. Once, a dragon shows against the sky and is gone, and Ward writes it in the watch book. Runa stands at the K Endeavor's rail watching the hearth on the mountain that never goes out. Duncan says you do not go ashore at a house unless you are named. Ward agrees out loud.

Before dawn on Sunday, in the K Discovery's chart room, Imani reads Emeric for the seventh morning. The core is shut, a little larger than at the lock, with no tracery. She writes the week's last line: seven days, no tracery, pressed once at Aumere with leave, closed. Under it: he may teach. She tells him the week is done, that she will read whoever he teaches one at a time, and that he will not hurry her. He says he was not going to. She writes that down too.

At first light the fleet comes in on its diesels to lie off the cone's lee. Six go ashore in a launch with an engine and no stone in it, so nothing of Kaldo's touches her air: Emeric, Ferra and Imani, who have eaten stones; Imani because Corvan's arms are owed to her; Ward, dense enough for the cone and going where his king goes in a house that wants his king dead; Arthur, because Sohra's house sent word in October that she would want to see the man with the gun she could not feel, wearing a personal scrubber at the collar against the thick air; and Grau to interpret. They carry knives only. Runa stays aboard, because a child does not go into a house where a man wants her father dead, and Grau agrees. Ashore Emeric spends nothing. His body takes in outside mana only where there is room, so as long as he casts nothing he stays full and nothing comes in, but the moment he spends, the densest sky he has ever stood in would fill him back without his choosing. So he casts nothing, not a pull on a pebble, not warmth in the rain. Ferra knows why his hands are cold and says nothing.

Corvan sits on warm rock above the landing shelf, four weeks on, his arms still bound to his body and his wings flat under Imani's canvas, two adults of his house standing behind him. He looks at Emeric and does not speak; he said it at the ring. Imani asks through Grau whether both his hands are warm. Corvan will not answer a human. One of the adults says something short in the house's speech, and Corvan holds out both bound hands as far as the canvas allows. Imani looks at them from three paces without touching, and nods: both warm, the colour right. She tells him the canvas can come off in two weeks if he keeps still. He is unchanged.

Mid-morning Sohra lands on the shelf in her humanoid form, gold-eyed, dark bronze scaling at throat and wrist, her left horn a stump at the root. She does not look at Emeric. She looks at Ferra and Imani and asks through Grau, without explaining, whether the two of them will walk with her. Ward says no. Emeric says yes, because he can see she does not know why she is asking, and he would rather she found out with him watching from the shelf. A short climb above the surf, in a hollow of old lava where the ground is warm from below and the rain steams off it, a very large body lies under a low drift of shed bronze scale gone dark, mostly a shape now. Sohra says it is her mother. She was four hundred when her mother died, and it has been a hundred years. She does not say what her mother died of. The scale over her is the mother's own, shed and given back over her, the most a house gives anyone. Nothing is taken from the place. Sohra stands with the two women by her mother and says she wanted them here and cannot say why. Imani does not push, and Ferra does not ask the next question. Nobody says bond.

Late in the morning Saldis comes down a path no one made, old, visibly, never helpless, and the two adults step back without being told. She stops in front of the six and breathes in, as Sohra did on the K Endeavor's deck in May, and goes completely still, the way an animal does when it has found something it was not looking for. Before she says anything to anyone, she turns her head and says one word in her house's speech to Corvan, and he gets up and goes up the mountain with the two adults behind him, slowly, because of his arms. She waits until he is above the first ledge before she speaks again, and what she says then is a greeting to the king, in orc, through Grau, and nothing about the two women. Ward sees an old soldier clear a room. Ferra sees Ward see it.

Chapter 103: One Charge

That afternoon on the shelf, Saldis lets her granddaughter go first, because this is hers. Arthur comes with nothing in his hands. He had thought of bringing the rifle's empty case to show what it was, and Ward told him a dragon who has been shot does not need a lesson in what shot her. Sohra asks through Grau whether he is the one. He says yes. She asks why she could not feel it. He says it has no stone in it; it is iron and powder. She thinks about that for a long time. He says the fleet was under attack, two hundred people on a burning deck, his commander gave the order, and he would fire again. He does not apologise for the shot. Then he says the other half, which he has told only Mira: he knows now what she was doing on her first pass, the warning, nobody on that deck could have read it, she had the right of it as she read it, and he took her horn anyway. He does not ask her to forgive it. She looks at him a long time and touches the stump, not for him. She lets it stand. Then she asks whether he will be on the climb tomorrow. He says he will be on the K Amara's apron. She says she will be beside the K Amara. Nobody says what that means.

Emeric speaks next, plainly, in orc, Grau carrying the hard words. As her house reads the Leaving Terms, his crossing broke them, and his fleet's charging in the Band was the proof. He says both. He cannot answer the charge itself, because neither he nor Ferra has seen the text, and he says that too. Saldis names her own seal in her own words: the land's people would stay behind the line their side drew, her kind would stay out of their sky and not come over it to take back what was taken, and every house that set its seal would answer a crossing. No text, no date. Ferra hears it, cannot check it against anything, and writes it down as said. Then Saldis says she knew his face. It sat at her fire when it was family, and then it built the quiet ground that took her mate out of the sky. Nobody names the man. She sets the line apart, as a condition rather than a comfort: he has the face; he did not do the deed; what he does in her sky is his.

Kaldo offers only what it can keep: to stop drawing on the Band wherever the fleet can avoid it, and to let the dragons' own observers measure the mana and confirm it rather than take Kaldo's word. Ferra adds her part: a column of every draw, where it was taken and how much, for whoever the dragons send to read it. She asks whether she may write the seal's words in her book as Saldis's own, with her leave under them, so whoever reads it in Kaldo reads what the house said and not what Kaldo made of it. Saldis says, "Write them as mine." Ferra does, on the shelf in the rain, and writes beneath: her words; her leave. Imani offers to regrow Sohra's horn, and says the cost plainly as a doctor: a body as dense as Sohra's costs the mending at that body's tier, far beyond any human's, and it would take days, and she would do it. Saldis refuses for her granddaughter. The stump is her house's record of the breach, and it stays until the debt is paid. Sohra agrees, and touches the stump as she says it, a young adult's own wish set against her house's duty.

Emeric asks her leave, once, for the fleet's way home, which is a climb to the Band through her house's sky. He tells her the route first, because a leave given on half the story would have to be renegotiated: the fleet goes north first, to the top of the world, for a month, to read a clock nobody wrote, in the ice. No house keeps that sky, she says, and no dragon flies over it. He asks why. "It's the one place colder than the listening." Nobody on the shelf knows what she means. Grau renders it word for word and shrugs, Ferra writes it down, and Emeric takes it for a dragon's way of naming the Band law and lets it go. He follows it to its condition: then nobody there can give leave to draw, so the fleet will draw nothing there. Saldis says, "Then you'll be cold," the nearest she comes to a joke. She grants the climb once, at dawn tomorrow, the hour her house flies its own morning climb, with Sohra going up beside the fleet to watch the racks: every neck shut all the way through her sky, and one charge, taken at the top in the Band, of what the climb needs and no more. Ferra works out each hull's figure that evening from Armand's barometer and wind readings and reads it aloud on the shelf. It is the first agreement Kaldo has ever made with any dragon. Asked why she grants it, Saldis says she is three thousand four hundred years old and will not leave this to her son. She does not forgive the breach. She grants the passage. The dragons' answer stays open; she will carry word to the other houses that a kin-face keeps his word, so far.

No gift is offered, because dragons want nothing any people makes and a gift from the rule-breaker is its own insult. But Sohra asks, straight, how her house will hear Ferra's column once the ships are gone. Ferra answers with the eighth set, not as a gift but as the column's line: Kaldo's debt read to the creditor on the first of every month in Ferra's own voice. It will be kept on the first warden's line stack. No field is asked, since a field in a dragon's sky is the very thing her seal answers, and the seed hand takes nothing from the cone. Back aboard by dark, Runa moves to the K Discovery with Mochi and Grau, because Emeric said he would teach her himself, and that is where he will be.

When she takes the set, Emeric asks her one thing back: how a hatchling learns to be small. Hatched whole, she tells him flatly, and taught small at the Fall by the eldest. He is doing it backwards from a fingernail, and from what his letters say his father came out the right way round and nobody has taught him how to come back. Nothing is offered.

At dawn on Monday, through Sohra's eyes, six hulls lift off her grandmother's sea together, every neck shut, climbing on full racks on a north-westerly heading. Sohra comes off the rim and takes station off the K Amara's port quarter, level with the racks she burned in May. Ferra warned on the shelf that the K Amara's figure was the thinnest, the heaviest hull with the least to spare, and Emeric took the hour anyway, because a guest does not haggle with a host over the hour she names. About two-thirds of the way up, where the trade winds give out and the westerlies begin, the wind at the Band's foot is stronger than the barometer suggested and against them. Every hull spends more than its figure, the K Amara most. Ferra, reading her gauge every five minutes from the K Discovery's radio room, calls her below the reserve mark short of the Band's foot. She falls behind and settles by the head.

Two safe options are said aloud on the set. Ward, on the K Amara: open her necks for ten minutes here, and she would make the Band's foot with margin, though the dragon beside them would see it. Ferra, who counts everything, gives the other sum: in an hour in this sky, the densest air any of them has touched, Emeric could drink and press at once and carry the K Amara's bow to the Band's foot on his own gravity, without opening a neck. Sohra hears none of it and sees all of it. She feels for the pull she felt from him in May, the Band-thief drinking, and there is nothing: a dense weight in him that does not leak and does not take. She knows the shape of it: a small hard knot of what a dragon is all through, held in one place in a thin body. It was made, not born, and she has never felt anything built that way. She tells herself it is his blood, and that settles her a little. It is the conduct, not the knot, that moves her this morning. On the set Emeric gives the order in one sentence: empty the K Amara's forward water tanks into the sea. About forty tonnes of fresh water fall out of her belly in a long silver sheet that catches the first sun under the cloud. Sohra watches it fall all the way down. The K Amara lifts her head. The whole fleet slows to her pace and climbs together, an hour slower than planned. For an hour the K Amara's people are below the reserve mark in a dragon's sky, the bridge quiet, the apron knights planted, Maeve on deck with her hands in her sleeves, and a second climb was never granted. Ward looks down once at empty water where a pickup pair would have been, and says nothing. Every hull floats; it is the one moment anyone feels the pair gone. The K Amara's people go on a drinking ration of two litres a head until the hoses go across at sea. At the top the fleet opens its necks once, in the Band, and each hull takes its figure and no more. The K Amara takes her figure, not what she spent, and goes on north short by the wind's share. Ferra writes both numbers in the column she promised. At the Band's foot, before the necks open, Sohra rises level with the K Amara's port racks and looks at the shut necks for a long moment. Then she looks along the apron to where Arthur is planted, and he lifts one hand, empty. She turns back and drops away down her grandmother's sky without a sound. Corvan watches from his ledge, grounded and unchanged. Emeric stands at the rail the whole climb and never flies in her sky.

That night in the K Discovery's after cabin, the hull steady in the Band with her necks shut and Runa asleep on Mochi through a thin wall, Emeric thinks. Three thousand four hundred years, and Saldis has buried a mate and a daughter. His own blood is part dragon. Ferra and Imani have eaten stones, and nobody can read their ceiling. Will they be here that long? And one more thought he does not build on: an old woman still enforcing in person a seal she set two thousand years ago is what a ruler who never leaves looks like. Imani notices first, then Ferra, because he let her climb figures stand without a single correction, which he has never done. He tells them plainly. Imani gives him the facts. Ferra does the sum on the back of the climb's figure sheet, writing "extrapolated" beside it. Last, as half a joke because the alternative is saying it seriously, the golem option, with his mother as proof. He kisses them both, the same, in turn. Ferra laughs. It eases him, and answers nothing.

Chapter 104: The Circle

On Tuesday 17 November, in the Band on the leg north, the class begins in the K Discovery's chart room. Ferra and Imani are there first, before the knights of the K Discovery's watch, and he knows why, and nobody else does. Duncan teaches the Current himself, since it is his to give, on one condition: he teaches it and Emeric does not, because "you'll teach it wrong," said again with less heat. Emeric teaches inward stasis drills, padding by its proper name, and the Kindling, with Imani reading every room before a hand is laid on anyone. Two teachers who argue and are both right. Only steps that take nothing from outside are allowed; the press that draws on the air waits for home. Nobody presses aboard, Emeric included, from the climb to the return. He tells Duncan the rule he teaches is the rule he keeps, and notices with mild irritation that his core will be no bigger at the Long Table than at Aumere. Duncan, who reaches a slow count of sixty with stasis by the Long Table, says the march has waited thirty years and can wait two more months, and that he wants to press his first core in front of the steward Hale.

At the chart table Ferra records why the fleet goes north. Ice keeps the air of every year it fell in, and every people's account of the war disagrees, so Armand wants the year the mana fell from a clock nobody wrote. Gisela has a saying, that the makers rested the south and kept the north. The route goes north-west through the Band, outside the wall and west of Ostrellin, about 4,300 miles up the Western Ocean to a relief rendezvous, then about 2,300 more round the ring's north-western corner and across the polar sea to a near coast at about 84 degrees north, then about 600 inland: some 7,000 miles. They go outside the wall rather than over home because of Ward's secrecy rule: a fleet seen over Ostrellin's coasts would be in every pulpit by the week's end. They fly the first stage in the Band, above the autumn storm track and where thin air makes every mile cheaper, at an economy pace, necks shut the whole way. Nothing is drawn from the Band north of Saldis's sky. The fleet will fly on the one charge, the sea and the relief, and no relays go north, since a relay is a thing sitting in the Band drawing on its own stones and nobody there can give leave. Ward points out that the drums are now thousands of miles south, and the relay net that would find a downed hull finds nobody up here. Calls must go by skywave, and some nights nothing will get through. That night, privately, Emeric tells Ferra and Imani that on his world children believe a man in a red coat lives at the north pole and makes the gifts. Ferra asks why anyone would live somewhere so stupid. Imani, without looking up, asks what he eats.

That afternoon, on the after cabin floor, Runa sits facing Emeric with Mochi at her back and Grau at the door for the words her Kaldo does not yet have. Imani reads her and tells Emeric the size of the whisper. Runa holds his hands while he runs her mana left to right, into his left, and back to her, round and round. She tightens against it the way she tightens against being held, and he stops at once, because mana pushed into a body that will not have it is forcing, and forcing scars. She glares at him for stopping and tells him, in Kaldo, to begin again. She fails twice more. Then she has it, all at once, and her ears go straight up. It is the thing she has done with the air all her life without knowing it, felt from inside for the first time, a warm loop through her hands like water running round a stone. She says it smells like him. Grau does not translate that and does not need to. She tries it herself, drops it, and does it again. Imani finds no tracery and her room a little wider already, and Ferra writes her into the register under her own name, the first child in it.

On Wednesday the fleet comes down to the sea at the relief rendezvous, about a thousand miles off Velmoor's west coast, outside the wall, past the storm belt's worst, to lie on the water and charge low from the thick air. The K Amara goes alongside the K Endeavor and hull four in turn, fresh water crosses by hose, and her ration is lifted by Thursday. The whole class now gathers on the K Endeavor's hospital deck. The padding knights are ahead from the first night: Arthur, Mira, Bren and the Crestless, and the rebuilt knights with passive padding, Ward among them. Bren is loudest and least patient. Ward comes as a skeptic, asks what it costs before he asks what it gives, and is bad at it, which he takes better than anyone expected. The mages struggle with inward work, and Maeve is quietly furious to be behind the knights and works harder than anyone because of it. Ferra keeps the register. Imani reads every room and watches for tracery with the authority to stop anyone, and begins teaching Bettany to read a room for the Kindling, because one reader cannot spread anything. Bettany is slow and stubborn, which Imani says is the right kind. A few of the crew decide by the second night it is not for them, and Ferra writes their names in the register's last column without comment. The deckhands who have had headaches since Aumere come first to Duncan's count, and later say the headaches are less; Imani writes that she cannot tell how much is the breath and how much is being eight months out. Zale's first Thursday ring gets through from Aumere: the pair are well, and Abloh has started with the sea-silk weavers. On the hospital deck, Imani and Grau type the blood of every people aboard: Kaldo's humans, Duncan the Westgone human, Grau the werewolf, Runa the half-blood, Neris of the merfolk, Siv the Murr, Gisela of the Sabir. They all share the same blood groups. Imani writes it down with no explanation, because she has none. Mochi has no blood. At hull four's bench Bram begins broad ice pads for the K Discovery's legs.

Chapter 105: Hulls Five and Six

On Friday 20 November, on the K Amara's deck at the rendezvous, a grey gale-edge afternoon, Runa watches Mira's spear lesson. Mira walks at Maeve down the wet deck with the shaft low, Maeve says "Sunday" under her breath and does not move, and Mira stops a hand off her ribs. Then comes Maeve's lightning drill with Emeric, whose lightning is off his affinity and poor, which is why she drills him. Runa hears his bolt crack off the masthead for the first time and is flat on the deck with her ears flattened under both hands before the thunder finishes. He crouches a body-length off and teaches her one word: when he says "Ears," she drops and covers them, no questions. For a week she does it at the wrong moments, every time anyone says the word, and on the second day Bren discovers this and abuses it until Mira stops him with the butt of her spear. At dusk the gale comes through properly, about force nine, the hulls riding to sea anchors. Emeric goes off the K Amara's apron on his core envelope, a few hundred yards to windward and low, inside Ward's sight, a helm knight keeping his heat in the night glass's grid, and pins himself. He hangs dead still for about a minute while the spray goes past him sideways, turning his face out of rain that would not let him breathe. Armand, below on the K Endeavor, feels the thump and says "the pin" to nobody. It costs him about a fifth of the core, the answer to what a storm would cost. Imani times him and tells him off on the apron in front of a ten-year-old. His notebook line that night: air in a storm, a reserve? Runa asks whether she can learn to do that. He says later, again, and she says he says that about everything. From then on he is the one who gets her clothes, and he has the relief's furs cut down to her size at hull four's bench. She pretends not to care, and her tail gives her away.

On Saturday's call, half the slot sheet gets through by skywave before the band closes. Harrold reports the stadium's bowl rising now that the spire crews are off it, Corbin chairing the bread-price hearing in the Port Town and keeping it from becoming a riot, and the city Shell's seventh spire commissioned. "How's he holding up?" "He's working." Sela's line on Harrold's sheet reports the night school's adult readers this term, her teachers' college's first class sitting, and the first radio lesson heard in three holdings at once. Amara is the last slot. She reads him through his voice in about four sentences and says he sounds like a man who has been doing sums. He is glad of her in a way he cannot explain on an open line under Ward's rule, and says he will tell her at home. She lets it go.

On Sunday hulls five and six come up on the expected bearing on the day in Ferra's book, with Cade again nursing hull six. They bring about two months' food, about seventy-five tonnes packed for the cold; the cold kit, furs and mittens for everyone going on the ice, fire-stone heaters with battery-warmed floors for the Band suits, goggles and snow tents; diesel in drums for the K Discovery's generator at the summit; the heating element and about seven hundred metres of armoured cable for a thermal drill; liquid mana for hull four's tanks, which Ferra's "drawn where" column records honestly as Band mana condensed at the Well over Kaldo, home's own charge and not the fleet's; medical stores for frostbite and altitude; and Tess's letters and the home news. Nothing from the collection goes home with them, and nothing living crosses either way. Ferra reads the food sum aloud on hull five's deck: about sixty-five tonnes when the pair arrived, about a hundred and forty with the relief, about one and a half a day from the 24th in the cold, every soul on the cold ration, and about seventy at home on 10 January. The margin is the plan. Bram tests his ice pads by setting the K Discovery's legs down on hull four's deck on the swell, grumbling that he is building skis for a ship; Dagny says it is the first thing in eight months he has built that he understands. The pair turns south at first light on Tuesday.

Then the fleet runs north low with its necks shut, about eleven hours round the ring's north-western corner, past the wall's northern end, over the polar sea, and it is dark from the first hour: polar night. In the eighth or ninth hour the sky over the pack goes green, a curtain and then three, moving slowly from the north-east with a red fringe at their tops. Nobody aboard has seen one; Kaldo lies too far south. Gisela gives her people's word for it. Emeric gives Kaldo's: the aurora, the sun's wind meeting the lodestone pull of the world, in one line. Under the near ice cliffs, the wind off the dome pours over the edge and pushes new sea ice offshore as fast as it forms, so a stretch of black, steaming open water lies along the cliff foot all winter. Ferra's chart calls it the Lead. Holding station in the Band over the summit for a month would mean drawing in a sky nobody can give leave for, after promising to draw nothing there, and big hulls never sit on ground, so the five big hulls lie a month at the Lead on the water, charging low from the thick sea air. The cost is icing. At thirty below and colder, with the wind off the dome and the sea steaming, spray freezes on everything, and every watch the knights break ice off rigging, rails and rack necks with mauls and their hands, while Ferra keeps a column on how low each iced hull sits.

On Thursday Neris goes by launch to the foot of the cliffs and asks to taste the sea, because a Rim smith reads water the way Bram reads iron. Iron, she says, and nothing big living in it, in water this thick, where at Aumere the thick water swarms. She says it is wrong. Gisela writes it in her collection beside an old merfolk saying about the cold current. Nobody pursues it. Zale's Thursday ring gets through, faint. Maeve tells him she has not done anything stupid in the cold yet. He tells her it is early.

Chapter 106: Polar Night

On Friday 27 November the K Discovery lifts from the Lead in company with escort one, because no hull flies alone past the wall, and Ward reads the rule his own way: a hull set down on two miles of ice is a camp, not a flight, and every flight between camp and Lead goes in company. Bram flies three hours inland over a dome so flat he does not believe his altimeter, to the summit at about ten and a half thousand feet. The K Discovery sets down on her new ice pads, which sink a hand and hold. The camp is the K Discovery as the house, snow tents, a heated work tent over the drill site fifty paces off with a fire-stone brazier and the generator's line, a snow trench for the cores, and rope lines between every door. Escort one and the apron tender rotate crews in weekly shifts from the Lead.

Imani's rules are posted on the K Discovery's ladder and read aloud to every shift. Pairs: everyone outside has a partner who checks their face every half hour for white patches. No bare skin on metal, ever. Two hours outside, then the tent. Drink, because cold air is dry. Never thaw a frozen part that might freeze again; bring the person in first. Heaters on in every Band suit, and knights included in the pairing rule, because body enhancement keeps the core warm, not the nose. And the height: ten and a half thousand feet at the top of the world sits higher in the body than at home, because the polar air is thinner, so headaches are reported, not hidden. In the first week a deckhand's two fingertips are frostbitten, brought in, thawed once and kept, and a knight's cheek is frost-nipped, his partner's fault, which his partner knows. The knights work in shirtsleeves under their plate for the first hour until Imani makes them stop showing off. It is night for the whole month, and the night glass earns its keep, showing the camp's people as warm smudges in a world with nothing warm in it for six hundred miles.

Bram and Dagny's thermal drill is a ring-shaped hot-point, an annulus heated by Tess's element on the generator's current, that melts a ring downward and leaves a column of ice standing in the middle, broken off and wound up on the armoured cable half a metre at a time, with a fire stone in the head as a boost when the generator lags in the cold. The cores are logged in the heated tent for minutes only, then kept in the snow trench, then in the K Discovery's cold room for the voyage home, because ice that warms loses its record.

On the first clear, windless night, Emeric sets one of Ivo's two plate cameras on the drill tent's roof pointing straight up and leaves it open four hours. The plate shows every star as a sharp circle round one point almost overhead. He climbs the drill derrick with the K Discovery's spotting glass and finds the stars twinkle less up there than at snow level, the cold layer hugging the ice discovered by a man on a ladder. To Bram and Ferra he says: if anybody ever builds a big glass, it goes here.

Runa comes up with Grau for her first rotation. In the K Discovery's hold Mochi goes still and pulses at the floor plates every hour it is there. Runa says it is sulking, everyone blames the cold, since slimes slow in the cold, and Emeric does too. When the drill passes a few hundred feet, he sends his sense down the hole as he always does, and below a few hundred feet it goes quiet, quieter than ice should be. He decides cold ice holds its mana tight and thinks no more about it. In the second week Armand, at the K Discovery's rail, says aloud that this is the first sky past the wall with no dragons in it. Nobody asks why, and nobody, Armand included, sets it beside Saldis's line about the listening. On Saturday's call the aurora is up all night, the set hisses, and nothing gets through. Ferra closes the slot sheet unread. At home, the families sit on the line room's bench and go home.

At the Lead, through the long nights, the class continues in the K Endeavor's hold. Hull four's bench at last forges Duncan's bladed staffs from the Sea Hold's iron the fleet carries: a glaive, a curved blade on a long haft, the heavy blade of a guandao, a war scythe with its blade turned to stand straight off the pole the way farmers once did when they had to fight with what they had, and last the spear-staff, where Mira lives. Emeric is bad for a week and then fast, and Duncan's pleasure is badly hidden. Duncan waits for home to press his first core, and for the first time says the steward Hale's name aloud. The ice on the rigging comes back every watch.

Chapter 107: The Year the Mana Fell

Over three weeks in the heated tent, Armand reads each length of core under a sensor-stone needle, since all matter holds a little mana. In the upper few hundred metres they count the snow's yearly layers against a lamp, summer's grain and winter's different, and Ferra keeps the count; at the summit's rate, about six hundred metres is about three thousand years. For a long way down the needle reads high and even. About Monday 14 December, at about four hundred and thirty metres, in about a metre of ice, the needle falls and does not come back. Below it, high; above it, low, all the way to the snow they are standing on. Armand says it once: the world's mana fell in one band of years and never recovered, the dead ground and the burning of the groves seen from the one place nobody could bend the record. It is the war's date, from ice, two thousand years ago within a century. Ferra boxes the figure and writes "the fall" beside it, next to her three readings of the Leaving Terms' two dates. A century's error is wider than thirty-eight years, and the ice dates the fall, not the sealing. She still does not choose. The ice dates the fall; nobody claims it names the cause or the makers. The hole stops at about six hundred metres on Friday 18 December, when the cable's last splice is reached. Gisela asks whether that is what "kept" looks like. Armand says he is looking at the year the world went quiet, and does not know what kept means. Emeric still thinks it is a saying. One night in the second week the aurora runs in a dead straight line across the sky for a minute, edge-sharp, then breaks up like any other. Sorrel, on the K Discovery's ladder at the watch change, draws it and writes under it: hands cold, line wrong. She is sure she drew it badly.

On Thursday 17 December, Zale's ring sets the rendezvous. Zale rings from Below that week on Ilenne's gift set, through the Rim's ground relay, because radio does not pass seawater and he is with her, and the mark is said in clear, though the card in every set warns that anyone with a set and patience can hear the rest. Maeve asks Linnet to put her brother on after the figures. The mark: Sunday 3 January, Year 18, at first light, over open sea at the logged mark of the fourth relay drum out from Kaldo on the chain to Aumere, about six hundred miles out from Kaldo's coast and seven hundred from Aumere, inside the ring. A drum hangs at a known place and beats on a known rhythm, so the pair can steer by it and the fleet can find it from the north. The pair can reach it from Aumere in about four days, leaving about 30 December, keeping south and east of the Smoking Rings on the water as the dragons' law of approach requires. From that morning Zale's ship will be under the homeward line as the fleet's pickup, and Zale will be beside Maeve. On the set Maeve tells him she has not done anything stupid in the cold. He says he will be there for the stupid things, then. Nobody knows what that line will mean. Mira stands at the K Amara's rail, not listening, listening.

On Saturday's call from the summit, Tess, on the call sheet for once rather than an urgent call, reports the eighth spire commissioned and the city's Shell lit whole, and wants him to hear how she split the last store's cells. Then she asks whether the limpets ever got used. Ward's rule says nothing of the fleet goes home ahead of it, so he tells her to bring a bottle to the quay. She hears the answer in that and is insufferable for the rest of the call. Saban, short, says the King's Word needs a day. Ward allows the date and nothing else: about the tenth of January. Saban says he will have Lantern Square swept. Harrold reports the stadium's centre stage poured. "How's he holding up?" "Working." Ferra watches him ask it.

On Sunday 20 December Dagny goes to drill a check core on a blue-ice field above the Lead's cliffs, a band of old snow worth reading twice, roped to the drill sledge's frame because Bram made her. A snow bridge gives way, and the sledge and Dagny drop thirty feet into a crevasse, wedged and pinned, swearing. The K Endeavor comes up from the Lead in twenty minutes and holds briefly overhead on Ferra's count. Two knights go down on ropes, and a knight's heat layer finds her, the only warm thing for miles, thirty feet down the crack. The twelve gravity anchors Tess built for Zale's ship go down on lines, and the knights slap their magnets onto the sledge's steel frame and dial the lift stones up. The sledge and Dagny come up the slot together. She has two cracked ribs, and the crevasse leaves its mark on her afterward. The check core next day reads the same fall a second time. That night Bram says it out loud: "the limpets." He is glad they brought them, and Ferra writes it down for Tess.

Chapter 108: The Long Table

On Friday 25 December the five big hulls lift from the Lead at noon, which is dark, fly the six hundred miles in company, and hold briefly over the summit through the evening, about six hours, Ferra counting the charge by the hour to be paid back at the Lead over the next two days. The apron tender brings every hull's people down to the ice in turns. Trestles stand end to end on the ice beside the K Discovery, a hundred yards of them, with lamps and fire-stone braziers along their length, the cooks' best from the relief stores and the last of Aumere's cane sugar, and the aurora green and violet overhead. The knights push their visors up for once. It is Runa's first Long Table. She does not know what it is and Grau cannot explain it, so Bram does, badly. She is suspicious of a feast nobody hunted for, eats more than anyone her size, and falls asleep on Mochi under the table, and her father in practice carries her aboard with Ferra holding the door. Dry Thunder plays unplugged, because the valves sulk in the cold: guitars, a drum and whoever can sing. Maeve sings, which nobody expected. Maeve and Mira sit side by side, nobody says anything, and Emeric glares at Bren.

At the table's dark end by the last brazier, Ward tells Duncan he likes him, flatly, a month and a half later than he promised himself. Duncan says he knows. They go on drinking. No gift sets are given, because the sets are for people Kaldo likes and there is nobody here. Ferra says so when Bren asks. Twelve stay in the locker. Nothing is opened, rung or launched. Nobody on the ice knows what is under it.

On Saturday they build a head-high cairn of ice blocks over the capped drill hole. Inside it goes a steel box holding every name in the fleet in Ferra's hand, including the two dozen with the pair at Aumere, Zale's first, because he would mind if it were not; the chart, with "the Granary" written on it from Gisela's saying; a Kaldo flag; one of Runa's drawings from under the Long Table; and the date. Half a mile upwind they drive a spare drill rod with a bronze cap as a stake for the big glass that might one day stand there, and Sorrel draws it in the summit's diary. Ferra fixes the cairn and the stake by the stars and the chronometer, and writes the position to the second. Bram tells her the second is a wish at the top of the world. She writes it anyway.

Every clue has been dismissed: Mochi still and pulsing at the ice, put down to the cold; the straight aurora, blamed on Sorrel's cold hands; Neris's iron sea, which nobody pursued; Emeric's quiet ice, which he decided holds its mana tight; the sky with no dragons, remarked on and never asked about; and Saldis's "colder than the listening" in Ferra's book, a dragon's way of talking. Nobody puts any two of them together. On Sunday the camp is struck, the cores go into the cold room, and the hole stays capped under the cairn. On Monday 28 December the fleet comes up from the Lead in company, and the K Discovery lifts off her pads for the last time. Bram leaves the pads on the ice beside the cairn because they are too big to stow, and says nobody is coming back for them. Ferra closes the polar leg in her ledger: what was spent, what was drawn and where, what was taken from the ice, and the fix of the cairn. Then they go south over it, and not a single person aboard knows what they are standing on.

Where the Story Stands After Chapter 108

Emeric answered the summons and kept his word. At Saldis's cone he owned a breach he cannot read the text of, offered only what Kaldo can keep, an end to drawing wherever it can avoid it and the dragons' own observers to measure, and was granted one climb home. When the wind turned and the K Amara ran short, he dropped forty tonnes of drinking water into the sea rather than draw, and Sohra, watching beside the racks she once burned, put down her grudge on what she saw. Arthur faced her without apologising for the shot or pretending she was wrong, and she let it stand. Saldis wrote her seal into Ferra's book in her own words, refused to let Sohra's horn be regrown until the debt is paid, said she knew Emeric's face from her own fire before it built the quiet ground that killed her mate, and will tell the other houses that a kin-face keeps his word, so far. Corvan is unchanged, and the eighth gift set now carries Ferra's monthly column of every draw to the dragons' house.

At the top of the world the fleet drew nothing, because nobody keeps that sky. The ice core dates the fall of the world's mana to about two thousand years ago, within a century, and Ferra still will not choose among her readings of the Leaving Terms. Mochi's stillness, the straight aurora, the iron sea, the quiet ice, the empty sky and "colder than the listening" have all been noticed and set aside. A cairn of names and a stake for a future telescope stand at the summit, fixed by the stars to the second. The class has begun aboard, with Duncan teaching the Current and Emeric stasis and the Kindling, Runa Kindled first among the children, Ward bad at it, Maeve furious and working, and Bettany learning to read; nobody has pressed a core since the climb. Every people aboard shares the same blood groups. Ward has told Duncan he likes him. Dagny survived a crevasse thanks to Tess's limpets.

The fleet now turns for home through the Band. The pickup pair will meet it at first light on Sunday 3 January at the fourth drum's mark out from Kaldo, a place said in clear over Ilenne's gift set, and from then Zale will be beside Maeve, ready for the stupid things. At home the city's Shell is lit whole, the stadium's centre stage is poured, and Saban has the tenth of January for the King's Word. The fleet carries seven drums, twelve gift sets and about 86 tonnes of food.

This batch is about restraint as a language that even a dragon can read. Emeric pays for his promises in water, cold and fear, and in return earns a single grudge laid down and a single house's guarded word that he can be trusted, so far, while the greatest secret of the voyage lies unrecognised beneath the ice where the fleet sat down to its Long Table.
