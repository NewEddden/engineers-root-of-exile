Book 2, Batch 6: Downwind

Chapters 44–49 | Friday 17 July – Wednesday 29 July, Year 17

The fleet is still at the orcs' treaty-house on the Sun River. The forged letter has been exposed and the siege lifted, Ward has killed a man at the water stair and won the house's bout, and Grau's apprentice Lisk lies dead because the writ that would have let Kaldo go after him was sealed a few hours too late. The bridge's sitting is about to open with a joint printed edition of both treaty originals and a cook's blind-measure method on the table. Four Pelt-Sworn killers are still in the hills. And since the day he landed, Emeric has felt one small humanoid signature among a wolf pack above the camp, a child, and has told nobody.

This batch belongs to that child. For two days a ten-year-old girl has circled Kaldo's camp, sure no one knows she is there, and the one man who has known all along has the gold eyes from her mother's worst stories. He does not chase her. He feeds her, twice, and lets her smell him, and on the third day, when she runs at four grown killers alone, his sense is the reason anyone reaches her in time. The rest of the batch is about what you owe someone you have just saved: not a price, and not a promise you cannot keep.

Two courts that are not Kaldo's decide things here, the bridge sitting and the werewolves' moot, and Kaldo respects both. Then Kaldo leaves the coast the orcs' way. The fleet stays out of a dwarf hold's sky, and Ferra walks the hold road unarmed under orc escort with the house's letter, to discover that someone ruined by Kaldo at the bridge has bought her plan of march and her guide's brother with it.

Chapter 44: Downwind

On the night of the Guest Fire, Friday 17 July, Runa lies flat in the ferns above the camp, downwind and smug. She has circled it for two days and nobody has noticed her. Across the water, on the rock, the fire burns in the house's yard, and on the wind comes Varrow's ox roasting over smokeroot coals. She has not had cooked meat in four years. It smells like home, and that makes her angry. Every night she counts her list in her head: seven scents. Four of them are up the hill right now with Lisk's blood still on them. Bracken's pack lies higher up. The reader meets her through her ears, her bare soles on the ground, the burr and grey feather in her hair, and knows what she is before anyone in the camp does.

On Saturday Ward has the knights dig a proper ditch and raise a barricade around the camp. Emeric says it is unnecessary, because his sense covers the whole valley. Ward builds it anyway: the camp stands on door ground with its arms locked and tallied, and four killers are still up there. Both men are right. In Emeric's own thoughts the reader learns what he has known since they came through the door: one small humanoid signature inside a ring of wolf signatures, a child by its size. Three months of Aumere's thick water taught him to pick one person out of dense-land noise. He remembers Lisk, and leaves her alone one more night. Ferra goes to the sitting with the joint edition under her arm and Kettrin's two clerks beside her.

That afternoon Mira, who carried bowls to Grau's party at the Guest Fire, asks Ondra, the party's guide over the pass, what is up in the hills with the wolves. Ondra says it flatly: "She isn't one of ours. She's theirs." Emeric hears it from the next fire and says nothing.

At dusk Dorrit's roast comes off the spit. Emeric salts a cut himself and walks it out past the lights and the ditch, far enough that she will not think it a trap and placed where the wolves will not reach it first. The knights try to work out what he is doing. Is he wasting food? Has the king finally found religion? Ward says, "If he's praying, I want to know to who." Someone complains that he never salts their meat. He does not explain.

Runa smells the meat and smells fire on it, the same fire-smell as the man. She takes it anyway, because she is starving, and bolts the second she has it. Clean away. He finds her by sense alone, up through the dark trees. She has known he was coming for a mile and let him. She is under the roots of a fallen cedar by the creek, the same kind of place her mother used to hide her, eating with her back to the wood, with Bracken lying a few yards off. Emeric sits down at the far edge of the clearing and lets her smell him. She sees his gold eyes in the dark and bares her teeth. He stays where he is, sets a water flask on the ground, and leaves. She is sure she let him.

Chapter 45: By Lot

On Saturday 18 July the bridge's sitting opens in the house's hall: three arbiters, the first warden planted in her doorway, both towns' councils on their benches, and Kettrin keeping the roll. The joint edition is witnessed by both towns' clerks and read aloud. Kaldo sits on the contractor's bench with no voice. Ferra watches Varrow's and Hasker's clerks read the same two columns at the same speed and find nothing to object to in the layout, which is the first thing ever agreed. Under a second writ, with notice given to the four killers at the boundary, wardens carry Coldspring's old witness down on a litter at a walk. She is very old and asks for Grau by name.

On Sunday Kettrin puts the blind measure to the sitting and says where it came from, a cook's table. Varrow's ox-roaster laughs and then votes for it. Both towns accept it because neither appointed it. Surveyors are drawn by lot from three lists, both towns' and the house's. At Ferra's suggestion, Bram's riverbed figures go into the pot without Kaldo's mark, and Kaldo lends its level and sounding gear. She records that in her own book and nowhere else. She also notices that the galley's roast has come up a cut short two nights running. She counts it again and asks nobody.

At home in Kaldo's line room, Maren has booked her call and waits her turn on the bench. She hears Harrold's half of his report: a magistrate Keller sent unescorted to observe a Cinderhale meeting was beaten on the road and his horse killed. Keller issued named warrants at once. One man has been identified, sheltered by the mill lords' watch, whose captain calls it a dispute over local jurisdiction, and the case is open. She hears the pause before Harrold answers a question he does not repeat. Corbin's call follows. He asks to go to Cinderhale himself, and Emeric refuses: a prince of the old court walking into the mill yard would start the very fight they are trying to prevent. Corbin asks for an escorted observer instead, and Emeric says he will answer once Keller's audit is in. Maren sees Corbin's face as he puts the receiver down.

Then Maren's own call reaches Dorrit. First a cooking question, whether the fleet's bellfruit roasts like a squash. Then: "Did you salt it at the end?" "Both." Silence on the line. "Did you win?" "Second." "Who beat you?" "A desert cook with a jelly." "And him?" "Fourth." For the first time on any call, Maren laughs. Ambrose looks up from the board. Saban, in the doorway, decides not to have heard.

On the hill, on the second night, Emeric sets the meat closer to the lights, and this time he sits down beside it. Runa comes out of the ferns and takes it from a yard away from him. They share no language, so neither speaks. This is the night the bait becomes an invitation. Back on the rock late, he smells of pine. Imani, half asleep, says, "You've been feeding something." Ferra, from his other side: "Two cuts of roast." He says nothing. They let him, and sleep. He does not, much.

Chapter 46: The Stream

On Monday 20 July, hunting the four, Runa crosses a fresh trail that smells like her mother, four years after her mother died. It is impossible. She stands over it far too long, then leaves it, because the four are moving.

She has trailed them for days, and at a stream off neutral ground, about three miles up the valley, she goes for the smallest, the boy, where they water. The others are nearer than she smelled. It goes wrong fast. She is ten and they are grown, and the leather man, the big one with tanning oil on his hands, has her by the hair with his knife up.

In the camp, Emeric feels her signature move fast up the valley toward four others, stop, then twist. He does not wait for anyone's seal. He remembers Lisk. He tells Ward over the field set as he lifts. Rule one of the Charter is rescue, unconditionally. No rifles go past the stones; what goes is Emeric's own magic, Ward's hands and the knights' grappling. Emeric flies first. Ward runs, above cheetah speed, three miles in under three minutes. Arthur and Mira follow in their suits, flying and unarmed. Grau and Ondra run behind.

Emeric pulls the leather man's knife hand down with gravity aimed past it, so the arm becomes too heavy to lift and the knife drops into the water, and he takes the girl out of the grip. He kills nobody. The leather man changes and comes at the two of them in his wolf form, and Ward kills him with his hands. The cousin, also changed, goes for the girl in Emeric's arms, and Bracken and two of her pack take him in the stream. He dies there; it is the pack's kill, not Kaldo's. The woman who smells of burnt hair cuts Arthur's forearm through his undersuit before Arthur and Mira put her down with the knights' grappling, and she yields. Nobody touches her after. The boy runs up toward the pass. Nobody chases, because Emeric will not put the girl down and Ward will not leave her. Two are dead, one is taken alive for the werewolves' moot, and the boy is at large.

The first thing Runa sees clearly is the man who pulled her out, and he has gold eyes. She bares her teeth at her rescuer. He lets her and does not move. It is the first trust between them, and it is his to give. Before anyone can stop her, she goes to the bodies and the captive, nose down and ears flat, and names each of them by smell in a language none of them speak. Then she turns in a slow circle. Grau, arriving, will not translate at first. It means: "Where are the rest?"

Afterward Ward washes his hands in the stream. He is fine, he notices that he is fine, and he does not like it. It is his second killing in eight days, and this time there was no seal to wait for and nothing to regret. Nobody says anything to him. Mira sits on the bank near him until he is done. Someone says aloud what the foreign party failed to do for Lisk, and the rescue does not erase it; the earlier hesitation is owned as a costly error.

That first night, at the camp's edge under a cart, with Bracken lying in the dark beyond the lights, Runa speaks to Emeric through Grau: "You smell like fire." Her nose reads what nobody else can. Then she offers her bargain. She will work, scout, fight and obey. "Get every one of them. After that you can do what you want with me." Grau stops halfway through translating it into orc speech, an adult who does not want to carry that sentence. Emeric's answer is short and plain. No. She is not for sale, and he will not take her as payment for anything. He will help find out who did what and stand with her before their law. He will not promise to kill anyone because she tells him to, and he will not let her die trying. She can come with the fleet or stay with Grau's people. She hears no, and thinks he is refusing her. Late that night she tells Grau about the trail that smelled like her mother. He says it could be kin. She will not believe him.

Chapter 47: Unnamed

On Tuesday 21 July the first warden hears Ferra, Ward and the knights on the rescue and finds it a lawful rescue under door custom: a child attacked by adults past the stones, those adults already under the door's notice since Lisk's writ, Kaldo unarmed beyond the stones, and the only man Kaldo killed was killing at the time. The pack's kill is nobody's to judge. It is the third host entry in Ferra's ledger, entered under the Charter's first rule with no question attached.

The sitting closes the same day. The lot's surveyors' figures are read blind and agree within a hand's breadth, and nobody knows whose were whose. The bridge reopens at once on the temporary crossing for people, pack beasts and handcarts, while the treaty dispute continues. A neutral maintenance authority is created for one renewable term of five years, its surveyors drawn by lot and its first fund Isbrand's forfeited toll farm. Both towns sign. Because the joint edition has survived a sitting, the house orders a letterpress with orc type, to be cast at home and delivered after the return, and the lithograph stone and Neris's ink recipe stay at the house as gifts. Ferra writes the order and sits for a moment with her pen raised, because a cook gave her the method and Varrow's roaster voted for it. Grau's charter is entered that day too, its seal-clay matched to its register line and the old witness's mark beside it. The pass road is now Coldspring's refuge road under the house's charter roll, and Gorm's claim to it is barred at every orc door that keeps the roll. That was why the four came down the pass after Grau. Mek's caravan crosses on the trestle that afternoon, Tahl waving at Dorrit's tent.

Kettrin counsels Ferra about the next stop. A dwarf hold that sees strange hulls in its sky shuts its gate; the war came to the holds from the air, and a hold can block its land gate and wall itself in. Strangers come to a hold by its road, under orc escort, with a house's letter, or not at all. Ferra takes the counsel and files the fleet's plan of march under the voyage's seal that evening, as the escort custom requires.

On Thursday the heads of the borderland packs come down to the stone ring outside the boundary stones, the one ground every pack accepts for a case between packs. The wardens keep the ring's edge, and Kaldo is asked to stand outside it. At a moot anyone may speak, but scent counts as testimony only with a second witness, so Runa's nose alone is not enough. She chooses to let Emeric delve her memory of the raid and show it to those heads who consent to see it, the way Amara's death was once shown to Harrold. It has to be her choice, because a mind from this dense world pushes out any uninvited reader. Grau, a healer the packs trust, stands inside the memory with her, and that is why the heads accept it. She relives the worst night of her life by her own choice. The captive says, to hurt her, "Your mother rode with us." Afterward Grau confirms it quietly: her mother was one of the hunters. Then the heads speak, in front of her, about the pelt bounty paid under the gold-eyed king of the old war, and she understands what the man who pulled her out of the stream is.

The sentence is unnaming. The captive's name is said once, to strike it, and the reader never hears it, because Grau will not carry a struck name into another tongue. No pack may shelter, feed or speak to her, and she must leave the interior. The house frees her from its keeping at the ring, and she walks east along the coast road alone. It is lighter than death. Runa hates the sentence, and hates Emeric for respecting it. The moot must also let Runa go, so Grau speaks for Coldspring's dead and the court releases her into Kaldo's care. As Emeric promised, the choice is hers: the fleet or Grau's people. She chooses the fleet for practical reasons. The gold-eyed king's fleet is the thing most likely to find the rest of her list. At the ring's edge, Maeve sees the air lean toward the girl's face when she lifts her head to the wind, and is the first to realise Runa is casting. Ferra writes the moot's sentence and release in its own words, reads them back to Grau, and enters them as the first outside entry from a people with no house to keep a book.

Outside the ring, Emeric hears the Pelt-Sworn's case spoken by the captive: the pelts, the humans, and a half-blood child who should not exist. Privately he recognises the shape, a real wound grown into a creed about blood, the way it went on Earth. He says nothing aloud; it is not his ring. He thinks of who at home would say the same about a girl with a tail, and does not like the answer.

That night by the camp's edge, through Grau, Runa asks him: did his family's gold-eyed king order the pelts? He does not know. Nobody alive can tell him yet. So he promises the only thing he can keep: he will find out and tell her the truth, whatever it is. It is the first promise he makes her. She does not answer and goes up the hill.

She sleeps with Bracken's pack one last time before the fleet sails. Emeric follows by sense, as he has every night, and stays where she can smell him. He does not reach into the wolves' minds; he learned that lesson on the orcs' ox. Bracken lifts her head, looks at him a long time, and lets him stay. At moonrise a sick wolf, mad with the bite-sickness, bites Runa on the calf. Emeric and Grau are there in minutes. Grau kills the wolf and takes what he needs from it. Imani knows the method of a vaccine but has had no strain until now; the sick wolf's tissue is the strain, Runa's blood helps make it, and the series needs about two weeks of drying.

On Friday, on the K Endeavor's hospital deck, an orc mother from the flats brings a boy of about eight born without his left leg below the knee, walking on a carved peg he is proud of. Imani offers both choices once, plainly, with Grau interpreting: regrowth over months of sessions, or a better leg. The boy is asked as well as his mother, and his wish carries the weight. He keeps the peg; he carved the toes himself. Bram measures him in chalk on the deck for a running blade to be built after the return and sent by the orc house's post, and the other offer is written in the house's roll to stand forever. Runa watches from her corner.

Being held still is what she fears most. The infection rides the bitten person's own mana. A cuff drains it, but in open dense air the air refills her, so the cuff alone buys only days. Inside the K Endeavor's iron skin, under the cabin mark, the air does not refill her, so the cuff and the cabin together can hold the sickness for the weeks the series needs. Imani explains every step before she does it, and Runa may refuse anything except what keeps her alive. She refuses most of it, and accepts the cuff only after Grau tells her what he saw bite-madness do to the bitten people Coldspring sheltered. The cuff takes the magic in her nose and leaves her ordinary nose, so she is weak and furious and can still smell everyone who comes down the ladder. Emeric moves her mattress into the corner without a word. There is a jar of honey. At Bram's bench she helps mend a broken lamp, the tools passed without anyone asking. Imani heals the new bite clean and offers to heal the old scars. Runa keeps the crescent on her forearm from Bracken's pup, a family mark, and lets the scar through her eyebrow fade. After watching the boy with the peg be asked and allowed to say no, she takes the honey.

Grau signs on that afternoon, his terms read aloud by Ferra through Ledra: wages, freedom to refuse any mission, freedom to report home to the packs' heads and the house, his own people's law kept, passage home from any port, and a column of his own in Imani's book where they disagree. He and Imani disagree about the cuff in front of the patient, which the patient enjoys. He says the cuff is a prisoner's thing and the girl will hate them both for it. Imani says yes. Both are right. Ondra and Grau's other two go back up the pass under writ with the old witness, and Ondra carries the second of Kaldo's gift sets, so Grau's freedom to report home means something a sea away. She turns the crank once, says it smells of the ship, and straps it on her back. That night, alone with Imani in her surgery, Emeric tells her about the first boy on his other world saved by a dried series after a bite, a long time ago, and why he knows the method works on someone already bitten. Imani writes down the dates and asks how many doses.

Chapter 48: Two Halts

On Saturday 25 July, Imani lets Runa up to the K Endeavor's rail in her cuff, wrapped in a blanket, as the fleet lifts. Zale's ship and the K Tam run on the water below. Bracken's pack follows along the shore to the edge of the borderland and stops there. Runa watches from the rail until she can no longer smell them, which with the cuff on is sooner than she would like. Bonds tie signatures, so she will be able to find Bracken again someday. Nobody says it, and she knows it.

At the house, the first warden stands planted on the causeway, Petrok's hand is mended, and Brega does not come down but sends her grandson. The door families' choice about the Isle waits for October. The evening before, Emeric gave Kettrin the third gift set for the house and the roll, with Ferra's card read aloud first. Kettrin entered it as a gift under the giver's seal and asked whether the house could call the fleet about the relief pair's berth in October. Yes, that is what it is for. Seventeen sets remain. Emeric also asked the first warden for ground on the house's flats for machines that fly on wings, which Kaldo has not yet built. She agreed on the house's terms, because a door that only opens to people on foot is no longer a door: the field is the house's ground under door law, no side taken, no arms past the stones, every people's fliers counted the same, kept by the house's wardens, leased by Kaldo, which pays and trains a crew of the house's own, and built by the valleys' road gangs on Bram's drawings from the crossing contract's surplus. Ferra drafted it on Aumere's model. Emeric again named the catch himself: no regular flights until the old promise is answered. In Imani's quarantine bay sit the seed hand's crates, the rice, the pumpkin seed, cuttings and a sack of smokeroot, and the first orc pieces are in Armand's register.

The fleet flies a few hours west, low along the green wall of the range, and sets down on the sea off an orc road-town by afternoon, out of the dwarf hold's sky. Zale has been cleared for duty and is insufferable about it. That night on the K Discovery's deck, Ferra unbuckles her revolver and hands it to Imani, who has come across to see her off. Orc roads are off neutral ground, foreign parties carry no arms on them, and the house's wardens are the escort. Emeric does not like any of it and says so once. "Kettrin's road, Kettrin's rules." "Kettrin isn't the one walking it." "No. I am." She takes him by the collar for a moment, then goes to bed early because of the road. Emeric is staying behind because the house's letter asks the hold to receive Kaldo's envoys before its king, and because of the hold's sky.

On Sunday at dawn the road party goes ashore by boat: Ferra with the Crown's seal, the house's letter and the plan of march; Ledra as interpreter; Arthur, Bren and six knights from the K Amara. The house's escort is Gadd as guide and five road wardens under their captain, Petrok among them at Brega's family's request. Four pack mules carry the gifts: two casks of Emeric's beer, which the dwarves will accept, iron bar, glass and Kaldo lamps. Ledra presents the letter at the road-town's warden hut, and Ferra pays the escort fee at the house's rate. The road climbs into the range with the drop to the sea opening behind them, over the dwarves' cut-stone drains. Arthur is unhappy without his kit. Bren is cheerful about it.

At the first halt Gadd proposes tomorrow's stop at a particular shepherd's stone, the exact one on Ferra's sealed plan of march, which she has told nobody on this road. Ferra says nothing and writes it down. On Monday morning she makes two changes. To Gadd alone she mentions the east fork, as a shortcut if the weather holds. To the escort's captain alone, the west fork. Then she asks Bren and Petrok to stroll up the east fork ahead of everyone, scouting the wrong ravine on purpose. They find it prepared: a rope across the ravine floor, levered stones on its lip, and a bowstave's end where a man has shifted his weight in the rocks. They keep strolling, turn round like men who have decided the shortcut is too steep, and walk back down. The west fork has nobody in it.

Chapter 49: The Fold

Monday night, at the halt below the forks, Ferra and Bren take Gadd aside with Petrok and Ledra and accuse nobody else. Gadd does not make them work for it. His younger brother Yaro, a drover on the hold road, was taken on Thursday and is being held at a shepherd's fold up a side valley. A token of Yaro's reached Gadd at the coast house on Friday with instructions and a paper listing Ferra's halts: bring the Kaldo party by those halts, then into the east fork's ravine on the third day. He was coerced, not volunteering. Ferra asks him one question, whether he would have walked them in. He says he does not know. She believes him.

The man behind it is Isbrand, ruined by Kaldo's measures at the bridge and desperate to get back into the river lenders' guild. The guild carries the hold's grain debt, secured on its mine's ore contract and due at the autumn reckoning. A Kaldo that reached the hold with food that keeps and fans that keep a mine working would break the guild's grip. A Kaldo party robbed and bloodied on the hold road would confirm what the hold's ledger-keepers already believe about humans, keep the gate shut, and let the guild take the contract, which Isbrand would bring to the guild as the price of his name. A roll clerk at the house, in debt to the guild, copied Ferra's sealed plan, and it went west by coaster. The ravine was built for an unarmed party: Isbrand's dozen hired men, out-of-work toll men and road outlaws, wear shard charms that matter to nobody, since Kaldo has no guns on this road.

The plan is made quickly: no fight in the ravine; get the brother first. The captain is told now, and his runner goes down to the road-town's wardens. That night Bren, Arthur, Petrok, Gadd and four knights go up the side valley to the fold with working knives and nothing else. The knights bring their helms, since a helm is not a weapon, so the night glass shows them the fold in grey while Isbrand's men work by a shaded lantern, and Petrok, who has no glass, moves by Bren's whispers. Gadd walks in first, to report as expected. Then comes the knights' grappling in the dark, quietly. A man with a sling gets one stone off and hits nothing. Yaro, hungry and very angry, makes himself useful in the struggle, and the disarming is not effortless. Yaro is out, nobody is killed, and four of Isbrand's men are taken. On the way down the scree, Bren's knee goes, a mule bolts over the edge with its load of iron bar and the field set, and two lamps are lost. The party is now out of touch with the fleet.

Tuesday is lost to it. The road-town's wardens round up the ravine's men on the captain's runner, and that night Isbrand is taken at the road-town on Gadd's word and one hired man's, and sent back to the coast house under seal for its market court. Kaldo asks nothing of the court. Ledra knows the wardstone at the roll room's door takes every signature that passes, and her note about it goes to Kettrin with the captain's runner. From the road, the party sees the fleet's silence all day: no flight knights over the ridge, nobody in the hold's sky. Bren, strapping his knee, says he had half expected the king. Ferra says she would have been angry if he had come. Arthur says she would have been angrier if he had not been worried. Nobody disagrees. The runner reaches the fleet's boat on Tuesday evening with Ferra's note. Ward did not send flight knights into the hold's sky, and Emeric did not fly in. Being lawful costs them again, and this time it holds.

Under the escort's law, the captain could take a warden who betrayed his charge. Ferra speaks for Gadd under door custom: he was coerced through his kin, and he brought the plot into the open himself once asked. The captain refers the case to the house's court and lets Gadd guide on under his own word.

On Wednesday they make the last climb. The road narrows. High on the range's seaward wall is a cut-stone face, with ore chutes and a slag hill below. Ledra says the deep people live beneath it and will not name them to strangers. The gate is shut. A dwarf appears at the wicket and speaks in a tongue Ledra puts into merfolk speech for Ferra. Gadd speaks for them first, as an orc road warden of the house: these people took my brother back and did not hang me. Then Ledra presents the house's letter, and Ferra the Crown's seal. The wicket shuts while someone goes to ask, and they wait.

Where the Story Stands After Chapter 49

Runa has joined the fleet. Rescued at the stream without anyone waiting for a seal, she offered Emeric everything she is in exchange for her list of killers, and he refused her as payment while promising to stand with her and to find out the truth about his own family's gold-eyed king and the pelt bounty. She heard it as a door shutting, and every small kindness since has been him proving otherwise. She now lies cuffed in the corner of the K Endeavor's hospital deck, holding off the bite-sickness while Imani's vaccine series dries, with Grau signed on beside her as the fleet's werewolf physician. She has learned that her mother rode with the hunters, she half believes Grau that the trail she smelled was kin, and she has said nothing in Kaldo's tongue yet. Maeve knows the girl can cast and has told nobody.

The moot unnamed the captive, two of the four killers are dead, and the boy is at large up the pass. Ward has now killed twice in eight days and noticed that the second one did not trouble him. The bridge has a neutral authority chosen by lot and funded by Isbrand's forfeited toll farm, the treaty dispute remains open, the house has ordered a printing press for delivery after the return, and Grau's charter has secured the pass road for Coldspring. The orcs have also agreed to a future landing field on their flats, and three of Kaldo's twenty gift sets have now been given.

Ferra's road party stands at the shut gate of the first dwarf hold of the Sea Reach, out of touch with the fleet, with Bren's knee strapped and Gadd, who would have walked them into an ambush three days ago, now vouching for them. Isbrand and the clerk who copied her plan await the orc house's court. The hold's mine is in trouble, its grain debt is carried by the lenders' guild and falls due at the autumn reckoning, and the coal question the Rim's smiths raised still waits on the dwarves. At home, a magistrate has been beaten on the Cinderhale road, Keller's warrants are out, and Corbin, right again, has been told to wait for the audit.

This batch is about what is owed to someone you save: not ownership, not vengeance on command, but presence, honesty and choice. Kaldo gives that to a wolf-raised girl, to a coerced guide and his brother, and to two foreign courts, and arrives at the next door with a friend instead of a body.
