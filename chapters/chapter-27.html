Book 3, Batch 8: Older Than the Kingdom

Chapters 73–80 | Wednesday 20 December, Year 19 – Saturday 26 May, Year 20

The Titles Act has passed its first vote and the hereditary council has taken the year the king's own Charter allows, so its final vote cannot come before late September. The beast line is renewed for a year with one empty research pen at the Claim. The Seed Act sits in committee. Gil Haverty drank the beasts' blood, died and rose on the third day, and Keller has ordered the register's dead burned. A grove close at Wayford was burned by a forced dead zone, and Mochi is wintering on its ash. Corrick and Kezia are at Turnstone until July, and Gethin comes out in January.

This is a quieter batch, the first stretch of home years in which the institutions mostly run without crisis. A man who has sat in a chair for nine years asks Imani for his legs back, and gets the first spinal repair anyone has tried. Elected reeves take command of the small towns' watches, a hereditary captain who refuses is removed by a magistrate's ruling rather than the king's force, and the king is not even asked. Golems make their first real rescue in a burning warehouse.

The past and the future both surface. A dig on the west hill finds a road, a waystation built with the old races' keyed stone, and a layer of char nobody can explain. The first fruit falls from the first hundred trees and is counted under a law the king lost. And on the cage over a replanted pair at Wayford, a sparrow sits that will neither answer Runa nor refuse her.

Chapter 73: The Long Table

On Wednesday 20 December, at the Grayrock clinic, through Imani's eyes, Reuben Orley asks her whether his legs can come back. He is about forty, a Grayrock miner caught by a roof fall in Year 11, before Kaldo's timbering rules reached the old drifts, and he has sat in a chair for nine years with his cord scarred across low in his back and his legs gone thin. He has a wife who carts and two children who push him up the hill to the chapel. Imani tells him plainly what it is: she can cut the scar out and build the cord across it in sessions, because both ends are alive; she has never done it; his legs will come back before his strength does; it will take months; he will be hungry the whole time, because the body pays for what it builds; and Emeric will be holding his weight while his legs remember how. He says yes on the spot, then asks his wife, and she says he already has.

On Monday 25 December, in the castle's long hall, through Runa's eyes, the Long Table fills again, with a few new chairs: Ada Lovell, who has nowhere else to go at midwinter and whom Tess brings without asking; Liora, at Imani's invitation, who writes her thanks on her slate and then says them; and Mabyn Hoskin, because Imani asked and she said yes before she could think better of it. Last year Runa did not understand why everyone gave each other things. This year she has made something at the school bench for every person at the table, thirty-one of them, and hands them out in order of how much she likes people, which everyone has worked out by the fourth one. Ward's is seventh. He is delighted and tries not to show it, and fails. Ilenne comes without a tally this year, bringing gifts and no paper. Harrold notices and says nothing. Zale tells Emeric afterwards that she said a table that pays you back in a year is not a debt, it is a habit.

Late that night, through Emeric's eyes, on the castle roof with Ferra and Imani on a cold, clear night, it is work first and fun later, and tonight they all agree it is later. Imani tells them about the Grayrock man who asked for his legs back. Emeric asks what it will take. Imani says months, and Emeric holding him up. Emeric says he is free on Tuesdays, which he is not.

The week before, Runa loses on Wednesday to an older girl in the school's yard because she spends her padding in the first exchange, and wins on Saturday against the same girl on less. On the shortest day Emeric takes her up to the Band. She doesn't get sick. She grins the whole way and says she's cold.

Chapter 74: The Latch

On Tuesday 2 January, in the hospital's sterile ward, through Imani's eyes, Emeric maps the old damage in Reuben's cord widely first, which is his part, and then steps back. Imani cuts the scar out and builds the first span across it, a short one. The first spinal repair anyone has tried has begun. Sessions follow through January and February, about twelve of them, a fortnight apart at first and then weekly.

From Tuesday 9 to Friday 12 January, in Keller's court, through Vantic's eyes from the public bench, Elwin Gorran pleads to the undeclared import and the sale abroad. His defence is his brother's wheel, and it is true. He gets nine months at Turnstone, to be out on 12 October, with the money he handed back counted in his favour; the shard itself is gone past recovery into Velmoor's treasury. The miller sits in the gallery, thanks the court for nothing in particular, and leaves before Vantic can be seen to know him. He still does not know that the note on his wheel is hers, and she means to keep it that way.

On Sunday 14 January, at dawn, through Gethin's eyes, he is released from Turnstone after a six-month sentence and nine and a half months inside. Maudie meets him at the spur halt with a coat. Corrick cannot come to the gate, and sends a line by Gethin's hand to the League about the minutes. That night, in Cinderhale's lower street, Gethin finds the officer who walked free years ago lying drunk on the cobbles, and walks him home. At the door he opens the man's latch with the fingers Imani regrew, looks at the hand, and goes. Nothing is said, and nothing is explained.

Chapter 75: Reeves

The small towns' watches' temporary licences under the Arms Act end on Friday 23 February, and the towns elect their reeves three weeks earlier, on Saturday 3 February, so command passes on the day with nobody in between. Through Maudie's eyes in Cinderhale, the first elected reeve is the old mill lord's cousin. The League ran its own candidate, a dyer who sat on Maudie's night table, and he lost by eighty-odd votes. Maudie writes the count on the League's wall under the two columns of months and writes nothing under it. Pim asks whether they were robbed. She says no, they were outvoted, and that is worse and better.

Through Corbin's eyes, in Tarrow Bridge, a market town on the Tarrow in the duchy of Tarrowmede, the watch's hereditary captain refuses on Monday 26 February to hand command to the elected reeve, under the duke's custom ordinance of October calling the captaincy ancient custom. On Tuesday the Inspectorate serves its warrant for an unlawful command, with the town's own magistrate, and with no crown force and no knights. The captain keeps the door shut and sends to the duke.

On Friday 2 March, in Keller's court, through Keller's eyes, he rules that a lord's custom ordinance cannot set aside an Act of the Assembly; the captaincy ended with the Act and the licence, and the reeve commands. He writes carefully that the ordinance is still good for whatever the Act does not reach, and that what the Act does not reach is shrinking. The captain stands down the same afternoon. The king is not asked and does not go. Corbin reads him the ruling at supper, and he says only that Keller wrote it better than he would have.

Chapter 76: Names

On Monday 5 March the citizen's band rollout's third year begins, with Grayrock, Milford and Wayford, and the three dukes' miners queued behind them. On Tuesday 6 March, Lantern Night, the anniversary of First Light, through Ferra's eyes at the river wall, the year's names go up with the lanterns: Gil Haverty, written by his sister; Brask's three; Lowen Hennet; Cal Pascoe; Matt Lowrie; Sir Edwin Hartley; the four commissioners; and the Stannary nine. Before the launch Ferra reads the year's graduates by field from the wall, as she does every year: more nurses than ever, and the names of the first twelve readers among them. Then come the lanterns, Fennick's fireworks and the river.

The fire golems had their first drill on Saturday 20 January in a smoke house built for it, driven by hand and then on their stones; one of the six walked into the wall, and Tess had its stone re-read. At about one in the morning on Thursday 15 March, through Ward's eyes, a rope and tar warehouse in the dock quarter catches from a stove in the night watchman's cubby. The warning bands in the quarter buzz fire, short, short, short. The brigade's pump and the fireboat's cannon work from the water side, and three fire golems go in through the smoke where the brigade cannot, their stones deciding the path and who is alive: two men on the upper floor, the watchman and a carter sleeping off a drink in the loft. Night glass is no use in smoke and nobody pretends it is. The floor comes down between the two men and the stair. A rescue golem, brought up on the brigade's cart and driven by two of Ward's knights, braces the fallen floor where its stone says to and stops where it says to, and the two men crawl out under the brace. It is the first real rescue. The warehouse is lost, and nobody dies. Ward signs the brigade's sheet this time without a note on it.

In mid-March, in the hospital's rehabilitation room, through Bettany's eyes, Reuben's feeling comes back in his feet before anything else, then his toes move, and then for three weeks nothing does. His muscles are paper and his bones are thin. Bettany writes his schedule, food, the bars, the pool, rest, and pins it to the wall by his bed anyway. At the bars Emeric's gravity takes two-thirds of his weight, so he can stand while his legs relearn.

Chapter 77: Older Than the Kingdom

On Monday 19 March, at the foot of the west hill, through Armand's eyes, the dig opens once the ground has thawed: Armand for the history, Linus for the survey grid, Tess for the keywork, Ward because he asked to come, and a dozen of the Polytechnic's history and survey students on shovels. Emeric comes the first morning and then stays away on purpose. That morning Armand asks Ward straight out what he knows. Through Ward's eyes, it is one thing: his master brought him here once as a boy, put his hand on this stone and said it was older than the kingdom. That is all. There is no secret behind it, and Ward is a little embarrassed that people have wondered.

Under the scrub they find a cut stone road bed running east and west, toward the coast one way and the hills the other. On the road stands a waystation, a long building with stalls for animals at one end and a hearth at the other, and keyed stone in every footing, the same fine branching as a block found long before, all of it dead. Tess says it is keywork, the same family as the old races' golem armour seams on South Sentinel, and that whoever cut it meant the building to be held by something that is not there any more.

On Friday 30 March, in Keller's court, through Corbin's eyes, Keller throws out the hereditary council's first lawsuit as premature. The Titles Act is not law yet, and a bill takes nothing; the lords can bring the suit again the day the Act passes, if it passes. The quoted passages of Pryor's brief are answered in one paragraph: the Assembly's imbalance is a reason to widen it, which is what the Act does. The council has spent its money and kept its year, and the delay runs on, which is what it bought. Corbin thinks that is the cleverest thing the lords have done all year, and tells nobody but Ferra.

On Friday 20 April the dig reaches a burn layer: the waystation's whole floor lies under a hand's depth of char. The building burned and nobody rebuilt it. The ground does not say who burned it, or when, or whether it was the war or something else. Armand writes "older than the kingdom" in the site book in Ward's words, under the char, and dates it.

Chapter 78: Kindled

On Monday 2 April, in the Kindling hall beside Kaldo's ring close, through Mabyn's eyes, the Kindling becomes free at every Kaldo clinic, and the first halls stand just outside the Grove Watch's fences where the air is thickest. There are about sixty readers now, with Mabyn at their head, one at every Kindling. The queue goes round the block by nine. Many people are half-lit already and Kindle in one sitting: a carter, a schoolteacher, a woman who has sold mana bread at the ring market for eight years. Nobody under eighteen is Kindled, and Mabyn turns away a boy of sixteen who brought his older brother's papers; he argues, and she is right. By Thursday the readers are tired, and the second week's appointments run a month long.

The week the date was printed, Armand posted his letters, one to each name he copied from under the tower's mark in the brown calf book, children he read at ten and did not send, grown now. Most never answer. Two of them are in Mabyn's queue with the letter in their hand. The woman who has lived in Kaldo for years Kindles in one sitting; the man down from the capital's thin air takes three, and comes back for all three. Armand comes at noon to see, reads them both, and enters them in his columns as the first two from below the mark.

On Wednesday 11 April, through Ferra's eyes, the Seed Act passes by a wide margin, amended against the king. The Grove Watch answers to the Assembly's own inspector, the Inspector of the Groves, a Penhallow surveyor the Assembly names, not to the crown, and each state's share of seed for its own groves is set by the Assembly's vote every spring. Osric keeps the Watch's captaincy and gets a second signature on every count. Wayford's member gets the burned close paid for. The king obeys it. He tells Ferra once that a Watch that answers to a vote will one day be voted smaller than it should be, and that he would rather it were his to keep big. Ferra says that is exactly why it is not. The next day the Inspector of Sealing's second quarterly report says the research pen is still empty, the houses are clean, and the fund has its one claim.

Merryn Pellow and two of the March's hunters have been tipping their bolts with pup claw, for no better reason than that the pups seem to die quicker from them. On Saturday 14 April, on a peat creek behind Saltreach, through Merryn's eyes, she nets a pup alive, about one and a half metres, one of the never-seen, waking up thin out of the winter on a creek bank with a shard-dust cut in it. Her dog stops in the reeds and points the way it will not go, as before. Knowing how the Inspector reads the nest clause, she sends for him, and he seals the pup into a portable shut case for the pen. On Monday 16 April it goes into the research pen at the Claim under the Inspector's seal, and the July report gains its first line.

On Monday 2 April, her thirteenth birthday, Ward sets Runa her first birthday problem: track someone across the ring close, take one hit without losing her feet, spot the false clue, get home. She comes in late to her slot at the Kindling hall with mud to the knees, home last and angry. On Friday 13 April Mabyn reads her Current, steady through three interruptions, and Ferra writes her second row in the register. The third, Runa is told, is stasis, and not this year.

Chapter 79: Crystal and Fruit

From Wednesday 18 to Friday 20 April, in the pen at the Claim, through Tess's eyes, with the Inspector watching, she nicks the pup's shoulder with a steel blade on one side and with a pup's claw tip on the other. The steel nick closes in about an hour. The claw-crystal nick stays open two days and then closes slowly from the edges. Ward writes it up for the hunters: claw-tipped bolts, and why. Tess writes in the Register's technical appendix only "own-kind crystal holds a wound open," because that is all it proves. The pup stays in the pen, fed a measured ration of shard dust so its growth can be read. The same week in the Motion Shop, Ada's tables tell the right hand from the left on the cap's trace four times in five, though nothing moves yet, because nothing is wired to anything. And Ada makes the first screen anyone in this world has made, a light stone cut into a grid of sixteen by sixteen cells, each woken by its own wire, spell TESS, badly.

At dawn on Friday 27 April, at Milford's first close, through Osric's eyes, the first fruit falls: the first hundred trees, planted two years ago, let their pods fall into the drop cages over two days, close by close. They are counted under the new Act, with Osric's signature and the Inspector of the Groves' beside it on every tin. Inside a mile of the first closes, light stones are brighter for the same charge this spring; the pickets noticed first, because their lamps last longer.

On Saturday 28 April, in the count room, through Ferra's eyes, the count comes to about seven hundred blank seeds, about seven a tree. On Monday 30 April the Stone Hearing's spring sitting, timed to the drop as always, grants nine, and its works round hears the fire brigade's thanks and grants nothing new. About seven hundred seeds, every one with its keel written at the count.

Chapter 80: Between the Bars

On Wednesday 2 May, in the Assembly, through Ward's eyes, the Assembly votes each state's share of the replanting half under the Act: Kaldo's states, the crown demesne around the old capital, and, for the first time, the three duchies that asked for the band, each with a few pairs on its own guarded ground. Through Corbin, Emeric asks for two soak runs this spring to get more trees in before winter. Ward, from the knights' bench, says one run: the racks hold about four hundred, every new close needs its post and its pickets first, and he has posts for about one run's worth. Ward wins by a clear margin. On Thursday about three hundred and fifty seeds go up to the K Meridian's racks for three weeks' soak, and about three hundred and fifty are kept for the Hearing's share, the works and Ferra's reserve. From Thursday 24 May the soaked seeds come down and go into the ground the same day, close by close, with a Watch squad at each.

On Friday 25 May, in the hospital's rehabilitation room, through Bettany's eyes, Reuben Orley walks the length of the parallel bars with nobody holding him, and with Emeric's hands in his pockets on purpose. Four steps, a rest, six more. His wife counts them out loud. He will need a stick for a year, and the chapel's stairs are a later victory.

On Saturday 26 May, at Wayford's river close, through Runa's eyes, Mochi comes down from the airship carrying Wayford's replant pair for the last leg and puts them into ground that was ash in December. The ash is soil now, dark and crumbly and full of worms, and the saved tree has new leaves. Osric signs Mochi off the chain of custody, and Mochi goes a pleased pearl colour at the pen this time. Ferra writes "she" in the book again. That afternoon a sparrow sits on the drop cage over the new pair. Runa asks it something, the way she asks every animal, where the water is, whether anything is hunting. It does not answer, and it does not refuse. Every bird she has ever asked has done one or the other. It just sits and watches the pair in the ground. Mochi will not go near it. It goes flat and cold-coloured on Emeric's boot, the way it did at Gil's blood, and stays there till the bird flies. Runa tells Emeric the bird was wrong, and cannot say how, except that it was not anybody. He does not know what it means, and says so. That night Ferra lays the sighting against her air column, the first entry in a new margin, and does not know what it means either.

Where the Story Stands After Chapter 80

Reuben Orley walks between the bars after the first spinal repair anyone has tried, and will need a stick for a year. The free Kindling runs at every Kaldo clinic, with about sixty readers and queues round the block. Brask's seven are home. Gethin is out of Turnstone, and has opened a drunk officer's door with the fingers Imani regrew; Corrick and Kezia come out on 14 July. Elected reeves now command the small towns' watches. Cinderhale elected the old mill lord's cousin over the League's dyer, and Keller removed Tarrow Bridge's hereditary captain with a ruling, not a force. The council's first lawsuit was thrown out as premature, and its year of delay ends on 27 September.

The Seed Act is law, amended against the king: the Grove Watch answers to the Assembly's Inspector of the Groves, and each state's seed share is voted every spring. The first fruit has fallen from the first hundred trees, about seven hundred seeds counted with their keels written, about three hundred and fifty soaked and planted after Ward held the racks to one run, and the three duchies have their first closes. There are now about five hundred trees, Wayford's burned close is replanted in soil Mochi made from ash, and light stones burn brighter near the closes.

The research pen holds one live pup, and the first test in it showed that a beast's own crystal holds a wound open. The fire golems and a rescue golem made the first real rescue. Tess's cap tells right hand from left four times in five, and a glow grid has spelled her name. The west hill dig found a road, a waystation built of the old races' keyed stone, and a layer of char that answers nothing; Ward's master said the footings were older than the kingdom, and that is all Ward knows. The mother is still unseen, five young are still in the wild, and the count stands at day 610 with five sales. On the cage over Wayford's new pair a sparrow sat that neither answered Runa nor refused her, and Mochi would not go near it.

This batch is about the slow middle of reform, when the hard laws have passed and the institutions carry on without a crisis to prove themselves. Good things grow quietly: legs, trees, readers, rescues. At the edge of all that growth something new has started watching, and nobody yet knows what it is.
