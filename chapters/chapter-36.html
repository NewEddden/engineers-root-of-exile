Book 3, Batch 17: The Crypt

Chapters 171–179 | Monday 7 March - Monday 23 May, Year 24

By the spring of Year 24 Kaldo was inside Ostrel on the emperor's terms, opening the Church's stones one at a time under neutral eyes. Every stone made the woman trapped inside the pope a little more whole, and the pope had run out of ways to stop it from a distance.

This batch is the crypt. It spends one Thursday in March a room at a time: a priest who disobeys an order to use his own people as a wall, knights told to hold men they could easily kill, a king humming at a cracked casing, and a name said aloud for the first time in two thousand years. Then it follows what the victory costs. Kaldo has the prisoner, but an imperial court decides how often it may speak to her.

Chapter 171: Out of Options

On Monday 14 March, through the eyes of Brother Ruel, a lay brother who carries water for Father Orlan's kitchens and reveres the pope, an order is read in the papal close's refectory. On Thursday the faithful of the lower city are to keep vigil in the crypt's chapel "for the protection of the holy dead." Orlan hears it beside him and says nothing until the reader has gone.

Ruel only knows the close's gossip. An old cardinal of the pope's own bench argued in her rooms for a week that going down would put her in front of the king. The pope goes down anyway. Every stone Kaldo opens costs her, and only she can open them to choose which to destroy.

Chapter 172: The Second Passage, Again

Before dawn on Thursday 17 March about two hundred of the lower city's faithful fill the crypt's chapel because they were told to come. Orlan reads the order again, understands what it is for, and disobeys it. He leads them out through the second passage, the one the register burner used in January. Tamas holds a lamp at the narrow turn and Ruel carries water down the line. The loyalists' captain shouts after Orlan, and Orlan doesn't stop.

Keeping the passage clear pulls Bren and two knights off the lower stair, and the close's juggernauts come down behind the pope's guard. Through Arthur's eyes, the Crestless are under orders to hold them, not kill them. The juggernauts' wounds close as you watch, so the knights drain them with cuffs instead of hurting them. They lose the turn twice and win it back once. Mira breaks two fingers. A knight takes a fist through his visor and is carried back to the medics.

Chapter 173: Ostrella

Through Amara's eyes, the pope walks into a surviving bay of the crypt under her own guard, in Augustine's body with the Gilded Eye at the throat. Under the terms nobody can stop a pope from entering her own crypt, and nobody tries. Ferra writes down the hour. The pope opens one pair of stones with the tune and drains it, one hand flat on the stone for most of a minute while it turns grey from the edge inward. Grey flakes fall on the floor.

Behind Emeric a preservation bank cracks, and the casings begin to fail. He is at the worst one with both hands on it, holding it shut with gravity and fire and humming without noticing. The pope's hand stops a finger's width from the next stone because she has looked up. He looks up too and says the only name he has for her, read off the spine of a book in his mind: Ostrella.

For one short section the page belongs to Ostrella. A word she doesn't know, in a voice she does. She spends everything she has saved over two thousand years of being carried, knowing it will cost her voice, and holds the way out shut from inside.

The hand moves again, but the way out doesn't. In a voice none of them has heard, the same mouth says: You knew. There is no answer. Amara sees what no one else can: two minds in one body, and one of them has gone quiet.

Chapter 174: The Window

Oruse lashes out the only way left to her. Through Adana's eyes, she reaches down the reverence chain into Brother Ruel, who has come back from the passage with an empty water skin to see his pope. An override that is torn back scrambles the person it ran through. Adana isn't calibrated in her new body and walls him anyway, because protecting a person is the one thing she said she would do. Ruel lives. The cost is the golem's fine work, set back for months.

Through Jerome's eyes, he holds the braid exactly as long as the procedure needs, which was his condition. Emeric finishes the tune. With Ostrella pushing from inside, the part of the mind that carried the old mission shows itself as separate, and its hold breaks. On the stair the juggernauts keep fighting until their last orders run out, and then they are drained one by one and sit down.

Chapter 175: The First Hour

Through Ward's eyes, imperial soldiers strip every stone from the body on the crypt floor, the pendant first. The body goes up the stair on a stretcher. At the stair head a cheap stone is overloaded to throw a dead zone about thirty yards across for about an hour, far enough from the crypt that the casings below are safe. Merryn Pellow's crew overload more stones down the corridor so the body travels inside dead ground all the way.

The cell is a chamber under the close held jointly by the empire and Kaldo, with slag-wool walls, a standing dead zone, plain locks and knights. No control stone may ever touch the body. Kaldo holds Augustine's own latch stone. Ward walks beside the stretcher the whole way and lets no one else, and tells Harrold on the stair. By night the mission's hold is broken, Oruse is awake and the only voice, and Ostrella is silent but has lost nothing.

Chapter 176: He

On Friday 18 March, through Ferra's eyes, the Core's gazette prints under the emperor's seal that the Holy Father is in imperial custody after an attack in the crypt in which no one died. It says "he" throughout, and Ferra doesn't correct it. The Holy See's remaining officers call it a kidnapping, and a provincial bishop shuts his cathedral's registers to the emperor's clerks.

On Monday 21 March Deacon Varro testifies before the emperor's council as the Church's witness. The order to wall the chapel with the faithful was wrong, he says, and Father Orlan was right to disobey it. He also says Kaldo kept every clause. The freed cardinals split, and Benedikt asks to be judged first. Orlan buries a neighbour the next morning with the town's consent. The emperor names a transitional commission of religious and lay members to hold the Church's registers, schools and poor-relief. Kaldo may advise, and Ferra turns down a seat.

Chapter 177: Seventeen

Runa turns seventeen on Saturday 2 April, in her first year on the knights' course, with a letter and a drawing from Liane. A letter from Ferra brings news from Ostrel: speaking through the glass in Augustine's voice, Oruse told the Church's claimants that one of her stones is held as a lawful relic in a provincial cathedral, where no warrant in the Core can reach it. She is right, and she said it to cost Kaldo time.

On Thursday 14 April, through Tess's eyes, the rewritten beast core passes its second test. The balance loop now runs at both speeds, and the parts the beast never used are gone. The frame walks the proving floor and the rubble course with its brakes set. Tess signs it off. A small core can now run a bigger body. The fourth fruit comes in at about twenty thousand pods. Imani explains why the fourth rat died: its blood had gone too grey for any treatment. The injection works like dialysis, only faster, and she moves on to people.

Ward's fifth birthday problem has a coat with Runa's own name sewn in it, left on a fence as the false clue. She refuses it and gets home before Ward.

Chapter 178: Seal Numbers

On Monday 11 April, through Lord Brannick's eyes, a carter at Brannick's Ford rises as a Crownborn in his own house two days after he died. The nursing school's first class clears the street, and Brannick's men burn the creature in the yard. Two people die before it is down. In the house there is a flask with a Clean Ground Service seal number. His widow drank from the same flask in a smaller dose, and the school's Kaldo instructor sees the flecks on her clinic band.

On Monday 25 April, through Saban's eyes, the widow becomes the first person to receive the new injection, by her own choice and inside the window. Her flecks clear in four days. The Assembly's Inspectorate has already doubted two crews whose turn-in volumes ran short, and the seal belongs to one of them. Saban and the Inspectorate open the file but arrest no one yet, because a seal number proves where a flask came from, not who sold it. Honest crews start getting searched. Brannick asks for Velmoor's own Service crews, and Ferra says yes before Emeric does.

Chapter 179: The Review

On Monday 9 May, through Ward's eyes, Emperor Severin sells Kaldo the core of one of the beasts killed under Ostrel in January, a young Rampart-kind. He says he hasn't forgotten who made him let a foreign king into his crypt, and he sells it anyway. With the rewritten core's freed room, it can run Ward's machine, the Bastion. The frame is laid the next week.

On Monday 23 May, through Ferra's eyes, the Core's high court reviews the custody. The Church's claimants argue that a foreign king holds the Holy Father unlawfully, and Lell answers for the Core. Ferra asks for daily sessions so the hunt can begin. The court rules that the joint custody holds and allows two sessions a week, each with an imperial physician in the room and the claimants' observer behind glass. The next review is set for November. Emeric argued for more and lost, and says so on the stairs. Ferra tells him the court was right to give less than they asked, because it's the court.

Where the Story Stands After Chapter 179

The pope is defeated and held in a dead-zone cell under joint imperial and Kaldo custody. Publicly the pope is still "he." Everyone in the crypt saw two minds in one body. The one that ran the Church is awake and talking, using what she knows as leverage. The woman held inside her is silent, but she shut the door and has lost nothing. Nobody died in the crypt. A priest disobeyed his superiors to save his people, and the Church's own witness said he was right.

Kaldo won and still doesn't control what happens next. A court sets how often it may question its prisoner. A commission it won't sit on holds what the Church used to run. A new medicine has saved its first person, and the first scandal in the beast-clearing service is being handled by others with patience, starting from a seal number. The first session in the cell is booked for Monday 30 May.
