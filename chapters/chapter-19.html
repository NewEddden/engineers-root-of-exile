Book 2, Batch 19: The Answer

Chapters 140–143 | Saturday 8 May – Monday 14 June, Year 18

The Isle's first beast is dead, fourteen people with it, and the works record lies in Ferra's office in order, twelve papers from the hollow's first beat to the waking, with Harrold's signature on page five and Bryn's two warnings on top. Kaldo has told its city that the king fought the beast as a dragon. The Heirs' broadsheet says the gold-eyed king's shaft bred a beast with his gold in its eyes. A Church priest's cipher letter is on its way to Ostrel. The beast's core and both gold-veined eyes are in Kaldo's shut cases, held for the Isle Compact. Dacey, Bryn's sister, is standing at the castle gate with her brother's warnings copied in her own hand.

This short last batch of Book 2 is the answer. The king walks down to the gate and names what happened before she has to, then hands the decision about what it costs to a review he does not control, with a chair for the families of the dead, an empty chair for the orc families, and the mill lord who votes against everything. He also sends the pope one of the beast's eyes with a five-word question, against the advice of nearly everyone at the table.

What comes back is the first time he has heard the enemy afraid: a letter in a two-thousand-year-old hand with a name begun and struck out, an accusation, and one fact that can be checked against the record. He does not do what it tells him. The book ends in a plain chair below a magistrate's bench, with a one-word answer.

Chapter 140: The Gate

About nine on the morning of Saturday 8 May, the gate guard has let Dacey stand inside the arch out of the wind, which is as far as his orders go. Emeric comes down alone, in his ordinary coat, with no Ward, because a king with a knight beside him at a gate is a different picture and he knows it. She does not curtsey. She holds out the two copies. He reads both standing, all the way through, though he has read the originals three times. Then he says it before she has to: her brother warned them twice; the first warning was filed as not enough to stop the work; he stopped the shaft three weeks after the second, and it was already too late; the clause that let it go on was his, and his father signed under it. He does not say mistakes were made. She says she knows all that, because she has read the record's copy at the Islanders' lodging, which Ferra had sent there on Thursday, and asks what he is going to do.

He does not say sorry for what he did not do, and he does not say it is nothing to do with him. He says he is not going to be the one who decides what it costs, because he would decide it in his own favour without meaning to, and so would anyone in his chair. He tells her what he means to set up, and that the families of the dead will have the right to ask anyone questions in it, him first if they like. She asks whether he will answer. He says yes. She asks whether he will do what they say. He says that on the crown's part of the works, yes, in writing, before they have said it. She asks for nothing for herself and says she will stay until it sits. She does not thank him, and he does not expect her to. That afternoon, in the long room, Harrold says he would sign the exception again on the facts he had in September, and that the facts he has now include a datum moved forty yards. He asks to go first before any review, since he signed. Emeric says that is the review's to decide, not theirs, which is the point.

On Sunday 9 May, in Keller's working room, the terms are settled. It will be called the Isle Works Review, and Kaldo will call it the review. Keller, the crown's magistrate, takes the chair; he rules on evidence and on what is sealed, and votes on the finding only if the members split evenly. Three delegates are elected by the Assembly from its own floor, excluding anyone on the crown's council and anyone holding Isle works shares or wages. The Island families name one member under the Compact's say over the bores. The orc doors' families name one member too, and that chair is kept empty until they do. Duvrenne gets an observer with the right to put questions through the chair and to receive the finding the same day as Kaldo, but not a member's seat, because one crown cannot sit in judgment on the other's king under that king's law and Duvrenne's own captain is a witness. Ferra is recused: she counted the oil the exception served, built the record, and was censured in February, so she gives her case book and every paper and sits nowhere. The six are all witnesses. The families of the fourteen may attend every sitting and put questions to any witness through the chair, the king included, and Dacey speaks for Bryn's family.

The review can compel any paper and any witness in the crown's service, the king, Harrold, the regent's papers, the works' logs and the line room's sets. It can find the facts and name who decided what, including the king's clause, Harrold's exception, the Assembly's deferral and the works' foreman. It can decide the crown's part of the Isle works, whether the oil bores reopen and on what terms, and what is done with the hollow, whether left sealed, surveyed, opened or destroyed; for Duvrenne's and the Islanders' shares its finding is a recommendation they are asked to take. It can award the families of the dead and the hurt, paid from the crown's own purse, not the Assembly's. It can recommend what happens to the clause itself, which the king will put to the Assembly word for word. It can refer anyone to Keller's court, and it will publish its finding whole on the day it is given. It cannot remove a king, convict anyone itself, bind Duvrenne or the Islanders, or put Kaldo's secrets in public. Sealed matters go to Keller in his room, and Keller says flatly that he will seal nothing because it is embarrassing. The king signs the binding before Keller's clerk: to abide by the finding on the crown's part, to appear when called and answer every question, the families' included, to pay what it awards, to put its word on the clause to the Assembly himself, and to publish the finding whole. Harrold signs the same on his own line, without being asked.

At noon on Monday 10 May, on the same records hall steps as the King's Word, with Ferra watching from the crowd because she is recused, there is no speech beyond the terms. Keller's clerk reads them, and the king and Harrold sign the binding a second time on a table in front of the square, so it is seen. Dacey stands at the front with the Islanders from the lodging. Somebody in the crowd shouts that the king is buying himself a jury. Somebody else says a king who wanted a jury would not have picked the mill lord, and the mill lord has not been picked yet. Ferra writes both down. Kaldo News prints the terms whole on the late sheet. Corbin writes that the review is outside the chair's authority and hands it the works' papers that came through the chair while the king was away.

The same day, for the first time and on Ferra's say, Kaldo's line room rings a gift set past the wall: Kettrin's, at the orc treaty-house. Through Kettrin and the house's speaker, Ferra reads the short version first, so the orc families hear it from Kaldo before anyone else: the zone held; nothing inside it was touched; the beast came from the hollow outside it; the names of the dead; and the review, with a chair kept for them. Hull five goes at dawn on Tuesday with Ferra's April letter, a fair copy of the twelve papers, the terms, and four sets: three sold to the treaty-house at the house's own price, the first trade sets, and one given to the doors' families so their member can hear the sittings read. Eight gift sets are left.

At a special sitting on Wednesday 12 May, forty-two members vote, and through the crews' man's eyes the three delegates are elected by ballot under the rule: the mill lord who votes against everything, the crews' man himself, and a delegate who voted for the survey fund in February. The mill lord stands and says he will find against the king if the evidence lets him. The crews' man says he will find whatever it says. On Thursday 13 May Duvrenne's post brings Kesrath's answer in the old king's formal hand: the king's leave to visit Duvrenne's half of the Isle, granted for June or after, with Oskel's report attached and the blank where the king's name should be still blank, and a request that Duvrenne sit on the review. The king answers the same day with the observer's seat. He does not use the leave, because he will not stand on the Isle as a king while the review is hearing what he did there. He puts it in a drawer. Alina's letter to Waylon, by the same post, Waylon keeps to himself.

Chapter 141: Take Your Beast Back

On the evening of Tuesday 11 May, in the long room, through Imani's eyes, Emeric puts it plainly to Ferra, Imani, Armand and Saban: one eye, a note, the inner post to the pope. Ferra says it pokes the one thing the Letters are holding still. The switch works because the pope prefers quiet to a war, and a box with an eye in it is a question nobody asked, telling the pope that Kaldo thinks the Church has a hand in the Isle while a review is sitting on it. She would rather he did not, and writes down that she said so. Armand says he is asking a liar a question he cannot check. Emeric says he is not after the answer; he is after how fast it comes back, and in whose hand. Saban says the inner post is spent the day it carries a box, since a chamberlain who receives a jar of spirit in an iron case will be asked about it by someone. Emeric says it was always going to be spent on something, and better on a question than a threat. Imani says the juggernaut in her ward types like a man, and this thing types like nothing, with a strand in it she has seen in every people aboard and cannot place. Whatever grew it, it was not the Church's wombs. Emeric says he knows. He does not think it is the pope's either. He wants to see what someone who has always known more than him says when accused of the wrong thing. Ferra asks, as she asked about the Letters, whether he thinks it is the pope's. He says no. She says that was not what she asked. He says then maybe it was made the way the Church grows things, and he is not sure of anything else about it, and that is why he is asking. It is an eye rather than a claw: a claw is a weapon and reads as a threat, an eye is a question, and the gold is what the rumour is about.

From Wednesday to Thursday, through Ferra's eyes, the question of whose it is to send is settled. The eyes belong to the Compact, held by Kaldo because it has the only way to keep them, and Ferra will not let one go without its owners. House Ducatis holds the escrow, and Vantic releases it on two signatures besides the crown's: Oskel's, by Waylon's set, who signs and asks why, and is told the king wants to know who it belongs to; and Mother Sabbe's word, given at her fire to Waylon: send it back where it came from. Vantic asks what it is for. He tells her. She does not lie and says nothing, which from Vantic is an opinion. Ferra notes that the Church's priest was refused an eye in April because the Church is not an owner, and now an owner is sending one.

On Wednesday 19 May, in the dome's yard, through Ward's eyes, the Grove Watch is sworn on the Roll with its own oath, Osric its first captain, and the crews call it the pickets. The first closes are walled and caged, and in that week the first fifty pairs, a hundred seeds soaked first on the K Meridian's racks and entered in Kaldo's own sky as soak, go into the ground, one close in every holding of Kaldo's states, with a squad at each and the seeds in the soil the day they come down from the racks. The vault holds about a hundred and eighty now. Ward's last line: now they're worth burning.

On Thursday 13 May, through Tess's eyes at the Motion Shop, one gold-veined eye comes out of its shut case into a sealed glass jar of spirit with a ground-glass stopper, waxed and wired. Glass holds its mana and does not hide it, so whoever lifts the lid will feel what it is at once. The jar goes into a small iron box with copper crush gaskets like the first shut case, so no courier's mage feels it on the road. The other eye and the core go into the steel-and-glass case of record, finished the same week. Emeric writes the note himself on one sheet, straight onto the sheet with nothing under it, no draft and no ghost mark: Is this one your doing? Take your beast back. No signature, and the inner post's words on the cover. On Friday 14 May a House Ducatis rider takes it to the House's factor in Ostrel, who will hand it to the pope's chamberlain with the words on the cover, as the Letter went before. The rider is told to wait three days for anything handed back.

On the evening of Sunday 23 May, in Ostrel, the chamberlain rakes a lamp across the note out of habit and finds nothing pressed into it. He carries the iron box in. The pope opens it, lifts the jar to the lamp, and turns both hands over, the way the chamberlain has seen for twenty years and never asked about, then sends him out. For an hour there is no sound at all through the door. Then the pope asks for paper, with no seal, and for the rider who brought the box. The chamberlain does as he is told and does not write any of it down, which is the first rule he has ever broken.

Chapter 142: Paper Thirteen

On Friday 28 May, by Kettrin's set, the orc doors' families answer. They will name their member when they have read the record in their own hands, not before. They ask that no finding touch the hollow, the zone or their dead until that member sits. And they will choose who comes to see their dead after the zone has held a winter. On Saturday 29 May, through Dacey's eyes, Mother Sabbe comes off the K Endeavor at the castle quay with her stick and a bag of the Isle's dried fish for the Islanders' lodging, the first time she has left the Isle in forty years and the first city she has ever seen. She has named herself the Island families' member. She looks at the Shell's spires for a long time and says the field breathes, and so does this place. The carcass on the Isle is half grey by now, she says; the condenser's cells are filling; the children will not go near it and the goats will not stay away. Ferra's May column under Kaldo's own sky has its one line, the Well's draw by weight, with no dragon yet to read it.

That morning in the line room the third lesson goes ahead with the king and his son not speaking. Emeric interprets every word Sohra says and adds nothing of his own, and Harrold answers the set and not his son. Sohra, who can hear exactly what is happening, asks whether humans always teach like this. Nobody answers. Ferra writes it under the column's line: third lesson, back in an hour, no partial form yet.

On Tuesday 1 June the Ducatis rider comes to the castle's side door with a packet handed to him in Ostrel by the pope's chamberlain: no seal, folded once, without the inner post's words. Ferra reads all the king's post, so it comes to her first, and before she reads a word she sees the hand. The letter forms are two thousand years old. She has seen them only in the oldest papers the voyage brought home, the ones the Sea Hold and the orc house let her copy: closed loops where Randillia's hand leaves them open, and a stroke through the long letters that nobody has written for a hundred generations. They are written fluently, by someone for whom this is simply how you write. Above the first line is a name begun and crossed out, three letters. Ferra reads struck lines, and reads this one: "Ava." She enters it under her uncertainty marks as what it could be: the first three letters of a name everybody in Kaldo has argued about since January, or a woman's name, or nothing. She does not say the name aloud, and nobody else does.

The letter itself is three lines. It calls the king a stupid piece of shit who called them here. It asks whether he knows what its writer had to do, plan and scheme for two thousand years, and says he undid all of it for the sake of being kind, and now they are finished. It hopes he is happy. Under it, smaller and tighter in the same hand, an engineer who cannot stop has written what is useful: it sleeps until the air gets in, seven weeks, give or take, and then it comes up; it came up from under your island; there is more than one room like that in the world; burn it before it wakes again. That night in the king's study Ferra, Emeric and Imani read it, and nobody else, because its "them" might be anything and he is not spreading what he cannot explain. Emeric asks aloud who "them" is. Nobody answers, and Ferra writes "them: unknown" under her marks. The next morning Armand is shown the hand but not the text, and says he knows those forms only from copies of copies, and would have said nobody alive could write them as a living hand.

It is the first time Emeric has heard the enemy afraid. The Church's head, who has never shown fear of anything, is terrified of what came out of the Isle, and that tells him more about the beast than the beast did. He says the competent thing aloud to Ferra and Imani: if it is not the pope's, the pope knows whose, and the pope is frightened of them, which is worse news than if it were the Church's. Imani says, and you called them, apparently. He says he would like to know how. On Wednesday 2 June Ferra lays the letter's line over the record. The waking was Monday 19 April at 05:40; seven weeks back is about 1 March; paper eight, the night shift's last charges at the shaft's foot, was Tuesday 2 March. Forty-eight days. Nobody in Kaldo had dated the breach, and Tess's gauge on 15 April said only that the seal was broken. The letter dates it to the week, and the works log agrees. So the stop order came after the seal was already gone, the plug closed nothing that mattered, and the decisive cause was the charges fired under the exception. Ferra says it in one sentence. He says it means his father's signature and his own clause under it, and she says yes.

That day in the long room he tells Ferra and Imani, once, that he will not burn it. He gave the hollow to the review nine days ago, burning it is digging by fire, and the hollow's fate is one of the things he bound himself to let them decide; a king who hands over a decision and takes it back the first time it is urgent has not handed anything over. The ground is not his alone: it is the Compact's, it lies beside the orc families' dead, and they have not been heard and have asked that nothing touch it before their member sits. Going into that ground with fire, uninvited, is the harm. The advice comes from an enemy who wants something he cannot name, and "burn it" might be the truest thing in the letter, or the thing it was written for. And nothing is going in or out tonight: the shaft was filled to the surface by the end of May under Waylon and Oskel, and the chamber has its seal back. He says what it might cost: if the room wakes again before the review decides, that will be on him too, and he will say so to the review. Ferra writes down that he said it. He thinks that the first monster is never the only one, and that the people who make monsters keep the receipts. He does not say it. On the Isle he already said the version Kaldo can use.

On Thursday 3 June the letter's one dated line goes into the record as paper thirteen, at the bottom in date order, copied in Ferra's hand with its source named: from a letter received from Ostrel on 1 June by the inner post's return, the whole under seal with the magistrate. The whole letter goes to Keller under seal. Keller reads it alone in his room and says one thing to the king: he will decide what the members see once he has heard what the record says without it. Nothing else of the letter leaves the study.

From Sunday 6 June, through Saban's eyes, the Church's chapels in the old capital and the northern baronies preach the same sermon word for word, which tells Saban it came down from Ostrel and not up from a priest: the beast of the crown's ground, a thing with gold in its eyes out of a gold-eyed king's shaft, and a king who became a beast to hide it. By the second Sunday it is "beasts of the crown." The Heirs' angrier half takes the phrase onto its broadsheet within the week; Honor Linden's half does not. Saban still has not found who organised the poisoning in January. Kesrath's court has Oskel's report and the Church's version both, the Empire's court has only the Church's, and past the wall the Leeward has heard and reads it as Avarran's line building monsters again. That is the High Shore's unanswered charge.

On Thursday 10 June a second letter comes by the Church's formal post, addressed to the King of Randillia at Kaldo under the Holy See's seal, in the Church's chancery hand with the pope's ordinary signature. It is correct and cold. The Holy See has received no earlier letter from the king and sent none; the object the king sent by an irregular road has been burned as unclean; the Church prays for the souls killed by the beast of the crown's ground and trusts the crown will answer for what came out of its land. It makes no threat. Ferra lays it beside the first: two hands, one seat. She does not need her marks to tell the king which one was frightened. That evening Emeric tells Ferra and Saban what the switch is worth now. The beast told the world the king is a dragon, but it told nobody that control stones exist or that the Band can be reached by anyone with a hull, which is what the Letters threaten, so the Church moves to words, the pulpits and the courts, because the switch still bites on hands. The second letter is written to stay inside it. Then he names its weakness aloud: the day beasts like this one are being cut open by hunters for what is inside them, stones in carcasses will be something every hunter knows, and the Church's scarcity will be gone whether the Letters go out or not. Then the switch will be worth nothing, and the pope will know it before they do. "We have until then." Saban asks how long. He says he does not know, and that the first sale of a beast part by someone who is not Kaldo is the day to start counting. The heartbeat goes out at the month's end in Ferra's ordinary post as usual.

Chapter 143: Below the Bench

On Saturday 12 June, through Dacey's eyes, the review's first sitting opens in the records hall's long hearing room under high windows. Keller reads the fourteen names first, in the order of paper twelve, before any oath. Then come the oaths. In the chairs sit Keller; the mill lord, the crews' man and the third delegate; Mother Sabbe in her own chair with her stick across her knees; and the orc families' chair, empty, with their answer by Kettrin's set laid on it in Ferra's transcription and the set itself on the table beside it so their member can hear the sittings read. Duvrenne's observer, a Kesrath jurist, sits at the side table. On the families' bench are Dacey for Bryn, a Kaldo hand's mother, the two Islander families' cousins from the lodging, and others. Keller rules flatly. The review agrees to the orc families' condition, sets the order of witnesses, reads into its own record the binding the king and Harrold signed, and receives paper thirteen, noting that the whole is under seal with the chair, who will say when he has heard enough whether the members see it. The records hall's copy clerk assigned the review's fair copy is Honor Linden, and she copies everything and says nothing either way. Harrold asks to be heard first. Keller refuses: the clause was the king's, the exception was signed under it, so the king goes first. Harrold sits down.

That afternoon, through Neris's eyes, the review having sat, the fourteen names go up first on the First Room's wall, on a plain board under the entrance wall, in the order Keller read them: Bryn's among them, and Duvrenne's two and the Islanders' by their families' leave. Nothing else about the Isle goes up. Neris, who said in April she would check it herself, reads them all twice and finds one spelled wrong, an Islander's name written the Randillia way. She tells Halloran's people, who fix it before closing, and she stays until they do.

On Monday 14 June, in the morning, in the same hearing room, Keller calls the king. The witness chair stands below the bench, a plain chair lower than the members'. The families' bench is full, the public seats are full, and the mill lord has a pen out. Ferra sits in the public seats, recused, with nothing in her hands for once. He sits. He does not look at the six or his father. This is the room where nobody needs him to be right. Keller's first question is the one the record opens with: when did you write the clause? He answers with a date: the twenty-ninth of August, last year, the Saturday night he wrote the midpoint order and its exception by relay from the Sea Hold. Keller glances down at the record and asks the second question: and whose decision was it to include the exception? "Mine."

Where the Story Stands After Chapter 143

Book 2 ends with the Isle Works Review in session and the king's first evidence given. Its finding is still owed: the crown's part of the works, the fate of the hollow, whether sealed, surveyed, opened or burned, the clause itself, which goes to the Assembly, awards from the crown's own purse, and any referral to Keller's court. The orc families' chair stays empty until they have read the record in their own hands, and nothing touches the hollow, the zone or their dead before then. Kesrath's leave sits in a drawer, and Duvrenne's observer sits at the side table. Paper thirteen is in the record, and the whole Ostrel letter is under seal with Keller. Keller's older reserved question, whether a court of the crown can judge the crown's own act in its own sky, still stands over the king's written account of the beams of 4 January.

Ferra's box holds two letters from one seat: the first afraid, in a hand two thousand years old with "Ava" struck out, and the second cold, denying it. The letter accused the king of calling "them" here and said there is more than one room like the Isle's in the world, and nobody knows who "them" is. The eye has been burned as unclean, by the second letter's word, and the other eye and the core are in Kaldo's case of record. The pulpits preach "beasts of the crown," the Heirs' angrier half has taken it up, and Honor Linden copies everything and says nothing. The Church's switch still bites, and its weakness is named: the day hunters sell beast parts, the scarcity is gone. Past the wall the Leeward says Avarran's line is building monsters again. That is the High Shore's charge, and nobody has answered it.

Fifty pairs of seeds are in the ground under the Grove Watch, and the vault holds about a hundred and eighty. Waylon has gold eyes and no change, Emeric's full form is public, and Ferra's book still holds the question of whether not being sorry about the Church's hulls is a problem. The winged ones' fund is UNPAID on the First Room's wall, the box beside it counted every Friday, and the king will bring it back next budget. Eight gift sets remain, the first trade sets have gone to the treaty-house, and the AM-T2 has three rounds at the Claim. Fourteen names are the first thing on the room's wall about the Isle.

Book 2 began with a king going out to meet the peoples his ancestors drove away, and it ends with him in a chair lower than everyone else's, answering for a decision that killed fourteen people. The whole book has been asking who gets to decide, and at the end the king gives that power away in writing, refuses an enemy's advice to take it back, and then says the one word that owns it.
