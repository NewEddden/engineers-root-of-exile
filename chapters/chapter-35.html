Book 3, Batch 16: The Vault

Chapters 162–170 | Monday 3 January - Sunday 6 March, Year 24

At the turn of Year 24 the Church's secret was out. It had kept a beast under its holiest ground and a machine behind its gate, and in a crypt nobody knew existed lay two bodies Amara was sure were her parents. The emperor had broken with the Church and asked for the king's band, but he hadn't yet let Kaldo in.

This batch follows Kaldo into Ostrel, step by step, under other people's authority. The emperor sets the terms. A pool of neutral inspectors from the Kesrath compact watches every room, and a Church deacon stands in all of them. A cardinal agrees to show his own chains. Emeric opens the stones because only he can, and the first one shows him a man with his own face.

Chapter 162: Luggage

On Monday 3 January, through Tess's eyes, the first four thousand wristbands leave Kaldo for Ostrel's garrison and lower city under the emperor's own seal. Saint Ilda's and Coldharbour were fitted the week before. Emeric's arm, Maeve's hands and Zale's shoulder have all healed.

On Thursday 6 January Emeric tries his first pass at rewriting a beast's core. He has stripped one of the Isle young's cores of everything it doesn't use: the fifteen body plans for other kinds of beast, its growth notes, and its endless repeated self-checks folded into one. Set in a test frame with every brake on, the frame stands, takes one step and falls over sideways into the catch rail. Tess finds the mistake by the afternoon. One of the "repeats" wasn't a repeat. It was the frame's sense of balance running at a second speed, and he had folded its sense of falling into its sense of standing. He writes it in her book as hers to catch next time.

On Saturday 8 January the Undertow swims for the first time in the Cradle's bay, with Zale in the sphere and the Bulwark standing in the water to catch it. Ilenne watches from the bay wall and says it swims like a man who learned from merfolk. The same week Imani tries the new gland injection on rats: three recover and the fourth doesn't, and she won't try it on a person until she knows why. With the glands, Kettrin's roll sends the boatmen's count from the ford: the young killed there, the pods on the reed island and the pods still in the mother come to twenty-four. Tess sets the Isle's Register beside it, twenty-one killed, one starved, one pod burned and one in the pen, which also comes to twenty-four, and writes "the Isle's clutch is closed?" in the restricted pages, question mark and all. The Isle's mother is still unseen. Rowena's mech, the Larkspur, is laid down on the Glassrunner's core, and the first Aegisframe's frame takes the Murr's core, with Torvild's hands on the fitting.

Through Ferra's eyes, the cold came in still on Tuesday 4 January. By Thursday the wind rows are standing idle and half the Mere's pumped water is spent. That morning her office invokes the pot lines' drought clause, and the old coal station is lit for the hospital and the water works, the first time since the green turn. On Friday and Saturday the Capital Line runs half its trains. Kaldo News puts the members on television on the Saturday, trains against aluminium: the Narrow March wants its trains, and the Works Town's member wants the pot lines back because his men are being paid to stand still. Nobody wins it; the weather does. Emeric says nothing on television. In Ferra's office he looks at the Headwater date she wrote down in December and doesn't argue with it. From the Monday after, she prints a weekly power figure beside the bread price.

Chapter 163: The Second Report

On Thursday 13 January, through Cardinal Benedikt's eyes, the Core's coroner sits on the lower market's dead in Ostrel. This time the evidence isn't the soldiers'. The coroner's officers went down into the undercroft with the emperor's engineers and brought up what they found: iron chains anchored in the rock and bitten through, feed troughs lined with crystal grit, and empty casks stencilled for the Church's stores skimmed from the Band. It was a pen.

Benedikt testifies because he was summoned and because he won't lie under oath. In November he asked, as a cardinal of the Holy See, to see the lower level, and Deacon Varro refused him in writing on the pope's standing order. He hands the court the letter and adds nothing. The coroner finds that the beast was kept in the cathedral's undercroft by people in the Church's service and escaped through negligence. It names nobody higher. Benedikt walks out a cardinal who has testified against his own cathedral, and he knows it. It is the report the emperor can't bury, because it came from his own court.

Chapter 164: Narrow Terms

The cold broke the day before, Sunday 16 January. Nobody died, and the Assembly's argument is in the record.

On Monday 17 January, through Ferra's eyes, the terms are signed in the emperor's council chamber, drafted by his lawyer Ansgar Lell. Kaldo comes in on three separate authorities. The first is the danger itself: Kaldo's beast hunters may clear anything still penned below. The second is Amara's claim to her parents' bodies, under witnessed custody. The third is an inquiry into unlawful imprisonment, which opens only if a freed cardinal agrees to show that a leash works. Kaldo concedes neutral inspectors at every step, a fixed review date on any custody (Monday 23 May), no stones leaving the capital without the emperor's leave, and a preliminary inventory published before Kaldo goes home. The inspectors are a Solreth jurist and a Duvrenne surveyor, named by the Kesrath compact's clerk from its pool, and, at Ferra's asking, Hulda of the Sea Hold, because her inquiry was waiting for exactly this order. The emperor's condition is Deacon Varro in every room.

Emeric goes because only his tune opens the stones. Amara goes for her claim, and with them go Harrold, Ward, the Crestless on foot (no mech enters Ostrel), Saban, Ferra, Imani and Merryn Pellow's crew. Ferra asks for one more thing under Celestine's archive catalogue: film of the two oldest printed plans of the cathedral close, so the inspectors can map the lower level before they enter it.

Through Severin's eyes, he tells Emeric privately that he is letting a foreign king into his holiest ground because his own court found a pen under it, and that he will remember who made him do it either way. On Wednesday 19 January he seals the film batch himself, and Celestine carries the film to the legation in person.

Chapter 165: The Second Passage

On Monday 24 January, through Deacon Varro's eyes, the K Sorrel sets down on the crater lake, and for the first time Kaldo's people step onto Ostrel's soil. Varro meets them at the lower gate and reads the terms aloud, every clause, before anyone passes. He is cold and exact, and nobody can fault him. Below the undercroft two more beasts are still in their pens, smaller than the one that broke loose. Merryn Pellow's crew kill them in their pens while the inspectors watch and the Crestless hold the stair, their night glass down because the heat picture finds the beasts behind the bars in the cold rock. Their cores belong to the emperor, whose ground it is. Kaldo carries them in shut cases with their hidden listening notes silenced. Their heads go too, and on Monday 31 January Tess reads both marks in Kaldo. Neither says "under the Isle." One was born under the High Shore's interior, the other under the outer islands off the Sun River's mouth. The Church didn't catch Randillia's strays. Its hidden hulls took young alive from past the wall, and Ferra adds a line to her page: hulls past the wall, taking live beasts.

On Wednesday 26 January, through Saban's eyes, a lay brother posted to register work weeks before the terms, and on Varro's own list, reaches the register room by a passage nobody had mapped. He burns one volume of the crypt's registers before the guard at the main door smells smoke, and a run of tombs is now of unknown contents. Saban finds the passage from the way the fire was set: it was lit from the back of the room, where neither plan shows a door. From that hour Ferra's custody log covers every item, every step and every hand. In the inspectors' minutes Varro defends the Church's right to a witness, and in the same minutes says the burning was wrong. Saban writes that down as the first honest thing anyone in the close has said to him. The first item in Ferra's custody log is the pens' keeper's book from a desk in the undercroft. It lists feed brought down from the Band, and parts bought on Kesrath's quay from 25 September Year 18 on, every one paid in new-struck imperial gold and drawn on one of the two imperial houses Tullio named to Vantic in October Year 18. Saban takes the name to Vantic. She reads it twice and says her brother was right about who lends to anyone with a war to sell. The mint year Saban had called worth very little for five years turns out to be worth this. It proves the Church bought parts and kept beasts. It doesn't say who made them.

Chapter 166: The Leash

On Thursday 3 February, through Benedikt's eyes, the vault holds rack on rack of latch stones, each tagged with a cardinal's name, and his is among them. Emeric tells him what it is and what it does, in front of the inspectors, Hulda, the imperial witnesses and Varro, and tells him Ferra has a gentler proof if he says no. Benedikt agrees, narrowly, to one test. He is asked to write one sentence on the inspectors' page: the Holy See has kept beasts beneath this close. His hand won't. It doesn't shake. It stops, the pen a finger above the paper, while his face does everything his hand can't. The witnesses sign what they saw.

On Friday 4 February Emeric ends every live leash with the tune, and each stone goes to dust in front of the inspectors and Ferra's log. Benedikt's goes first. He sits down and writes the sentence without stopping, then writes a second one nobody asked for. The freed cardinals are many, and they are divided.

Through Hulda's eyes, there is an older rack, tagged not with names but with the houses that signed the old treaty with the other peoples. Most of its stones are already dust, spent long ago. One is still live: the Sea Hold's. Emeric reads it in front of her: one sliver of a mind with one order, refuse. It burns to dust like the others. She doesn't thank him. She asks for its dust in a sealed jar for her house's ledgers, and gets it. Earlier, on the stair, while Emeric was re-seating a hinge on a rusted casket, Ferra told him he always hums that when he's fixing something.

Chapter 167: Behind the Door

On Monday 7 February, through Amara's eyes, two unlabelled stones sit apart from every rack in a casket nobody tagged. Emeric's tune opens them, and Amara hears her mother before anyone else does, the way she heard her parents while the Church held her. Emeric bridges each mind into a golem body made to their exact likeness, one hand on the stone and one on the golem. Jerome goes first, because Adana says so.

Through Jerome Zahir's eyes, he looks at his daughter, a woman now older than he ever was, and says she has her mother's way of standing. Then he asks what year it is, does the sum, and is quiet. Within days he can talk and hold a mind, and he tells Ward he will hold only while the agreed procedure is followed. Adana Tano's first words are about the golem, and none of them are kind. Her fine work is unsafe at first, and when Emeric suggests a shortcut through her stone she refuses: she will wall a mind to protect a person, but she won't be a shield for a plan nobody has tested. Jerome tells Ferra and Imani, because they happen to be in the room, that there were two women in the stones with them once, and that the women left. He doesn't know who they were. Armand, who studied under Adana as a boy and only half remembered her, looks at a golem with her face and remembers everything at once. She tells him he's got old, and he tells her she hasn't. Jerome tells Harrold he knew his father.

On Wednesday 9 February their two bodies are lifted from their crystal casings under Varro's eyes and Imani's and carried up the stair on Ferra's log. The K Sorrel takes them home to Kaldo's healing dome, Jerome's for about a year and Adana's for about three, and Amara rides with them. At the coat she checks the button once and doesn't touch it.

Chapter 168: The Bench

On Monday 14 February, through Emeric's eyes, he opens one stone from a bay the beast never reached and enters it with Amara's hand on his arm. The memory is ordinary: a workroom, a bench, a hinge, and a man mending it and humming, seen through a woman's eyes. The man has Emeric's face. Emeric doesn't remember it, but he knows the hum. The memory carries a signature he has known since the night something cold and very large came up through the Root: the pope's. A woman's memory, in the pope's signature. He says it to the room: the pope is a woman. In public nothing changes.

Through Amara's eyes, a new book has appeared at the back of his mind's library, where the spines have always been blank. She can read this one: Ostrella. She almost tries to open it, and doesn't. He tells her, once, what frightens him. It isn't that he might have been someone important. It's that people will start treating his choices as if the other man already made them. He saw the woman in the memory remember him. That night Ferra links the name to Ostrel and to the continent itself, writes it in her book, and calls it a lead, not proof. The inspectors' inventory records only that a stone was opened and read.

Chapter 169: Two Plans

On Friday 25 February, through Ferra's eyes, the two plans lie side by side on the light table. The older one shows a stair off the crypt's west passage that the newer one doesn't. It doesn't lead to the crypt. It leads to a single room beside it that appears in no register Kaldo has seen. The inspectors go first, with Varro and Ferra's log. Behind a very old sealed door, in crystal, lies a woman with copper-brown skin, an ash-silver braid worn like a crown and a burn scar on her left wrist. Beside her lies something nearly human: bald, with grey-lavender skin, pale crystal scales at the neck and blotched hands. Nobody in the room knows who the woman is. For once Varro has nothing procedural to say. The inspectors list them as two unidentified bodies, to stay under guard where they lie.

On Monday 28 February, through Imani's eyes, she draws a drop of the figure's blood through a seam in the crystal. It matches no type she has ever typed. She writes down only what that proves: something tried to grow a person, and it didn't learn how here. She doesn't guess who the woman is.

Chapter 170: Lantern Night

On Thursday 3 March, in Kaldo, Hulda closes the stones inquiry at its third and last sitting. The order has been found: an order written into a stone in the Holy See's vault at Ostrel, set against her people's return, and burned to dust in front of the inspectors. The finding names the vault and the order but not the writer, because nobody can prove one. She tests a Sea Hold stone on the inquiry's table, and it lights.

On Sunday 6 March, through Jerome's eyes, he walks among Kaldo's lanterns in a borrowed body with his daughter and his son-in-law the king. He has never seen electric light. He asks how the dome's glass is made and doesn't let Bram stop explaining. Adana stays in the dome beside her own body and says she has seen enough lanterns.

Through Saban's eyes, word comes from his man in Ostrel that the pope didn't come to the Sunday office in the cathedral, for the first time anyone there can remember. The lamps in the papal apartments burned all night. On Saturday a second order to seal the lower level went to the emperor's safety officer and was overruled under the terms. Saban pins the report on Ferra's page, doesn't guess, and turns off the line room's lamp.

Where the Story Stands After Chapter 170

Kaldo is inside Ostrel on the emperor's terms, and it has kept to them. The Core's own court found the pen. A cardinal chose to prove his own leash, and every live leash and treaty-guarding order in the vault is now dust. The dwarves' inquiry is closed and their stones light again. Jerome and Adana are out of the stones after sixty years and home in borrowed bodies while their own heal in Kaldo's dome.

The inner circle now knows the pope is a woman, and a name has appeared in a book in Emeric's mind that he can't open. A sealed room beside the crypt holds a woman nobody has named and a figure grown by something not of this world. The pope has stopped appearing in her own cathedral. Every move she has made to stop Kaldo has failed or only bought time, and only she can open the stones she most wants destroyed.
