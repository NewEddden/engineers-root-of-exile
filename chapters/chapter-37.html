Book 3, Batch 18: The Year in the Room

Chapters 180–191 | Monday 30 May - Sunday 25 December, Year 24

By the summer of Year 24 the pope was in a cell under Ostrel, and an imperial court had given Kaldo two sessions a week to undo two thousand years of damage one stone at a time. The mind that had run the Church was awake and talking. The woman held inside her had said one word in the crypt and almost nothing since.

This batch is the year that becomes routine. A court sets the pace, a commission Kaldo refused to sit on judges the cardinals, and the Assembly votes an export with conditions Emeric would have written differently. Twice during the year the institutions save him from himself. Meanwhile most of the year's good news comes from people who never asked Kaldo for anything.

Chapter 180: The Label

On Monday 30 May, through Imani's eyes, the first live session opens. Emeric holds a stone in a gloved hand and keeps his other hand on Augustine's wrist, and Imani watches two separate signatures in one body. Oruse says yes first, flatly. A long minute later Ostrella's yes comes, faint but unmistakably hers, and the pair closes. The imperial physician writes "no event." The pairs will go back in the order of the popes' reigns, oldest first, following the Church's register.

On Friday 10 June, through Amara's eyes, a stone labelled for the third year of the 112th pope shows a different chapel, a younger man dying in a different bed, and a clerk's hand altering a page. Someone changed the labels on purpose. Ferra's clerks suggest matching the stone to the next label in sequence to save weeks. Amara refuses to sign. From now on every stone must be read before it is placed, and a pope the register gives only eleven days becomes visible: the one man who fought her when she took him. Amara writes his real name in Ferra's log, and Varro asks for a copy.

Chapter 181: The Certain Point

On Friday 1 July, through Ferra's eyes, Emeric tells her and Imani at breakfast that his mana core has reached its limit, the point where it rebuilds the body the way a control stone does. Ferra writes to the custody court that two sessions will be missed for "a medical matter" and nothing more.

He sleeps for six days under the tree in Kaldo's dome, beside the frames where Jerome's and Adana's bodies are healing. Through Imani's eyes, she watches him break down and rebuild and barely sleeps herself. Harrold comes on the third day and sits beside Ferra without saying anything. Emeric wakes with wider channels and every element in him at once, a colour Imani has no name for. He knows what it is and doesn't use it. Then he asks for eggs.

Chapter 182: First Walk

On Thursday 21 July, through Tess's eyes, the first Aegisframe walks on Kaldo's proving strip. It is thirty metres tall, weighs about 1,280 tonnes, and runs on the Undermaw's core. Hattie Roan drives it from the head sphere: an ordinary woman with no stone and no crown. Twenty paces, a turn, a kneel, twenty paces back. Tess tells the reporter from Kaldo News that a first walk doesn't mean the machine is ready for export, before he can ask.

On Tuesday 26 July, through Ward's eyes, the Bastion walks, plants its shield, and holds it while Bram's team throws a loaded wagon against it. Ward climbs out and says it leans left. It does, and Tess fixes it in a week.

On Monday 1 August, through Runa's eyes, the emperor signs the Law of the Peoples. Every people is protected everywhere on imperial land, and trading in the peoples' bodies or parts is now a crime. Peoples who agree can try their own members in their own courts, and the werewolves' moot has asked for time to decide. The dragons count as a people; the Drifters don't. Runa reads the clause about bodies and parts twice. Ward finds her with the paper and doesn't ask why.

Chapter 183: The Twenty-Ninth

On Tuesday 16 August, through Jerome's eyes, the hunt reaches the empty twenty-ninth tomb. Long ago, as a cardinal, Jerome read the Church's letter-books and noticed that the twenty-ninth pope's letters stop in the middle of his reign. A cardinal of that time had taken the body and its stones and disappeared. Kaldo's genealogists follow his descendants to the Brenmoors, bell-founders in Aldane's duchy, who keep a grey stone in a casket on their foundry altar.

Jerome goes in person, in his borrowed body, because a cardinal's word is the only one they might believe. Old Mistress Brenmoor listens to all of it and says no. Her ancestor died for that stone. Whatever is in it belongs to them, and a foreign king's priest in a borrowed body is the last person she would hand it to. Jerome can't argue. That night a clerk points out that the crown could ask the Assembly for emergency powers. Emeric refuses, and tells Jerome to keep visiting and help the family recast their big bell.

Chapter 184: The Man Who Didn't Look

On Monday 29 August, through Emeric's eyes, a stone recovered from a Church airship's hull holds a memory of Avarran at his bench. He signs off a sheaf of reports on the war's conduct for Ostrella without looking up, talking about a hinge the whole time. A second closed book appears in Emeric's library. He recognises the habit of not looking at what he signs, and for the first time he says plainly to Ferra that it's his own habit too. Nobody mentions the bounty.

On Monday 5 September, through Celestine's eyes, she and her brother Marius arrive in Kaldo on the exchange. Marius finds that nobody stands when he enters the Polytechnic's first-year hall. Celestine takes the Library's entrance examination like any other candidate and places second of eleven. On Thursday her father writes that the young imperial engineer Petrus Ostby's heavy rail steel has passed Kesrath's independent tests on the fourth attempt. The rail line's continental extension will run on imperial rail. Severin asks her to tell no one until Bram has read it, and she tells Odile anyway.

Chapter 185: The Pendant

On Monday 12 September, through Imani's eyes, the imperial physician has been replaced. The new man, Dr. Lorens Halvane, is not a believer and passes Imani's screening. Under his collar he wears a Gilded Eye pendant, a courtesy gift the Church's claimants gave every officer of the court in May. Nobody thought of it as a stone. Partway through a pair, Oruse feels her own code in the room, and a third signature starts up in the physician. For two seconds his hand moves toward Imani's cutoff. Amara walls him off, since Adana's skill is still months from recovering, and Imani throws the cutoff. The physician is dazed but unharmed. From then on every object in the cell is listed, no Church-made stone of any kind is allowed in, and the physician watches from behind glass.

On Thursday 15 September, through Saban's eyes, the crackdown's file closes on the crew traced in April. Their summer turn-ins ran short again, Mochi the slime goes flat at flasks seized from a crewman's shed, and the crewman's own clinic band has been climbing since spring because he has been dosing too. Five men are arrested, three from the crew and two who sold the flasks on in Velmoor. The trial will be next year.

Through Saban's eyes, the arrests are run as an operation. Two places must fall in the same minute, so neither can warn the other: three of the crew at their station hut on the Narrow March's salt marsh, and the two sellers in Brannick's Ford, where Brannick's own constables make the arrests under Velmoor law. Nobody may die, because the crewman who has been dosing would rise as a Crownborn. For a week the relay eye watches the hut by day under Ferra's written order and a sealed log, a precedent she says she doesn't like; it never looks at Velmoor. They rehearse at the knights' base with the hut laid out in tape and frames, and everyone on the ground carries a pocket set with an earpiece and a throat microphone.

Through Runa's eyes, on the night of Wednesday 14 September, she is the scout, seventeen, plainclothes, on her course's field term with orders not to touch anyone. Since August she and Ferra have recut her ledger into an evidence file under the Law of the Peoples. She asked Emeric anyway; he said yes and listened from the line room. At 01:40, three hundred yards out on the wind, she reads four people in the hut, not three, and the fourth is a sleeping child. She says the doorway plan is too risky now, and everyone holds. At 02:20 the dosed man goes out to the flask shed and Arthur and Bren take him there; he's strong, and he cracks a dead-zone stone from his coat. Every visor dies. The pocket sets keep talking. The second man runs into the reeds, and Runa follows him on her nose, which no dead zone touches, and talks the team onto him. She could cut him off at the ford herself. She says that's too risky and sends Bren, who takes him there. The third man walks out with his hands up, and the medic carries the child out in a coat. At 02:31 Brannick's constables take the sellers. Five arrested, all alive. When Runa said too risky, Emeric didn't speak; it was Arthur's net. At dawn he says good call, and nothing else.

Chapter 186: The Ladder

On Thursday 29 September, through Ferra's eyes, the Assembly debates the Aegisframe Export Act, which makes this one machine an exception to the export rules. Sir Humphrey Ketteridge moves to strike the allowance and the service lock: Avarran gave his allies machines with no keys held back, he argues, and Avarran's heir should do the same. Emeric rejects both the policy and the borrowed authority. A machine nobody can stop is a gift to whoever steals it, and nobody in the room knows what Avarran would have done, "including me." The room laughs, which he didn't intend. The act passes by nine votes.

On Monday 3 October, through Alina's eyes, Duvrenne wins the Imperial Core limited shipping rights through Velmoor's ports, with real protections for Velmoor. Kaldo isn't in the room. A week later the preliminary orders come in: two machines for the emperor and one for Duvrenne. Velmoor refuses the clause on internal conflicts and declines to order. Its countess says so in a letter so honest that Ferra frames it.

Chapter 187: The Berm

On Thursday 20 October, through Hattie Roan's eyes, the Aegisframe runs the slope trial. With its power allowance nearly used up on a steep grade, she sets the machine in its secure stance, and the stance fails. The downhill foot loses the ground and the frame slides slowly into the catch berm. Hattie climbs out with a bruised hip.

Through Tess's eyes, the rule is rewritten: no work on steep ground when the allowance is nearly spent. The public show is pushed back a season rather than claim a certification the machine hasn't earned. The emperor's officer writes that he'd rather buy a machine that failed in front of him than one that never had. A week later the first memory chip from Kaldo's new silicon line works on the third wafer, and Tess pins it to the hall's board.

Saban's report on the September arrests says the eye went blind for the night, and a child was in the hut that no glass found; Runa's nose found her. Emeric sends Tess a page asking for a drone, his word for a small machine two people can carry that hovers and watches where the eye can't: at night, under cloud, under a roof's edge. It has to work without magic first, like everything else in her hall, and it never carries a gun. On Tuesday 25 October her first rig, two open rotors stacked on a frame, hovers at head height for four minutes and is as loud as the mill race. Hattie, limping past, says nobody alive will fail to hear it. Tess sends that back to Emeric too, and he takes the problem home for the winter.

In Tess's hall that autumn, Wayford's telephone exchange goes electronic, and Ambrose insists that its switchboard operators are retrained at the line room first. Linnea Voss, a chemist from the Polytechnic, brings in a pinch of salt drawn out of a ton of sour-seam ore, carried in a lead pot. The ore counter screams, and Tess has it locked in the yard store under Imani's rules. On the Sky Yard's stand a hydrogen-fuelled stage burns for about forty seconds.

Chapter 188: Forty-Seven Years

On Wednesday 2 November, through Imani's eyes, Emeric, Ferra and Imani visit the cell outside a session. There is no bridge and no observer, and the imperial warden stands out of earshot. Oruse offers what she knows about the convoy in exchange for a written promise, filed with the court, that her knowledge will never be taken from her by force. Emeric signs. Her makers seeded this world about twelve thousand years ago, she tells them, and the convoy has been travelling longer than that. Nobody aboard remembers a world they didn't take. Emeric asks how fast the ship can go. It is already at full speed. He works it out on Ferra's pad: forty-seven years from the Well. When he asks what they fight with, she can't say, because that part of her is gone. He says it costs him nothing. Imani stops calling Oruse "it" in her notes.

On Monday 7 November, through Ferra's eyes, Imani asks who the woman in the sealed room is. "Her. Who else would I keep?" Imani asks why Oruse never destroyed the body. Oruse answers once. Centuries ago, in one pope's last winter, she tried with lamp oil at the crystal. Ostrella took control of the hand and kept it for three days, and the Church recorded a stroke. Oruse never tried again. Now Kaldo knows the sealed room holds Ostrella's own body and the failed body Oruse was printed into.

Chapter 189: Midnight

In November, through Ferra's eyes, word comes by letter that a storm has flooded the water works at Wenmouth, a Velmoor port, while Kaldo's crews were busy with a beast ashore at Saltreach. Solreth's tradespeople and its first locally trained repair crews restored the works in a fortnight. Velmoor's countess writes, "Thank Solreth, not you." The commission clears Cardinal Benedikt, finding that every act he committed was done under the leash.

On Thursday 17 November the court postpones its review until the pendant inquiry reports. The custody order runs until that review, so the authority for sessions lapses at midnight on Sunday. Through Imani's eyes, Kaldo moves a session to Sunday evening to finish a pair. At a quarter to midnight the imperial warden tells her, correctly, that the order lapses at midnight. Ostrella's yes is coming, the slowest it has ever been. At midnight Imani cuts the session anyway. Emeric backs her in front of the warden.

For two weeks no session may begin, and Kaldo does nothing but file papers. On Monday 5 December the inquiry finds no proof the pendant was meant as a weapon, and the court allows one session a week, with the next review in May. Ferra says it was the right ruling. Emeric agrees, and this time he means it.

Chapter 190: The Relic

On Thursday 8 December, through Ferra's eyes, Kaldo goes to court for the last stone. It holds Ostrella's name, and it has been venerated for five hundred years as a relic in Saint Aldric's cathedral at Harrowmere in the Core's north. The bishop who shut his registers in March speaks for the cathedral: the stone is theirs, and no foreign king's petition reaches it. Varro testifies that the register calls it a pope's relic carried in the field, and that the register can't be trusted. Ferra asks for a loan under seal, not ownership. She tells the court she could have asked the emperor to seize it and chose not to.

On Thursday 15 December the court rules that the relic belongs to the cathedral and stays theirs. It must be lent under seal, with the cathedral's dean present, once the separation is authorised, and whatever remains afterwards goes back to Harrowmere. When she is told through the glass, Oruse says nothing at all. Imani notes that this is the most interesting thing she has said all year.

Chapter 191: The Long Table

Through Celestine's eyes, the year's last news arrives in letters. The Imperial Core has built its own runway, and the first passenger airship lands at Ostrel. The Capital Line's extension breaks ground north from Wayford, running on imperial rail under licence. The Year 25 budget passes.

The Year 25 budget funds the foundation of the Farstrike's first battery, in the eastern hill country of Dunholt with the duke's leave. Celestine asks the question her father will ask: could it reach the Core? Ferra reads her the treaty line and the range, and Celestine writes both down.

On Sunday 25 December, the Long Table. Celestine sits beside Odile. Marius sits beside Arthur, who has beaten him twice at the knights' school and is the first person Marius has ever liked for it. Jerome's own body is almost healed. A letter from Benedikt is read aloud. Through Harrold's eyes, the king watches his son eat and notices he doesn't glow. Later he asks Ferra why he expected him to.

Where the Story Stands After Chapter 191

Thirty-one of the crypt's fifty-three surviving pairs are back together, every one by two yeses. A family has refused to give up one stone, and a court has ordered a cathedral to lend another, the one that holds Ostrella's name, once the separation is authorised. Kaldo knows whose body lies in the sealed room. It has heard the convoy's count, and it has promised in writing never to take what Oruse knows by force.

Twice this year Kaldo's own rules stopped it: a pendant nobody counted as a stone, and a session cut at midnight because the law had run out. Emeric has been rebuilt and has power he hasn't used. The first export machine has walked and has fallen, and the Assembly has voted to sell it with conditions. Most of the year's victories belonged to someone else.
