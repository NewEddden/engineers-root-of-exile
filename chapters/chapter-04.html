Book 3, Batch 2, ch 10-18: Drift

10. The Sale

When: Monday 30 August to Friday 17 September, year 18. Where: Kesrath's tower on the Isle; Vantic's counting house; the Cradle under the covered berth, South Sentinel. POV: Alina, then Vantic, then Tess, then Wren.

On Thursday 2 September at Kesrath's tower, the ministers who asked on 29 August why their guard failed now ask what Randillia wants with the core. Oskel says he doesn't know and doesn't care. The thing killed two of his men at the gate in April, and he wants it off the island one way or another. He's put a soldier's price on it, what a 30-metre carcass's heart is worth to the only people who can keep one from crumbling: about 6,000 Gold Rand for Duvrenne's third. Alina argues for the sale as Waylon's wife and her father's daughter in the same breath. A thing Kaldo can keep and Duvrenne can't is better in Kaldo, and the council can tie Kaldo's hands with a condition. The condition is the council's, written by the minister who asked the hardest question on the 29th: whatever the core goes into never crosses Duvrenne's half of the Isle without Kesrath's leave. The old king signs it.

On Friday 10 September, at Vantic's counting house, Duvrenne's observer signs for Kesrath. Mother Sabbe gives the Islanders' share by her mark through the Claim's set: it's a thing that kills people, so keep it. The crown's share is the crown's, and the purchase sits in the escrow as the Compact's other money does. The king still doesn't say what the core is for, and nobody in the room asks twice. Vantic notices that the condition is about a thing it goes into, not about the core, and that the Duvrenne minister knew exactly what he was writing. She files that.

From Monday 13 to Thursday 16 September, under the covered berth, the shut case is opened in the lab for the last time. Tess reads the stripped core whole once more through the sealed reader stone before it goes near the frame, and finds nothing new. Listen stays hidden in the repeated self-checks, and she doesn't see it either. Emeric writes the rewrite, the gait matched to the frame, and signs it with his runes, and Tess checks every line. It's sealed in its iron housing behind Kaldo's firewalls: signed, written by touch, with a tamper guard, canary notes, a mundane kill switch and sprung brakes. Dagny's canisters are fed, Noll Finch's stones go in the sphere, Ottoline's sprung brakes go on every joint, and Armand fits the small head stone. Ferra's book says hull refit, K Endeavor class.

On Friday 17 September the body lies on its back on the dock. Wren lies in the crown in its head, with Imani's hand on her wrist, Tess in the second seat with the head stone and Sorrel's card on a clipboard. Wren moves one finger, and the four-metre hand closes. Nobody says anything for a while. Then the noise comes: Bram's crew, Dagny swearing happily, Sorrel telling everyone to be quiet so she can write down the time. Wren doesn't stop talking for an hour. Emeric says almost nothing all day.

Endpoint: a four-metre hand closes on a floating dock, and nobody says anything, and then everybody does.

First appearances: the Duvrenne's-half condition; the Bulwark's core in its frame (the machine still unnamed on the page).

11. Drift

When: Saturday 18 to Friday 24 September. Where: a night boat off the cove; the cove beach; the Islanders' stone store; the Claim's condenser shed; a watchtower east of Saltreach; the March marshes; the Saltreach shingle. POV: a night boat, then Mother Sabbe, then Waylon, then Merryn Pellow.

On the nights of Saturday 18 to Monday 20 September the Isle mother births her clutch in the kelp shallows off the cove, offstage, in the dark, where she's helpless and nobody sees her. The pods drift. The Islanders' night boats see faint lights in the water three nights running and call it sea glow, which every fisherman has seen.

At dawn on Tuesday 21 September Mother Sabbe goes down alone with her stick to see what the goats still won't. At the kelp line a grey animal the size of a dog, plated like wet basalt, looks at her with two gold eyes too big for its head. She saw that face come up the shaft on 19 April. She backs away and doesn't run (she's old; it's young), and it doesn't follow. It goes up the beach toward the Islanders' stone store, a timber shed of lamp stones and the cutters' fire stones, not sealed iron. She rings the Claim from the cutters' set, and then she walks round every house on the slope and tells them to keep the children up. Nobody on the Isle dies, because the goats wouldn't go down and she kept the people up.

The same morning Waylon and the Claim's watch find it inside the store, with the door bent in and the stones going dark. The watch carry crown rifles on tracked issue and pikes, and they're padded on inward strength. Rifles at close range chip its plates, and two of the watch at its legs with pikes do the rest. It takes eleven rounds and a pike through the throat fan. A second is under the sand at the store's back wall. Waylon feels its weight through the ground, the way he's felt the ground since the convoy road, and they dig it out and kill it. Nobody is hurt worse than a broken wrist. Both heads go into shut cases in the Claim's condenser shed, shutters down, and the bodies go under the condenser. On Wednesday 22 the K Endeavor, on the Kesrath shore for Duvrenne's oil, flies the cases home.

On Thursday 23 September, at night, at a watchtower east of Saltreach on the Narrow March, a pup comes up the shingle to the tower's lamp-stone store in the dark. Lowen Hennet, the watchman on the store, goes toward the noise with a lantern, thinking it's a dog after the bread, and it kills him. It doesn't eat him. The tower watch has no night glass, which is knights' kit, and shooting into the dark store would hit their own, so they hold the door and wait for light. At dawn on Friday 24 they kill it at the store door with the rifles they hold under their public licence, about fourteen rounds at close range.

Merryn Pellow, a fowler from the March marshes who had her fowling gun bought back in June under the Act, is out at first light with her dog. The dog stops in the reeds by a sluice and won't go on, whining. She goes back for the watch instead of going in, and they kill a second pup in the sluice's culvert. She asks the corporal for a rifle and is told the Act doesn't let her have one. Then who does it let? Nobody on the shore knows yet. The March's boatmen, who have watched pods and dead pups wash up along the tide line, call them drifters, because they come in on the tide like drift.

On Friday afternoon Waylon crosses to Saltreach (his coast, his watchman dead) and sees Lowen Hennet buried. An unhatched pod has been found on the shingle, and by his order it's burned on the beach where it lay, before anyone can think of a price for it.

Endpoint: a fowler without a gun watches a pod burn on the shingle and learns the word the boatmen already have for what's in it.

First appearances: the Isle mother's young (the first Quiet beasts on the page since the guard); Lowen Hennet; Merryn Pellow; the word drifter.

12. Born

When: Wednesday 22 to Monday 27 September. Where: Tess's office; Honor Linden's desk at the records hall; Kaldo's harvest festival; the long hearing room. POV: Tess, then Honor Linden, then Emeric.

On Thursday 23 and Friday 24 September the first head's case is opened in Tess's office, shutters up. Behind the eyes is a grain of written crystal about the size of a lentil. She reads it through a sealed reader stone, never bare, with Armand on the clock and Emeric in the room but not in the stone. It's her first real line of the Quiet's language, two words she can read and one she can't. The first is the same word the guard's core used for home in the recall note she stripped in July: the place the cradle calls from, under the Isle. In front of it is a word the guard's core never had anywhere in it, the sign the core used for its own body, written as if it belonged to something else. She works at it a day and gives it to Emeric plain: this one was born; something gave birth to it, and that something was made under the Isle. The third word she can't read, and she says so.

The mark alone can't tell them whether the cradle under the Isle is printing again or whether something it made in April has bred. Emeric is told first, Ferra and Imani that night, and the review on Monday. Nothing about cradles is said in public. The king says a second animal from the hollow, which is true.

On Wednesday 22 Kaldo News's late sheet prints Waylon's four sentences from the Claim's set: a grey animal the size of a dog with two gold eyes, which the Islanders took for a seal pup. On Friday, at her desk in the records hall, Honor Linden finds her own copy-book page. The site visit's record holds the K Endeavor's log for 19 April, which Keller asked for in July because the hull was a witness ship on the day, and Honor copied it in August: seal, small, into the water off the cove rocks, 09:41. Nobody read the line twice. She spends Saturday's harvest festival not at the festival. She doesn't take it to the Heirs. On Monday morning she hands Keller the page before the sitting, and says it's the first thing she's said to him in four months that wasn't a question about a margin.

At the sitting on Monday 27 September Keller reads the line into the record himself and asks who on the K Endeavor wrote it. The deck watch, a Kaldo seaman, confirms it by the line room's set from the hull. Ward is called and says it under oath, flat. In the gallery on 19 April he heard something small going away toward the cove in the dark and didn't follow it, because he had five people to get out, and he logged nothing. The mill lord asks why not. He forgot it; he had a man dying. The king is called and says, before he's asked, that when the venue's zone cleared at the wedding he felt something large and alive in the sea off the cove for a moment and put it aside, and that he should have said so then. Tess's read goes in as a paper: born, under the Isle, one word unread. Dacey, from the families' bench, asks one thing: are the Islanders safe? The king says no, and that the goats kept them alive on Tuesday, not him.

The crews' man says aloud what the room concludes: three people half-saw it, and the record put it together. Something else left the hollow on 19 April, small. It's had five months in the sea, and these are its young. Nobody says mother until Imani does, from the gallery, because she's the one who knows what a thing that gives birth is called.

Kaldo News prints the sitting whole on the late sheet, Honor's page included, under her name, because it's in the record. The Heirs' angrier half print it too, under beasts of the crown. Honor says nothing either way, again, and this time nobody misreads it.

Endpoint: Imani, from the gallery, says the word the room has been walking round: mother.

Status line: a finger / sea glow / born.

First appearances: the cradle mark read on the page; Honor Linden's voice in the record.

13. Outside the Fence

When: Tuesday 28 September to Saturday 2 October (morning). Where: the review clerk's table; the long room; the long hearing room; Tess's language office. POV: Corbin, then Keller, then Ferra.

On Tuesday 28 September Corbin asks again: now will you write an order? No. The king will ask the Assembly for a law. Corbin drafts it in a week, angrier than in July and faster, because it's his Act and he knows every clause it has to fit.

The same day the king writes to the review through its clerk. He asks for no emergency. He asks the review to say whether its condition covers the beasts, and to put a question to the doors' families by Kettrin's set: will they lift their condition early for a listening post on the shaft's cap? The condition is theirs to lift, not the review's to break.

On Friday 1 October Keller rules, flat. The condition stands whole: nothing touches the hollow, the zone or the dead, and nothing goes through the fence, man or beast hunter. The beasts aren't the review's to protect or to license. Outside the fence and the zone they're an ordinary danger, and the ordinary law and the crown's ordinary officers answer them: the watches, the Claim's watch, the knights, and whatever the Assembly votes. The review records, in one line, that the king asked for no emergency power. Inside the zone a beast is killed only if it's endangering someone in the act. Nobody digs or follows one below ground, and every such case is reported to the review and read to the doors' families. Anyone may listen from outside the fence. The review takes the young's marks, the log and Ward's evidence as papers, and adds a question its finding must answer: what else the hollow holds. The early listening post is the doors' families' to give or refuse, and Keller puts it to them himself, by the line room.

The same Friday Tess's language office opens the Register of Drifters, public, and Kaldo News prints its first page. It takes the March boatmen's word, Drifter, and keeps one line per beast: the date, the place, who killed it, its size, its mark's read, where its parts went, and for the licensed, the rifle's tag. Stage 1 pups get no line name, because every pup is the same animal, so the first line name waits for the first Stage 2.

On Saturday 2 October, in the long room, Saban reports. His man in Kesrath, on the venue team's hire since August, heard about the Saturday market. On Thursday 23 September Oskel's men killed a pup on the tower's beach with their powder guns. A fisherman cut out its claws, which went grey and kept their shape, and its two gold eyes, which he put in a jar of spirit sealed with wax. On Saturday 25 he sold the claws to a carver and the eyes to a buyer nobody on the quay knew, for new-struck imperial gold, and the buyer took passage north that afternoon. Saban's man lost him at the quay. The coin's mint year matches the gold that paid the labour broker who hired the venue team. Saban says what that is and what it isn't: the same purse, or two purses paid from the same mint. He doesn't say Ostrel. Nobody in the room does.

The king says it aloud, as he said in Book 2 he would: a beast part sold by someone who isn't Kaldo. That's the day. Start counting. Ferra enters Saturday 25 September in the heartbeat ledger as day one, under the line where the October heartbeat went out on time. The switch holds. Nobody in Ostrel has said anything, and the pulpits say what they've said since June. The next Sunday Duvrenne's chapels add the young to beasts of the crown, the Narrow March hears it from the pilgrim carts, and the Velmoor and Solreth envoys write home that the beasts keep coming from Randillia's island, which is true. Doran, Kaldo's cardinal, reads Ostrel's circular and doesn't preach it.

Endpoint: Ferra writes a Saturday in September at the top of a new column and the king says, that's the day; start counting.

First appearances: the Register of Drifters; the count's first day.

14. Waist-Deep

When: Saturday 2 October (afternoon) to Monday 4 October. Where: the east bay, South Sentinel; the Cradle sunk to the waist; the berth's edge; the K Endeavor's deck. POV: Wren, then Sorrel, then Ward.

On Saturday afternoon in the east bay at South Sentinel the Cradle sinks to set the depth, and the water comes in over the dock to a giant's waist. Mira and Maeve fly chase in their suits overhead (Mira a flight knight, Maeve slower and plateless) and keep the sky clear, because a test nobody sees from the Band stays secret. Wren is in the crown and Tess is in the second seat on the head stone. It wobbles. It stands. It takes one step in the water.

Sorrel calls the card from the dock's rail, one line at a time, and it stops on her call. She writes down the time. Imani has her hand up the whole time, ready to stop it, and never needs to. Wren cries in the crown and denies it over the speaking tube while everybody can hear her.

Ward watches from the berth's edge with his arms folded, and when it's sitting again he asks Emeric how soon he can have one. Emeric says when the next core is read. Ward says he'll go and get one.

On Monday 4 October Arthur, Mira and Bren go to the Isle on the K Endeavor with a knights' squad and one of the K Amara's dragon rifles, one of the two fired in April, to hunt outside the fence under Keller's ruling. Neris goes with them, at her own asking. Ward stays, as the review's witness, the city's security and the Bulwark's doctrine.

The same day Imani's rule for hunters goes out by Kaldo News and every depot: gloves, no bare hands in the blood, wash. Every hunter and watchman who handles a carcass goes on her register beside the four crews, the Claim's watch and the March's tower watch first. Nobody's flecked yet. Pups have little blood, and it greys in days. Kaldo's own stores are already sealed iron. Kaldo sends iron and two smiths to seal the Islanders' stores, and to Duvrenne's half the iron and the drawings only, because Oskel won't have Kaldo's men in his sheds. Duvrenne's smiths seal their own, slower.

Endpoint: the machine sits in the water again with its pilot denying she cried, and a knight on the berth asks how soon he can have one.

First appearances: the Bulwark standing (named on the page here).

15. Grey Track

When: Monday 4 to Thursday 14 October. Where: the hollow's fence on the Isle; the long room; the line room; the kelp shallows off the cove; the shelf's edge. POV: Neris, then Ferra, then Zale.

On Wednesday 6 October Neris listens outside the hollow's fence, her hands on the fence posts' footings and not inside them, with the Islanders' watch beside her. In April she heard one heart with an echo. Now she hears nothing beating at all. She tells the watch, then Arthur, then the review by the Claim's set, in the same words: nothing's beating, and that isn't the same as nothing's there. The review takes it as a paper.

On Thursday 7 October, in the long room, the question is why the king doesn't go himself. He could fly there in three hours, and he could go into the sea. Ferra says it before he does. A gold-eyed dragon diving alone at night after a beast whose size nobody knows is two stories at once, the rumor's and a funeral's, and the people who live in that water are a set call away. He agrees, and calls Aumere.

On Friday 8 October the doors' families answer Keller by Kettrin's set, with Ferra at the board as she runs it (she's recused from the review, not from her own line room). The answer is no. Their member sits after a winter, as they said, and until then nothing goes into that ground. They give their reason through Kettrin and the house's speaker: the last time a crown put a pipe into that ground for a good reason, fourteen people died, and their dead were there before them. They add that the woman from the Rim may listen from outside, as the sittings said. The king accepts it in a line to the review. It's the third time he's been told no about the hollow, and the first time by the people whose dead are in it. He thinks it's wrong and dangerous, and he obeys.

Ferra enters September and October in Kaldo's own sky by weight, the ninth and tenth monthly entries since the return. No dragon has come to read them.

From Saturday 9 to Thursday 14 October Zale brings six of the queen's watch on the K Discovery's next run from Aumere, at Ilenne's word, and they search the kelp shallows off the cove by day. They find grey dust where the pods lay, pod and casing crumbled. From it runs a grey track grazed off the seabed's shard, a broad ribbon of dead grey crystal going north from the cove along the slope and off the shelf into deep water. Arthur goes down with them on a held breath in the closed loop, with a lime cartridge and no bubbles, to the shelf's edge at about 40 metres, and watches the track go over the edge into the dark. The watch's captain says they don't follow something that size into the dark without a reason to die, and Zale agrees. Nobody sees her.

Amara has turned the relay's glass on the strait every night since 27 September, covertly, and nothing large has surfaced. The king can't tell the review why he's confident she isn't on the surface, because the sight is secret. The mother is somewhere deep, north of the Isle, fed and unseen, and Kaldo doesn't know how many young there are. Emeric says what he said over the guard's carcass, to the same people: we don't know how many, so we build as if it's a lot.

Endpoint: a grey ribbon of dead crystal goes over the edge of the shelf into the dark, and nobody follows it.

Status line: outside the fence / start counting / nothing's beating.

First appearances: the closed loop on the page; the grey track.

16. Bad Harvest

When: Monday 11 to Tuesday 19 October. Where: the crown book's counting house in the capital; the Assembly; Pryor's Gate in the rain; the Saltreach depot and the March shore. POV: Tullio, then Vantic, then the crews' man, then Agatha Vell, then Merryn Pellow.

On Monday 11 October, at the crown book's counting house in the capital, the Brask miller comes in after a short harvest with his wheel-house to pledge. Tullio rescues him into a loan he can't escape, callable at thirty days, and the miller thanks him.

Then Vantic comes in on her trail and asks whose imperial credit a Duvrenne faction would draw on to hire men to raise a storm. Tullio doesn't know that one, and says so. He does know the imperial houses lend to anyone with a war to sell, and he names the two that would. Then, unasked and honestly, he warns her about the one paper: a note under one board is a bad harvest away from ruin, and he lent to a bad harvest this morning. Vantic leaves with two names and a better understanding of her brother than she wanted. Her trail isn't finished.

On Wednesday 13 October the Assembly argues Corbin's beast line for a day. It's a schedule under the Arms Act. Licensed hunters may be issued crown rifles on tracked issue, each issue with a purpose (beasts), a term, a stretch of shore, a round count and the issue tag. Every carcass or part taken in the kingdom is sold only to a crown sealing house at a posted price, which Ferra sets by the pound for plate and claw and by the pair for eyes. The first two houses are the iron shed at the Claim and a new one on the quay at Saltreach, each with shut cases and copper gaskets. Keeping a nest alive, keeping a pod or selling one is a crime under the schedule, punished like keeping a plague. The money is an ordinary budget line, not emergency spending, so Ferra's lost fast-track power doesn't touch it and Ilsa's stay isn't needed.

A member from the northern baronies says from the floor what the pulpits say: the crown's beasts, the crown's guns, the crown's buying house. They make the monsters and sell the guns. The mill lord, who votes against everything, says he'll vote for this, because the alternative is a fisherman selling eyes on a quay, and it's in the record that one already has. It passes, and Kaldo News prints the vote by name.

On Saturday 16 October, in the first autumn rain at Pryor's Gate, the pass pensions are paid at the Gate's door on the quarter day, from the Gate's own tolls, to the widows of pass guards. Agatha Vell's husband, a pass guard, went under a rockfall on the pass eleven years ago. Pryor pays her himself, in the rain, and remembers her husband's name without the clerk's book. He says the crown never paid a widow on this road and he always has. She believes him, because it's true. Pilgrims' carts go by to the Gate behind her, changing coin at his rate. One carter has the Duvrenne chapels' broadsheet on his seat, beasts of the crown. Pryor doesn't look at it. Agatha does.

On Tuesday 19 October Merryn Pellow is issued a crown rifle at the Saltreach depot, with her issue tag, her stretch of marsh and sixty rounds. That afternoon, with her dog stopped in the reeds pointing the way it won't go, she kills a pup on the March shore and sells it to the Saltreach sealing house at the posted price.

Endpoint: a fowler sells her first kill to a crown sealing house at the posted price and signs the Register with the rifle's tag number beside her name, the first name under Hunters.

First appearances: the beast line; the crown sealing houses; Agatha Vell (Movement II's named widow); the first licensed hunter.

17. Tideshell

When: Monday 18 to Saturday 23 October. Where: the long hearing room; Duvrenne's works at the cliff foot; the shard road across the island; Kesrath's tower; the proving floor on South Sentinel. POV: Harrold, then Alina, then Arthur, then Wren.

On Monday 18 October Harrold is called in full at last. He defends the exception as he did in February: lawful, under his son's clause, the oil needed, the date and the reason written. The mill lord asks the question the room has been waiting for since the young came ashore: would he sign it again? On what he knew that day, yes. Then, before anyone can be pleased or angry, he says nobody brought him Bryn's warning, and he didn't ask for it, and that isn't an excuse; it's what he did. He signs nothing else and adds nothing. Dacey writes it down on the families' bench. Emeric, in the gallery, watches his father give the same shape of answer he gave in June, and for the first time hears how it sounds from the outside.

In September one pup went into the tide caves under the cliffs of Duvrenne's half and lived for a month on an unsealed shard cut at the cliff foot, where Duvrenne's cutters work at low tide and Duvrenne's smiths hadn't yet reached. A month on rich shard and the tide committed it. It's now about eight metres long, plated like a shell, at home in water and on rock.

On the night of Wednesday 20 October it comes up out of the tide onto Duvrenne's works at the cliff foot and into the cutters' camp. Oskel's men meet it with powder guns and pikes, and two of them die before the guns are reloaded. Waylon, at the tower, feels it through the ground and goes down with a pike. The Crestless and the knights' squad are at the Claim on Randillia's half, an hour's march across the island by the shard road. Alina gives them leave to cross on her own word, as the crown's daughter present. It's the leave her father refused her convoy in August, and she tells Oskel she'll answer for it. Oskel takes it, because his men are dying.

The night glass's light grids work under starlight and the camp's lamps, but the heat layer is no use with the cutters' still furnaces glowing behind the beast. It reads magic and cancels the squad's casts, so it comes down to steel and inward strength. Arthur fires the dragon rifle from the cliff path through the plates behind its head, four rounds. When it beaches itself on the dry slope, Mira puts her spear into the neck breathing folds. Bren and the squad, padded, go at its legs, and Waylon calls where it's going to move through the ground before it moves. Waylon doesn't change. He's sure enough of himself now to know he could, and he won't be a dragon again on Duvrenne's ground in front of Duvrenne's men unless someone's dying in front of him. Nobody else does. It dies on the slope. Nobody of Kaldo's is killed. Bren's arm is broken.

Burying their two at dawn, Oskel's men call it a tideshell, because it came up out of the tide in a shell, and the Register takes their word. It's the first line name in the Register, named by the men it killed. It's Duvrenne's carcass on Duvrenne's half, and its clock is about two weeks. Alina rules on the beach that it's Duvrenne's, and that Kaldo boxes the core, because Kaldo is the only one who can keep it, in a shut case held under Vantic's escrow at the Claim, as the guard's was. Reading it needs Duvrenne's leave. Arthur cuts the core out with the Claim's crew under her eye, so Kaldo's hands are the only ones on it. Oskel keeps the plates, and Duvrenne's carvers will sell them. That's outside the beast line, which is Randillia's law, and Ferra enters it as the count's second day. It's the first Stage 2 core anyone has held.

On Saturday 23 October Duvrenne's post brings Kesrath's letter. The old king lets his daughter's leave stand, with a rebuke in the same letter for giving it without asking, and one sentence the council didn't write: his guard failed on the road in August and hers held on the slope in October because she asked for help. Alina keeps the letter.

The same Saturday, on the proving floor at South Sentinel, Ottoline's sprung brakes are set, the Cradle is raised, and the Bulwark walks off the dock onto rock for the first time. The first try fails: a knee locks on the brake's first catch, and Sorrel calls it before it falls. The second walks forty paces and stops on Sorrel's call. Ward says nothing until it's sitting again, then asks the same question. Emeric, watching, thinks about a machine he can't take across half an island he doesn't own, and a sea he can't search, and lets Wren have the day. It's still top secret. The Assembly's ledger shows a hull refit, and the review knows nothing it has a right to know.

Endpoint: a giant walks forty paces on rock with its brakes set, and the man who started it stands back and lets her have the day.

First appearances: a Stage 2 Drifter; the first line name (tideshell); the first Stage 2 core; the Bulwark walking.

18. Hollow Night

When: Sunday 31 October, dusk to late. Where: the Grand: the stadium under its glass roof, the History museum's door and its name-it-first wall, the Peoples and Civilizations museum; the lane by the campus. POV: Neris, then Emeric, then Honor Linden.

On Hollow Night the Grand opens in full, free and with no speeches, as the First Room was. The stadium is under its glass roof for the first time, the stage sunk flush for the crowds. The History museum and the Peoples and Civilizations museum open, and the other three wings are still being fitted out behind boards. There are pumpkin lanterns at every door, the first Kaldo-grown pumpkins from the orc valleys' seed, and turnips beside them, because Kaldo's had turnips since year 11.

On the name-it-first wall at History's door are the fourteen names of the Isle's works dead, first, in the order Ferra's record put them. There's nothing else about the Isle: no beast, no shaft, no young, because the review hasn't ruled. A visitor from the capital asks a keeper where the beasts are, and the keeper says after the review, which is the museum's whole answer.

Half the children in the lane come as grey pups with gold paper eyes. Since year 11 the rule has been that every child has to know their creature, so every one of them is asked for the story of what they're dressed as, and none of them can tell it, because nobody knows it yet. The parents mind more than the children do. Doran says, again, that here he'll call it a costume. Ward has a bowl. Runa goes as a slime again, because it was her best night last year. Ferra won't wear anything, and Imani goes as a slime to match Runa. They make the king wear paper ears, and he wears them all night and kisses them both under the stadium's new roof where half of Kaldo can see. The orc interpreters stand a long time at the turnips and the pumpkins side by side. They say the pumpkins are better and the turnips are older, and they don't say which they prefer.
