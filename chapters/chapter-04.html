Book 2, Batch 4 — Named

Chapters 29–35 | 10 June – 3 July, Year 17

Emeric has come up from the floor of Aumere into a stack of held messages. While he kept his word below, Zale was shot with Kaldo's own lost rifle, trapped in a breaking bell, and changed into a sea dragon; Ferra and Imani fired at people for the first time; Ward crossed the markers with the stone to save a stair full of burning men; and at home the Church raided Kaldo, Declan died on the ridge, and Harrold and Corbin both changed into dragons. Ushar sits in the watch's dark, believing Zale drowned, because Ilenne ordered that nobody say otherwise. Taurre's record holds a mountain of evidence against the Rim's house, and all of it is useless while the reason behind the attempts, the custom that frees a widow by killing her husband, has never been named in court.

This batch belongs to the woman who names it. Nobody does it for her. Kaldo assembles the evidence, Ferra lays it out, Taurre seals it, Ressar authenticates the line on the stones that lets her speak, the old families vote, Zale kills, and Ilenne rules. Emeric's part is to stand in the water, say nothing he cannot keep forever, and pay whatever her court charges. Her first acts as queen cost Kaldo: the K Amara and the stone sent out of her water, the Challenger's berth refused, the domes refused, the grove's seed held back.

At home, in the same weeks, Corbin, Tess and Harrold hunt the wrong man for the right reason, with the oil reserve gone and the whole city having watched a dead queen fly. Then come the last good days at Aumere: the bench, the boards, the surf, the lūʻau, the fetch line. Part One of Book 2 ends with the fleet loud and happy, and already being talked about by everyone it has not yet met.

Chapter 29 — Columns

On Saturday 13 June, the morning after the stack, Emeric has not eaten since the stages. Before anything else there is Declan, one of Emeric's first four paid men, killed on the ridge. Emeric writes to Declan's family before noon. Ward, on the set from the Grey Mark, says only that Declan called him on the 8th about running the ridges, with Osric's and the regent's leave already in hand, and that he told him it was right. He asks Ferra for Declan's pay record so the back pay can go home with the letter.

At breakfast Ferra steals Emeric's toast to make him reach for it, and a whole hand comes, unasked: scaled and clawed, lying on the table between the cups. Nobody says anything. Then Imani laughs, first and helplessly, and it breaks the morning open. He eats the toast with his other hand. The change has a cost. He eats for two through the calls home and is hungry again by noon, all from one hand. That afternoon, after assessing him, Imani lifts her ban on testing the change, on her own terms: sessions, logged.

The calls home are rationed through the line room's switchboard. Harrold is flat and complete: the dam, the oil, the dome, Declan. Emeric asks whether he should come home. "No." The regency is sealed, the raid is over, and a king who flies home over a finished fight tells the city its regent cannot hold it. Emeric hears that his father is right, that his father is a dragon, and that he said neither of those things. Corbin is dry: "I held a river. It was mostly standing still." Emeric asks how he is holding up, as he always does, and cannot ask the next thing. Corbin says Doran has been insufferable since. Amara is last. She hears something wrong in his voice and asks. He deflects, and she lets him, which is worse. She tells him what his father said to her on the dome, "You missed one," and that Harrold has been insufferable too. That night Emeric reads every failure of the raid back to a line in his own drawings for the city's Shell: the edge on the wall line, rules that judged only speed, a single store at the dam. He starts redrawing it at the cabin table, with the edge pushed past the walls, eight spires, stores beneath them, a rule on mass and no tailgating. Ferra sits with him and counts the cost when he asks.

Maeve comes in on a delivery boat to see her full brother's gills and tells Zale he looks ridiculous. Then the news from home reaches her: her father and her brother Corbin, both changed on the same night. She asks Emeric whether it hurts. He says the first time, yes.

On Sunday and Monday, Ferra works in the watch's record room with Taurre, building one long sheet in columns: every attempt in time order, its pieces, who holds each one, and each piece's word, seen, measured, remembered, guessed or blank. Lusse has his colour back and speaks, and he leads the watch to Oddrin, who brings his tally and testifies that he raised the rifle from the logged position and sold it to Maudrec for the house. Bram reads the bell's charge casings against the furnace casting books Ferra saved from the fire: cast in the house's own furnace. The cascade rings, the shutter's catch, Keddrin, Maudrec and Pirro's hide all go in. Most lines are seen or measured. Bram's chamber drawing is still marked guessed where the shutter fell, even though everyone now knows exactly how it falls, and Ferra will not change it, because the word records what he knew when he drew it. At the station, Sarrou decides: the house sent her down to taste knowing what was in the dish, and she will testify against it in the water.

On Monday the watch publishes. The rifle was declared lost by the land in Aumere's own hazard log on 6 May, with position and mark, raised by a Rim salvager, bought by the house and fired by a Rim mage. The dead zone was a cascade laid in the house's yard and logged before the land's ship could be heard. The house's line, through Sevrin, that the land's gun shot the queen's daughter's husband and the land's stone made the zone, is dead by noon. The finding speaks of "the shot at the husband" and says nothing about whether he lived. Ferra writes in the margin that the declaration that leaked the rifle is now the published proof, and draws no line between the two.

That night in the hard room, Ferra frames the throne for Ilenne in private, like a contract the court has already signed: him, or the change she has refused twice, or her. Ilenne listens, colourless, and then asks for the sheet. Not a copy. The sheet. She wants to say it herself, in the order Ferra wrote it.

Chapter 30 — Named

On Thursday 18 June, for the first time, the story is told from inside Ilenne. In her own head she is fluent, and her language reaches the reader as plain speech. She dresses in the queen's house with Zale's ship somewhere above her at the gate and no word from it, by her own order. Thalune, whose colour never changes, waits at the door. Ilenne knows exactly what Thalune reported, the bell, the husband, something from the deep, and that every house heard it by nightfall.

The hall is wet, and everyone walks. The accused and the questioned stand below the seat. The guests stand where guests stand: Emeric in the king's coat over the Mark II, no sword, Ferra and Imani beside him in their suits, Armand behind. Her mother sits as regent. Ressar reads the line he has spent the recess on, first to the Keepers, who must agree among themselves that it says what he says. They show their agreement in colour, and from then on it is the court's reading: a custom is named by one it binds.

Ilenne names it. In the water, wet, her voice level, she says the custom: married once until one dies, and the living one may marry again. Then she says aloud, in that hall, for the first time anyone can remember, the killing that frees a widow. Then she lays out the attempts in Ferra's order, each with its word, in her own language. Ressar's colour does not move. Somewhere behind her a deep lord's colour goes slate and then pale. Taurre confirms each piece under the watch's seal. Sarrou testifies standing, against the house she served, and holds her colour, and the hall can see what that costs her. Nobody asks whether the husband lived. To ask a woman in the water whether her husband is dead is the cruellest question a court can put, and she holds her colour so nobody is tempted. Sevrin, for the house, says little, carefully, and nothing he cannot keep.

Now the court can judge what it can name: widow-making is a crime. The old families of the shallows break first, their head showing his colour with Dessaun behind him, for their burned furnace hands and for the drain stones cut to frame them. Two deep houses follow, and the Keepers' voice is for. The Rim's house is against, and one house holds its colour. The finding carries.

Her mother puts the question of the throne. Ilenne answers wet: "You can have my husband, or the change I've refused twice, or me." The hall hears a widow's bitterness. She means every word. The houses show for her, and Yssaure lays the regency down before the court. Ilenne goes up to the seat. She gives up one thing: from now on the great houses may review any heir's dynastic binding, though they get no veto over a sovereign's other acts. Ressar says aloud that the precedent is general, so nobody can claim later it was not heard. Any heir of any house may now name a custom that binds her. It could be turned on Ilenne one day, and she knows that as she does it.

Emeric says nothing in the water all day. Ferra's frame came out of Ilenne's mouth, and nobody in the hall knows it was ever Ferra's, which is how Ferra wanted it. That night Ilenne climbs the stages alone to the gate for the first time since the furnace night and sits on Zale's deck with him. She tells him she is queen. He says he has been told worse things on that deck. Neither of them says the other thing yet.

Chapter 31 — The Seat

On Friday 19 June, Ushar is brought out of the watch's dark and held on the floor below the seat, looking up. He sees Ilenne alone and is glad, and his colour tells the whole court so before he can stop it. Then Zale comes in, two weeks into his change and not yet healed, and walks the whole length of the hall slowly, because in the queen's hall everyone walks. The blue rises up Ilenne's throat in front of every house. Toward Ushar she shows nothing at all, then or ever. Zale sits down beside his queen.

She questions Ushar colourless, the way a thing in the deep is spoken to. He will not confess, and seeing Zale in that seat makes him furious. The only warning anyone gets is that his tail comes in. In that half-second he breaks the watch's bond and boils up at her from the floor. Zale, in his dragon form, draws a line with his claws, and the water becomes a blade that takes Ushar's head. A wave of Zale's hand clears the blood from the water near her. The guards take hold of the body and the head and wait for an order, and the house cannot move. Zale sits back down and waits for her word like the guards. Ilenne gives it, still without colour: the body goes home in its bond. Then her colour returns, and she asks whether anyone of the house will speak to the charges. Sevrin does, and the court gets its confession from the family itself.

Zale stands again, his wound open and blood on his shirt, lifts her out of the throne, sits down in it, and settles her across his lap. The hall erupts in colour. An old lord says, "Your Majesty... that is the queen's throne." "Yes." A pause. "She's sitting on it." Her hand presses on his bad ribs. "Was there something you wished to bring before the crown?" "No, Your Majesty." "Then sit." Not looking at him, she says, "You are bleeding on my throne." "Am I?" "Yes." "Should I move?" "Not until I've finished." Emeric does not move or speak. It is not his court. Watching his brother, he is afraid of Zale and proud of him in the same breath for the first time, and he keeps it off his face. Ferra writes down the time.

That afternoon the house is struck from the court's count and its seats are gone. Its family keeps its lives and nothing else. Its holdings pass to the crown, the certifying bench with them. The pilots keep working for the crown's fee under Taurre's published rate book, with licensed second pilots and loss of bond and office for any falsified route. The furnaces and glassworks run under their own stewards, and the dwarven iron trade is now brokered by the crown. Sevrin names who knew. They lose their places, and the workers who did not know keep theirs. Maudrec and Keddrin are sentenced under Aumere's own law, and Kaldo asks no voice in it. Lusse is released to the watch's bond for his testimony, and Oddrin loses his salvage licence for a season for selling a posted hazard without reporting it. In the house's seized papers are a Rim smith's drawings of the rifle's action and the weights of its rounds, and the smith's own tally shows one tracing missing. Kaldo recovers the rest. Nobody here can make Kaldo's steel or a carbide core, so no working copy will appear soon, but the idea of the gun is now loose past the wall.

On Saturday, from Ferra's side, Kaldo pays its bill. Ward comes down for the first time, braced, in a Mark II, and stands on the floor to answer for his crossing. The deaths at Aumere are judged: the market knife killed by Ilenne's bident, the men at the bell killed by Zale, Pirro killed by Mira, and the arsonist at the hatch, now named in the record for the first time as Davrel, killed by Ferra. The court finds that each was trying to kill at that moment. Ferra and Imani stand on the floor and answer in the water. Ferra says three rounds, and that she would fire them again, and that is true forever. Imani is asked about Keddrin and says she shot him and then kept him alive. A deep lord asks why, and she says that is the other half of what she is for.

Then Ilenne rules Kaldo's terms herself, every one, her first ruling that harms an ally. Ward's crossing is entered as rescue and breach, with Esseth's harm named, and no charge against Ward. The breach is entered under Emeric's name, by his own wet word, and Ward's jaw sets at that and stays set. The K Amara leaves Aumere's water at once, beyond the fetch line, with escort three. The stone may not return without the certifying bench's inspection. Any land hull returns only by permit and escort. Kaldo pays restitution to Nimo's family and the skiff family; Ferra checks what was already paid against the court's figure, finds Kaldo a little short, and pays the difference before the sitting ends. The Challenger's berth is refused, partly because the question of whether she hastened the seal's failure can now never be answered, and a hull the court cannot clear does not berth beside the domes. The old promise charge lapses with the house that made it, unanswered, since nobody in the hall can answer it without the text, which is kept at the elves' ledger-house. Ushar's motion to set the binding aside has no mover, and the binding stands as made. The crown hands the rifle back across the floor as Kaldo's property.

Ferra writes Davrel's name in her book under Nimo's and asks the watch for his family. Nobody on the Rim will say, and the line stays open in her ledger, unpaid. That night Mira, told the whole story by Ferra, is asked what she thought of the water blade. She says she would have needed three rounds. Imani is furious about the reopened wound, Ilenne in private is worse, and Zale submits to both while looking pleased with himself, which does not help.

Chapter 32 — Blue

On Sunday 21 June, at the queen's first audience, a noble formally complains that Zale dishonoured the queen's seat. Ilenne studies him and says, "You seem to have mistaken my permission for his authority," and dismisses him. Zale, beside her, smiles, asks a threatening lord with genuine curiosity what consequences he has in mind, and obeys her the instant she speaks.

Bram brings his dome drawings and pumps for a second grove, and the new queen gives her first sovereign no: nothing installed, and no crews until her own engineers ask, though samples and pressure studies are welcome. Bram takes it badly for an hour. On the quay afterward, Neris tells him why in one line: who fixes your seals when your ship is gone? In open audience, Dauvre, the furnace hand Kaldo carried off the stair, asks why the pilots who took the house's coin in the season of the fire are still piloting. Ilenne answers with what would happen to the channels tomorrow without them. He does not like it. Neither does she.

That afternoon on Zale's deck she tells him what his staying costs her. A land man beside the throne in a new queen's first season lets every great house say the land rules through her husband, and the throne beat made him the king they are afraid to offend. She does not send him away. He chooses to go. His ship is the fleet's pickup pair for people in the water, his crew sails with it, and the farther he is from the hall, the more her court has to deal with her. Then the old anger comes out at last, in daylight and loudly. He is angry that she knew what the binding made him and let him learn it from a court widow. She gave her reasons long ago and said she was wrong, and she does not invent a better excuse now, because there is none. Linnet clears the crew off the deck and they listen from the hold. Nobody wins. It simply ends, and that is how the crew learns they have made up.

On Monday Ilenne is crowned at the floor. Ressar speaks the crowning song he can no longer sing. Envoys from two of the other seas watch, bow correctly, and leave before the feast, and the daughter cities send their own envoys. The feast is held at Sael, on the furnace yard rebuilt enough to stand in, with the burned welcome. As guests, Kaldo is asked what its people do to celebrate. Emeric says "this," and they play rock music through the valve amplifier under the open sky. Emeric plays guitar in his own way, past the Strings. Arthur plays a second electric guitar, badly and earnestly. Mira plays bass, Bren plays drums, Finn plays concertina for the sailors' set before it, and Zale plays squeezebox for the shanties and then sits down because Imani is watching. Imani refuses to play. Ferra keeps the set list and counts them in. The band has no name yet.

The crowd of merfolk becomes a light show, the whole Rim cycling through colours with the music, and Emeric nearly stops playing when he sees it. The bass travels down through the caldera wall, and people from Below who never come up rise to see what it is. In the middle of it, Ilenne goes blue at Zale, and the whole Rim sees. This time Zale sees it too. He asks her what it means, and she tells him. He says he thought it was the cold.

From the stage, watching a city's colour answer a guitar, Emeric privately pictures a ring of seats at home large enough for everyone, around a stage that rises out of the floor, with halls around it to keep what the voyage brings back. That night he draws it on the back of Ferra's set list: the first sketch of the Kaldo Grand Museum, five museums set like the legs of a star around a central stadium. Ferra and Imani watched him at the front of the crowd, doing something just because it was fun, in front of everyone, without counting the cost, and they talk about it later in bed at length.

Chapter 33 — The Unopened Letter

At home, from the morning after the raid on 10 June, the port tank yard is still burning, and large oil fires can burn for days. Someone in the city knew which tanks were full, and the burned streets want the old faith rounded up. Corbin, in the chair, refuses a roundup. He knows what being used by the Church looks like from the inside, and a city that arrests its faithful is doing the pulpits' work for them. He follows one lead instead: Elias Prewitt, a tally clerk of the old faith seen at the tank yard's gate the day before the raid with a works pass. Prewitt bolts through a relief kitchen line, using the frightened crowd for cover, and Osric refuses to take a reckless shot. Corbin catches him on a back stair. Tess sees at once that the pass is forged, since the works passes are hers. On Prewitt is a sealed letter to the tank yard's foreman, never opened. He had heard in a chapel in the capital that the Church would come for the oil "when the fleet is far." He could not take it to the line room, because a man of the old faith telling the regency the Church would attack would be held as its accomplice. So he forged a pass to reach the foreman, was turned back at the gate, and kept the letter. The warning went unread. On the night of the raid his neighbours searched his house and broke his wife Hettie's front window. Corbin goes himself to have it glazed and pays for it, and the street sees him do it. He makes no speech. Kaldo lost two days on the wrong man.

Saban works out the real leak and marks it guessed. The clerk's nephew, inside every condition of Harrold's exception, read the customs house's bonded-stores ledger and sailed home with the Velmoor ship at the end of May. He broke no condition. What he carried home in his head reached the Church through hands Kaldo cannot name. Harrold revokes the exception in writing, with the date and the reason, keeps the Velmoor contract because the cash runs the kingdom programme, and does not apologise to Saban. Saban's objection stands in the minute. The morning after the raid Saban changes the relay heartbeat's pattern, which the listener had learned, so no burst can be timed just after a beat again, and any relay's last gasp now raises the alarm. The reason to change nothing is gone, because the Church knows Kaldo heard it.

Tess draws the rebuild of the dam's first stage the week after, with a new rule across it in her hand: no liquid mana stored at a dam's foot ever again. Her plan puts a harbour-class Answer Tower on the dam's crest, so water mages stacking a reservoir can be answered at the source. With the reserve burned, the fuel for the relief pair, hulls five and six, and for the Great Answer Tower must now come from the Isle's oil. Checking the bore map for that order, Tess finds an old surveyor's line that does not match it. She logs it, and nobody explains it. Wren is kept on the Motion Shop bench and is furious about it.

Harrold buries Declan in Kaldo. Rylan and Osric carry him, Harrold speaks briefly, Doran says the words, and Ward's held message is read at the grave. In its drained cell at the knights' base, the captured juggernaut is read by Amara, the strongest delver at home. The Church sent it blind: closed memories, no route, no names but its handler's. What it does hold is its life, and she brings that out: made in a womb by the Church, raised to orders, with nothing in between. She comes out of it quiet. Harrold refuses its execution a second time, to a steward who asks, and keeps it alive, fed, with a man assigned to sit with it. Nobody knows what it is for yet.

The pulpits have changed their story. They no longer preach about the king who left for the cursed sea. They preach necromancy: a dead queen flew over Kaldo and brought down a ship of the faith. Saban's rule holds, never deny, never explain. Amara walks to the market the next morning in daylight and buys bread, and to anyone who asks she says, "People tell me I look like her." Kaldo's own streets split between pride and fear. At the regency's sitting in the capital, Lord Cassel's report has arrived ahead of him, and a lord asks Harrold to his face. Harrold says, "Ask the king when he's home." Then he seals the relief pair's orders on Ferra's figures from Aumere: a rendezvous off the Sabir coast between 1 and 10 October, with the orc river mouth on 20 October as the fallback.

From 23 to 25 June, Corbin finds that Cinderhale's mill lords have raised a forty-man "watch against riot" in new Dunholt cloth and iron. Ilsa tells him who holds Cinderhale's mortgages: Tullio's book. "That's how they paid for me," Corbin says. The levy right is lawful, and he cannot touch them. He writes it up for the council call Ferra's office will book.

Chapter 34 — No Mark

From 26 June to 1 July, the certifying bench, now under the crown, finally tests Kaldo's steel against dwarven iron and publishes the figures. There is no mark, because the mark is for Aumere's own makers. The crown now buys iron, so the figures matter to it, and the crown's iron buyer reads them twice.

Bram and Dagny do pressure studies with the divers of Keeper Lirrae, who keeps the access ledger for the grove: a district map of thinning, repair and actual hours. Jorra, a net-maker whose district terrace has closed, stands in her district's line with her son beside her instead of at school. Emeric proposes pumping thick water up to the Rim. Lirrae proves him wrong in a minute with a jar on a cold stone and a warm one: dissolved mana leaves warm, moving water, so it has to be grown where it is breathed. He says so. The deep houses offer to fund the second grove in exchange for controlling it. Ilenne refuses and gives it its own governing body, with the Keepers publishing access records under the crown's seal and a duplicate held by the Rim-born councils. Kaldo contributes pressure studies, Ferra's ledger method and samples.

The glassmaker who answered Bram's questions and stopped speaking to Ferra and Imani after the furnace night declines a berth. On Kaldo's last morning, though, he lets Bram watch him key a pane of held glass from start to finish, and explains none of it. Bram asks to buy one of Oddrin's animals. Oddrin tells him it is a deep thing made bigger and stranger by the caldera's water and would sicken in Kaldo's thin water within a month. Bram settles for a drawing. The recovered rifle is back in service on Bram's bench with the salt out of it, and its eight rounds are condemned. The Rim's smiths, freed of the house's monopoly, ask Kaldo for coal so they can smelt their own ore. Emeric does not answer yet. He will not sell what he does not understand.

On the salvage quay Bram turns over a heap of the black lumps the Rim uses as ballast and path gravel, scratches one, and goes quiet. Emeric knows what such deep-floor nodules may hold, manganese, nickel, copper and cobalt, and what nickel would mean for an engine he has not built yet. He asks what the dwarves pay for them, and the answer is almost nothing. A scratch only suggests ore, and only an assay proves it. He does not sit on it. That same day he tells the host, says no price should be set until Kaldo has met the dwarves, and has Bram say nothing further in the meantime.

Neris signs aboard on the K Endeavor's deck in her own name, with her terms read aloud by Ferra first so nobody can say later they were not heard. Her beads stay hers, nothing is copied without her consent and her share, she may leave at any port, and she writes to Tess by relay. The watch records it, and Tess's first relay message to her arrives that same evening. At the bench, Neris is shown a knight's diary drawing of herself, fixes the colour with his own pencils, and asks what will happen to the page. Her terms later become the museum's rules.

Imani handles the trade settlement: the cane strain and pearl meal, bought with Kaldo iron at the crown's market, sea silk for Abloh, and ash cement. She enforces quarantine on every crate. Nothing grown comes aboard without her clearance, and the pearl meal is heat-treated and stored sealed. Taurre appends to Ferra's ledger the watch's adverse finding on the crossing and on Nimo, publishable without Kaldo's certification, the first host entry in her book. Ferra reads it and does not argue.

When the iron contract is signed in a hard room under the Rim, Emeric asks for one more thing before the queen rises: a runway, a long flat paved road on the ring for machines that fly on wings, which Kaldo does not have yet and will. Imani watches Ilenne grasp it faster than her court does, since her people think in currents and lanes, and she calls it a fetch line in the air. Her reasons for agreeing are her own. The iron already needs a quay, so Kaldo will be on the Rim whatever she decides. Fliers from the dry lands will come sooner or later, and whoever owns the ground they land on sets the terms. And Aumere would become the crossroads between Randillia and every shore beyond. She also hesitates aloud, once and colourless: the last time dry people ruled the air over the sea, their dead zones drowned her people's floating cities. Nobody answers. Emeric names the catch himself: there will be no regular flights past the wall until the dragons' old promise is answered, so the field is built for the day it is. Ferra drafts the terms. The ground stays Aumere's under Aumere's law and Kaldo leases it. Every landing needs the crown's permit. The watch keeps it and a trained, paid Rim crew maintains it. Nothing armed is stored there, and the queen can close it. The Rim's crafters will build it on Bram's drawings while the fleet sails on, paid in iron and coin: lava rock and black sand behind a sea wall of ash cement, which sets in seawater and grows stronger, paved in the same cement, about three kilometres planned with the first half built first, the way Earth once built runways on atolls. Beside it go the iron quay and the Rim's ground relay. Ilenne's last word on it: "Ask me what I want in return when you come back." Ferra writes the line down exactly.

On the Rim's quay, with a plank on two trestles and Ferra and Imani on a crate with nothing to do but watch, Emeric shapes a surfboard with his scaled hands up to the forearm, long curls of wood falling away, his eyes called up to read the grain, and a wing spread as an awning when the sun gets bad. Imani calls it a cleaver doing a scalpel's job. Ferra, in his own words, reminds him to use only what the job needs. He calls it exposure therapy. Imani says that is not what it means. He says it is exactly what it means, and she does not argue, because it is. A child in the water asks Zale whether all the dry king's people can do that. "No, just the one, and he's showing off." The wing costs more than the hands, and before the second board he sits down on the crate between the women and lets them hold the plank. Imani logs it. The next day, at the reef station, he uses a spokeshave and block plane and no claws, and says so. The children are disappointed, and one child's rail turns out better than his, which he also says. A cutter's wife brings holdfast, thinned and brushed on as a board coat, and it beats what he had at home. Bram asks for a barrel. The tools go to the reef apprentices in the gift ledger.

On the last morning Ressar comes up to the Rim, which he never does, carrying a rubbing of the burial song Armand misread. It goes into the collection on Ressar's terms: as a song, labelled in words he dictates, with his name as the Keeper who allowed it, never to be read aloud as a record. Armand writes the label as dictated. Ferra enters it in the gift ledger at no value, because Ressar will not have one put on it.

Chapter 35 — The Fetch Line

On Thursday 2 July, the surf day, Ferra's shoulders are still pink from standing under the edge of the wing. Imani paints zinc oxide on her nose, cheeks and shoulders, and Ferra is furious about the face part. The merfolk change colour at will in hard sun and find it hilarious that humans have to paint it on from a pot. The apprentices ask Ferra to do another colour, and the only one she can manage is red, which she then does. Arthur's group paint stripes to match on purpose, Maeve with them, she and Mira matching. Imani puts some on Emeric's nose for fairness.

The break is on the outer face of the ring, inside the markers by permit, with a watch skiff and a pilot standing by. Emeric is good at it. Arthur accuses him of using the stones and falls off the next wave, which settles the matter. Ferra stands for about two seconds with Emeric holding the board, and counts it. Zale, cleared by Imani that morning only to lie on a board, stands on his third wave and is smug for one ride. Ilenne body-rides a wave too steep for any board and surfaces beside him, and he falls off watching her. Imani calls him in.

Before the feast, in their cabin, Ferra and Imani hold Emeric off because they are covered in salt. He says he does not mind salt in his food. Imani laughs first, Ferra hits him and does not let go of his arm, and the fresh-water rinse turns into a long, playful, heated afternoon together. They are late to the lūʻau, and Arthur says so.

Kaldo hosts the lūʻau on the black-sand cove. An imu is dug in the sand, with vent rock brought hot from the furnace yard by sledge and two fire stones keeping it. There is no pig, which is mourned aloud, but there is Ilenne's fish in kelp and salt pork soaked and wrapped so there is some pig after all. Emeric catches fish in his partial form, and Ilenne sends them back as one of the heavy kinds nobody eats, while a reef apprentice of about ten wades in with two of the right ones. Ferra says, "Again?" Imani, writing: "Session two." The forearm leaves him cold to the bone by the pit for an hour, so Imani wraps him in the cook's blanket and writes: one forearm, the hour after. There is poke, which the merfolk take to mean he has been eating at their tables. Imani keeps her dose register all night, and the knights grumble. Some of the burned furnace hands come. Dauvre comes, still refusing to choose, and eats anyway. Esseth does not come. Ward sits with Taurre and the furnace hands and says almost nothing, which the Rim takes as respect. Neris is there, newly signed. Ilenne comes as queen for the first hour and stays as herself. Armand keeps well back from the imu and explains to the reef apprentice what a museum is. Ilenne gives Emeric advice, queen to ally: "Go to the orcs' door before anyone else's. They'll count you coldly. Everyone listens to who the orcs count."

Before she leaves, Emeric gives her the first of the twenty gift sets from the K Discovery's locker, in front of her people, as a gift and not a term. It is a sealed radiotelephone in a brass case with a gasket all round, a water stone in the lid set to push so that any weeping seam weeps outward, a warm stone on the valves, a call lamp and a charging crank. Radio does not pass seawater, so its mast rides on a glass float on a line that she lets up from the water and winds in afterward. Ferra reads the card in the lid aloud before Ilenne takes it: it rings the fleet and its ships, not Kaldo's home board; the fleet's radio room hears what it receives; anyone with a set and patience can hear the rest; and she may throw it in the sea whenever she likes. Ilenne asks who else has one. "Nobody yet. You're the first of twenty." She asks who the other nineteen are for. Emeric says he has not met them. Zale, watching her turn it over, says she will use it to tell him off. "Yes," says Ilenne. After the feast, Abloh's first sea-silk pieces are delivered to the cabin for Ferra and Imani, and Emeric finds out later than he would like.

Later, walking barefoot on the black sand with the pit still warm, Emeric tells Ferra and Imani alone about Australia and Hawaii, quietly, and says this place looks like the second. Ferra asks whether his other world had waves like these. "Smaller. Same water, though." He tries to pull them back into the water, and Ferra says she has been thrown by the sea enough for one lifetime. From the cove they can see Zale and Ilenne in the water off the reef, but not what they are saying.

Off the reef in the dark, gills working and stitches pulling, Zale hears Ilenne ask for the sharing, the stone rite between them. He gives his first wet promise since the binding: "When I come back." It binds him to return to her, and he knows as he says it that it is true forever. Then she takes him down the stages to the queen's house. She is queen now, so the watcher's post at an heir's door is finished, and her first act is to thank Thalune and send her home. Thalune's colour changes for the first time. In the bell, on solid ground, with her hand flat over his stitches more than once and her whole body blue in the dark, they consummate the marriage. He does not sleep on the floor. Neither of them mentions it the next morning, which is how his crew knows. On deck, Zale tells Emeric one line: "I said it wet." Emeric asks what, and Zale tells him. Emeric does not make a joke, which Zale notices. He also does not ask where Zale slept, which Zale notices more.

On Friday 3 July the fleet lifts and flies low out through the fetch line, Zale's ship on the water beneath it with Zale on deck, and a queen on the breakwater who does not wave, because queens do not, and goes blue anyway. Before the breakwater is out of sight the set on Zale's deck rings, the gift set's first call, and Linnet passes him the handset while the whole crew pretends not to listen. The K Amara and escort three rejoin outside the fetch line. Ferra closes Aumere in her ledger: what came, what was spent and promised, the host's entry, and the breach under Emeric's name. Ahead of them, the charge about the old promise went south with one of the winged ones in May, and nobody aboard can answer it.

Far away, on a dragons' ground, Sohra stands before her elders and tells what she brought home: a thief of the Band with the old face, who beat her and then stopped, and a gun that cannot be felt, only seen when it flashes. The elders sit on it. One of them, older than Sohra, gold-eyed, kin to a dragon the old war killed, listens to the end and leaves before they decide.

Where the Story Stands After Chapter 35

Part One of Book 2 is complete. Ilenne named the custom, made widow-making a crime, and became Aumere's first reigning queen, giving the great houses review of dynastic bindings in exchange. Ushar died in the hall when he attacked her and Zale's water blade took his head, and the Rim's house has been struck from the court, its holdings passed to the crown. Zale chose to sail on with the fleet, consort of Aumere with no office, his wound healing, bound by a wet promise to come back. Ilenne stays to rule, with the first of Kaldo's twenty gift sets.

Kaldo leaves having paid. The K Amara is barred from Aumere's water, the stone cannot return without inspection, every land hull must return by permit and escort, the Challenger's berth and the grove's seed have been refused, and the breach is entered under Emeric's name. Ferra and Imani have answered for their shots in the water. Davrel's family remains unfound, an open line in Ferra's ledger. The idea of the dragon rifle is loose past the wall through a missing tracing. The old promise charge lapsed unanswered and has gone south ahead of them; its text is kept at the elves' ledger-house. In return, Aumere has granted a runway on the ring, building while the fleet sails on, with Ilenne's price to be named on the way home. Neris has joined the fleet on her own terms. Coal for the Rim's smiths and the deep-floor nodules wait on the dwarves.

At home, Kaldo caught the wrong man and learned the right lesson: Prewitt's warning went unread, the clerk's nephew was the real leak, and the exception is revoked. The pulpits now preach necromancy, Lord Cassel has carried the sight of Amara flying to the capital, and Harrold has answered: ask the king when he is home. The juggernaut lives in its cell, the relief pair's orders are sealed, the fuel must come from the Isle's oil, Tess has found a survey line that does not match, and Corbin has learned that Cinderhale's mill lords have armed themselves with money from Tullio's book. Emeric can now call his dragon hand and forearm at a cost Imani logs session by session, and one of Sohra's elders has already left to find him.

This batch hands power to the person whose fight it was. Kaldo leaves Aumere with an allied queen, a court that still fears the land, and a bill it paid in full, and it sails on toward the orcs, where it will be counted coldly.
