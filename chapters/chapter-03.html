Book 3, Batch 1, ch 1-9: Middle Ground

1. The Second Week

When: Monday 21 to Saturday 26 June, year 18. Where: the records hall's long hearing room; the review clerk's table; the records hall steps; the Heirs' wall by the Works Town. POV: Dacey, then Emeric, then Saban.

On Monday 21 June, in the records hall's long hearing room, Keller gives the families' bench the morning. Most of the families ask about money and burial. Dacey asks the king one thing: did he read her brother's first warning when it was written? He answers it straight. He didn't. It was written in early November, while he was at the Guano Isles, and the works office filed it as not enough to halt the work under the exception. He heard it named in February, in one engineer's finding at the Assembly, didn't ask to see it, and the survey was put off. He read it for the first time on 6 May. He doesn't add that he was a thousand miles away in November. The mill lord adds it for him, sharply, as a question, and the king answers that it doesn't change February. The mill lord then asks whether he would have stopped the shaft in February if he'd read the warning then. The king says he doesn't know. That goes in the record too, and it's the line the mill lord underlines.

On Wednesday 23 June the king files a request in writing through the review's clerk, as any party must: leave to set a listening post on the shaft's cap. It would be a sealed iron pipe into the fill with a stone on it and Neris's ear, with nothing dug and nothing opened. The Ostrel letter, under seal, says there's more than one room, and Neris heard an echo in April. He wants to know if anything else down there is beating.

On Friday 25 June Keller refuses it flat. A pipe into the fill touches the hollow, and the review agreed in June to the orc families' condition that nothing touches the hollow, the zone or their dead until their member sits. It can't keep that promise and break it for the party it's examining. The king may renew the request when their member sits. Keller adds one line: the Islanders' watch on the fence may report anything they hear, and the review will receive it.

The king puts his reasons in the record in full, without the sealed letter's words, which only the chair has read. He says he accepts the ruling, and leaves. He thinks it's wrong and dangerous, and he obeys it. On the steps Ferra asks what he'll do. He says he's going to ask the Islanders to listen, and to pay them for it. Mother Sabbe says they'd have done it for nothing, and he can pay them anyway. It's the first ruling against him. It's small, and it costs him something he thinks matters.

On Saturday 26 June a broadsheet goes up on the Heirs' wall by the Works Town. It has one word a foot high, MINE, then his answer to Keller word for word from the public record, then beasts of the crown, then a gold eye drawn badly. All of it is true and lawful, and the quotation is exact. Because the fair copy is Honor Linden's, the Heirs' angrier half assume she gave it to them. She didn't. The sittings are public and the printer had a man in the seats. She says nothing either way, and her silence reads as a yes to both sides. Saban notes it. The king reads the broadsheet and tells Saban to leave it up. His own word against him is the price of having said it, and tearing it down would make it a different word.

Endpoint: a king reads his own word a foot high on a wall and tells his spymaster to leave it up.

First appearances: Book 3; the families' bench questioning; the listening post refused; the broadsheet.

2. New Names

When: Wednesday 30 June to Monday 5 July. Where: the crown stores at the Works Town; Stannary's mine office; Sayer's weigh-house roll (by report); the southern-circuit court; the long room. POV: Corbin, then Treloar, then Corbin.

On Wednesday 30 June every private command in the kingdom dissolves under the Arms Act. The surrendered guns are in crown stores and the buyback has paid. On Thursday 1 July the men aren't gone. Stannary's are mine wardens, Fairhythe's are quay porters on Sayer's weigh-house roll, the Cocan March's are pass guards on the pilgrim road, and Tullio's crown book pays forty watchmen's pensions to men who aren't old. None of them carry guns, because the Act holds. They have clubs, lanterns and numbers.

Hiring porters, wardens and guards is lawful. A command under arms isn't, but a case needs proof of command and arms, so every case has to be built by hand. The Inspectorate opens 31, and they crawl.

At Stannary's mine office Treloar is told the stope timbers need replacing. The same afternoon he signs for the wardens' coats. The timbers wait.

On Monday 5 July Tullio's counsel asks the court to stay the Act's emergency schedule. He argues that it rests on the same kind of emergency prerogative now under review, and that a crown whose clause is in the dock shouldn't run its own emergencies meanwhile. Keller can't hear it, because he chairs the review, so the southern-circuit judge does. He refuses the stay: the Act was passed by the Assembly, not ordered by the crown. The argument is printed and read anyway, and it's a good argument.

The same day, in the long room, Corbin asks the king for a decree, a crown order naming the four houses' new companies as commands in all but name. The king refuses. He's sitting in a witness chair over one emergency clause and won't sign another the same summer. If it's a command, the Inspectorate can prove it, and if it can't, it isn't one yet. Corbin tells him that's easy to say for a man with a dragon in him and harder for the porter on Sayer's quay. The king doesn't argue with that. Corbin goes back to building cases, angry and good at it.

Endpoint: the Chancellor goes back to building cases by hand, angry and good at it, because the king won't write the order that would make it quick.

First appearances: the four houses' new names (wardens, quay porters, pass guards, watchmen's pensions).

3. One False Line

When: Saturday 3 to Saturday 10 July. Where: Cinderhale: the League's room, the ex-watchmen's yard; the Assembly's states committee (by its minute); the League wall. POV: Kezia, then Maudie.

From Saturday 3 to Thursday 8 July, in Cinderhale, the ex-watchmen trace guard payments to Tullio-linked books. One chit names a poor guard as paid when he wasn't, and it's the only sample in the steward's own hand. Corrick decides alone not to publish it. He's right about the man and wrong about the procedure. One of the ex-watchmen leaves for a season. Kezia tells Corrick he was right about the man, and that the trouble is he didn't let anyone else be right with him. The Inspectorate case slows by months. It's one of the 31, which is how the two threads touch without the king in either.

On Saturday 10 July Maudie's petition for elected representation on the south road is deferred by the Assembly's states committee until the state map and the Charter amendment are ready. She publishes the exact minute, not an embellished charge, and pins it to the League wall. (The March's offer and Corrick's pinning belong to the following year and stay there.)

Nobody sends for the king, and the chapter doesn't cut to him. The institutions are working, slowly, and the cost of slow lands on people who aren't in the long room.

Endpoint: a minute is pinned to a wall exactly as written, and the people reading it understand that lawful delay is not neutral.

Status line: refused / mine / new names.

First appearances: none new.

4. Recall and Halt

When: Monday 12 to Friday 16 July. Where: the review clerk's table; Tess's office; Imani's ward; Vantic's counting house; the Cradle, South Sentinel. POV: Tess, then Imani, then Emeric.

The guard's core belongs to the Isle Compact, not to Kaldo, so reading it needs its three owners. On Monday 12 July Mother Sabbe signs in Kaldo, Oskel gives his leave by Waylon's set, and Duvrenne's observer witnesses and carries the request to Kesrath. The leave is to read the core and make it safe, nothing else, and it's given on that wording.

On Tuesday 13 and Wednesday 14 July, in Tess's office, Tess and Emeric take turns reading it through a sealed reader stone, never bare, with Armand watching the clock. They find two back doors, recall (walk home when the cradle calls) and halt (the cradle can stop it), and strip both, Tess checking every line he writes. The third, listen, is hidden in the repeated self-checks, and they don't find it. Tess says the core's language isn't like any delving she knows.

On Wednesday 14 July, on her ward, Imani goes over her register of the crews who bled the carcass under the condenser, about three months in. Their old injuries are easing, they recover quickly, their density is up on Armand-style readings, and the four who worked closest have grey-violet flecks under their nails. She names the cause: the beast's blood, on skin and breathed off the carcass, doing a little of what an eaten stone does, and doing it with the wrong pattern. She stops the work at once (the carcass is dust by July anyway, and the condenser's cells are sealed) and writes it to the review as evidence, because the crews are the review's business. Nobody outside Kaldo has the warning yet, and nobody has the drug. There's no blood to sell, because the guard's is dust. What she doesn't know is that the flecks are the first of the blood road.

On Thursday 15 July the king asks the Compact, through Vantic's escrow, to sell Kaldo the core outright, at a price the other two owners set. He doesn't say what it's for. Mother Sabbe says yes the same day: it's a thing that kills people, so keep it. Duvrenne's answer is slow, with Kesrath's court busy and the wedding coming.

On Friday 16 July, under the covered berth at South Sentinel, Bram's first castings come down the barge tunnel from the Sentinel Forge and are laid on the Cradle: the frame of a 30-metre body with nothing in its chest. Only the sworn staff know, and Ferra books it as a hull refit. The core will go in only after the sale. Wren is told, and is insufferable about it, happily.

Endpoint: Wren stands on a floating dock beside a 30-metre frame with an empty chest and won't stop talking.

First appearances: the guard's core read; the back doors named on the page; the Bulwark's frame (unnamed on the page yet).

5. The Site Visit

When: Saturday 17 to Friday 23 July. Where: the K Endeavor; the Isle: the fenced shaft, the hollow's fence, the condenser's dust, the gallery, the zone line; Kesrath's tower; the convoy road; Ferra's office. POV: Emeric, then Waylon, then Ferra.

On Saturday 17 July the review travels to the Isle on the K Endeavor's July run: Keller, the three delegates, Mother Sabbe going home, Duvrenne's observer, the families' bench by choice (Dacey goes), and the clerk with Honor Linden and the fair copy. The king comes as a witness on the review's summons, not as the island's host, and Kesrath's leave is used for Duvrenne's half.

From Monday 19 to Wednesday 21 July he walks it with them: the filled shaft under its fence, the hollow's fence nobody opens, the grey dust under the condenser that was the guard, the gallery where Bryn died, and the buried city's landward edge where the zone line held. Mother Sabbe hosts on her own ground. Waylon walks the review through the filling and Oskel through the night of the charges, both under oath. The king answers what he's asked and offers nothing else.

On Wednesday 21 July Waylon shows the king the road Alina's convoy will take from Kesrath's tower to the pitch lake, across Duvrenne's half. It's desert ground. Duvrenne's king has already refused a Randillian escort on his own half, by letter, before the king could offer one. Emeric respects it and says so to Waylon, who agrees with the refusal and says he'll ride with her guard himself, as a gesture to her people. Emeric doesn't argue. On Thursday 22 July, the last night of the site visit, Alina and Waylon fix the date at Kesrath's tower: Saturday 28 August.

The same Thursday the king asks the review to let the crown's share of the older bores run again. That means not the shaft and nothing near the hollow. Kaldo's tanks are low, the fleet runs on diesel, and his own pause has cost the city a winter's fuel stock. The review refuses until its finding. The crown's part of the works is what it's deciding, and it won't let one part run while it decides the whole. He dislikes this more than the listening post, because this one costs every house in Kaldo. He obeys.

Back in Kaldo on Friday 23 July, Ferra buys Duvrenne's oil from the Kesrath shore's houses at their price.

Endpoint: Ferra signs for Duvrenne's oil at Duvrenne's price and says, to nobody, that's Alina's.

First appearances: the review on the Isle; Kesrath's leave used.

6. The Plan

When: Monday 2 to Thursday 26 August. Where: the gill shop and the bay; the long room; Fairhythe's quay; Saint Edwy's and the hold of a hull; the line room. POV: Emeric, then Ward, then Ruth Penhale, then Wren.

In early August the review's refusal sends Emeric back to the one thing under the Isle he can plan for, the day somebody has to go down. A dead zone takes the masks' pumps and the casters' air, but a held breath doesn't care. In the bath he presses half a litre of air beside his core, scalds himself a little doing it too fast, and does it slower. Arthur learns it first after him, because he has a core and the Crestless are the ones who'll go into dead ground at the wedding. In two weeks Arthur holds about half a litre at 200 bar. Imani's rule is to vent before any fight you can see coming. Arthur asks what a fight you can't see coming is. She says that's the one where it helps.

On Monday 16 August, in the long room, Ward lays out the wedding plan whole. The venue is the pitch lake. Each house walks in from its own end, Verandel's Claim and Kesrath's tower, and they meet in the middle, with Doran and a Duvrenne cleric officiating together. Ostrel sends no legate, and people notice. Envoys come from Velmoor, Solreth and the Imperial Core. Zale and Ilenne fly in from Aumere on the K Discovery with twelve of the queen's watch, who go into the water at the cove and hold the shore at dusk. Abloh dresses the couple.

The security plan has ships aloft and the sky watch, the dragon rifle on the lead hull, and mana inducers at the venue and on every hull against a forced zone. There are card checks keyed to signatures, yellow cards for foreign staff, delvers at the gates and Ward's own eye for juggernaut tells. Imani's people are in the kitchens, nothing that burns goes near the pitch, the merfolk hold the shore, and every hull can set down and float. Amara is on the relay sight, sweeping the approaches covertly. Ward names the gap aloud: the convoy on Duvrenne's half. The hedge is Amara sweeping the route between venue checks (Emeric can't tell Duvrenne why he's confident, because the sight is secret) and a quick-reaction team sitting on a hull with its engines warm.

On Saturday 14 August, on Fairhythe's quay, Sayer settles a feud fairly, and Ruth Penhale leaves sure she's had justice. Then the weigh-house fee lands on both families. Sayer is Book 3's second house head; Pryor and Tullio come later.

Saint Edwy's whole orchestra and its composer go to the Isle by hull. The organ stays home, and the composer grumbles about rearranging without it. The first tuning goes sour in the hold in the heat, and the first cello thinks of putting pans of water under the strings and the built piano. They'll play from a tent on an open stage on the lakeshore, far enough from the pitch that nothing near it burns. Wren rehearses the processional.

Ferra's August column gets one line: Kaldo's own sky entered by weight again, and no dragon has come to read it.

Endpoint: the orchestra tunes in a tent with pans of water under the cellos, and it holds.

Status line: read / not for sale yet / that's Alina's.

First appearances: the held breath on the page; Ruth Penhale (Movement II's named widow).

7. Processional

When: Saturday 28 August, morning to mid-afternoon. Where: the pitch lake: the staff gate, the stands, the orchestra's stage, the burning stand; the lead hull. POV: Emeric, then Arthur, then Emeric.

An hour before the processional, Ward flags a man at the staff gate who has a juggernaut's tells: how he stands, how he doesn't sweat. He's taken quietly, and nobody says a word about the Church. He was one of six in the guest staff.

Wren sings the processional, and the two houses walk in from their ends. The other five force stones for a dead zone under the stands. The inducers fire and the zone refills from liquid mana in about a minute, and in its dark the Crestless take the team with the night glass and the monoculars. A few staff and guards die, unnamed.

Then a charge goes off in an empty stand near the orchestra. Nobody was sitting in it, because the plan had put the guests elsewhere. The composer's copyist has gone back for the parts, and Arthur goes into the smoke after her. He breathes from the held breath, open, exhaling into the smoke and inhaling from his stomach, and brings her out. It's the held breath's first use on the page. The broken chair and the missing stand come from here.

Amara, watching the venue as the plan says, flags the convoy road within minutes of a storm rising there: there's no wind for that. The quick-reaction team launches. Emeric stays at his post, because the venue holds the most people, the guests, the orchestra and Wren, and that's the right post for the king with the most force. He hates it.

When the venue's zone clears, his sense comes back all at once, and for a moment he feels something in the sea off the cove, faint, alive and large. Then it's gone under everything else. He puts it aside in the wreckage. No beast appears on the page.

Endpoint: the zone clears, and for a moment the king feels something in the sea off the cove, and then it's gone and he puts it aside.

First appearances: the wedding's attack; the held breath used.

8. The Convoy Road

When: Saturday 28 August, the same minutes. Where: the convoy road across Duvrenne's half; the dunes. POV: Alina, then Waylon, then Emeric.

Alina's convoy crosses her own ground with her own guard, on her father's order. Waylon rides with her guard in Duvrenne's colours over his own, as a gesture to her people. Mathilde Aurenne, Alina's lady since they were girls, rides beside her and complains about the dust. The air is still, and then there's a wall of sand. The road opens under the lead wagons, and Duvrenne's escorts go into it in their armour. Mathilde goes under reaching back for Alina's hand. Alina watches her own guard fail on her own ground.

Waylon feels the field holding the storm up, the way Emeric once told him a field feels, and overpowers it. He turns their own magic back on them and senses them through the sand. Then he changes for the first time, into a sand dragon, moves through the dune like a sandworm, and buries them. It's over before the quick-reaction team lands. He can't find Mathilde under the sand in time. He finds her after.

Emeric arrives with the quick-reaction team's second lift, after the venue is secure, and finds it finished. His plan's warning and response were fast, but Waylon solved it himself. Emeric looks at his brother for a long time and says nothing that would make it about himself.

Endpoint: the king lands on a road that's already quiet and looks at a sand dragon that's his brother.

First appearances: Waylon's dragon form; Mathilde Aurenne (named and lost the same chapter).

9. Sand in His Scales

When: Saturday 28 August, evening, to Monday 30 August. Where: the pitch lake at dusk; the shore; Kesrath's tower; the cove slope; the records hall (one line). POV: Ferra, then Emeric, then Alina, then Mother Sabbe.

That evening at the lake Alina marries Waylon as she'd planned to, because she won't let the men who killed Mathilde have the day. He marries her as a dragon, with sand still falling out of his scales, because he can't change back yet. A first full change empties the changer. Emeric lay on the Isle's field about seven and a half hours unable to be a man, and Waylon is empty the same way. He's a man again by the small hours. The orchestra plays a stand short with a broken chair, and Wren sings again. It's bubbly, a bit ridiculous and earned. The envoys of Velmoor, Solreth and the Core sign as witnesses. Doran and the Duvrenne cleric note Ostrel's empty chair to each other and to nobody else.

That night on the shore Waylon asks whether he likes the desert because he's a sand dragon, or is a sand dragon because he likes the desert. Emeric says he still doesn't know which way round it goes for himself.

On Sunday 29 August Duvrenne's council arrives at Kesrath's tower with the convoy's dead counted: about a dozen escorts, and Mathilde. Ministers ask why their own guard failed where Randillia's held. Alina defends her father's refusal in front of them. It was the right call, it cost lives, and she says both. The envoys take their own readings away. Solreth's watched the king keep to his post when he had the strength to take the road himself. Velmoor's watched a Duvrenne faction try to restart a war on the Church's continent. Two trails begin: Vantic follows the faction's money toward the imperial banks, and Saban starts on the venue team's hire. Neither finishes in this batch.

Late on Sunday morning Mother Sabbe's goats won't go down to the cove beach where they always graze in summer, and she has to drive them back up the slope herself. She doesn't know why. Nobody does. On Monday 30 August the review sits again in Kaldo, and the clerk records the king absent on the leave the review gave him.

Endpoint: an old woman drives her goats up from a beach they've always grazed and doesn't know why they won't go down. Batch 1 ends.

Status line: the stand burned empty / no wind for that / sand in his scales.

First appearances: Waylon and Alina married.
