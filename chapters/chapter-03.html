Book 2, Batch 3: Below

Chapters 20–28 | Saturday 30 May – Friday 12 June, Year 17

At the end of the last batch the queen invited Emeric down to the full court at the floor of Aumere, where a land king can only be heard properly in the water. He accepted on the dry. He had already given one public word: the stone aboard the K Amara will not come inside the outer markers without the queen's leave, and if it does, the breach is his. The K Amara and escort three lie thirty miles out at the Grey Mark with most of the fleet's knights. Ward holds a private rule that he may come in to save life, and that if he does, Emeric's word breaks rather than the queen's. Zale and Ilenne share a bell in the queen's house, not yet reconciled, and Zale climbs the bell stages every dusk to read the post with his crew.

This batch is about what happens when the one man who could break the rules is out of reach and keeps his word anyway. Emeric goes down to the floor, where his sense turns to fog and his radio is useless. The people he left above fight the following nights without him: Ferra and Imani in a dead zone with their revolvers, Ward choosing between Emeric's word and a stair full of burning men, Mira on the roofs, Zale in a broken bell, and at home Harrold and Corbin on the night the Church comes. Everyone has to solve their own night. Emeric's part is to keep a promise while it costs them.

Ushar's week follows one plan, with three aims in falling order: win Ilenne by custom, which the court has already blocked; make her protectors look like breakers of the old promise; and remove Zale in a way that looks like what happens when land things come into the Rim. Every attempt is deniable, each is caught by someone different, and each leaves evidence the court cannot use until a certain custom is named.

Chapter 20: Below

On Saturday 30 May, Rowena calls first thing by field set, dry as always, and then proud: Corbin is good at this, people listen to him, he has changed. Emeric thanks her and sits with the set in his lap after she has gone. Ferra sees him do it and knows why.

Before he goes, Emeric says aloud what worries him. Every day he spends at the floor, he is as far from a ride home as he can be, and if the Church is listening, it knows the fleet has not moved. He goes anyway, because refusing the queen's summons would cost Ilenne the one thing the binding bought her. He leaves orders: Ferra and Imani carry their revolvers, Sir Hadden Voll's knights keep the gate, and nothing about the stone changes. He dresses in the Mark II suit with the king's coat over it, because the court should see the coat, and Ferra asks how many hours he will spend steaming it dry this time. Imani's card goes into the suit's pocket. Down there he must breathe the bells' air, never his own flask, because the bells' gill plates hold the oxygen below the level that harms, and his stone braces him against depth but does nothing about chemistry. Imani has written something on the back that he does not read until he is down.

At the head of the stages, where Imani's set last answered, he sends his last message. A Rim pilot on the steps above watches him go, and someone watches the pilot. Ushar knows the hour. Emeric descends bell by bell with Ilenne, Zale and Armand. Ilenne and Zale are polite to each other the way people are when they have not made up, and Zale goes down with his cracked ribs strapped.

For the first time Emeric sees Below from inside: the city running down the caldera wall around him, merfolk walking the floor by pressing themselves down with the water, which is why they build bridges, warm water near the vents, the queen's house above on the wall, and the Breath at the floor's edge, closed to land guests and seen once at a distance as a change in the water's colour. Then his sense goes. The water near the grove is the densest in Aumere, and he can feel only a few yards before it turns to fog. He tells Armand plainly, as a fact rather than a complaint. It frightens him more than he lets anyone see.

The full court sits wet, everyone walking, the accused and the questioned standing below the seat while those who are told sit. The queen opens. Ressar lays out the floor's procedure for the strangers, crisply and without patience. A Keeper may call a recess to read the stones. A statement made in the water before witnesses is wet and lasts forever, and when wet and dry testimony disagree, the wet stands until shown false. Nobody may argue a custom that has never been named before the court, and the stones hold one old line on how a custom is named: by one it binds. The deep lords want a wet word from the land king on the stone and on the old promise. He gives none, and explains why: his dry word stands, and he will not say anything in the water that he cannot keep forever, and he cannot promise forever about a sea he has known for a month. Some deep lords take it as an insult. Privately, Ressar takes it as the first sensible thing a land man has said.

They lodge in a Keepers' bell beside the court's hall, pumped dry for two land men. Armand is delighted by the teaching stones on its wall and appalled by the cold. Emeric reads Imani's card. On the back, in her hand: Come back up.

Armand reads one of the stones aloud as the First City's record, the lift and the fall, the way he would read an etched stone at the tower. Ressar corrects him in front of two Keepers. It is a burial song for the city's dead, not a record. A record and a song are cut differently, and a man who cannot tell them apart should not read either aloud. Armand takes it and asks to be taught the difference. Ressar's price is that nothing learned in this bell is read aloud outside it, and that Armand comes every day the court sits, on time. Armand agrees, which means staying Below for as long as the court does: cold, out of radio reach, and by his own choice.

Chapter 21: One Body Heavy

On Sunday 31 May, at the Grey Mark, a number does not add up. Since the K Amara moved out, her quartermaster has weighed every delivery again at the rail and read the figures to Ferra by set. Crate fourteen weighed about seventy kilos more on the Rim's quay than on the K Amara's deck. Fish do not get out and walk. That is one body's worth.

Ward gets the K Amara under way at twenty knots on the sea, so no boat can come alongside to take a man off, with escort three in company and the move reported to the watch's station officer. It tells the stowaway he has been found, and Ward decides that is the price. The knights' boots are loud on the deck plates and the stowaway hears them coming every time. Mira takes her boots off and searches the knights' apron stores with Arthur in their socks. They find his hide and his slate, but he is already moving.

The courier, a merfolk on his legs, breaks for the rail. As he clears it, off the deck, Mira fires a tag round into his shoulder, loaded with dye, fennel and sense-stone flour. He is running, not attacking, and she chooses the tag over a ball round knowing exactly why. Then a Rim boat appears off the bow, the trader's boat come for the empty crates, and a figure in it stands up holding something long. Mira has the shot. Ward says, "Hold." It is a boy with a boathook. While everyone was looking at the boat, the courier went under it and away.

With Armand Below, Ferra reads the slate by set, using Ilenne's word book. It holds the K Amara's watch rotation, exact; the delivery boats' times; her time from anchor to the ring at cruise, timed off a drill; and Zale's dusk habit, up the stages and back down after the post. Ward's routine is readable from outside the ship. He changes the rotation that night. Zale, reached by set at the gate, refuses to change his habit. His crew are alone at the gate, he is their captain, and he will not be frightened into hiding by a man who could not say it in the water. Ward does not argue it twice.

Ward makes his own decision instead. Arthur, Mira and Bren will go in on the next delivery boat with the watch's leave, berth on Zale's ship and cover his habit, logged at the water door with their rifles carried on the body. It is Mira's first time on the Rim. Maeve, on the K Discovery, finds a reason to be on the boat's run out to the hulls, and she is on the quay when it lands.

Chapter 22: Guessed

On Tuesday 2 June, Dagny services the gill-pack harness of Zale's Mark II after Abloh's repair and finds a sliver of drain stone set under the back strap. It would have sat against Zale's spine in deep water and drawn his strength away a little at a time until he could not swim, a drowning nobody would call anything else. The suit hangs in the bell Below at night, so someone with access to the bell's door put it there.

On the salvage quay, Oddrin's animal will not leave a spot beneath the piles. Oddrin goes down and comes up pale. The courier is tied under the quay with a drain cuff on his wrist, alive and drained grey, stinking of fennel and stained blue with Kaldo's dye, so the watch sends for Kaldo. Rifles are useless underwater, and nobody pretends otherwise. Zale goes down in the Mark II, ribs strapped, with Finn as dive lead and Ilenne directing from the water while the Challenger's floods light the piles. They bring the courier up alive. He cannot testify yet: drained, he cannot hold a colour, and without colour he cannot form half his words. The station takes him under the watch's guard, and the watch records his name as Lusse. Ushar's men had drained him so they could carry him off and question him, stashed him under the quay until dark, and the animal found him at dawn. Oddrin is now in the watch's eye and cagey about it. Bram still wants one of his animals.

Bram reads the two drain stones. On the surface, both carry the maker's mark of a cutting yard belonging to the old families of the shallows, Dessaun's people and the court's swing vote. That is what the stones were made to say. Ferra notices the aim: a cutter who means to kill does not sign the knife, so a man who leaves his name on it wants it read. Saban says the same by relay, better, and adds that drain stones are rare and dear anywhere, the mark of a house rich enough to waste them. Tess opens the two-lock box under the mill at home and matches the cleave beneath the mark, its grain and deliberate shaping, to the bident tip that came out of Ilenne on South Sentinel. Same hand, years apart. The house that chased her. Arthur and Mira visit the old families' yard. The cutter admits the mark is his and the cleave is not. He sold marked blanks, unfinished, to a Rim house's furnace steward named Maudrec. Maudrec paid in iron bars, and Ferra knows them by her own stamp and weights. Kaldo's pilot fee has come back to her as evidence. She writes it down without expression. Nobody accuses anybody. All of it goes to Taurre's record. Someone argues for taking matters into their own hands and narrowly loses, and the restraint costs them time and danger.

On Wednesday afternoon, between the bench and Neris's yard, Bram walks down the furnace yard's stair because he wants to see how furnace hands get Below. The chamber's keeper shows him the two-stage door: a held seal, and behind it a bronze shutter on its catch and counterweight, with the winch on the chamber side. Bram admires it aloud. Magic first, with the mundane underneath, Kaldo's own rule cut in someone else's rock.

That night he draws the chamber, and Ferra makes him mark every line with one of her five words: seen, measured, remembered, guessed or blank. The seal: seen. The stair: measured, since he paced it. The winch: seen. The shutter's drop: guessed. He admired it and never saw it fall. He is annoyed to have to write it.

Chapter 23: Taster

On Thursday 4 June, the food comes down from the Rim as it always does. Fire belongs to the Rim, so anything cooked that goes Below comes from Rim kitchens, and the house that holds the furnaces feeds the land husband, a courtesy that is also access. By custom, the sender's taster eats from every dish in front of the receiver's people. Everything Kaldo's people eat Below now passes Imani's station at the head of the stages to be weighed and carded for the heavy fish. The house's taster, Sarrou, eats in front of her and goes down with the basket. Imani is slow with the cards that morning because she is thorough. An hour later, at the second bell, Sarrou's breathing goes wrong. Imani stops the baskets, gets her up, and keeps her alive all morning using merfolk physiology she learned at the station two weeks earlier and her own hands. Zale had not eaten, because his card was not written yet. The dish marked for him is the poisoned one. Lying on the station's pallet, Sarrou works out that her own house sent her down knowing she would taste first. She says nothing about it yet.

That afternoon Zale goes up to the market to buy rope and salt for his crew, with two of Voll's knights. Ilenne goes too, still angry, because she will not let him walk the Rim without her. It is the nearest she can come to sorry. Imani goes because Ilenne is her patient and she no longer trusts anyone's food.

On the market stairs a hired knife named Orsaule comes at Zale. The guard charm catches the blade by its speed, and the knife goes into his sleeve instead of his ribs. A knight fires a ball round, and the attacker's own charm takes it with a flash and a crack. Two more rounds empty that charm, but he is already in the water off the stairs. Then Ilenne boils: tail in, a vapour sheath around her, a straight line at about eighty miles an hour on one breath, loud, and the bident. In the same minute the reader learns that charms stop bullets and what the merfolk are in their own water. Working on Orsaule at the water's edge, Imani sees the film on Ilenne's skin and names it in her head the way she would name a symptom: the slick. Orsaule dies under her hands.

A Rim man lies dead on the stairs with land guns still smoking, and the crowd comes up the stairs at the knights, not to murder anyone but for the dead man. The knights switch magazines by the notches at the left hip and fire baton and stun rounds, legs and belly, never above the belt. Ward's rule breaks under pressure: in the press one baton round goes off inside twenty yards and breaks the ribs of a twelve-year-old Rim boy named Nimo. Imani treats him where he fell. Ferra pays his family that evening and writes his name in her book.

The house got what it wanted: land guns fired in a market, a Rim man dead, a Rim child hurt. By dusk the Rim is saying Kaldo killed a man in its market. That it was Ilenne's bident does not travel as far. That night at the station Sarrou tells Imani she has not decided whether to speak. She serves a house, and she knows what it did. Imani does not push. She writes it on a card.

Chapter 24: The Post

On Friday 5 June, an hour before dusk, smoke rises over the Rim. A flue fire has started in the kiln racks, the watch's horns sound, and the captain of the twelve takes his men up the fast way to fight it. It is the Rim's own yard, and nobody on Zale's deck thinks more of it than that.

Linnet reads the post on deck at the evening set: Tess's latest, held messages from home, and a joke from the Crestless about Zale's bunks. Arthur, Mira and Bren stand on his deck covering the habit at knife range, because a knife is what everyone expects. Zale walks the breakwater to the stage head in the Mark II, and Ilenne is there, waiting for him at the stage head for the first time since their quarrel. Neither of them says why.

Then comes a sound nobody on the Rim has heard before. The charm takes the wake of the round and empties in one catch. The 20 mm round arrives slowed and tumbling and goes through his side instead of his chest. Before Zale has hit the steps, Mira has marked the muzzle flash on a roof above the kiln yard, about four hundred yards out.

Ilenne takes him down the stages rather than to the Rim's healers, because her mother's house is safer than a Rim she now knows is trying to kill him. His gill pack breathes for him, her hand is on the hole, and her voice talks the deep things away from his blood in the water. In the bell, with its air, its light, its anchor chain and its two pallets, she sends Thalune for her mother's healers and cuts the Mark II off him herself to reach the wound. The suit and its gill pack lie on the pallet. He tells her the charm is the best jewellery he has ever owned. She does not laugh.

Then the first charge hits the bell's shell.

Chapter 25: The Shutter

At dusk Linnet's voice comes over the set, and Imani runs for the furnace yard's stair with her bag, because it is the fastest way down to the court's water for anyone on legs. The first fire is at the far end of the yard, and the stair head is clear. Halfway down, every lamp goes out at once and the yard above her flares white. The arsonists have opened the necks of the yard's full furnace stones and then forced the core stone, so each ring bursts the next, a cascade that throws a dead zone over the yard even in thick air. The shutter drops in front of Imani, bronze on stone, and the water comes up behind it to its lip and stops. A dozen furnace hands come down the stair after her out of the flare, and there is nowhere for anyone to go. Smoke follows them down.

In a dead zone a healer's magic is gone, though the body's own mana still works. Imani triages with her kit and her hands: burns, airways, who sits nearest the shutter where the air is best. Among the hands is a young man named Dauvre, whose mother works the watch's skiffs.

Ferra is in the bench's office when the flare comes. Fire still burns and ordinary light still works in a dead zone, and the bench's clerks are running. She does three things in order. She gets the yard's people moving the right way, with Neris, who has come up from her own yard behind the kilns, leading them out through the kiln cellars she knows. She has the bench's record room's books, the furnaces' casting books among them, carried out by the clerks who would otherwise have left them. And she calls Linnet on her field set, mundane and still working, for Ward.

Then she goes to unbar the fire hatch so the stair can breathe, and two arsonists come back with spears to keep it barred, because a stair that breathes is a stair people climb. One goes for her. Ferra draws and fires, and he dies at the hatch. The other keeps coming. Imani, up the stair through the smoke at the sound of the shot, fires twice and drops him with a hip wound. Then she kneels and keeps him alive, because that is the other half of her. His name is Keddrin.

With the hatch open, the smoke lifts off the stair and the hands climb up into the yard. Ferra counts her cylinder by touch, three spent, and then counts it again. Neither woman says anything about what they have done yet.

From the west comes a rising roar that nobody on the Rim has ever heard: the K Amara's burn, audible from thirty miles out. Ferra knows what it is and what it means for Emeric's word.

Chapter 26: Breath

In the bell, more charges come, and the shell begins to fail in sections. Ilenne catches the first break with water magic and holds the sea out. A second side goes. She ends up holding a bubble of air around Zale with the whole sea pressing on it, and a dense pocket of water outside it to turn the attackers' spells and spears, both at once, unable to do anything else. Her nose bleeds. Thalune, outside, sees colour and hears nothing, and goes for help that is all up on the Rim fighting the fire. She returns to the bell's door with no help, in time to see a shape go up through the dark carrying the heir.

Zale knows two things: Ilenne will die before she lets go, and he is dying anyway. Bracing saves him from the pressure, not from drowning. So he whistles, the way he casts, a hard crack of air past her ear close enough to break her hold. The water comes down and the bubble collapses.

The sea fills his lungs, and his body answers before his mind does: gills, webbing, the dark resolving into shapes, the water he has never had rising up in him. This time he holds the flood off her. The change does not heal the wound, but it gives him the escape the shutter took away. He can swim. Ushar's line, that he would not speak in the water to a man who cannot breathe it, is answered in the dark where nobody hears.

The house's men come in to finish the job and find a sea dragon in a broken bell. The fight is short and Zale bleeds through all of it. Some of them die, and the rest run and will report a beast. He rises through open water with Ilenne in his arms, past the shut door, to the gate where his ship is, outside the zone.

On the K Amara, Ward gets Linnet's relay of Ferra's call. He asks the watch's station officer at the Grey Mark for leave twice and hears nothing from the ring. Then he raises the anchor and burns. Escort three burns behind him but holds at the outer markers, so one hull breaches and not two. He reports to the watch as he moves. The stone comes in with the K Amara. He knows what that does to Emeric's word, and he does it anyway.

The K Amara cannot fly over the yard, so she stands off low over the water at the zone's edge, floods on. The merfolk find the light ugly and are glad of it tonight. Flight knights land outside the edge and walk in. The Crestless are already on the stair carrying hands up, and the K Amara's knights take them out across the edge to the water stairs and the watch's skiffs. Speed has a cost. The ship's downwash sweeps over the evacuation line and turns over a skiff. Esseth, who works the watch's skiffs, is pulled out half-drowned. Her son Dauvre is one of the men just carried up off the stair.

At the gate steps Linnet and the crew haul Zale out of the water with Ilenne, and Imani, clear of the zone, is there within minutes with her hands in his side on his own deck. On the steps where she was bound, Ilenne gives an order: nobody is to say he lived. She is furious with him for the whistle until she is not, and then furious again. They do not talk about the quarrel. The watch orders the K Amara back out past the markers at once. Ward obeys as soon as the last man is off the stair, and she returns to the Grey Mark with the stone.

Chapter 27: Walked Down

The night before, Mira had the flash marked at dusk, and Arthur sent her to the stair instead: people first. She hated it and did it. The shooter is still on the Rim, because Taurre closed every channel at the overload and the watch holds the boats. He is somewhere on the roofs with a gun nobody can feel.

After midnight Mira goes back to her marked roof line with Arthur and Bren behind her. She finds a hide, a casing in Kaldo brass with the lot stamp, and a trail over the roofs and down the water stairs. Through her night glass the roofs come up grey, and the hide's stone still holds his warmth, which tells her how long ago he left. It cannot find him, because the Rim's warm vent-stone roofs swamp the heat with false bodies, and it cannot feel the gun. He fires once more at her from three hundred yards in the half dark and misses. The chase breaks into the open at first light on the kelp racks.

He is a Rim mage named Pirro, with a shield the thick air keeps topping up. Her first round hits the shield, and it holds but knocks him flat, because a shield stops damage, not momentum. She walks toward him, firing as she walks. He throws the last attack he has, and she slips it, because she has been hurt before and will not be hurt again for underestimating anyone. Each time his body moves she puts a round into his head, so he can never get his feet or his thoughts back. The shield pays full price for every round, faster than the air can refill it, and only when it breaks does a round reach him. That one kills him. She takes the rifle back out of his hands. Maeve is on the quay when the boat brings her in and asks nothing.

Ferra reads the serial. It is the one in her log from the dragon fight. Somebody read the hazard board. Her honest declaration is how the rifle was found, and it is also the only reason Kaldo will be able to prove whose it was. Bram examines what the salt did to it and finds fresh tool marks on the action's pins: somebody has had it apart. It goes to Taurre's record as evidence, and Ferra signs for it as Kaldo's declared property. The box held ten rounds when it was lost. It comes back with eight.

At dawn on the 6th, Below, a runner from the queen reaches Emeric. The queen waited for first light so she would have the night's facts first. Folded inside is Ferra's note: Z shot, side, lives, Imani has him. I. says nobody says he lived. Ward crossed with the stone. Stay down if you can. Emeric felt nothing of the Rim from the floor and never could. He stays down, because a land king running up the stages at dawn would tell the whole court his brother is alive. The queen calls the court that morning. Taurre's report comes by runner, the old families go slate at the news that the stone came inside the markers, and Ushar's people say the land's stone made the dead zone. Then Emeric gives his first wet words ever, and they are an acceptance rather than a promise: "If my ship came past your markers last night, it was my word she broke, and not the queen's." It is true forever, and it lifts the breach off the queen in front of every house. The hall does not know what to do with it. Ressar notes that it is well formed. Asked about his brother, Emeric says his brother is his brother's business and the queen's daughter's.

Keddrin, kept alive by Imani, names who paid him: Maudrec. Maudrec, once held, names whose word he took. The watch may hold anyone named to a fire or a dead zone in its water until the court sits, and Taurre does it: a great house's heir, Ushar, in a closed bell. Ilenne asks that he be told nothing, and a held man is told only what the court allows. Ushar believes Zale drowned.

Armand stays Below on Ressar's terms, at the Keepers' bell every day, learning to tell songs from records. When Emeric goes up on the 7th, Armand is the land's only person at the floor, by his own choice. Up top, Zale is on his own deck, grey and webbed and laughing at him. Emeric asks: does he like the ocean because he is a sea dragon, or is he a sea dragon because he likes the ocean? Ferra writes Zale's line on a sheet she is keeping about need and nature: the question is asked.

By daylight the yard tells its story. The burned furnace hands are at the station with Orrane among the healers. Bram and Dagny read the burst stones in their rings, a cascade and no accident, and the shutter's catch, which dropped exactly as his drawing guessed. The glassmaker who answered Bram's stupid questions watched Ferra and Imani fire and will not speak to them now. Bram minds that more than they do.

Ward comes into the city for the first time to answer Esseth's family himself, in his own words. Emeric stands behind him and owns the order without lifting any of the harm off it. Dauvre will not choose between "you got me off that stair" and "you nearly drowned my mother in front of my street," and nobody asks him to. Ferra pays. Afterward Ward and Emeric disagree, for real and quietly. Ward thinks he was right, and that the word he broke was a mistake to give. That night in the hard room Ferra tells Emeric about the serial in her accounts voice and does not ask to be forgiven. Imani tells him what Keddrin's hip looked like and what she did about it. He holds them both, and nobody sleeps much. Ferra counts her cylinder by touch in the dark, and he does not stop her.

On the morning of the 8th Corbin calls, booked through the line room himself, a younger brother asking for advice on something real. The stewards' weekly reports have started telling him what he wants to hear, and he has noticed. Emeric tells him to ask every one of them for one bad number a week and thank them for it. Corbin laughs and says he will try it on Harrold. Emeric ends the call and goes back down the stages for the court's hearing on the furnace night, and does not sleep that night at the floor.

Chapter 28: The Long Night

On Monday 8 June at home, Lord Cassel, the capital lord who went white in April at the sight of Harrold and Amara together, is back in Kaldo on regency business and stays the week. He does not go white again. He watches, and he is polite to her. Harrold explains nothing. Declan calls Ward through the line room. With Osric's and the regent's leave, he has been running the home knights up the ridges above the river on night drills, because that is where he would put mages if he were the Church, and he wants his old captain to tell him it is right. Ward says yes.

On Tuesday night the Church comes. Its listeners set their clock to the relays' heartbeat, so the first thing the raid does is burst a dead zone on the relay over its route just after that relay's beat. The zone closes in minutes at Band height, and the relay drops and recovers a quarter of an hour before anyone understands what happened. Hulls appear in the dark over the city. The half-grown Shell holds only some of its faces, so the hulls come over the dark ones, and one comes through a lit face at walking pace, read as slow and let through. They drop dead zones on the streets, and the streets stay lit, because Kaldo's lights do not run on magic. The fibre lines keep the Answer Towers joined. Saban holds all outgoing traffic, the line room is a target, and Ambrose holds it. The fire teams go for the port tank yard's full tanks first. The clerk's nephew never broke a single condition. Under escort every hour, inside the customs house, he simply read what was in front of him, and the customs house keeps the bonded-stores ledger, the oil included. Osric and the fire company cannot save the reserve. The drop teams throw the Church's hush mines at the line room and the dome's stones, and stones die where they land. Amara's sealed heart does not, so her golem keeps fighting. The harbour's nearest stones go quiet for the night while the Board's impellers keep running.

Harrold is at the dome, where the tree stands in the densest air in Kaldo and Amara's own body lies healing, while Amara in her golem tears through the drop teams and he protects both of her anyway. The Church's dead zone on the dome drowns within minutes in its density. Amara flies, up through the dark, and brings the bombing hull down in front of the whole city, but it has already released. Harrold lights the sky so the flak and the towers can see. He moves the dome two hundred yards with mirage, so the guided spells land on nothing. The last bomb is a plain heavy one aimed at the dome's crown, and images do not fool it. Harrold changes without deciding to, fully from the first instant, a dragon of light over the crown with wings spread, and takes the bomb on his scales. Glass is everywhere and the dome is cracked but standing, the tree and Amara's body untouched. He cannot change back for hours. She lands beside him. He says, "You missed one."

Corbin is at Doran's old chapel in the lane, talking about Augustine and whether redemption is something a man like him gets. Then the blast: the Church blows the dam's first stage, the liquid mana store at its foot goes up and makes the gorge thick, and water mages stack the reservoir into a wave. Corbin walks to the middle of the old bridge, and Doran follows because he has not finished his confession. Corbin changes, and stops the wave in its tracks with stasis, the whole face of it held glassy above the bridge towers and the dragon on the bridge perfectly still. Then the city does the saving. Tess blows the relief cut into the old marsh, Armand's students drain the held water sideways a sluice at a time, and Corbin lets the wave down a hand's width at a time on Tess's count, all night.

On the ridge, Declan's knights take the mages the way they drilled it the night before. A juggernaut guards them. They shoot its grey-robed handler, but this handler's last order was to kill whatever came up the ridge, and a juggernaut cut off from its handler finishes its last order, so it goes for the knights. Declan holds it while the others finish the mages, and it kills him. They drain it and take it alive. At dawn Harrold refuses to have it executed.

Near dawn on the bridge, Doran says, "I was telling you something." Corbin answers, "You were," and finishes his confession. Harrold, burned and grey with glass dust, comes down from the dome and stands beside him. Neither of them says anything.

On the Rim on Wednesday the 10th, the held messages reach Linnet's set in a single stack. Ferra reads them in order and writes Harrold's and Corbin's lines beneath Zale's on the need-or-nature sheet: nature picks the kind, need picks the moment, and now two of them say so. She decides not to send it Below. The raid is over, nobody can fly home in time to matter, and the court is two days from its recess. Imani says send it. Ferra does not.

On Friday the 12th Ressar calls a recess for the stones, to read an older line before the court goes on. All week Sevrin, Ushar's cousin, has spoken for the house in his place. Emeric comes up the stages into the stack. Ferra tells him she held it back. He says he would have wanted it. She says she knows. Neither of them says she was wrong. Amara's message is on top.

Where the Story Stands After Chapter 28

Everyone solved their own night, and Emeric kept his word through all of it. Zale was shot with Kaldo's own lost rifle and then trapped in a breaking bell, and the sea change finally came: he breathes water now, hidden on his own ship, off duty for weeks, while Ilenne has ordered that nobody say he lived. Ushar sits in the watch's dark believing Zale drowned. Ferra and Imani have fired at people for the first time, one man dead and one alive because Imani kept him so. Ward crossed the markers with the stone to save a stair full of men and nearly drowned a mother doing it, and Emeric's first wet words took that breach off the queen and onto himself. Mira walked the shooter down and brought the rifle back, and Ferra knows her honest declaration both leaked it and proves it.

The evidence is piling up: the drain stones and Tess's match to the old bident tip, the cutter and Maudrec, Ferra's own iron coming back, Sarrou and the poison, Keddrin's account, the bench's casting books, the cascade rings and the shutter's catch, the rifle with eight rounds and tool marks on its pins, Lusse who will soon be able to speak, and the watch's times showing the dead zone before the burn. It is enough to prove the house guilty of everything and still not enough to touch Ushar while the custom has no name. The market rumour that Kaldo killed a man still runs on the Rim, Nimo's ribs are broken, Esseth and Dauvre have their grievance, and the glassmaker will not speak to Ferra and Imani.

At home, Kaldo won without Emeric and lost Declan. Harrold and Corbin have both changed into dragons, the dome is cracked but standing, the dam's first stage and the liquid mana store are gone, the oil reserve is burned, a juggernaut has been taken alive, and the whole city, Lord Cassel included, has seen Amara fly. The court is in recess while Ressar reads an older line on the stones, with Sevrin speaking for the Rim's house, and Armand remains Below learning songs from records.

This batch proves that Kaldo's people can carry the worst nights without their king, and that keeping faith with someone else's law has a price paid by the people who keep it.
