Book 2, Batch 16: Sunday

Chapters 98–104 | Monday 28 December, Year 17 – Sunday 10 January, Year 18

The fleet leaves the top of the world with its ice cores in the cold room, a cairn of names behind it, and a promise to Saldis's house that it will draw nothing where nobody can give leave. It came north on one charge and the sea, and it goes home the same way. Ferra's column for the dragons' observers is almost closed. The pickup pair is due to meet the fleet at first light on Sunday 3 January at the fourth relay drum's mark out from Kaldo, a place Zale named in clear over Ilenne's gift set.

This is the batch of the trap. Two days from the rendezvous, home reports that the third drum out from Kaldo has gone silent, not dead but shut, because somebody tried its door with a note that was not Emeric's. He says aloud that it is bait and goes up anyway, with Arthur's team, a spare drum, his sister and a written plan. In about twenty minutes at the Band's foot, Maeve is taken, a single word stops a spear, a beam narrower than a finger kills the man holding her, and her heart stops.

What follows is the rage the whole voyage has been building toward: the oldest mage alive held flat on a deck until a ship's rail goes under, a doctor walking through the weight, and three hulls let fall out of the sky. Then a storm, a second bolt, and a dragon nobody knew Maeve carried. The fleet comes home to a city held at its own Shell by the king's own rule, and the man who keeps Kaldo's secrets works out how the Church knew where to wait.

Chapter 98: Kaldo's Own Sky

On Monday 28 December, before the lift, Ferra lays out the way home at the chart table in one line. The fleet will make the polar run south low, the way it came, since nobody can give leave in that sky; spend three days at the relief box charging on the thick sea outside the wall; climb at the box with the necks shut, because the Band leg south-east has the westerlies behind it and saves two days' diesel and a week of crew time; come down on the thick sea outside the wall for a last low charge; then cross the wall low into the ring for the last six hundred miles to the fourth drum. The fleet comes up from the Lead and the summit about noon, which is dark, and runs south low for about eleven hours with the night glass on the pack, and is on the water at the box about midnight.

For three days it lies on the cold sea, charging low. The last ice comes off the rigging. Dagny goes back to light work with her ribs, and the class meets on the K Endeavor's hospital deck as before. Nobody presses, Emeric included, and Runa has her sessions every day. On Thursday the old year goes out without a feast, because the Long Table was a week ago and Ferra's food sum is Ferra's food sum. At the midnight watch change somebody who should not rings the K Amara's bell twelve times, and the night glass stays on the swell.

On Wednesday 30 December, at first light, Ilenne stands on Aumere's breakwater and watches her husband's ship go out through the fetch line behind Taurre's boat, with the K Tam astern under Nell Haddow and Abloh at the rail with six weeks of sea silk in a chest. She stands where she stood in July and in November and does not wave. She feels him go, the way the rite said she would. The sense of him, like the sea under a hull, thins with every mile until it is a pull at the edge of her and nothing more, and it does not stop. She goes back Below to a council about the field's lamp line, because a queen does, and Ressar sees her colour hold still the way a highborn's does when she is working hard at it, and says nothing. The pair runs low on their veins and on the water at night, south and east of the Smoking Rings, Linnet Shaw steering by the fourth drum's beat.

On Friday at dawn the fleet climbs at the box on racks filled by three days of cold sea. The climb is paid from what the sea gave, nothing is taken from the Band, and the necks stay shut all the way up and all the way across. Ferra writes it as promised: climbed on the sea's charge; drew nothing. The Band leg runs about fourteen hours at the economy pace with the westerlies behind, and the fleet comes down at dusk on the thick sea outside the wall, about five hundred miles west of the fourth drum, west of the chain and well north of the Smoking Rings, because the law of approach Saldis's house gave holds whichever way a fleet is going.

That night in the K Discovery's chart room the question left open at the cone is settled. Inside the ring the fleet will draw nothing, and it needs nothing: Saturday's low charge outside the wall will carry it the last thousand miles low, and the thin sea inside the wall would give little anyway. The voyage's column closes clean, one charge in Saldis's sky to her figure and the sea's everywhere else. Then Emeric says the harder part plainly. The offer to the dragons said wherever we can, and that has to mean home too, or it is a guest's manners and not a promise. The Well has drawn on the Band over Kaldo for years: the city's lights, the Shell's core and the liquid mana in hull four's tanks all come from it, and he cannot stop it this year without putting out the city. So the honest version is the column. Ferra opens a new heading, Kaldo's own sky, to take the Well's draw every month from the return. She says that is a column that will get longer before it gets shorter. He says yes. She writes the heading anyway and underlines it, as she does for numbers she means to watch.

On Saturday 2 January, in the forenoon, the fleet lies on the water outside the wall charging low, and the K Discovery's set reaches the chain's outer drums again for the first time since the fleet went north. Before the slot sheet, the line room's watch officer reports that the third drum out from Kaldo, about four hundred and fifty miles out and one further in than the rendezvous, raised an alarm at 03:12 that morning and has been silent since. It is not dead, he says; the board shows a tamper code. Tess comes on an urgent that really is one. Her decider, built to tell a deaf neighbour from a dead one, makes the difference plain from Kaldo's end: the drum is shut, not dead. Somebody tried its door with a note that was not his. The lock held. She is proud of the lock and furious about the door in the same sentence. Saban, short, says a heartbeat is a lighthouse, which he said before the voyage, and adds that a lighthouse that goes dark on purpose is one somebody wants you to sail up to. Emeric says he knows. Harrold reports the Shell commissioned whole and the stadium ready for the crowd on the tenth. "How's he holding up?" "Working." Ferra watches him ask it. Under Ward's rule, nothing of where the fleet is or what it will do goes home ahead of it. Emeric says they will see to it, and Harrold, who knows what that means from a king, does not ask.

That afternoon the fleet's council meets in the chart room, with Ward across by boat, Ferra, Imani, Armand and Arthur. Emeric says it before anyone else can: somebody who can delve tried the door, and when it did not open they turned Kaldo's own chain into a lighthouse with the lamp out. The people who did that want him to come and look. Ward says then don't: drop a new drum from a hull at height and leave the shut one hanging. Emeric says he is going up to it anyway. Ward does not argue in front of the others. He asks for the plan in writing, which is how Ward argues. At dusk the fleet lifts low, crosses the wall in the dark with the night glass on the swell, and runs through the night for the fourth drum's mark.

Chapter 99: The Fourth Drum

At first light on Sunday 3 January the fleet comes down out of the dark onto the water at the fourth drum's logged mark, about six hundred miles out from Kaldo, and the pickup pair is already there, two small hulls on a grey swell with their lamps lit, where Ferra's chart said they would be. Zale's ship is on the water under the homeward line again. Maeve goes across in the K Amara's launch before anyone has had breakfast and tells her brother she has not done anything stupid in the cold. Zale says it is early. He looks at her for a long moment, the way he has not looked at anyone in six weeks: a sea-changed man checking his sister is still the shape she was. Nobody on either deck mentions that she went across before the king did.

Bram brings the limpets across himself on the K Endeavor's crane. "We used them." Zale asks on what. "Dagny." Dagny, on light duty at the K Endeavor's rail, says she was attached. Zale's word at Aumere is paid, since he told them to keep the limpets and bring them back, and they go into his hold while Ferra enters the crate as returned to its owner with its charge recorded. In the forenoon Zale comes to the K Discovery, where the pickup's master takes his orders. He hears about Saldis, the ice and the shut drum in that order, and asks the only question a man five months married who has shared with his wife would ask: how close does the pickup lie to it? Under it, Emeric says. That is what a pickup is for. From midday to dusk the fleet moves low at the pair's pace about a hundred and fifty miles north-east along the chain, the pair on the water under the air hulls, to the third drum's logged mark, and is on the water there by dusk, inside the Church's reach, under a low cloud deck at the Band's foot. The third drum is the first on the chain that no Kaldo hull ever goes near between voyages, and within a night's flight of the Church's hidden hulls off Ostrellin's coast.

At dusk, at the K Discovery's rail, Maeve asks to come up. She can read a lightning kill on a shell. If the drum died of a strike, or a bolt thrown at it, the burn will say so, and she is the only one aboard who knows what a mage's lightning leaves on plate as against the sky's; she has trained his lightning for months and taught him what a struck stone looks like. It is a real reason, and he knows it is. She also says, because she always says the second thing, that she has been on a ship for nine months and has seen the drift line once. She will be in a Band suit, it is a repair, and he has the escort. He says yes.

That evening, and again later to Ward alone, he gives the reasons for going himself. Only his note opens the drum's door: a shut decider cannot be released and dropped, and a new drum beside it would leave the old one hanging in the Band with its pin held, a thing up there nobody can talk to. A gap in the chain is a gap in the line home, and every voyage after this one goes out along it. The drum's log will say who tried, and that is worth knowing before the Church knows he knows. And leaving them there is worse than finding them: whoever tried the door is either still beside it or gone, and he would rather know which with his own sense than guess for ten years. Ward says the last reason is the one that worries him.

Ward writes the ride-up rule in his own hand, with a reason beside every line. Arthur's team goes up with the king: five flight knights in MK3 plate on ride-up rigs, Arthur, Mira, Bren and two of the Crestless, carrying a spare drum on its harness rings in case the old one cannot be woken. The K Amara stands off below at about two miles with her 20 mm and the dragon rifles uncovered, and check fire is called the moment anyone of theirs is in the line. The pickup lies on the water under the drum: Zale's ship, with a surgeon aboard, because a rider who falls from the Band comes down on a chute into the sea and the pickup must be able to cut a suit open and work on what is in it, with the ride's count on the set and a sense pointed up the line. Everyone else lies a mile off, necks shut, the night glass on the cloud. Nobody goes into the cloud without a word on the team's set first. Zale's deck will hold Zale, its master; Imani, who takes the pickup herself because it is Arthur's team and the king on a ride she thinks is a mistake, and because she is the only one aboard who can push mana into a brain, leaving Bettany the K Endeavor's ward; Ferra, on Linnet Shaw's set with the count and the drum's cipher, which is hers; Armand, because a relay can be felt rather than heard, sitting on the hatch cover with his eyes shut to call what he feels up the line; Linnet Shaw at the set; and Zale's crew of about a dozen.

Ward's plan has a line zero: a man who says out loud that a drum is bait sends someone to look at it before he goes up. Two in stealth plate are to go up and back before midnight, no closer than a quarter mile, nothing touched. Mira asks for it and gets it, because the stealth MK3 is hers by habit, with one of the Crestless as her pair. She sees the cloud deck from below and then from inside, grey on grey in the glass, the heat layer showing nothing, because there is nothing warm in cold cloud and it cannot see through cloud anyway. Above it, at the drift line, she finds the drum hanging on its pin in its stone net, the shell whole and the radio silent, and a drift about a hundred yards off, a river of wild gravity stones going by in the dark at the Band's foot, every stone moving together. Nothing warm, nothing still, nothing quiet. She goes a quarter mile and down.

About eleven, on the K Amara's apron, she reports to Ward and Emeric: the drum whole and shut, a drift passing at the drift line, which drifts do, cloud, and nothing else. Armand, from Zale's hatch cover over the set, confirms it from below: the drum's stones, the drift's stones, and nothing else he can feel. Then Mira says the drift bothers her, and she cannot say why, and she says that too.

Chapter 100: Sunday

On Monday 4 January, before first light, on the K Amara's apron, Mira checks Maeve's Band-suit clips, the breath bottle's seal, the suit set's switch and the chute's ripcord, then does it all again from the top. Maeve lets her. Bren, getting the same check from nobody, asks who checks his. Mira tells him to check his own, louder than she needs to. The K Amara climbs to her standing-off height with Ward on the apron. Arthur's team goes off her apron on the rigs with the spare drum slung between Bren and one of the Crestless. Maeve is in her Band suit at the inside edge of the screen, where Ward's plan puts the one who is not plated. Emeric flies beside them on his core envelope, his first core flight since the Isles, because this one needs it: the core's own gravity carries him through the thin miles between sea and Band without taking anything from outside, and the envelope is his body shield. Through the cloud, above it, to the drift line. The drift is passing a hundred yards off, as it was at midnight.

He puts his hand on the drum's shell and his note into its decider, and it opens to him. The log says: another note, Saturday 2 January, 03:12, refused, shut. He reads it to Ferra on the set word for word, because she keeps the cipher. Maeve looks over the shell for a lightning burn, finds none, and says so: it didn't die, it was asked.

In the next ten seconds, through Mira's eyes, the drift moves wrong for one heartbeat, and three hulls come out of it on Maeve's side of the screen, which is Mira's side, and Mira is turned toward the drum like everyone else. A line with a drain barb comes out of the nearest hull's rail on a reel. The barb takes Maeve in the suit's chest strap, the drain stone in its head biting through the suit to her body, and the reel pulls her in across fifty yards of air before Mira has turned round. Then Maeve is on a Church deck with a man's hand on the back of her neck. It is the same lesson Mira learned long ago, about what you cannot see, and this time someone else paid.

Then the hatches open on Emeric, everything the Church has, all at once, and none of it holds him. What they brought is sized for the wrong man. Their picture of him is the king of the air battle over Kaldo, a gravity-and-fire mage who flies a suit, casts through glyphs when it suits him, and can be drained like any mage. Nobody outside the fleet has seen the core, the envelope, the pin or the Crumple. Gravity from about a dozen casters on three decks lands on him like a wet coat, because he is too dense to be moved bodily, and it is the men on the decks who sweat. Water is thrown round him and frozen in sheets, and it cracks on the body shield where it touches him; he leaves it cracked where it is. Stasis is laid on his limbs, at a shard stone's price a breath for the Church's few stasis casters, and inward stasis is what his core is made of. He can feel exactly where theirs grips and where it is thin, and could lift it off in a breath. He does not, yet. Nets of every element they have are padded by the envelope, and nothing reaches skin. The first thing they try is a drain line. It takes what it can of the mana in his body and stops dead at the core, which nothing outside can touch, and the Band refills him faster than the line can take. The caster on the reel stares at his gauge and does not understand it. That is the moment the Church stops trying to drain the king and starts trying to hold him, and finds it cannot do that either. The Band feeds every caster up there, not only him, and still it is not enough.

What actually holds him is one hand. The man on the nearest deck has his hand on the back of Maeve's neck and a drain barb in her. A man who breaks his holds is a man about to move, and the hand would close first, so Emeric keeps still on purpose and lets them think it is working. That is the whole of his restraint, and he chooses it. The casters' weight comes down on Arthur's team too, and the knights who have drilled inward stasis by its name since November hold their bones under it the way they could not have in October, slowed rather than crushed, but none of them can reach Maeve with a hand on her neck. A dead zone is out of the question: in the Band it would seal in moments and drop every gravity stone inside it, theirs, Kaldo's below, and Maeve. The Church sends no juggernauts, since every one is a door any delver can walk through. His arithmetic is one line: he is fast enough to take them all given a breath, and none of their holds would stop him taking it, but the man holding Maeve is too close to her, and no spell he has gets there before that hand moves. Two miles below, Ward has the K Amara's 20 mm laid on the nearest hull and check fire called, because Maeve is on that deck and Arthur's team is in every line. He watches through the glass and cannot fire, and knows it, and does not.

Mira is the nearest knight to the hull that has Maeve, and she goes at it with the spear low, the only thing she knows how to do with a friend on an enemy's deck, into a dozen casters and a man with his hand on Maeve's neck. On the team's set Maeve says one word under her breath: "Sunday." It is the word from their game, from the river beach and the spear lessons, the slow walk that might be a strike, where the one walked at waits to be sure. Here it means: don't come in; I'm going to move; be ready when I do. Maeve chooses what she protects, Mira out of the line of a dozen casters who would have killed her at the rail, and a spear held at the edge rather than thrown. Mira stops at a spear's reach off the hull's rail with the shaft low, and waits to be sure, watching Maeve and not the man.

Under the holds Emeric's partial change shows without his choosing, the way it came at Sohra: the fire skin over his face and hands, the gold in his eyes gone bright. The man with his hand on Maeve looks at him and goes still in a way nobody goes still on purpose, then jerks like a struck dog, his face slack for a moment and then his own again, frightened. His hand comes off Maeve's neck by the width of a hand. In the same breath the nozzle comes back to Emeric, the same mana through a smaller hole. Staring at the man's chest, he lays a line to it without knowing he is doing it, a tube of cast stasis pushing inward, the Aperture he has had for months and never laid as a road, and the fire goes down it: a beam narrower than a finger, through every single-layer hold on him, through the man's chest, and on through the hull behind him. The man is dead before he falls at Maeve's feet.

In the same breath, Maeve is free of the hand but still on the barb, the drain stone biting, the reel taut. Mira's blade goes in at the reach she has held and takes the barb's head off Maeve's suit a finger's width from her ribs, the stop Duncan put a finger's width from Mira's own bruised side on the K Discovery's foredeck in November, and does not touch her. The barb falls away down the line. It is Mira's spear and her knowledge of Maeve, where her ribs are inside a suit and which way she will flinch, that changes the outcome. With the drain off her, the Band fills Maeve in a heartbeat. Not to her full self; enough.

On the second hull a mage who has just watched a beam go through his officer and the deck behind him does the sum everyone up there is doing: it is over if the girl goes free. He throws pure lightning at her. Nobody can get there. Emeric is still half in the holds, Mira is at a spear's reach, and Arthur's team is slowed under the casters' weight. Nothing outruns a bolt. Maeve does not try to stop it. She uses the water she has kept hidden all her life, the second element Rowena taught her in a palace yard where nobody could see, drawn from the wet, dirty air at the Band's foot. She throws a rope of that water between herself and the mage, takes the bolt into it, far past anything she has ever held, and sends it back down the rope into him. He dies on his own deck. The current goes through her on its way. Her last thought is that at least one of them goes with her. She finally got her trip with her brother, and her need to be beside him put him exactly where he said it would. He told her it was dangerous. She did not care. Her heart stops. Branching lightning scars come up across her collarbone, chest, stomach and side under the suit. Mira, a spear's reach away, sees her go slack on the deck and cannot reach her through the holds still coming off the other hulls.

Chapter 101: The Weight

The beam has shown Emeric what the holds are, a single layer each, and he goes through them the way the fire went, one road at a time. He has Maeve off the Church deck before the man on the reel has looked up. He does not look at the hulls. He drops with her held inside his envelope, about four miles straight down through the cloud, to Zale's deck under the drum.

Through Imani's eyes, he lands on the planks on his knees with his sister in his arms and lays her down. Imani has the suit's chest open with the shears from her kit before he has let go. No pulse. She starts chest compressions the Earth way he taught her, then hands them to Zale on her count, because she needs both hands for the other thing: her own mana into Maeve's brain, keeping it fed while the blood is pushed round by hand. Ferra calls the time to Linnet Shaw. Then Emeric stands. He does not look at any of them. He starts to rise off the deck, not fast, slowly, the way a storm builds, and everyone on the deck knows what he is going to do.

Armand stops him. The oldest mage alive throws restraints of every kind, and Emeric cancels every one with its exact reverse. Armand pushes through. Emeric locks him in stasis. Armand breaks that too, with eighty years of it in his hands. So Emeric holds him down with gravity, flat to the planks, and keeps holding. There is so much of it that that side of Zale's ship sinks in the water, the rail dipping toward the sea and green water coming over the scuppers. Armand genuinely almost dies under it, his ribs, his breath and his old heart all giving way. The weight is on everyone. Ferra goes down on the planks by the set and cannot get up. Zale's arms will not lift for the next compression. Linnet Shaw is pinned flat against the wheel housing. The compressions stop, because nobody on the deck can lift their arms.

Imani gets up anyway. Her nose bleeds. The muscles in her legs tear as she steps, and she heals them as she goes, step after step across a tilted deck under a weight that is crushing her teacher, toward a man she loves who is not looking at her. She says nothing. She is a doctor, there is a patient on the deck behind her going past the window with every step, and the only way back to her is through him. He sees Imani. He lets go of everything at once, Armand, the deck, the weight, and Zale's ship rights herself with a roll that throws water the length of her. The bill is plain: Armand's ribs cracked and his breath short for an hour; Imani's legs torn and mended and her nose bleeding, emptied by the walk and the mending both; Ferra bruised flat on the planks; a tonne of water over Zale's rail and her starboard pump running till noon; and Maeve's compressions stopped for the minutes it lasted.

He does not go back up. He stands on the righted deck with his face to the cloud and feels the three hulls' stores above it, the densest points in the sky now that the hatches are open and the beam has holed the iron. This time on purpose, he lays three roads up through four miles of air and fires down each one: three beams, each narrower than a finger, straight up through the cloud. The stores go. The hulls lose their lift and start to come down. Four miles is a long barrel, and the three beams empty him to the core, and the core is storage, not a pump, so he stands there with the floor of himself and nothing on it, inside the ring where the air gives nothing back. He will feel it for two days. He does not catch them. He could, a minute from now, with what he has not got. He does not want to.

Through Arthur's eyes, the hulls come out of the cloud in pieces, burning, and nobody asks the king. The knights go out after the falling crews in their MK3s, catching men in the air as they jump, one each, down to the water and back up for the next. The K Amara's apron launches every flight knight she has after them. Ward does not order it and does not stop it. On the second hull, falling, its deck tilting, a man in a grey coat with no badge keeps casting: at Bren, who has a limp crewman over his shoulder and cannot fight, and at the men in the air the knights are catching, his own people among them, because a crewman Kaldo catches is a crewman who talks. Arthur goes in on the tilting deck. The officer is spent, his hand empty, for one breath. Arthur could kill him and does not. He gets a drain cuff from his own belt onto the man's wrist and drags him off the falling deck, and while he does it two crewmen go into the sea, one of them never found. In the grey coat is a sealed order. Bren is burned across the shoulder by the officer's last cast and does not put the crewman down. Mira goes out with the rest and catches three. She does not speak to anyone for the rest of the day.

The count is three small Church hulls with about forty-five aboard, a dozen of them casters. Seventeen come out of the sea alive, the officer among them: eleven caught in the air by the knights, and six who slowed their own fall with what gravity they had left and were fished out by the pair. About twenty-eight are dead, among them the man who held Maeve and the mage who threw. Two hulls go into the sea, and the third, the one the first beam holed, breaks up on the way down. Nobody of Kaldo's dies. Arthur stands with a cuffed officer over his shoulder, knowing exactly what the minute he spent on him cost.

Chapter 102: Past the Window

In the hour after the beams, Emeric goes to Imani first, on her knees by the hatch cover where she sat down when the weight went. Under his sense her heartbeat is steady, fine, and just empty. She tells him to go to his sister. Zale has the compressions going again the moment the weight lifts, on Imani's count from where she sits, and Imani's hand is back on Maeve's head with what she has left going into the brain. Ferra calls the time. Maeve is still dead, too long now. She is past the window, and Imani says so, because a doctor does, and keeps her hand where it is anyway. He kneels with his sister and thinks of every way he could have left her home: told her the wrong day and sailed early; chained her; set watches on her day and night; put her to sleep until the fleet had gone. He does not care which way. He would give anything for her to be unharmed. He says none of it aloud.

The sky is turning. Three burning hulls have gone into the sea with their stores blown, and a dozen casters' water and lightning have been thrown into the wet air at the Band's foot. A storm spins up over the fleet, the cloud deck lowering and darkening, the first rain heavy, dense and wrong-tasting. A bolt comes for his exact position, on the deck beside her with his hand on her. He could move. He does not care. It hits him and chains through him into her, and Zale is thrown off her by it and gets up. Her heart starts. She was past the window and it worked anyway: the Band had soaked her body with mana, the storm's rain was dense with it, and she had been holding on in a way no Ostrellin body could. The strike leaves its mark down his left arm and across his back, his dragon body taking what a human's would not; Imani reads his heart that night and finds it sound.

Through Zale's eyes, Maeve wakes in the half-form on her brother's deck in the middle of the storm: long swept horns with a shorter pair beneath them, their tips blue-white; storm-blue and slate scales with lightning running in the seams; wings of slate bone and storm-cloud membrane; a long finned tail; gold eyes. She cancels it. The lightning stops. The spinning sky slows and comes apart. The rain goes soft and stops, and the sea flattens round the fleet. Zale is the first face she sees, and Emeric the second, and neither can say anything for a while. She looks at the burned edges of her suit and the marks coming up through them and tells Zale she did something stupid. He says he was there for it. The form drops that evening while she sleeps on the K Endeavor's ward, as Emeric's did after Sohra.

On Monday afternoon and night, through Ward's eyes, the knights bring the caught down to the K Amara's apron and the pair's decks, and the K Tam and Zale's launch work the water downwind of where the hulls went in, by eye and by the hull loops, since the Church crews carry no Kaldo beacons for the drums to fix. As Linnet says aloud, a man in the sea who does not shout is found by looking. They work until the light goes, then by the night glass's heat layer for anyone still warm. The man who held Maeve comes up dead in the K Tam's net at about four. The prisoners are drained by touch to human level by Imani, and by Armand once he can stand; drain cuffs go on every caster; every wound is treated by Imani and Bettany, because a patient is a patient; and they are held in the K Tam's hold under Nell Haddow's watch with a knight at the hatch. They see the hold, its hatch and the knight, and nothing of the class, the Forge's work, the Shell or the king. Ward tells the knight at the hatch, for the second time on the voyage, "Nobody teaches them anything."

That night on the K Amara's apron Ward tells Emeric, once, that they are prisoners of the crown, taken in Randillia's sky attacking the crown's fleet, and they go to the magistrate Keller at home, and nobody questions them past their names until he has them, because that is the law Kaldo keeps when it is angry. Then the other thing, flatly: the guns were on them. He had the 20 mm laid on the nearest hull before the beams, and once Maeve was off that deck he would have fired. The king did not have to. Emeric says he knows. Ward does not say it twice. The officer's order is sealed under the Gilded Eye in a Church cipher Ferra cannot break aboard. She gives it no more than an hour, writes its seal and length in her book, and puts it in her strong box under her own wax for Saban and Keller.

On Tuesday 5 January, through Armand's eyes, the old drum, its shell holed by a stray bolt in the fight, takes Emeric's coded release and comes down on its chute into the sea, where the K Tam fishes it out. The spare drum goes up on the rigs with the whole of Arthur's team round it, Mira at the inside edge where Maeve was, the K Amara standing off below, and Emeric on the water, empty to the core and told so by Imani in a voice nobody argues with, talking its decider awake from Zale's set through Ferra's cipher. The chain is green by noon. Relays aboard: six. The old drum's log, read on Zale's deck, says only another note, 03:12, refused, shut, because the lock let nothing else in.

In the K Endeavor's cold box, a small cut stone sits behind the gold of the dead man's Gilded Eye pendant. Emeric reads it with his note. It holds only a coded viewer, no memory piece, cut in a code he has never seen and cannot open. When Imani looks at the man's brain, it is scrambled in a way she has never seen either. To Ferra and Imani alone, Emeric lists it: engineered bodies, hidden hulls in the Band, control stones, coded stones, and now a way to look through a man. How long has the Church been this far ahead? He does not answer it.

That night Imani copies her times into her numbered book from Linnet Shaw's log of Ferra's calls: heart stopped 07:41; compressions 07:42; the weight 07:44; compressions stopped; 07:51 resumed; past the window by 07:56; strike 08:09; pulse. Under it she writes that she cannot say what the seven minutes cost, and that Maeve was holding on in a way she has never seen. Duncan, who watched from the K Discovery's rail a mile off as the king held the oldest mage alive flat to a deck until a ship's rail went under, was shown this, and what he is shown he can write. He writes in his warden's log for a long time. Emeric does not ask what, and does not ask him not to. Ferra notices both. In her own book she sets Maeve's case, a storm dragon woken in a storm with lightning already her base, below Zale's question and Harrold's and Corbin's answers. It proves little, because her base mirrored the change. Another confounded case. She does not draw the line yet.

On Tuesday evening, on the K Endeavor's ward, Emeric apologises to Ferra and Imani for the weight on that deck, and to Armand most of all, over and over, an engineer explaining a failure who cannot stop explaining it. Armand, propped up with his ribs bound, says an old man like him has not had a massage like that in a long while: it was almost to die for. He means it kindly. Emeric knows how close it was. Armand adds, because he is Armand, that he is glad to know at last what the king can do when he is not holding back, and would rather not know it twice.

Chapter 103: Examples

From Wednesday 6 to Friday 8 January the fleet goes low at the pair's pace along the chain toward Kaldo, about four hundred and fifty miles, the pair under the line and the K Tam with the prisoners astern of Zale's ship, drawing nothing. On Wednesday Maeve has asked to be moved from the ward to her brother's deck, so she can be on her brother's ship. She tells Emeric it was not his fault. He does not believe her. So she tells him again with examples and makes him repeat them back, the way she taught him to swim by pushing him into a river: she asked to come; she gave the reason, and it was a good one; she chose the word; she chose the rope; the man who threw did the throwing. He repeats them back, gets the fourth wrong, and does it again. He does not believe her any more than before, and he says them right the second time, and that is the scene. She does not mention the seven minutes, and neither does he. Imani's book does.

All Wednesday afternoon, under a clear sky, Maeve tries to call the change, and what comes is thin: a sheen of scale, the horns half up, and it slides off her in a few breaths. That night a squall comes down the chain, the air goes sharp, and it comes on its own, strong: scale up her forearms along the new scars, the small horns through her wet hair. She puts it away and it comes back, three times, until the rain passes. Zale, holding the lamp, says his needs the sea and hers needs the sky. By Friday Ferra has noticed that Maeve's forearms itch an hour before the glass falls, and writes it down.

Runa would not come near him on Monday night, because he smelled wrong to her, of the storm and of something she has no word for. On Wednesday morning she brings him his breakfast without being asked, sits on the floor by his berth with Mochi, and asks Grau to ask whether he is still angry. He says no. She asks Grau to ask whether he is angry at her. He laughs for the first time since Monday and says no in Kaldo, and she understands that one.

On Thursday, on Zale's deck, Maeve, seated by Imani's order and allowed ten minutes, makes him do something, because she has decided she is still herself and is going to prove it on him. He lays the Aperture again, this time with lightning, the far end of the tube closed so the charge runs out along the bore and back, and a baton of lightning stands straight off his hand at a stride's length for four seconds before shorting harmlessly into the deck's ground chain. It is the first time, a drilled trick rather than mastery, and it costs him by the second. She tells him it was ugly. He says he knows. She says do it again.

At dusk on Thursday, through Mira's eyes, after three days of almost nothing said between them, Maeve comes up the K Amara's ladder in a borrowed coat to the place on the apron where they ran the spear lessons and shows Mira the branching scars at her collar, which will never fade. Mira shows her own, the one Imani asked twice to take away and she kept so she would see it coming next time. One of them chose hers. The other did not get a say. Neither will hide them. Nothing else is said, and nothing has to be.

That night in their cabin on the K Discovery, Ferra asks him once whether he is sorry about the hulls. He is not, and says so, and then says he is not sure whether that is a problem. He tries to change the subject to the officer's order. She does not let him. She makes him sit with it until he has said the whole of it out loud: twenty-eight people went into the sea because he did not want to catch them, he would do it again, and that is the part that frightens him. Imani, on the other berth, says nothing and does not pretend to be asleep. It is not settled.

On Friday Neris, who watched it all from a mile off at the K Endeavor's rail, has her line ready for the museum: so that's your Church. The class did not sit on Wednesday or Thursday. On Friday Duncan holds the count on the K Endeavor's hospital deck for whoever comes, which is most of them, and does not say why. At dusk the fleet comes down on the water about forty miles out from the bay, below the town's horizon and outside the Shell's edge, the pair alongside. It does not go in that night, because Saban has the tenth, because Imani's check comes before anyone sets foot ashore, and because the King's Word comes the morning after the landing, not before. No gift set is given on the way home, since there is no stop and no host, and the people under the line are Kaldo's own. Twelve come home in the locker.

Chapter 104: Bring a Bottle

On Saturday 9 January Imani checks every soul aboard: about eight hundred and thirty of Kaldo's with the pair's two dozen, the guests Neris, Runa, Duncan, Gisela, Grau and Siv, the seventeen prisoners in the K Tam's hold, Maeve, Armand and the king. Bettany and the K Endeavor's medics take the hulls in turn by boat, and Imani takes the guests and the wounded herself. Nobody aboard carries anything she can find that Kaldo has not got, and she writes that down as a finding, not a guarantee. Nothing living crosses without her clearance, and the seed hand's crates are checked against her quarantine list one last time. That night is the last night out. Every diary goes into Ferra's boxes under wax on the deck where it was written, all eight hundred and thirty and the pair's, and Mira's last page for Monday holds the date and nothing else. The collection is locked in hull four's after hold under Ferra's wax. The ice cores in the K Discovery's cold room are checked and frozen. The night glass watches the swell at every watch for the last time on this voyage.

On Sunday 10 January, late in the morning, the fleet lifts low off the water and comes in over the bay at the pair's pace. The ring of eight spires stands round the city beside the Great Answer Tower, the second pattern lit whole, its faint edge past the walls and across the river reach. He drew it on a deck at Aumere in June over his own mistakes, and Tess, Armand and the Works built it while he was gone. He looks at it a long time and says nothing, and Ferra, beside him, says nothing either.

In the Sand Room, Tess watches the board. The fleet's hulls carry key stones, but the Shell reads every note in a crossing, and there are two dozen unkeyed notes in the fleet, the guests and the prisoners, so the whole crossing reads as unkeyed, and the mass rule holds every hull at the edge at any speed. The king's fleet stops a hundred yards off its own city, exactly as the king's second pattern says it should. Tess has the watch drop a face for them, since Kaldo's own come in by a face the watch opens, but first she rings the K Discovery on the open line and asks whether he brought the bottle. He did. She is insufferable. He is delighted that his design held him and says so, and the Sand Room's watch officer writes down that the king said "good" to being stopped.

About noon, through Ferra's eyes, the K Discovery and the pair come in to the castle quay while the big hulls lie on the bay. Tess is there with the bottle, and Bram says "the limpets" before he is off the gangway. Ferra hands Tess the line she wrote at the crevasse, because Tess will want it, and Tess asks for the old drum and has it on a cart by evening. Amara, in her golem body, reads her son in about four sentences, as she does on the line, and he tells her he will explain later the thing he could not say on an open line. She lets it go a second time. Harrold stands flat, the regent's seal on its chain. Maeve comes down the gangway herself with the scars at her collar under a borrowed coat, walks to her father, and tells him herself, with examples. The page gives Harrold's face, not his words. That night she rings Rowena at Emberfell on her mother's set and tells her too. At the back stands Corbin with a ledger under his arm, who has run Kaldo for nine months and done it well. Emeric sees him, and the guilt that has built on every call lands on his face. He does not deal with it on the quay. Tomorrow, after the Word. Saban has had the square swept.

The crews go home to their families on the quay, sworn to Ward's rule for one more night, their diaries still under wax; Saban says the secret will not hold two nights, which is why the Word is in the morning. The guests stay aboard the K Discovery at the quay one more night by their own leave, to come ashore with the Word, Runa first, because they cannot be hidden and so will not be. The prisoners go from the K Tam's hold to the castle's cells under the knights and the watch, for Keller in the morning. The ice cores go to the hospital's cold room for the Science museum, the seed hand's crates to Milford's quarantine shed, and the gift sets to the line room's locker. The town has seen the fleet come home, and a girl with a tail at a rail, and has been told nothing.

That night in the castle, Saban hears Arthur's account and Mira's report of the scout, reads Ferra's copy of the old drum's log and the seal on the officer's order, and works it out. The three hulls sat inside the winter drift at the third drum's line, their stones read as more drift and their iron-skinned crews as the gaps. They knew the week because Kaldo told them, by sweeping a square and billing a concert for the tenth. And they knew where, because the rendezvous mark went out in clear on Zale's ring of 17 December, on Ilenne's gift set into the Rim's ground relay, where Ferra's own card says anyone with a set and patience can hear. Who listened he cannot prove. The Rim's news is the winged couriers' trade goods, and a struck house there still has a tracing to sell. He writes both down as questions and accuses nobody. The Church knows the fleet is home: three hulls have not come back, its people in Kaldo watched the fleet come over the bay like everyone else, and by Sunday night the pulpits have it. Saban goes quiet for a long time. Then he changes three things before he goes to bed.

Where the Story Stands After Chapter 104

The fleet is home on Sunday 10 January, Year 18, the K Discovery and the pair at the castle quay and the big hulls on the bay, held for a moment at the edge of the city's Shell by the king's own rule. The crews are with their families under Ward's rule for one more night. Every diary is still under Ferra's wax, and the guests, Neris, Runa, Duncan, Gisela, Grau and Siv, wait aboard to come ashore with the King's Word on Monday 11 January. Seventeen Church prisoners sit in the castle cells for Keller, questioned only for their names, the officer with no name among them, his sealed order in Ferra's strong box unread. About twenty-eight Church crew died, one never found. Nobody of Kaldo's died on the whole voyage. The chain is whole again, and the old drum is on Tess's bench.

Maeve is alive, scarred for life across her collarbone, chest, stomach and side, carrying a storm dragon nobody knew about that comes strong with the weather and thin without it. She has told Harrold and Rowena herself. She and Mira have compared scars; what is between them is still unsaid, and waits for home. Emeric has crossed a line he cannot uncross: the beam, the weight on Zale's deck that nearly killed Armand, and twenty-eight people let fall. Ward told him the guns were on them and he did not have to. Ferra made him say aloud that he would do it again and that this frightens him, and it is not settled. Armand calls it almost to die for and would rather not know it twice. Duncan has written it in his warden's log, unread. Emeric has apologised. He owes his mother an explanation of the golem option and owes Corbin the truth tomorrow.

The Church saw something through a dead man's eyes on 4 January, and its pulpits already have the fleet's return. A coded viewer stone from the dead man's pendant sits in the hospital's cold box, and Emeric is asking how long the Church has been this far ahead. Saban has worked out the drift, the iron, the week, and the place said in clear over Ilenne's set, and has changed three things. Ferra's new heading, Kaldo's own sky, is open with no entries yet, the honest cost of a promise to the dragons that has to mean home too. Twelve gift sets, six relays and about sixty-nine tonnes of food came home.

This batch is about restraint and its breaking. Every precaution Kaldo took was real and still failed at the one place nobody was watching, and the king who kept still to keep his sister alive could not keep still once she was dead. What saved her was a word between two young women, a spear held at the edge, a doctor walking through a weight that should have flattened her, and a bolt from a storm his own rage had built.
