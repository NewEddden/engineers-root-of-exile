Book 2, Batch 14: When I Come Back

Chapters 89–90 | Tuesday 10 November – Saturday 14 November, Year 17

Part Three of Book 2 begins as the fleet leaves the Guano Isles on the water, with the bill there signed and paid and the winged ones' sky closed to it. Emeric now carries a mana core, Duncan Hale of the Westgone march is aboard as a guest with his breath count and his staff, and Liora, healed, is flying to Aumere on her own wings to join the fleet. Emeric's promise to the dragon matriarch Saldis still holds: no climb to the Band and nothing new in it until he answers her summons, which comes next. First the fleet returns to Aumere, where Zale's wife is queen and where, in July, Zale gave her his first wet promise: "When I come back."

A sharing is two people opening to each other at once through a stone, and Zale has never let anyone into his head, not a delver, not a doctor, not his brother. Two days out from Aumere he asks Emeric to teach him how. At Aumere the fleet comes back in by the crown's permit, on the water, with the K Amara kept outside the markers. Ilenne walks Emeric down a kilometre of new runway on the outer reef and names her price for it. Then Kaldo puts a sled in her water that combs metal-bearing lumps off the floor without touching the silt, and stops by itself the moment the water clouds, and the deal is signed on terms that give Aumere every gram of its own metal.

A Westgone warden whose house's stories are about the war stands before a stone cut two thousand years ago by the people on the other side of it, with no interpreter, and none needed. Zale and Ilenne share in the Keepers' hall by the grove without a word, because the door already said the words. And the fleet leaves for the dragon's cone without its pickup pair.

Chapter 89: When I Come Back

On Tuesday 10 November, once the Isles' ring is astern and the condition of passage has been kept to the letter, the fleet lifts low. Duncan, who has never been aboard anything that can fly, stands at the K Discovery's rail with his staff across his shoulders breathing the count and does not let go of the rail for an hour. Ward, staying aboard the K Discovery for the crossing to watch the lessons, stands beside him and says nothing, which Duncan takes as a kindness, and it is one.

On Wednesday Bram turns Duncan's second staff on the K Discovery's lathe from the same spare hardwood, to Duncan's measure: a man's height and a hand, no taper, no iron anywhere. Duncan weighs it, walks the deck with it as a pole, and hands it to Emeric without a word, which means yes. Mira comes to show off with her spear, having watched four days of the king being bad with a stick. She is fast, and Duncan cannot beat her to a touch, but each time her point reaches him he has already turned it with the staff's end. On the fourth pass he stops his own strike a finger's width from her ribs, on the bruised side, and holds it there. She sees what he is showing her: the stop. She learns something and says so, which nobody on deck expected. Bren, on light duty on a hatch cover, laughs until his ribs hurt. In the chart room Emeric begins teaching Duncan stasis, holding a drop of his own mana still inward the way padding is held. Duncan cannot do it for an hour, because thirty years of moving mana have taught his body that held mana is forcing. At hull four's bench Dagny starts building a sled frame while Bram grumbles.

On Tuesday night, after Duncan has gone below, Zale asks his brother to teach him to let someone in. Emeric asks why now. "Because I said I'd come back." Emeric understands what is being asked, and that his brother has never let anyone into his head, and that Zale knows he knows. Zale asks, "Have you done one?" "Once." Zale does not ask with whom; he knows. Emeric does not mention that there is another kind. A sharing normally needs both partners to delve. Merfolk do it as an ordinary rite, so the opening a partner needs must be only the first step of a delver's craft, and Emeric, the only delver aboard who has been inside a sharing, teaches Zale that step. In the chart room he holds a stone between them, the way the rite will: open, then hold still. The first night Zale cannot. Every time Emeric's attention touches the edge of him he shuts like a hatch in weather, and he laughs at himself, which helps. Emeric's instruction is plain: hold one thing you would give her, and open the hand. Not the whole of you; one thing. The rest comes back, because her piece is coming the other way. Emeric admits he has done this only once, three ways, through a bead, does not know how her people do it, and tells Zale to let her lead. On Wednesday night Zale holds open for a count of thirty, and for the first time in his life Emeric feels what his half-brother is like from inside: salt and engine oil and a stubbornness he recognises. He does not say so. Zale says, "That's enough of you in there," and both are glad.

Later that night Zale finds Emeric on the foredeck with Mochi asleep on his chest, flattened after a feast. Emeric does what he has just taught: open, then hold still, and he does not go in. Mochi opens from its own side, the first dense-world animal mind he has ever been let into, after the orc ox shut him out. What comes, all at once and without words, is hunger, warmth, the vault's cold, three times, a coat lining, Emeric seen from below, and safe. Its colour goes from pearl to a warm amber. Zale asks, "Did it let you?" "It let me." From then the bond grows: Mochi comes when he calls across a deck, and the bead needs refreshing less often.

On Wednesday at the rail, Zale asks Emeric whether his ship can stay at Aumere. Not for Aumere's sake but for his own: six weeks with his wife after four months away, and he would be no use at the top of the world on a ship that cannot climb to it. Carrying the surface pair slung under the big hulls to the north pole and back would mean about nine thousand miles of dead weight and would make the pickup pair useless the whole way. At the watch change Ward objects: the pair is the fleet's pickup, and without it a hull holed over the Western Ocean or the polar sea has nobody under it. Emeric answers that every hull floats and runs on her diesels, the fleet flies in company so each hull is the others' pickup, the relay net finds anyone who goes down, and the second relief meets them about 22 November. Ward accepts it and does not like it, and asks Ferra to write his objection in his column. Ferra writes the terms that night. Zale's ship and the K Tam stay together inside Aumere's gate by the crown's permit, with Nell Haddow commanding the K Tam. They will meet the fleet on its homeward leg, over open sea, at a place and day set by relay in December, and come home under it as its pickup. Abloh stays aboard Zale's ship and spends six weeks with the Rim's sea-silk weavers. Tess's gravity anchors were built for Zale's ship, and Bram offers them back. Zale says, "Keep the limpets. Bring them back." Linnet will keep the ship's set on the fleet's watch every night, and Zale's ship will ring the fleet once a week. On the K Amara's set Zale tells his full sister Maeve, who is going to the top of the world without him, not to do anything stupid in the cold. Maeve says she will save the stupid things for when he is there to watch.

Ferra rings Ilenne's gift set from the K Discovery's radio room. Its mast rides a glass float up through the water, and the queen's voice comes down through the Rim's new ground relay's cable. It is how a land fleet asks a sea queen's leave to come home. The crown's permit says: in on the water from the Grey Mark under the watch's escort, the K Amara and escort three outside the fetch line as before, nothing in the air over the ring. Ferra writes her line of reasons for the stop: Zale home, because his word to his wife is the one promise on the voyage that is his and not the king's; the field seen and the queen's price asked, because she said to ask on return; the nodule deal signed with the dwarves told first, because Aumere has not been paid a fair gram for the nodules since the Sea Hold priced them in August; and water and stores before the cone and the long leg north. Neris goes home to her family's workshop on the Rim by her contract's right, and Ferra does not ask when she will be back, because asking would be the opposite of the contract.

At dawn on Thursday, Taurre brings the watch boat out to the Grey Mark, logs eight hulls, six to come in and two to stand off, and leads the six in on their diesels. The Challenger stays slung under the K Endeavor, since her berth was refused. Ward goes with the K Amara outside the markers by his own choice: he crossed them once in June and the queen ruled on it, and he will not make her rule twice. Arthur commands the knights inside. At noon Zale's ship comes in last under the watch boat's bow, Linnet at the rail and the crew pretending not to watch the breakwater. Ilenne is on it, as she was when he left. She does not wave, because queens do not, and goes blue from the throat down in front of the whole Rim. Zale does not wait for the gangway. He goes over the side and comes up at her feet in the water, because he can. Ferra writes nothing about it in her book.

That afternoon Sorrel walks the outer reef with Ilenne, Emeric, Ferra, Bram and the Rim crew's chief. In four and a half months the hand crews, using the Rim's gravity stones for heavy lifts, have built the whole first one and a half kilometres of ash-cement sea wall and some of the second stretch, rolled the lava-rock and black-sand fill behind it, and paved about a kilometre. The Rim's ground relay stands at the field's head, its mast in the trade wind and its cable down the reef wall to the bell nearest the gate. Not yet done are the last half-kilometre of paving, any lamp line or windsock, the apron, the second stretch, and the iron quay, half its piles in. Sorrel walks the paved kilometre at a pilot's pace looking for the thing she would hate to land on, and finds it already handled: the surface has a crown, high along the centre and falling to both edges so rain runs off. The chief did it without a drawing, because a sea people knows where water goes, and Sorrel likes him at once. Bram finds the one thing they did not know: the paving is poured in long unbroken lengths, and slabs that long crack across in the heat. He shows the chief where to saw the joints, the chief asks for the saw, and Bram gives him his own. Ferra pays the second instalment in bar iron and coin, and on her chart, spread on a bollard, writes that routes on from this hub to the Sun Shore must go round the Guano Isles: south of here, round the isles, on the water if at all.

At the end of the paved kilometre, with the surf on the wall, Ilenne names her price: "Take my people to the First City." The wreck of the floating city lies in Ostrellin's outer western water, too thin for any of her people to stay in, and their oldest memorial is a place nobody has stood since the war. The old families of the shallows want it more than anyone, and it would pay her coalition, which broke with Ushar for her, and honour her dead. She does not say the last reason, and Ferra writes it down anyway: it would put Kaldo's trust on her people's doorstep, where they can see it. Emeric says yes: after the return, in the Challenger, with mana-held cabins and Imani's dosed water, the old families first. "The hull she wouldn't berth beside her domes," Ilenne says. "Yes." She enjoys it. He names the catch himself. The old families remember a party that went back early whose stones went dark, and he does not know what the dragons will make of her people in Ostrellin's water. Ilenne says she lived a year in that water half dead and came home a queen, and then says what he is really afraid of before he can: it was the thin water, not the dragons, that killed that party. Imani's numbers will decide it, not either of them. Ferra writes it as the field's return term, in both hands, the crown's and Kaldo's. Emeric then asks for the sea grove's seed, as Ilenne told him to in June. She sends him to the Keepers, whose office it is, since a queen who overruled them over a seed would be a queen who overrules them over anything. Because pressing his core draws on the air, he also asks the host's leave for that. "We breathe the sea. Take all the air you like," Ilenne says, the closest thing to a joke she has made as queen. Ferra laughs, and the crew chief does not see what is funny.

That night, before anything is signed, Ferra rings Kettrin's set at the orc treaty-house and Jofrid's at the Anvil in the same hour, so the dwarves hear Kaldo's nodule terms in full, formally and from Kaldo's own mouth, before anyone signs them.

Chapter 90: The Water First

Before dawn on Friday 13 November, on the K Discovery's foredeck at the gate, Imani reads Emeric before and after three hours of pressing his core, no more, because a deal is waiting and the mind is the brake. Duncan keeps the count. The core is a little larger than at the lock, still shut, still in the pit of his stomach, and there is no tracery. She writes it in the week's column. He eats breakfast this time. There will be no core flight at Aumere: nothing of Kaldo's goes into the air over the ring without the crown's permit, and he does not ask, because the First City drowned under dry people's fliers. The knights notice and say nothing. There is no class either. Arthur asked on the crossing whether "after the cone" meant the night after, and Imani told him it means after she has read one core for a week, and the week ends Sunday.

Mid-morning, on the salvage quay where Bram scratched the first lump in June, Dagny's sled goes into the water. She built it in two days from Bram's drawing and hull four's spares: a steel frame the size of a handcart, four gravity stones to float it a hand above the floor, two water stones to push it at walking pace, a comb wheel whose tines dip just under a lump and lift it onto a coarse sieve so the silt falls back where it came from, a clarity eye, and a lift basket that rises on its own stone. Lirrae's divers take it down on a tether to a test patch about sixty feet down on the caldera floor, with Ilenne in the water and the court's iron buyer, the bench's assayer and Ressar watching from the quay. Imani has drawn a water bottle from the patch beforehand for her own count. The sled hovers, the comb turns, black lumps come up the sieve and into the basket, and the silt stays on the floor. The basket comes up on its own stone with the tether slack. Then Lirrae tests it the way she once tested Emeric's pumping idea: she swims down and stirs the floor with her tail until the water clouds. The comb stops by itself, and starts again when the water clears. Imani's second bottle reads as clear as her first.

On the quay afterward, still wet, Ilenne admits she spent the whole morning waiting for the part where the dry people strip her floor. She did not expect him to have thought about the water at all, and did not know he knew so much of it. Lirrae, behind her, says she told the court a jar proved him wrong in a minute. Ilenne says, "And yet." Emeric answers with something small and true: a works upstream turns a river grey for every town below it, and he has watched it happen at home. The silt on her floor is her people's air. Imani writes the clarity eye's mark in her book. At the Rim's smiths' yard, Neris tells her people the truth about the voyage: the orcs, the dwarves' holds, the desert, the winged ones' bill, and the coal. She tells the smiths she objected to Emeric's face, on paper, under her own name, and lost. Then she tells them what the deal does: the metal comes back to Aumere refined, from their own gravel, at a fraction of the old price. A smith says it is not the same as smelting their own. "No. It isn't," says Neris. She defends Kaldo no further than what is true, and Imani writes it down as the honest version of the deal. By noon the treaty-house's clerk rings back that the Sea Hold's factor has the notice, and Jofrid answers for the holds in one sentence: they will bid to refine.

That afternoon Duncan asks to see the teaching stones, because a march-warden walks toward what frightens him, and the merfolk are the other people his house's stories say the old land wronged. Ilenne gives him a land guest's leave to the Keepers' hall's hard dome, with Armand, who reads the old court script and catches more of Duncan's tongue than anyone, and Ressar. Duncan has no Kaldo and no merfolk speech, and the Keepers have no orc, but the stone needs no interpreter. Ressar shows him the one children learn the war from: the land's fires on the shore, cut deep, and the dead in the shallows, cut shallow, because they were shallow. Duncan stands before it a long time. His house's roll has the same war from the land's side, in the same year, and the dead on its side are the households that would not go to the rite. He says something in Westgone, and this time Armand translates it for Ressar: we left because of the men who did this. Ressar's colour answers, and nobody translates that. It does not resolve Duncan's fear. It gives it a size, which is what a warden wants. Armand asks whether the stone may be drawn and shown in Kaldo. Ressar says, "That one, yes. The land should see what it did." Armand draws it himself in his numbered book, with Ressar's leave written under it in Ilenne's hand, the first piece of the merfolk's own record the museum is allowed to show. Ressar also answers Emeric about the grove's seed: not yet, and not no. The Keepers' condition is the old one, that the shallows are part of the price. When the old families of the shallows have looked on the First City from Kaldo's hull, ask again, and the Keepers will give a seed and the method of its deep steep together. Ferra sees that the crown and the Keepers have agreed the same price for their own reasons, and records it as an answer. That evening Hila's gift set rings the fleet from the desert, with Wafa's letter read out by the Lift's keeper: the dry edge's kitchens and orphanage are open under the mutual society, every child fed, no High Mother, and the Given back at work.

That night Zale goes down the stages, thinks once of widow-making, and puts it away. In the Keepers' hall by the sea grove, Below, where the teaching stones are kept and the grove's light comes through the water, he walks the hall with his gills in the dark he can see in. Ressar keeps the hall, since the Keepers keep the stones, but is not in the delve. Yssaure brings the rite stone through which she and Ilenne's father shared, an heirloom, and a widow who carries the dead king's piece gives her daughter the door she went through. She says nothing, her colour held still; it is the first thing she has done in the story that was not under someone's pressure, and Ilenne knows it. Maeve watches through the glass of the hall's hard dome, brought in from the K Amara on the watch boat with Ward's leave, for Zale's house, because she is not going to miss it. Emeric is not there; it is not his. Nothing is said in the water, because a thing said in the water before witnesses is forever, and they said their forever at the door in May. The stone is held between their two hands, one thing in an open hand. Each sends one piece and takes the other's, faint, the way a smell brings back a room. He carries something of hers: her father's voice saying her name, which she has not heard in two years. She carries something of his: the weight of a net coming up full at first light on his first boat, the line through his hands. From now on he feels her the way you feel the sea under a hull, and she feels him the same. There are costs, each thought once. The distance: he leaves again in six weeks, and she will feel him go and feel every frightened hour after. The danger: widow-making is a crime now, but a shared husband killed leaves his wife carrying his piece for life. And the door: he has let somebody in, for good. He would rather have been shot again, he thinks, and then finds he does not mean it. Dead zones silence a sharing, and he knows that is what took the First City. The two of them walk out of the Keepers' hall in the dark, and that is all the page sees.

At dawn on Saturday a winged one comes in off the sea, low and tired after about two hundred miles on her own wings in two days, and lands on the K Endeavor's ladder deck with a letter in her father's hand for Imani. Liora is aboard by her own choice, and she did not break her elders' word on their water. By noon Imani has given her a ward berth and a job. Ward asks who decided this. Ferra says, "She did." In Imani's ward she will learn Kaldo's tongue from Bettany and the medics, with Runa appointing herself teacher of the rude words, and she speaks almost nothing aloud except Kaldo words to patients. The story of her face is already travelling Aumere's outer cliffs by courier faster than the fleet. At some point on the voyage home Liora says to Imani, "They're calling it your eyes." "I know." "They're my hands." "I know."

On the gate's quay at dawn, Zale comes up the stages and sits beside Emeric on a bollard. "She's in here." "Yes." That is the whole conversation. Ferra and Imani, on the K Discovery's deck above, can feel each other and him, look at each other, and leave the brothers alone. In the K Endeavor's ward Imani clears Bren for full duty, four weeks less a day after the dragon, ribs, lung and forearm healed without a mark, and the knight's forearm stitches come out the same morning. Nobody is off the board. Between the quay and the trade hall, Ferra at last finds Davrel's family through Taurre's record: his mother and a brother at a kiln yard. She brings what the ledger says is owed in Kaldo iron and says what she did and why. They will not take it. The brother says money from the hand that did it is not restitution, it is a receipt. Ferra does not argue. She leaves the line open in her book, closes it in her own mind, and does not tell Emeric until they are out past the fetch line.

On Saturday morning in the queen's trade hall on the Rim, Ferra reads the nodule terms aloud before anyone signs. Aumere owns every nodule and every gram of metal refined from them. Kaldo charges a published refining fee, its cost and a fixed margin, paid in metal at assay value or in coin, at Aumere's choice. Kaldo will buy nickel and cobalt at a published fair price only if Aumere wants to sell, and Aumere may sell to anyone, the dwarves included. The tools are built at cost and owned by Aumere, Dagny's sled first, and Aumere's own delvers will be taught to key and mend them. The water comes first: the Keepers choose which fields are worked and which rest, Imani's water checks travel with the assays, and the crown can stop any field any day. Assay is by Aumere's own bench, with books open both ways. Either side may end it with a season's notice. The dwarves were told before signing, their contract stands, and they may bid to refine too. And at the top, before Ilenne opens her mouth, Emeric adds the line she came to the hall to ask for: the vent crusts are left alone, because the vents warm the basin and make Aumere livable, and scraping them would cloud the water her people breathe. She does not have to ask, and she notices, the same thing she noticed on the quay. Ferra says once what each side gets, so the room hears it is both ways. Aumere gets copper, manganese and iron it has never been able to make, at a fraction of the old price, from gravel it threw on its paths, and keeps its water. Kaldo gets a steady, fair source of nickel and cobalt for turbine blades and plating, and a partner rather than a client. Ilenne signs for the crown, Emeric for Kaldo, the bench's assayer on the assay clause and Ressar for the Keepers on the water clause. The refinery itself will be built at home after the return.

Before the leaving, Neris comes up the K Endeavor's ladder with a sea chest of her own work. She is going to Kaldo, because she wants to see the place that made Bram and Tess, and because someone from the Rim should check what the land says about the Rim before it says it in a museum. Ferra does not ask her to say it twice. No gift set is given at Aumere. Aumere has its set, given in July to the person Kaldo likes most there, and a second set at her court would be a line into the crown's water that went round the queen, a season after a great house was struck for running its own. Thirteen stay in the locker. The seed hand's crates take nothing living this time. Ward and Duncan talk on the quay about short shifts.

About midday the fleet goes out through the fetch line on the water behind Taurre's boat. The K Amara and escort three rejoin outside the markers, and the six air hulls fly low over the sea, north-east, toward Saldis's cone, about 250 miles away, with every rack full and the promise kept. This time Zale stands on the breakwater beside the queen. The gift set that rang Zale's deck in July does not ring.

Where the Story Stands After Chapter 90

Zale kept his word. Taught by Emeric to open, he shared with Ilenne in the Keepers' hall through the rite stone her parents used, without a word spoken, and now each feels the other at any distance, a bond that dead zones silence. Zale's ship and the K Tam stay at Aumere by the crown's permit, to meet the fleet on its homeward leg at a place and day set by relay in December and come home beneath it as its pickup. The polar leg flies without them, over Ward's recorded objection. The gravity anchors stay in the K Endeavor's hold.

Aumere and Kaldo are partners now. The runway is half built and well built, with Bram's joints to be sawn. Ilenne's price is agreed: Kaldo will carry the old families of the shallows to the First City after the return, in the Challenger, with Imani's numbers deciding. The Keepers will give a grove seed and its method together after the First City. The nodule deal is signed with Aumere owning every gram, the vent crusts left alone at Emeric's own word, the dwarves told first, and the holds bidding to refine. Neris has chosen to come to Kaldo to check what the land says about her people. Liora has flown two hundred miles to join Imani's ward. Davrel's family refused Kaldo's restitution, and the line stays open in Ferra's book.

Duncan has held stasis for a count of three, taught Mira the stop with a staff, and stood before the merfolk's war stone, giving his fear a size; his letters remain unopened and Ward still has not said he likes him. Emeric's core grew a little at Aumere with no tracery, Imani's week of watching ends Sunday, and the class will begin on the climb out of Saldis's sky, with Runa first. Mochi has let Emeric into its mind and turned amber. Bren is back on full duty. The fleet flies toward Saldis's cone to answer her summons, with seven drums, thirteen gift sets and about 75 tonnes of food, and on the K Amara Maeve has promised her brother she will save the stupid things for when he is there to watch.

This batch is a homecoming and a farewell in one: a man learning to let someone in, a queen paid in respect rather than coin, and a fleet that leaves its pickup pair behind out of love, knowing the cost and choosing it anyway.
