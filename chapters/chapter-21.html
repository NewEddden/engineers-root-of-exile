Book 3, Batch 2: Drift

Chapters 10–18 | Monday 30 August – Sunday 31 October, Year 18

Waylon and Alina are married, and Waylon has changed for the first time, a sand dragon on Duvrenne's own ground. Mathilde and three people at the venue are dead. The Isle Works Review sits in Kaldo and has refused the king twice, and he has obeyed twice. The guard's core sits in Kaldo's case of record with recall and halt stripped and Tess's sworn finding that she will not sign it safe; the Compact has been asked to sell it, Mother Sabbe has said yes, and Duvrenne has not answered. A thirty-metre frame stands empty-chested on the Cradle at South Sentinel. On the Isle the cove's fish have gone thin, the king felt something large in the sea at the wedding and put it aside, and Mother Sabbe's goats will not go down to the beach.

This batch is about the drift: the young of a beast nobody knew was loose coming ashore on two coasts, killing a watchman and two cutters, and being found out not by the king's sense but by a clerk who remembered a line she copied. Kaldo learns a mother is loose and cannot follow her. Every door the king tries is shut by someone with the right to shut it, and when he asks the Assembly for a law it rewrites the law against him and he carries it out as written. The trigger he named at the end of Book 2, the first beast part sold by anyone not Kaldo's, arrives on Kesrath's quay, and the count begins.

Under the secrecy of a sealed works the giant on the Cradle gets its heart. A pilot closes a four-metre hand, stands waist-deep in the bay, and walks forty paces on rock. The batch ends at the opening of the Grand on Hollow Night, with half the children dressed as the thing that came in on the tide, and nobody yet able to tell its story.

Chapter 10: The Sale

On Thursday 2 September, at Kesrath's tower, through Alina's eyes, Duvrenne's council, the same ministers who asked on 29 August why their guard failed, now ask what Randillia wants with the core. Oskel says he does not know and does not care. The thing killed two of his men at the gate in April, he wants it off the island one way or another, and he has put a soldier's price on it, what a thirty-metre carcass's heart is worth to the only people who can keep it from crumbling: about six thousand Gold Rand for Duvrenne's third. Alina argues for the sale. The council writes one condition: whatever it goes into never crosses Duvrenne's half of the Isle without Kesrath's leave. On Friday 10 September, in Vantic's counting house, Duvrenne's observer signs for Kesrath, Mother Sabbe gives the Islanders' share for nothing by her mark over the Claim's set, saying it is a thing that kills people and to keep it, and the crown's share is the crown's. The king still does not say what it is for, and nobody asks twice. Vantic notices that the condition is about a thing it goes into, and files that away.

From Monday 13 to Thursday 16 September, under the covered berth of the Cradle, through Tess's eyes, the shut case is opened in the lab for the last time. Tess reads the stripped core whole once more through the sealed reader stone before it goes near the frame, and finds nothing new. She still will not certify it, and says why in one line: not finding a third note is not the same as there not being one. Iron does not stop a vibration. So the machine is made safe around the core rather than by trusting it: a mundane kill switch under the pilot's hand and in the second seat, and Ottoline's sprung brakes that lock every joint when the link drops. The rewrite is signed and sealed, and the whole thing stays a sealed works on the Roll. On Thursday evening Tess tells Wren plainly what she is getting into: the core came out of the thing that killed fourteen people; two of its hidden orders were taken out and Tess cannot swear there is not a third; the kill switch is under Wren's own hand; and she can say no now or stop at any moment, and nobody will ask why. Imani has the last word on Wren's body and senses and says she will use it. Wren says yes before Tess has finished, and then says it again once Tess has.

On Friday 17 September, through Wren's eyes, the giant lies on its back on the dock. Wren floats in the sphere in its head with the crown on, feeling the giant's body as her own and that body flat on its back. Imani watches her pulse at the sphere's open hatch, Tess sits in the second seat with the head stone, and Sorrel holds the card on a clipboard. Wren tries to move one finger, and the whole four-metre hand closes, because the guard's gait knew grips, not fingers. Nobody says anything for a while. Then everybody does. Tess writes "grip, not finger" on the card.

The deed the clerk reads at Vantic's table has five lines: Kaldo's crown owns the core outright; Duvrenne keeps no right to copy or inspect it; the condition runs with the core and binds any later holder; it cannot be sold or given on without Kesrath's leave, so the condition cannot be sold away; and the holder answers under the Compact for any harm it does. Vantic files the last line with the rest under a thing it goes into.

Chapter 11: Drift

From the night of Saturday 18 September to Monday 20, in the dark, where she is helpless and nobody sees her, something gives birth in the kelp shallows off the Isle's cove, eight or so pods a night over three nights. The Islanders' night boats see lights in the water three nights running and call it sea glow, which every fisherman has seen, and the September ebb sets hard toward the mainland. On Tuesday 21 September, at dawn, through Mother Sabbe's eyes, she goes down alone with her stick to the beach her goats still will not go to. At the kelp line a grey animal the size of a dog, plated like wet basalt, with two gold-veined eyes too big for its head, looks at her. She saw that face come up the shaft on 19 April. She backs away without running, being old while it is young, and it does not follow. It goes up the beach toward the Islanders' stone store, with its lamp stones and the cutters' fire stones. Nobody on the Isle dies, because the goats would not go down and she keeps the children up.

Through Waylon's eyes, he and the Claim's watch find it inside the store with the door bent in and the stones going dark. Crown rifles on tracked issue chip its plates at close range, and two of the watch at its legs with pikes do the rest; it takes eleven rounds and a pike through the throat fan. A second lies under the sand at the store's back wall, and Waylon feels its weight through the ground before anyone sees it. Both heads go into shut cases at the Claim, using the guard's spare copper gaskets in the condenser shed, and the K Endeavor, on the Kesrath shore for Duvrenne's oil, flies them home on Wednesday. On Thursday on Duvrenne's half Oskel's men kill a third on the tower's beach with their powder guns, and a fisherman cuts out its claws and its two eyes, puts the eyes in a jar of spirit sealed with wax, and takes them across to the Saturday market on Kesrath's quay.

On Thursday night, at a watchtower east of Saltreach on Waylon's own coast of the Narrow March, a pup comes up the shingle to the tower's lamp-stone store in the dark. Lowen Hennet, the watchman on the store, goes toward the noise with a lantern, thinking it is a dog after the bread. He is only on the store that night because Peder Brisk asked him to swap so Peder could be home for his daughter's first tooth. It kills him. The watch holds the tower till light, the side door barred, a lantern over the window, a runner sent, and at dawn they kill it at the store door with the rifles they carry under their public licence, fourteen rounds at close range.

Through the eyes of Merryn Pellow, a fowler from the March marshes whose fowling gun was bought back in June under the Act, she is out at first light with her dog when the dog stops in the reeds and will not go on, whining at a sluice. She goes back for the watch instead of going in, and they kill a second pup in the sluice's culvert. She asks the corporal for a rifle and is told the Act does not let her have one. "Then who does it let?" Nobody on the shore knows yet. The March's boatmen, who have watched pods and dead pups wash up along the tide line, already have a name for them: drifters, because they come in on the tide like drift. That afternoon Waylon crosses to Saltreach, his coast and his watchman dead, and stands at Lowen Hennet's grave with Lowen's mother and with Peder Brisk, who will not stop saying it should have been him. One unhatched pod is found on the shingle, and Waylon has it burned on the beach where it lay before anyone can think of a price for it. Merryn watches it burn, and learns the boatmen's word for what was in it.

Chapter 12: Born

On Thursday 23 and Friday 24 September, in Tess's office, the two pup heads' cases are opened in turn with the shutters up. Behind each pair of eyes is a grain of written crystal the size of a lentil. Tess reads them through a sealed reader stone, never bare, with Armand on the clock and Emeric in the room but not in the stone. Both marks say the same words in the same order, and it is her first real line of the language. Two words she can read and one she cannot. The first is the same word the guard's core used for home in the recall note she stripped in July: the place the cradle calls from, under the Isle. In front of it is a word the guard's core never had anywhere in it: the guard's sign for its own body, written as if it came from another body. This one was born. What it leaves open is whether the cradle under the Isle is printing again, or whether something it made in April has bred. In open sitting Tess's paper will say the mark is the animal's own, that it reads born and under the Isle, and that one word is unread, and the king will say that a second animal came out of the hollow in April and these are its young, which is true. What else she holds back goes to the chair under the review's ordinary rule for sealed evidence.

On Wednesday Kaldo News's late sheet prints Waylon's four sentences from the Claim's set: a grey animal the size of a dog, gold in its eyes, which the Islanders took for a seal pup. On Friday, at her desk at the records hall, Honor Linden finds her own copy-book page from August, when she fair-copied the K Endeavor's log for 19 April for the review's site-visit record: seal, small, into the water off the cove rocks, 09:41. The log runs forty pages for that day, and the review asked for it to fix when the charges and the waking happened, so nobody read that line twice. She spends the harvest festival on Saturday not at the festival. She does not take it to the Heirs. On Monday morning she hands Keller the page before the sitting. The watch's festival sheet crosses her desk the same night: a drunk carter threw fire at an orc interpreter by the cider stalls, the interpreter's yellow band caught it with a scorched sleeve and nothing else, the street stones' strike flare told the watch where, the caster's own spike told them who, and they had him in four minutes. Armand's street law, no fire in the street, is now a charge with a name on it, the Casting Code's first case. Honor files it straight and adds nothing.

On Monday 27 September, through Emeric's eyes, Keller reads the line into the record himself and asks who on the K Endeavor wrote it; the deck watch, a Kaldo seaman, confirms it over the line room's set from the hull. Keller says that having a record is not the same as knowing what is in it. Ward is called and says it under oath, flat: in the gallery on 19 April he heard something small going away toward the cove in the dark and did not follow it, because he had five people to get out, and logged nothing. He had a man dying. The king owns his own part: at the wedding, when the venue's zone cleared, he felt something in the sea off the cove and put it aside. Tess's paper is read. Dacey asks a question and answers half of it herself: the goats kept them alive. The crews' man says it aloud: three people half-saw it, and the record put it together. Something else left the hollow on 19 April, small, it has had five months in the sea, and these are its young. Nobody says the word until Imani says it from the gallery, because she is the one who knows what a thing that gives birth is called: mother. Kaldo News prints the sitting whole that night, Honor's page under her name. The Heirs' angrier half prints it too, under beasts of the crown. Honor says nothing either way, and this time nobody misreads it.

On Sunday 26 September, through Emeric's eyes, he, Ferra and Imani ask Runa together whether she'll let them adopt her, with Keller's form on the kitchen table so she can see it's a real thing. She says no and goes to bed. Ferra puts the form in a drawer and doesn't throw it away.

Chapter 13: Outside the Fence

On Tuesday 28 September, in the long room, Corbin asks whether the king will now write an order. No. He will ask the Assembly for a law. Corbin drafts it in a week, angrier than in July and faster, because it is his Act and he knows every clause it has to fit.

The same day the king asks the review, in writing, for no emergency. He asks it to say whether its condition covers the beasts, and to put to the doors' families, by Kettrin's set, whether they will lift their condition early for a listening post on the shaft's cap, since the condition is theirs and not the review's to break. The post is not for the mother, who is outside the hollow and whom nobody's pipe would find. It is for the hollow: whether anything else in it still beats, as the Ostrel letter's "more than one room" suggests, and whether it could come out the way the guard and the mother did. On Friday 1 October Keller rules flatly. The condition stands whole: nothing touches the hollow, the zone or the dead, and nothing goes through the fence, man or beast hunter. The beasts are not the review's to protect or to license; outside the fence they are an ordinary danger that the ordinary law and the crown's ordinary officers answer. Keller's sentence for the record: this review enforces a condition it agreed to; it makes no law and no crime, and hunting is the Assembly's. The review records that the king asked for no emergency. Inside the zone a beast is killed only if it endangers someone in the act, nobody digs or follows one below ground, and every such case is reported and read to the doors' families. Anyone may listen from outside the fence. The review adds to the questions its finding must answer: what else the hollow holds. And the early listening post is the doors' families' to give or refuse, and Keller puts it to them himself.

That Friday the Register of Drifters opens in Tess's language office, public, its first page printed in Kaldo News: the March boatmen's word, Drifter, and one line per beast, giving the date, the place, who killed it, its size, its mark's read, where its parts went, and for licensed kills the rifle's tag. Its public pages give the mark only as read or unread with a plain-word gist; a restricted appendix holds the rest.

The day before, on Friday 1 October, after Ferra's read, the king had his seventh lesson on Sohra's set and Waylon his first, patched through from the Isle. Sohra asks whether the gold-eyed are all going to come out the right way round from now on. Waylon's first question, in the first minute, is how you know where you end. That, Sohra says, is the right question, and his father has not asked it in six months. From then on Waylon goes whole most nights on the dunes on Duvrenne's half with Alina keeping watch on a camp stool, and on 29 October the tail comes off for the first time. At the tideshell he had stayed a man partly because the whole was still the only dragon he had.

On Saturday 2 October, in the morning, Saban reports to the long room. His man in Kesrath, on the venue team's hire since August, heard about the Saturday market. The fisherman sold the claws to a carver and the two gold-veined eyes in their waxed jar to a buyer nobody on the quay knew, for new-struck imperial gold, and the buyer took passage north that afternoon; Saban's man lost him at the quay. The coin's mint year matches the gold that paid the labour broker who hired the venue team, which Saban says is worth very little as a lead. Nobody says "not Ostrel." Emeric says it aloud, as he said at the end of Book 2 he would: a beast part sold by someone who is not Kaldo's. "That's the day. Start counting." Ferra enters Saturday 25 September in the heartbeat ledger as day one, under the line where the October heartbeat went out on time, and keeps two columns, days since the trigger and sales. The switch still holds. Duvrenne's chapels add the young to beasts of the crown the next Sunday, the Narrow March hears it from the pilgrim carts, and the Velmoor and Solreth envoys write home that the beasts keep coming from Randillia's island, which is true. Doran reads Ostrel's circular and does not preach it. Ferra writes the Saturday in September at the top of the new column, and the king says, "That's the day. Start counting."

Chapter 14: Waist-Deep

That afternoon, Saturday 2 October, in the east bay off South Sentinel, the Cradle sinks to set the depth and the water comes in over the dock to a giant's waist. Mira and Maeve fly chase overhead in their suits and keep the sky clear, because a test nobody sees from the Band stays secret. Wren is in the crown, Tess in the second seat on the head stone, Sorrel calling the card from the dock's rail one line at a time, and Imani with her hand up, ready to stop it. Sorrel came to the dock from the biplane's first real flight that week, three circuits over the farms off the Sky Yard's grass strip and down hard on the second bounce, and checks the brakes while she talks about it. Through Sorrel's eyes the brakes are armed, not locked, so either seat's stop drops every joint into the brakes' grip. It wobbles. It stands. It takes one step in the water and stops on Sorrel's call. Sorrel writes the time down. Imani's hand is never needed. Wren cries in the crown and denies it over the speaking tube while everybody can hear her. Climbing out onto the dock, she reaches for the rail with her own hand and waits for half a second for a four-metre hand to move, then misjudges the ladder's first rung by a foot, because her legs feel too short. Imani writes both down, sits her on a crate, and sets a rule: an hour off for every hour in the crown, and nobody pilots twice in a day until they know what that half-second is. The machine is named on the card now: the Bulwark.

Ward watches from the berth's edge with his arms folded, and when it is sitting again he asks Emeric how soon he can have one. Emeric says when the next core is read. Ward says he will go and get one. Emeric says it back to him as a warning: every core on that island belongs to somebody, the next one he can "get" will be somebody else's to sell or refuse, and this one took two months and three owners' signatures to buy. On Monday 4 October Arthur, Mira and Bren go to the Isle with a knights' squad and one of the K Amara's dragon rifles to hunt outside the fence under Keller's ruling, and Neris goes with them at her own asking. Ward stays. Imani's rule for hunters goes out in Kaldo News and to every depot: gloves, no bare hands in the blood, a cloth mask when cutting or boxing, report any cut or splash the same day, and every hunter and watchman who handles a carcass goes on her register. Kaldo sends iron and two smiths to seal the Islanders' stores, and only iron and drawings to Duvrenne's half, because Oskel will not have Kaldo's men in his sheds, so Duvrenne's smiths seal their own, more slowly. The Bulwark sits in the water again with its pilot denying she cried, and a knight on the berth asks how soon he can have one.

Chapter 15: Grey Track

On Wednesday 6 October, through Neris's eyes, she puts her hands on the footings of the hollow's fence posts, not inside them, with the Islanders' watch beside her. In April she heard one heart with an echo. Now she hears nothing beating at all. She tells the watch, then Arthur, then the review by the Claim's set, in the same words: "Nothing's beating. That isn't the same as nothing's there." The review takes it as a paper. On Thursday in the long room the king says he could fly there in three hours and go into the sea, and Ferra says it before he can: a gold-eyed dragon diving alone at night after a beast whose size nobody knows is two stories at once, the rumour's and a funeral's, and the people who live in that water are a set call away. He agrees, and calls Aumere.

On Friday 8 October, by Kettrin's set, through Ferra's eyes at the board she runs, the doors' families answer: no. Their member sits after a winter, as they said, and until then nothing goes into that ground. Their reason, through Kettrin and the house's speaker: the last time a crown put a pipe into that ground for a good reason, fourteen people died, and their dead were there before them. They add that the woman from the Rim may listen from outside. The king accepts it in a line to the review. It is the third of his requests refused since June, the second about the hollow, and the first refused by the people whose dead are in it. He thinks it wrong and dangerous, and he obeys. A Register line is read to the board: Mira's squad killed a pup on the Claim's north beach on 11 October. Ferra's September and October columns under Kaldo's own sky have no dragon to read them.

From Saturday 9 to Thursday 14 October, off the cove, through Zale's eyes, Zale brings six of the queen's watch on the K Discovery's next run from Aumere at Ilenne's word. One of the six is a sea-talker, and before anyone dives she asks the shelf's animals, the merfolk way, asked and not entered. They give her no picture of the mother, because animals keep clear of the beasts. What they give is a place they do not go. By day they search the kelp shallows and find grey dust where the pods lay, pod and casing crumbled, and from it a grey track grazed off the seabed's shard: a ribbon of dead grey crystal about four metres wide, oldest at the cove and freshest at the shelf's edge, running north along the slope. Arthur goes down with them on a held breath in a closed loop with a lime cartridge, no bubbles, about forty metres, to the edge of the shelf. The oxygen comes from the held breath itself, pressed air beside his core fed into each breath; the lime takes the spent air out of what he breathes back; and he loaded air rather than pure oxygen, because pure oxygen poisons from about six metres down and air stays safe to about fifty-five. He sees the track go over the edge into the dark. Zale says not without a reason to die. Nobody follows it. Amara has turned the relay's glass on the strait every night since 27 September, covertly, and nothing large has surfaced. Her last known trail leads north off the shelf into deep water, about the time of the birth. Where she is now, nobody knows, and Kaldo does not know the size of the clutch. The king says to the same people what he said over the guard's carcass: "We don't know how many. We build as if it's a lot." A grey ribbon of dead crystal goes over the edge of the shelf into the dark, and nobody follows it.

Chapter 16: Bad Harvest

On Wednesday 13 October, through the crews' man's eyes, the Assembly argues the beast line for a day. Corbin's draft, as the crown sends it, is a schedule under the Arms Act. Licensed hunters may be issued crown rifles on tracked issue, for the purpose of beasts, with a term, a stretch of shore, a round count and an issue tag. Every carcass or part taken in the kingdom is sold only to the crown's own sealing houses at a price the Treasury posts, the first two being the iron shed at the Claim and a new one on the quay at Saltreach. Keeping a nest alive, keeping a pod or selling one is a crime punished like keeping a plague. The money is an ordinary budget line, so Ferra's lost fast-track power does not touch it. A member from the northern baronies says from the floor what the pulpits say: the crown's beasts, the crown's guns, the crown's only buying house at the crown's own price; they make the monsters and sell the guns. The crews' man answers him, and then agrees with half of it. The guns are fair, since the Act already holds every one, but a crown that is the only buyer of a thing worth more every year, at a price it sets itself, is the thing the Act was written against.

His amendment, seconded by the mill lord, rewrites it against the king. There is no crown monopoly: any house that meets the crown's sealing standard, an iron store, the crown's shut cases bought at cost, copper gaskets, a sworn sealer and a chain-of-custody book, may be licensed to buy and seal, and the crown's two houses are only the first licensed. An Inspector of Sealing named by the Assembly, not the crown, licenses them. The price is set by the Assembly's formula. A levy on every sale pays the families of the people the beasts kill. And the whole line lapses in a year unless the Assembly renews it. Corbin reads the king's objection to the house: every new pair of hands between a kill and a shut case is a chance for a pod or an eye to go missing, and one went missing on Kesrath's quay three weeks ago, so keep the houses the crown's until the brood is counted. It is a fair argument, and it loses by eleven votes, the mill lord voting yes. The line passes by name. The king carries it out as written. On Thursday the Assembly names a Saltreach harbourmaster as Inspector, and he asks the crown for its own sealing records first, the Claim's shed, the two pup heads in Kaldo and the guard's spares, and gets them. The crown files its records with a man it did not choose. The Saltreach house cannot open until he has inspected it, so for five days the March's watches keep their kills in portable shut cases at the tower depots. Every depot now gets a portable shut case and a custody tag for every carcass, the price is for parts, never for kills, so nobody is paid to make more, and the Register checks every hunter's kills against the rifle's round count. On Duvrenne's half the line does not run, and Kaldo sends Kesrath the standard and the drawings.

That week Vantic follows her trail to her brother Tullio at the crown book's counting house, the morning after he rescued a Brask miller into a loan the miller cannot escape. Tullio does not know whose imperial credit a Duvrenne faction would draw on to hire men to raise a storm, and says so, but he knows the imperial houses lend to anyone with a war to sell, and names the two that would. Then, unasked and honestly, he warns her about the one paper: a note under one board is a bad harvest away from ruin, and he lent to a bad harvest that morning. Vantic leaves with two names and a better understanding of her brother than she wanted.

On Saturday 16 October, at Pryor's Gate in the first autumn rain, through the eyes of Agatha Vell, whose husband, a pass guard, went under a rockfall on the pass eleven years ago, the pass pensions are paid at the Gate's door from the Gate's own tolls. Pryor, the marquis, pays her himself, in the rain, and remembers her husband's name without the clerk's book. He says the crown never paid a widow on this road. On her way home she sees the Heirs' broadsheet on a carter's seat.

On Tuesday 19 October, through Merryn Pellow's eyes, she is issued a crown rifle at the Saltreach depot with her issue tag, her stretch of marsh and sixty rounds. That afternoon her dog stops in the reeds pointing the way it will not go, and she kills a pup on the March shore, the first kill by a licensed hunter. She sells it to the Saltreach sealing house, accredited the day before, at the formula's price, a tenth of it to the families' fund. She signs the Register with the rifle's tag number beside her name, the first name under Hunters. The fund's first claim is Lowen Hennet's mother's.

Chapter 17: Tideshell

On Monday 18 October, in the long hearing room, through Harrold's eyes, he is called in full at last. He defends the exception as he did in February: lawful, under his son's clause, the oil needed, the date and the reason written. The mill lord asks the question the room has waited for since the young came ashore: would he sign it again? On what he knew that day, yes. Then, before anyone can be pleased or angry: nobody brought him Bryn's warning, he did not ask for it, and that is not an excuse; it is what he did. He signs nothing else and adds nothing. Dacey writes it down. Emeric, in the gallery, watches his father give the same shape of answer he gave in June, and for the first time hears how it sounds from the outside.

One pup went into the tide caves under the cliffs of Duvrenne's half in September and lived on an unsealed shard cut at the cliff foot, where Duvrenne's smiths had not yet reached, and a month on rich shard and the tide committed it to a line: about eight metres long, plated like a shell, at home in water and on rock. On Wednesday night, 20 October, it comes up out of the tide onto Duvrenne's works at the cliff foot and into the cutters' camp. Oskel's men meet it with powder guns and pikes, and two die before the guns are reloaded: Remy Doucet, who had been cooking the camp's supper and went at it with the ladle still in his belt, and Luc Aubel, the camp's youngest, nineteen. Through Alina's eyes, the Crestless and the knights' squad are at the Claim on Randillia's half, an hour's march by the shard road. Her father's August refusal was one decision, not a standing ban, foreign armed men on Duvrenne's half need Kesrath's leave, and on the Isle Alina holds her father's envoy's warrant. She gives the leave on that warrant and her own word, valid and rebukable, while Waylon gets the cutters up the cliff path. Oskel takes it.

Through Arthur's eyes, the night glass's light grids work under starlight and the camp's lamps, but the heat layer is no use with the cutters' still furnaces glowing behind the beast. This one is new. It feels the squad's feet: Bren's half of the squad works round its blind side in the dark, and it turns on them before they are there, because it felt them coming through the wet rock, the first beast that cannot be flanked by not being seen. And it cancels a cast at the worst moment. A cutter is pinned under a fallen cradle-frame by the furnaces, the squad's one mage lifts the frame by gravity, and the beast kills the lift with the frame half up. Bren gets under the frame on his inward strength and holds it while two of the squad drag the man out, and that is his arm. So it is steel and inward strength: Arthur's dragon rifle from the cliff path, four rounds through the plates behind its head; Mira's spear into the breathing folds in its neck when it beaches itself on the dry slope; the squad padded at its legs; and Waylon calling where it is going to move through the ground before it moves. Waylon does not change. Arthur's rifle has one lane, the cliff path down to the slope, and a dragon in that camp would fill it and crush the path the cutters are still climbing, so he stays a man while the lane is working, with the change ready in him, and says aloud to Alina on the path that if the lane fails he goes. It does not fail. It dies on the slope. Nobody of Kaldo's is killed, and Bren's arm is broken.

Oskel's men, burying their two at dawn, call it a tideshell, because it came up out of the tide in a shell, and the Register takes their word: the first line name, named by the men it killed, with Doucet's and Aubel's names on its line. It is Duvrenne's carcass on Duvrenne's half, and its clock is about two weeks. Alina rules on the beach that it is Duvrenne's and that Kaldo boxes the core, because Kaldo is the only one who can keep it. Oskel does not agree at once; he is afraid of it and he wants it, and he will not let it leave the beach without paper. So the custody is written on the beach at dawn: an inventory of core, eyes, claws, plates and weights, signed by Oskel, Alina and Arthur; the seal put on the shut case in front of all three; Vantic's escrow as custodian, the case kept at the Claim's iron shed in the Compact's name; nobody to open it without Kesrath's written leave and a Duvrenne witness; nobody to read it but Tess's office on that leave; and copies of any read to Kesrath and Kaldo both. Arthur cuts it out with the Claim's crew under Oskel's eye. Oskel keeps the plates. It is the first Stage 2 core anyone has held. The old king, by Duvrenne's post on Saturday, lets his daughter's leave stand with a rebuke in the same letter for giving it without asking, and a sentence the council did not write: that his guard failed on the road in August and hers held on the slope in October because she asked for help. Alina keeps the letter. Duvrenne's carvers sell the first of the plates on Kesrath's quay that Saturday, outside Randillia's law, and Ferra enters it as the count's second sale, day twenty-nine. A second licensed kill on the March goes into the Register on the 22nd.

On Saturday 23 October, on the proving floor at South Sentinel, through Wren's eyes, with Ottoline's sprung brakes set, the Cradle is raised and the Bulwark walks off the dock onto rock for the first time. The first try fails: a knee locks on the brake's first catch and Sorrel calls it before it falls. The second walks forty paces and stops on Sorrel's call. Ward says nothing until it is sitting again, then asks the same question. Emeric thinks about a machine he cannot take across half an island he does not own, and about a sea he cannot search. That evening Tess says the one sober thing to him: it walks; that proves the gait, not that the core is honest. Her July finding still stands unsigned, and she wants it written beside every test card from now on. He agrees and has Sorrel print it on the card. The giant has walked forty paces on rock with its brakes set, and the man who started it stands back and lets her have the day.

Chapter 18: Hollow Night

On Sunday 31 October, from dusk, through Neris's eyes, the Grand opens, free and with no speeches, as the First Room did. The stadium stands under its glass roof for the first time, its stage sunk flush for the crowds, and the History museum and the Peoples and Civilizations museum open, the other three wings still boarded. Pumpkin lanterns stand at every door, the first Kaldo-grown pumpkins, from the orc valleys' seed, beside the old turnips. A boy in a pup's costume climbs the stadium's top rail for a better look at the lanterns and goes over, thirty feet. His citizen's band catches him, slowing him to a walking drop, and he lands on his feet beside a pumpkin stall, furious about his ears. The parents near him look at their own children's bare wrists, and on Monday the children's fitting hall has a queue round the block.

At History's door the fourteen names of the works dead stand on the name-it-first wall, first, in the order Ferra's record put them, and nothing else about the Isle: no beast, no shaft, no young, because the review has not ruled. A visitor from the capital asks a keeper where the beasts are, and the keeper says after the review, which is the museum's whole answer. Through Emeric's eyes, in the lane, half the children come as grey pups with gold paper eyes, and every one of them is asked for the story of what they are dressed as, the rule since year eleven. They can all tell pieces, since the papers printed the attacks, the sitting and the tideshell: it came with the tide, it eats stones, it killed a watchman, the knights shot a big one. One girl tells it the chapel broadside's way, that the crown made them, and her friend says that is not true, and neither of them knows. None of them can tell where it came from or how it ends, because nobody can yet. Dacey, in the lane with the families' bench and her brother's name on the wall behind her, tells a mother, not quietly, that dressing children as the thing that killed fourteen people, five weeks after a watchman and eleven days after two cutters, is indecent. The mother says the children are making it smaller so they can stand it. Both are right. The king, standing there in paper ears, does not settle it, because it is not his to settle. Doran calls it a costume, again. Ward has a bowl. Runa goes as a slime, because it was her best night last year, and Imani goes as a slime to match her. Ferra will not wear anything, and they make the king wear the paper ears all night, and he kisses them both under the stadium's new roof where half of Kaldo can see. The orc interpreters stand a long time at the turnips and pumpkins side by side and say the pumpkins are better and the turnips older, and do not say which they prefer.

In the winged hall Emeric sees Liora see the children with gold paper eyes. That night she writes home by Aumere's couriers the truth, unsoftened: the beasts out of the Isle have the king's gold in their eyes, laid in like cracks through grey and stopping short, and nobody in Kaldo knows why, and neither does she.

Through Honor Linden's eyes, she stands alone at the fourteen names. Her son's is not among them and could not be, since he died in the earlier Isle war and this wall is the works disaster's. A small boy in a grey pup's costume with gold paper eyes knocks on the wall beside her, as if it were a door, asking for a sweet or a story, and she has nothing sweet. So she tells him the only story she knows: it came in on the tide, a clerk copied the line where somebody saw it go, and nobody knows yet where it came from. He says that is not a story. She says it is the start of one.

Where the Story Stands After Chapter 18

A mother is loose. Something small left the Isle's hollow on 19 April, had five months in the sea, and birthed a clutch in the cove's kelp in September; her young came ashore on the Isle and across the strait on the Narrow March. Lowen Hennet, Remy Doucet and Luc Aubel are dead, and nobody on the Isle died, because Mother Sabbe kept the people up when the goats would not go down. Her last known trail is a grey track over the shelf's edge into deep water, and where she is now, nobody knows. Kaldo does not know how many young she had. Tess has read the word "born" behind their eyes, pointing back under the Isle, with one word still unread. Neris hears nothing beating in the hollow, which is not the same as nothing being there. The review has added to its finding the question of what else the hollow holds.

Every door the king tried was shut by someone with the right to shut it: the review would not protect or license the beasts, the doors' families refused the early listening post, and the Assembly rewrote his beast line against him, with no crown monopoly, its own Inspector of Sealing, a price by formula, a families' fund and a lapse in a year. He carries it out as written. Merryn Pellow is the first licensed hunter and the Register of Drifters is public. The count has begun: day one was 25 September, when gold-veined eyes sold on Kesrath's quay for new imperial gold, and the tideshell's plates there in October were sale two. The switch still holds, and on Hollow Night it is day thirty-seven. Vantic has two imperial houses' names from Tullio, and Saban has a weak lead in a coin's mint year.

The guard's core beats in the Bulwark, which has closed its hand, stood waist-deep and walked forty paces, still not certified, its pilot under an hour-for-an-hour rule, its existence a sealed works. The tideshell's core sits sealed at the Claim under a custody written on the beach, Duvrenne's, unread, and Ward wants a machine of his own. Harrold has given his evidence: he did not ask, and that is what he did. The finding's first part is due in late January. Liora has written home that the beasts carry the king's gold in their eyes.

This batch is about what a city does when danger arrives faster than its institutions and its institutions refuse to be hurried. The answers come slowly and from unexpected people, a clerk's copy-book, a fowler's dog, an old woman's goats, and they are better answers for not being the king's, though every day of slowness has a name attached to it on the Register.
