Book 2, Batch 12: The Falling Sky

Chapters 88–93 | Friday 23 October – Monday 2 November, Year 17

The fleet has left the orc river mouth with Corvan's attack still fresh. Emeric dislocated both of a dragon's shoulders in a pumpkin field to save Bren, Imani objected to his face, and the matriarch Saldis summoned him to her house before his ships leave these waters. Until he goes, he has promised that the fleet will not climb to the Band, launch a drum or lay anything new there. Bren is recovering in Imani's ward. The fleet carries ripe pumpkins for Hollow Night and a contract for sixty tonnes of guano with a clause Emeric asked for: the lot must come from ledges open under the Nestwardens' law. Ahead of it, a young courier named Tiel carries a letter with two accounts of the man with the gold eyes.

The winged ones of the Guano Isles have watched the fleet since Aumere's outer cliffs, and the last two letters to reach their stacks were Sefa's, about a king who walked into scalding water to keep his enemy alive, and Tiel's, about a gold-eyed man who came out of the sky and did to a dragon what the old princes did. Their people remember the falling sky: human fliers, the princes, and airships over the nests. So forty of them come out over the water with javelins. The fleet has done the only useful thing it can: every hull on the sea, nobody in the air, the guns covered, and the man they fear standing on a deck in a plain coat where they can see his eyes.

The lot Kaldo bought is lawful on the trader council's paper. But the Nestwardens closed that ledge in the spring, the traders reopened it in summer under their contract right, a young Nestwarden was blamed for the closing, and the stack it came out of has a nursery hanging above the hole. Then the storm season chooses its day. The fleet cannot climb over it because of Emeric's promise, the dug stack's walk falls, and Kaldo learns that being useful and being acceptable are different things.

Chapter 88: Ground Before Talks

On Friday night, 23 October, on the run north, Armand works out in the K Endeavor's chart room what the winged ones will see first, from what the orc house's clerks told him of the falling sky. Sorrel flying alone ahead of the fleet is a human flier. Knights in formation are a memory of human fliers who threw themselves at the nests. Emeric in the air, gold-eyed, is the princes. Sorrel takes it hardest, because she is the fleet's eyes and now must fly nowhere. Grau agrees to a second delve to refresh Emeric's borrowed orc speech, so he can talk with the couriers. Ferra writes her line of reasons for the stop: lift the guano lot Kaldo has half paid for; ask the couriers to carry Kaldo's answer to the Leaving Terms charge, since they carry everyone's letters and the charge travelled on their wings; hire their eyes and wind-reading for a fleet that cannot climb above the weather; and sit out the worst of the storm season in the Stepping Rings' lagoons, because a fleet that has promised not to climb has to cross to Aumere on the water.

Before dawn on Saturday Ward orders every hull onto the water a league out, no flier up, no suit on deck, no stone lit, the 20 mm guns and dragon rifles covered, and Emeric on the K Discovery's deck in a plain coat rather than below. Hiding him would be the worse lie; the letter has already told them he is aboard. The stacks rise white out of the sea, and the smell arrives a day before them. Mid-morning about forty winged ones come low over the swell carrying light javelins and stone-weighted darts, with the air magic to drop them hard. Sefa leads, the white primary in her grey wing. They circle the hulls at the height of the wave tops. In the air the council splits. An elder of the nesting council wants to strike before the gold-eyed man can rise. Sefa argues for questions, because she saw the Last Dawn. The young look at the hulls more than at the man. Nobody throws.

The council sets Tiel to the fleet as its courier, because he has watched it longest, and he carries the landing terms down to the K Discovery's rail. The hulls stay on the water in the landing's lagoon. Nobody of Kaldo's flies over the stacks. The landing is open to them, the stacks above it are not. The council will hear the king on the landing ledge on Monday. Emeric agrees to all of it before Tiel has finished saying it. Tiel watches him agree.

On Sunday Ferra sees the stack town. The stack faces are pocked with doorways and strung with cable bridges. The landing at sea level, in the lagoon's lee, is the only flat ground, and a walking people's town sits at the foot of a flying people's city. There are guano wharves, a quarter for hired crews, mostly orc gangs from the Sun Shore's valleys on season contracts, and catch-nets under the launch ledges. Imani finds a first-aid station under the main net. Each stack's rain lift is two cages on one cable over a wheel, the upper cage's tank filled from the cisterns at the top. Ferra reads the lift-keeper's gauge on the first day and understands the island's economy: in the dry season the cistern level decides how much freight goes up, so drinking water and freight compete, and this is the end of the dry season. The lift-keeper is a winged one who lost his flight in a fall years ago. On the guano wharf, sixty tonnes of sacks carry Kaldo's mark, and the tally-man says where they came from: the north ledge, reopened. Ferra writes "reopened by whom?" in her book. At the next wharf's tally stands a tall man with a hardwood staff across his shoulders: lean as the staff, grey in the beard, his hood down, a long coat stiff with mud at the hem. Tiel takes Sorrel up the walk on foot, by the walking law, to the lowest launch ledge, because she is the human who grounded herself first and carries her glider drawings. She finds the updraft stronger than anything at home, and does not fly.

That night is a call day. Harrold gives his flat report, then says, "The knight." Emeric tells him how, without a name, as the secrecy rule requires: a dragon came at a shore party carrying only knives under the orcs' law; ribs, a lung and an arm; on his feet in weeks; both of the dragon's shoulders out; and a promise to the dragon's house that the fleet will not climb until he has been there. Harrold asks what the promise costs the fleet before he asks anything else, a regent's question and a father's. Then: the Cinderhale shooter is in Stillwater prison, and Corbin walks the wing himself every night. Emeric asks how he is holding up. Having heard that the officer who ordered the shooting walked free, Emeric sends Corrick, the League's leader, a held message by Corbin's hand offering him a job in the crown's service: "Do you want to come and work for me?" It is a good-faith offer with the wrong word in it, and Emeric does not see it. Tess calls as "urgent" to report that the eighth spire of the city's Shell was commissioned on its own on Thursday, that the spire crews move to the stadium's earthworks on Monday, and that the north gate spire's counter has been right every night for a month. She has already solved the thing she called about.

At the K Discovery's rail that first night, with his sense up as always, Emeric marks a human note among the crews' quarter: raised in dense air, with no stone, settled like a man who has lived in thick air all his life. He tells Ward. Ward says, "Then he's watching. Watch him back."

Chapter 89: The Gauge

On Monday 26 October the councils hear Kaldo on the landing ledge, with Tiel interpreting. Ruvo, the Nestwarden, tracks the cliffs' cracks and the nesting counts. Veska sits for the trader council and holds the contracts. The elder who wanted to strike is there, and behind Ruvo, not speaking, stands Ari, a young Nestwarden. The trader councils control contracts; the Nestwardens control nesting access. Ferra puts on their record the promise Emeric made Saldis under the truce sign, which the council already knows from Tiel. It is the first conduct of the voyage a winged people has seen first-hand, and it is not enough to change anything yet.

Veska raises the guano lot, since Kaldo is a buyer and the islands' grain comes in on buyers' money. Ruvo answers it. The lot came off the north ledge of the Old Stack, the oldest worked stack in the lagoon. The Nestwardens closed that ledge in the spring because of what the digging was doing to the stack. The trader council reopened it in summer under its contract right. Ari was blamed for the closing, and her brother took dangerous work on the lower cliff to make up the lost money. Kaldo's clause trusted the traders' ledger. Emeric says Kaldo will not lift a sack until the two councils settle whose closure stands, and will pay nothing more meanwhile. He will not rule on their dispute; he has no standing in it, and Ferra says so before he can. He asks to see the cliff.

That afternoon Ruvo takes Ferra, Armand and Sorrel along the Old Stack's walk to Ari's crack markers: fifty-five-year-old grey masonry meant to save the walk, with water running out of the wrong crack. Ruvo says it, not Emeric: most of the weakening is the digging, and a storm will choose the day it fails. Armand's sense gets partway into the rock and no further. Below them, Ari's brother works on a crew line on the lower cliff.

On Tuesday Armand and Sorrel watch from the walk, by the readers' leave, as the old storm readers at the wind masts on the Old Stack's top read the weather: the swell's period lengthening, a high thin sheet of cloud to the south-east, sea birds coming in, and something they feel in the air for which Kaldo has no word. Three days, they say, and from which quarter. Armand asks to be taught. The oldest reader says he may watch, and that is all. Kaldo's barometers stay steady on Tuesday and fall only on Wednesday afternoon. Armand writes both times side by side in his book, and the gap is the lesson. The councils close every cliff to traders and crews on Tuesday, and the Nestwardens move the young they can along the walks to the lee nurseries. The Old Stack's nursery is on its north face, above the dug ledge, and its walk to the lee is the one the masonry was meant to save. They move half. Sorrel sees the lift-keeper's torn wing and thinks of her drawings.

At home on Tuesday night, in Cinderhale, Gethin's bandaged left hand rests on the lane wall. In late September a copied gun burst in it and took two fingers. Kezia was angry at him for using it and frightened by how close the fragment came to his face. The Kaldo clinic closed the wound and told him the specialist who could rebuild it is away with the fleet. He does not know the specialist is married to the man he resents. Then the Hearth brings down the watch house, which belongs to the mill interests who armed the watch. One guard runs for the riot bell. The night man is drunk asleep in the yard, and Kezia wakes him and asks him to walk to the chapel steps. He goes in his own time, swearing, and they wait. The runner comes back early and they lie flat in the lane ten yards from him. Powder goes into the cistern, the locks are broken, the posts cut, a dray team hitched, a corner holds, and the chimney comes down into the cleared lane onto the crew's own cart. Kezia sits on the kerb, and nobody congratulates her. A notice is posted. The widow Tench asks whether her house might be next. Corrick cannot promise what some future group will decide, and later, in the old workings, he files her question under "the cost of doing anything"; Kezia hears the phrase and stays silent. Maudie speaks in public: the grievances are true, the act was not voted, and the League's books stay open. Warrants go out against persons unknown.

On Wednesday the barometers fall. Riding over the storm would mean climbing into the Band, and Emeric will not; nobody asks him twice. Running north on the water would put the fleet in the storm's path, and Bren should not be moved hard. The landing's lagoon is a drowned caldera with a narrow mouth, a natural hurricane hole. The airships sit on the water like any boat with every anchor down. Dagny suggests using Tess's gravity anchors to hold the surface pair, Zale's ship and the K Tam, which ride lowest. Zale, who breathes water now, sets them forty feet down on the lagoon's rock shelf, each pad's grip stone holding to the rock and its lift stone dialled to push down, with cables to the ships' bitts, while Bram watches the needles. Ward sees the tall man with the staff walking the Old Stack's lower walk with Ari's brother at dusk. On the quay, before the wind arrives, Ari tells Sorrel that half the young on the north face are still there.

Chapter 90: The Nest Behind the Storm

On Thursday 29 October the storm arrives, the worst of it after dark. The hulls strain on their moorings. Bren is on the set in Imani's ward, wanting off his bunk. At dusk the dug ledge gives way first, and the Old Stack's north walk goes with it below the nursery overhang. Eleven young and four adults are left on the nursery ledge above the gap. The young cannot fly, no adult can carry a child in that wind, the walk down is gone, and the lift is on the far face beyond the break.

In the dark, against the council's word, Ari comes down a crew rope to the landing quay and asks for Ward by name. Tiel told the council that Kaldo's knights walk on walls, and she has seen the boots. "Can your people climb?" Ward cannot send fliers into that wind and would not send them up that cliff if he could. Six climb: Mira leading, Arthur second, and four knights, in their suits with the flight stones dark, their grip boots and palm stones live, since those hold to whatever rock they are pressed against, harnessed on a fixed line Ari leads them to. They dim their visors' glow, because a cliff full of frightened parents is no place for glowing faces. Ward stays at the foot with the crews, and the tall man with the staff is already there. Bren is on the set in the ward and hears all of it. Imani will not let him off the bunk, and he says so on the set until Mira tells him to shut up.

They climb silently in the wind, harnesses taut. Every visor carries Tess's night glass, light-stone grids with a ceiling on each cell, so every lightning flash blacks out cell by cell instead of blinding them, and the rain and rock come back the instant it passes. The sixth knight's stealth helm carries a coarse heat grid, and it finds the young: a cluster of warm shapes behind the overhang's fallen rubble, where nobody could have seen them.

The four adults on the ledge have heard every song about humans on a cliff at night. Armoured shapes climbing the rock in a storm are raiders. They throw stones with the air behind them, then a javelin. A knight's shoulder plate takes a stone, and another's forearm is opened by a dart through the gap at the wrist. Then one father stands between Mira and the rubble, javelin raised, three feet from the lip. Her rifle is on her back with a baton round chambered. A baton round would stop him, and in that wind, at that lip, it would send him four hundred feet down into the dark. She does not fire. She unclips the rifle, lets it hang off her line out of reach, and keeps climbing with her hands open. He throws anyway, and the javelin strikes her chest plate. He sees that she did not shoot. Ari's voice comes up the line.

The young go down the fixed line in harness bags, two to a knight, to the lower walk where Ward's team, the crews and the tall man take them. Three of the four adults come down the line. The oldest will not take a human line and waits on the ledge for the lift to be reached from above. On the lower walk the tall man frees a jammed line using his staff as a lever, and Ari's brother is brought up off the lower cliff, where the fall caught him on his crew line with a broken leg. Emeric is on the quay's haul team with the crews, on the ground, using nothing but his back, which is the job he has been given. The whole haul team is gasping, Emeric included, and the man at the head of the walk is breathing like someone sitting down. Emeric does not ask.

Before dawn the wind stops. In the calm of the eye, the eleven young sit on the quay under the K Endeavor's lamps, and the Old Stack has begun to groan.

Chapter 91: What Wings Can't

Through the night the islands' own system does most of the saving. The storm has filled every cistern on the Old Stack's top, so the rain lift can run at full, and the lift-keeper runs it all night, cage after cage, carrying the old, the hurt and the young from the lee nurseries down to the landing. About forty people come down before dawn. Ferra, who read his gauge on Sunday, understands it before anyone explains. At the back of the storm the lift's laid fibre rope begins fraying at the wheel. Bram offers the K Endeavor's steel wire rope, which is too heavy for anyone on the stack to carry up, so the K Discovery lifts it to the wheel at first light, low, and Bram's crew and the lift-keeper reeve it together. It is for the morning only. The lift-keeper hands it back on Sunday, because a rope he cannot renew himself is a rope he would need Kaldo for forever.

At first light, with the back of the storm blowing from the other quarter, the Old Stack's north face comes away above the broken walk while the last people on the upper ledges are still on it. The walk to the lift is gone, and nine people are left on the top ledges, where the lift does not reach, including the old one from the nursery ledge, who has climbed up to them. Ari runs to Ruvo on the broken walk. The Nestwardens control nesting access, so the decision is hers, and Ruvo shouts the airship's leave into the wind, because there is nobody else who can. The K Discovery lifts off the lagoon in the falling wind, low, a few hundred feet, nowhere near a climb, with Ferra counting the charge and Sorrel at the bow calling the gusts off the rock. The cargo net goes down to the ledge. Emeric, at the rail, takes the swing out of the net with the pull so it does not slam the rock while people climb in, and the gold in his eyes is plain to everyone. The old one comes last, because Ari is on the deck.

Among the nine is Liora, the island council keeper's elder daughter, nineteen, of the Old Stack's crown, the highest flight-only ledges. She was born with a full cleft of the lip and palate on her left side and named "unfinished" by the naming elders. Three healings failed, she has been silent since she was twelve, carrying a slate, and she flies only after dark. In the storm she could have flown off at any hour, and instead she stayed to get the old up the inner stair. She goes into the net second to last. At the rail the wind takes her hood, and she waits for the look. Emeric does not flinch and does not look away. He looks at her the way he looks at a jammed wheel, already working out how it goes. That is what she keeps.

From the next stack, the elder council watches an airship at a nesting cliff with a gold-eyed man at its rail, exactly what the songs are about, lifting their people off a cliff their own traders dug out. Nobody says which they see. Tiel writes both down.

Imani works at the net stations beside the islands' healers. She sets Ari's brother's leg under the main net, sews the knight's dart wound, checks the bruised shoulder and Mira's ribs, bruised under her plate. The count is seven dead before Kaldo came, two of them young; eleven young off the nursery ledge by the climb; about forty down the lift; nine off the top by the K Discovery. Imani gives Liora a cup, sees the water come out of her nose, and knows the palate. Through Tiel, the senior healer tells her: the keeper's unfinished one; three times; it came back. Imani says nothing that night.

At dawn on the quay Ward says to Mira, "You didn't fire." "He'd have gone four hundred feet." Ward nods, and that is the whole conversation. Maeve is at the foot of the line when Mira comes down, and Mira lets her look at the ribs. At home in Cinderhale, the night man reports to the temporary watch post in the chapel hall with his name still on the roster and is paid his full wage. He grumbles that the benches are harder, and puts his mark beside the pay entry. Nobody from the Hearth explains it. At dusk, standing on what is left of the walk, Ruvo tells Ferra through Tiel that the council will write to Kaldo. She does not say thank you, and Ferra notices.

Chapter 92: Hollow Night

On Saturday 31 October, Hollow Night, Emeric's first instinct is to cancel it, since the islands buried seven people yesterday. Ferra says cancelling it is what the ancestors did, hiding the dead to be comfortable. Imani says keep it to what it is for: lanterns and names, no games on deck. They keep it. Ferra's one rule, said once: on this voyage nobody wears a people whose ground they are standing on. The paper masks some crews packed stay in the sea chests. The seed hand opens every pumpkin himself, keeping the matron's chosen fruit apart from his own, counts and labels the seed, and lays it to dry on the galley's racks. The flesh goes to the galleys. The shells become the first pumpkin lanterns any Kaldo hand has carved, a year early. The seed hand carves at the same trestle as Emeric, because that is where the knives are, and does not look at him for most of an hour. Then he asks whether the eyes should be round or slanted, and Emeric tells him. It is the first thing he has said to him since the farm. Nothing is settled, but it is a start.

Runa, told what the night is, decides at once to be a slime, in a dyed oilcloth sack with eye holes, because Mochi is the only thing on the voyage she has never been afraid of. She goes deck to deck saying "sweet or story," and when a K Endeavor deckhand demands the story, she tells him Mochi's in four sentences of Kaldo's tongue, with Mochi following her round the deck. Ward has a bowl ready. At dusk about a hundred lanterns go along the rails on the lagoon's black water, and the crews say the names of Kaldo's own dead of the voyage and, because Imani asked the islands' healers for them, the names of the seven. Lights on the water read as signals to the winged ones, so Ruvo sends Tiel to ask. Emeric answers on deck: it is a night his people keep for the peoples Ostrellin lost, and tonight for theirs. Watching Tiel climb the walk with his wings half open for balance, Emeric privately counts: the merfolk, the orcs, Runa's wolves, the dwarves, the dragons, and now these. Every people this voyage has met that walks on two legs and is not human, his old world told stories about, and his old world never met one of them. After dark a winged one flies once round the lanterns without landing. Sorrel calls her the best night flier she has ever seen. Nobody knows who it is. It is Liora.

Chapter 93: Not Gratitude

By morning the split shows. Ruvo's reply comes down with Tiel: "Tell him we don't need his lamps to count our dead." That is the elders. Tiel asks for a lantern to take up to the young on his ledge, and Emeric gives him two. That is the young. Both are true.

At home on Sunday, at his office in Turnstone prison, Corbin still thinks of Corrick as the League's leader, offices or not. He offers him a desk and paid authority and reads Emeric's pitch aloud: "Do you want to come and work for me?" Corrick answers, "If my complaint becomes your wages, what happens to the complaint? You're offering me a desk." He refuses. Corbin offers in good faith, without knowing about the leash, and has his good faith refused.

On Sunday 1 November, Tiel brings the bill as the council's courier in a light cloudstone case: a written assessment in the Nestwardens' hand, with Ruvo's crack-marker readings, the nesting counts, and the places lost. The Old Stack's north-face nurseries held the young of twenty-three households, and none can be cut again in that face. It sets three terms, a deadline and a choice. Ferra reads it twice. There is nothing in it to argue with that is not true. The rescue is not mentioned anywhere, which is the point. She cannot talk her way through it, and does not try.

On Monday on the landing ledge, Emeric gives Kaldo's first answer, with Ferra holding the numbers and Tiel interpreting to Ruvo, Veska and the elder. Kaldo suspends all buying from nesting cliffs from today. The contract is void by its own clause, since the lot did not come from a ledge open under the Nestwardens' law. Kaldo forfeits the half it paid at the river mouth and lifts nothing, and who owns the dug lot is the councils' to settle. Kaldo accepts the levy: it pays the levy per hold on the sixty tonnes it contracted for into the Nestwardens' fund for the Old Stack's households, Ari's family first, a cost with nothing coming back. It agrees in principle to inspectors and restoration, the terms to be drafted that week and signed before the fleet sails. Then he says plainly what he cannot promise: anything larger than the expedition chest can carry needs Kaldo's Assembly, which votes the budget line by line, and he will not promise what he cannot make the Assembly do. Ruvo says, "Then promise what you can pay." He does. Asked what Kaldo will use instead of their cliffs, he names fertiliser made from the air, years of work at home that may never fully replace guano. Ruvo's agronomist explains why it cannot: guano from people fed on thick-water fish carries mana into the soil, while air-made ammonia feeds the crop and starves the soil of it. Emeric writes it down. Nobody claps. Veska is furious, and not wrong: the islands' grain imports run on buyers' money, and the first buyer to suspend makes every other buyer ask why. Emeric accepts that too. That night Ferra writes a held message for Saban: no guano this year, Milford's guano trial and the powder master's beds go without, and the bread price argument starts at home. She writes Kaldo's own loss into the ledger as plainly as the islands'.

In a crew-quarter room at the landing, the tall man with the staff, Duncan, a warden of the Westgone march, writes a short report for the next courier north. His council sent him by the coast traders' decks to watch the fleet, and he has been at the landing since early October, keeping the tally at a guano wharf because a march-warden does not sit idle. He writes that the king grounded his fleet through a storm for a promise to a dragon, hauled a rope like a crewman, and holds a net with gravity like a man pouring water through a sieve, and nobody aboard seems to know it. He adds one line last and nearly strikes it out: from the cliffs, the king has the founder's face, the one on the statue at home, and a warden reports what he saw. To a Westgone, gold eyes mean two things, the fliers of the old war and the founder, and he does not tell his council which he thinks this is. He signs it with the march's mark. He has not decided whether to speak to him.

Where the Story Stands After Chapter 93

Kaldo was useful at the Guano Isles and still not welcome. The fleet met forty armed winged ones by doing nothing alarming and accepted every landing term. It kept its promise to Saldis through a hurricane, mooring in the lagoon with Tess's gravity anchors instead of climbing. When the dug stack failed, Mira led the climb that saved eleven young and refused a shot that would have killed a defending father; the islands' own rain lift brought forty people down; and the K Discovery, by the Nestwarden's leave, lifted nine off the top, Liora among them. Seven died before Kaldo came. The islands answered with a bill, not thanks.

Kaldo has voided its own guano contract, forfeited what it paid, paid a levy for a hole it did not dig, agreed in principle to inspectors and restoration, and said aloud what it cannot promise without its Assembly. Still owed before sailing are a bond the Nestwardens' inspectors can draw on, an inspection agreement under which Kaldo buys only lots carrying the Nestwardens' mark, and Kaldo's share of the Old Stack's restoration. The elders want nothing more of Kaldo's lamps; the young asked for two. The research path to air-made fertiliser has been named, along with the agronomist's objection about mana-rich soil.

A Westgone march-warden named Duncan has been watching the fleet since it arrived. He breathes easily where everyone else gasps, and he has written to his council that the king spills mana like a split skin without anyone noticing. Imani has seen Liora's palate and heard of the three failed healings, and has said nothing yet. At home, the Hearth has brought down the Cinderhale watch house, the night man is still paid, the widow Tench's question hangs, and Corrick has refused Emeric's offer as "a desk," a word that has not yet reached Emeric. The fleet holds seven drums, fourteen gift sets and about 87 tonnes of food, and Bren is nearly back on light duty.

This batch shows that lawful is not the same as harmless, and that a rescue does not cancel a debt. Kaldo answers a bill instead of collecting thanks, and the people watching it learn more from that than from the airship at the cliff.
