Book 3, Batch 9: With (ch 77-84)

Sunday 27 May to Saturday 8 December yr20. Eight chapters, synopsis v1, waiting on your yes. The armed-houses arc's locked endings land here, and the Hearth's Movement Seven and the autumn of Movement Eight are placed whole. The batch ends on the League's last scene, and the year skips start after it.

Ch 77. Out

After a quiet June, Corrick and Kezia walk out of Turnstone. Corrick makes no speech, and Kezia notices that he used to. The night watchman comes to the League door to hear Corrick admit that nobody asked him whether he wanted the house gone. He refuses money and leaves when he chooses. In the Motion Shop a scrap beast core moves a steel arm when Ada closes her hand under the cap, and Wren laughs out loud.

Ch 78. Docket

Corrick files his first lawful complaint and gets a docket number and no date. At the Assembly's first debate on succession, Tullio's counsel reads the Bank Article out as proof that the king means to end the crown. Asked straight out, Emeric doesn't deny it. Corrick comes to the back gate angry that a political enemy had to tell the country, and leaves without shaking hands.

Ch 79. One Seed

The new two-signature count comes up one seed short. Abel Tilley, a picket with debts, sold it to a Fairhythe chandler. The seed is recovered, and the buyer never comes to collect. Ferra lays her Watcher sightings over her air column and they rise together. Emeric thinks, and doesn't yet say, that every good thing they do is loud.

Ch 80. Applicant

Emeric walks up from the station alone and waits outside the League's hall. Gethin, waiting too, asks how he walks about like a normal man, then flicks a pebble at him that stops a finger's width short. Inside, Emeric asks them to work with him, and Corrick notices the word. The League sets its terms and votes without him in the room. Corrick appeals the delay on his complaint through the court he once wanted the League to boycott.

Ch 81. A Stranger's Claim

A carter's widow in Wayford collects forty-two silver on an hours claim and goes to buy shoes for her boys. She has never met the king. The Titles Act passes with the March's guaranteed seats. Emeric dislikes those seats and signs. Elwin Gorran walks out of Turnstone to a mill wheel that's still turning.

Ch 82. Loud

Kezia passes the open safety examination. On Hollow Night a child dressed as a sparrow won't answer anyone. At the kitchen table Emeric tells the six that every good thing they do is loud. Saban asks whether they should stop. Emeric keeps planting.

Ch 83. The Count

An ironworker votes for Wystan by secret ballot, freely, and afraid for his job. Agatha Vell takes one of the March's seats. The League's candidate loses narrowly, and Corrick asks the young man who wants to call it stolen what evidence he has. He has none.

Ch 84. The Step

Wystan wins his exemption. At the League's meeting the mill lord cites it, though it doesn't cover his mill. Maudie sees a man lower his half-raised hand because the king is watching, and asks the king to leave. He hears the motion read through the door: forty-one Silver Rand in a tin, three delegates and their own lawyer. Then the mill whistle, then the count. Pim signs the roll with his name, in letters. Batch 9 ends.

Closed, and handed on

Nineteen items are closed in section 6. The main ones: who sets up the succession debate, the Seed Act thief and the seed recovered, the Titles Act's dates, the lords' refiled suit, the beast line for two years, the March's pilot town, and why the king was in the League's room at all.

Batch 10 opens on 9 December with the Long Table, the first cultivation classes in the pilot towns and Corrick's appeal. Then the year skips begin.
