Book 3, Batch 12: Blood

Chapters 105-114 | Thursday 26 December, Year 21 - Sunday 26 October, Year 22

At the end of Year 21 the king was held to account by the things he built. An inquiry named his promised date as a cause of the bridge collapse, and he withdrew it on television. His mother walked into the Long Table on her own feet. Three peoples past the wall had been hit by beasts, and Ferra's chart of the gaps between attacks had become a line. On the Narrow March's salt marsh, one of the Isle mother's last five young had grown wings.

This is the second batch that covers most of a year, and it is the year the beasts' blood becomes everyone's business. Kaldo kills its first winged beast, builds its first soldiers' mech, and finds by accident the cure for those who drink beast blood, in the blood of the gold-eyed. A hunting crew whose leader has drunk that blood for years takes Runa. The king brings her back and then does something his own law forbids, and the law he signed into being in Year 19 is used on him.

Chapter 105: The Doorstep

On Thursday 16 January, through Corrick's eyes, the winter has been six weeks of doors. The January backlog list is on the county board, the hours inspectorate's oldest complaints set out by age. The Corps' jobs have inspectors now, and the clinic's television showed a queen walking into a hall. In the rain on a lower-town doorstep, one of the five Hearth men nobody has ever named waits under the eaves and offers the old way for the cousin reeve: a night, a fright, no blood. Corrick says no, flatly, and doesn't explain. The man walks off into the wet.

Four days later, in the League's back room after hours, he tells Kezia what he has worked out. "I made myself a lord of a crew." "Of a crew and a town." "You knew?" "I was there." Neither of them says the five names, and he doesn't ask her to. He decides privately that loyalty will never again be his authority to act on anyone's behalf.

Chapter 106: Nine Days

On Saturday 25 January, through Runa's eyes, the winter has been Kindling sessions, Ferra's chart that she isn't supposed to read and does, and the new phone's ring hurting her ears. Ondra rings for the werewolf packs. Something came out of the High Shore's tundra margin and killed a herder's ponies. The packs tracked it for nine days through snow and a burned wood and killed it themselves with bog-iron spears and teeth, and lost no one. The meat is poison: a pup that ate from it died the same night. Then Ondra asks for the wolf-eared girl. Runa takes the set. Ondra asks who her people were; Runa doesn't know their names. Ondra says the packs will know her by her nose if she ever comes. Then Runa asks for the head, the way a pup asks an old wolf for something it has no right to: plainly, quietly, once. Two days later the packs say yes, and the head goes south sealed in one of Kaldo's shut cases. Ferra puts a fourth pin on the board, ninety-one days after the third.

On Monday 24 February, through Tess's eyes, the case arrives with its gauge still green. Behind the eyes is the grain of written crystal that says where a beast was born, and Tess reads it the way she read the Isle's. It points at a cradle on the far side of the sea, under the High Shore's own interior. Not Kaldo, not the Isle. She writes it up with her method for every set holder, so their own readers can check it. On 1 March Ilenne rings, having had the queen's own reader go over Tess's sheet. "Then it isn't yours." She doesn't apologise for asking. It convinces Ilenne and nobody else: the pulpits still preach that the beasts belong to the crown, and Velmoor's envoy writes home that Kaldo reads its own marks.

Chapter 107: Letters

On Monday 10 March, through Emeric's eyes, Lantern Night has gone by on the sets, the Capital Line's first piers are rising beside the old capital's ring line in Weekes's photographs, and the keel of Bram's second rack ship has been laid. Emeric writes a public letter to every crown of Ostrellin and every people Kaldo has a set with: come on Hollow Night and build with us. The Library will open, and nobody is asked to accept Kaldo's answer to anything. A second, separate letter goes to each crown's war office: its officers may watch an authorised defensive hunt, if one happens and can be watched safely. Ferra rewrites the first letter's second paragraph, because he used "we" four times.

On Thursday 13 March, through Maudie's eyes, a retaliation ruling in Cinderhale's county court gives Jago Crewe his job back, along with a clerk the mill fired the same month. It gives him his place and not his year. The League's fund paid him half wages; the other half is gone. Maudie writes that on the hall's board in her own hand, under the ruling. Jago goes back to the mill on Monday.

Chapter 108: Edge Crystal

On Saturday 12 April, through the eyes of Merryn Pellow, a licensed hunter, the marsh has been empty all winter. Runa turned fifteen on 2 April and asked for a whistle only she could hear, and got one. The Wednesday before, Tiel rang from the winged ones' stacks: a beast over the stacks at night, fought in the air, two of theirs lost, and nothing asked of Kaldo. That made the fifth pin, eighty-four days after the fourth. Now the flier that grew on the Narrow March's marsh comes back across the strait with the spring, bigger, with wings and the first edge crystal along its forelimbs: a Razorwing. Merryn's crew and two other licensed crews wait in the reed hides until it comes down to feed on the shard drift. By daylight, in about four minutes, they kill it with crystal-tipped bolts and a harpoon gun. A wingtip lays one hunter's arm open to the bone; a mender cleans the wound before she closes it. From a hut at the marsh edge, Hattie drives the dog across half a mile of ground nobody can walk at speed, carrying the shut cases out and the parts back. It is the walker's first real job. Kaldo has its first Stage 2 core since the tideshell, its first edge crystal, and the beast's tendon cable.

Through Tess's eyes, the core is read. A raw Stage 2 core runs a body about eight metres tall, so the frame of the first Axion, the knights' mech, is laid down that week for Arthur. On the Motion Shop's bench the first whip sword begins: segments, spikes ground from the edge crystal, the Razorwing's tendon for the cord. No one has killed the beast whose tendon gives the whip its snap-back, so the first sword uses a steel spring in the hilt, and Tess hates how slowly it returns. Then the first lithium cells arrive from the brine ponds on the Isle's flats, the works Randillia shares with Duvrenne, in a crate sealed by both kings. As everyone always said she would, Tess loses her mind. She runs a cell flat three times before lunch, swears at it, laughs at it, and drags Ada out of the ride room to look at the curve. At the end of April the groves give about five thousand fruit, and two thousand go in the ground.

Beside the lithium cells on the bench sits the floor's new valve counter, and it clicks faster over the crate of sour-seam ore than anywhere else in the room. Armand's note is pinned to the crate: the clouding follows the ore, not the mana. Emeric asks one question by set, whether it clicks near the stones. It doesn't. Tess moves the crate out to the yard store.

Chapter 109: Slow

On Monday 9 June, through Emeric's eyes, Saldis has sent word by Sohra's set that her house will come to the Gathering in person. The continental rail talks have opened, Duvrenne first. The Axion's frame stands in the Cradle's shed, and the guest towers' last floors are going in. At the bench he is handling a spike for the whip sword's last segment when it chips, and a shard opens the heel of his hand. It heals more slowly than anything has since his stone: minutes, not a breath. Nothing has slowed him before, and he doesn't like what it might mean. A drop of his blood falls into an open vial of the Razorwing's blood, and he watches the violet turn thin and pale gold, stop glowing, and settle into a fine grey sediment at the bottom. He sits down, sends for Imani, and doesn't touch the vial again.

Through Imani's eyes, through June, Harrold's blood clears a vial too. Ferra offers hers, because someone ordinary has to, and hers doesn't. The gold blood loosens the crystal the beast blood lays down, but something has to carry it out of a living body, and the right tool for that would come from a breeding mother beast, which Kaldo has never killed. So the first cure is ordinary underneath: a few drops of gold blood, then a run on a drum-and-cellophane kidney machine that Bram's shop builds from Emeric's memory of one, filtering the grey out over a course of treatments. The rats, cured by Bettany's own hands, prove the rest. Killed over the dose line and treated within a day, they don't rise. A rat that has already risen and is still young turns back; a grown one doesn't. Every cured rat loses its strength from the blood, and all its own mana with it. Imani calls the treatment the chelation. That Friday Hila rings from the dry edge, where a beast has hit the caravan road: a sixth pin, seventy-nine days after the fifth. On the last night of June, Imani tells Emeric what the vials mean: the gold and the beasts share a pattern. Neither of them says the rumour's name aloud.

Chapter 110: Ears

On Saturday 12 July, through Runa's eyes, she has spent the summer reading beast trails on the Isle's grey beaches with Declan's knights and Merryn's crew. By the smell, three young are left in the hollows. A hunting crew of eight from across the sea has heard what every hunter on the coast has heard: that werewolf noses tracked a beast for nine days, and that Kaldo has a wolf-eared girl who reads trails better than any hound. They don't want ransom. They want a tracker. They come out of a dead zone: a black-market round that kills everyone's magic on the beach for about four minutes. Runa's surge fires, and she marks two of them hard, a bite through a forearm and a knee broken with a rock, before they net her, gag her and carry her up into the hollows. When the knights' magic comes back, the two she marked are lying on the beach where their crew left them.

Through Emeric's eyes, the knights' set reaches Kaldo, and he goes up in dragon form, about an hour's flight. Air is his worst element, and Runa's breathing trick for reaching further on the wind costs him three times what it costs her; he is a dragon pretending to be a bloodhound, and he knows it. He finds her anyway. He comes down behind them in silence, a flash step puts him among them, and the dread hits before they understand why. The crew's old leader, who has drunk beast blood for years and has gold in his eyes, is the only one who knows what he's looking at. He starts to bargain. Emeric says only: "Runa. Ears." She drops flat and covers them. The lightning goes off in the hollow.

He picks her up, asks without words to pad her against the speed, and she gives it, holding on. He sets her down a mile up the shore on the cliff path where the knights are coming. "I forgot something." He goes back. The six men in the hollow are getting up and cutting nets, telling each other the dragon took the girl and left. Stasis holds them where they stand. He makes sure the leader is facing him. Then he changes all the way and breathes fire into the hollow until the stone runs and the sand fuses to glass, and holds them standing in it until there is nothing left to hold.

Through Runa's eyes, he is back on the cliff path before she has her breath, his coat smoking at the hem. Crouched, cutting the last of the net: "You marked two of them. Good." She smells it on him; her nose can't be lied to. She looks at him a long time, then takes his hand, and doesn't let go of it all the way home. She is bruised and rope-burned and nothing else.

Chapter 111: The Written Order

On Monday 14 July, through the eyes of Quarrie, the officer who reviews any force the king uses, Emeric files a written order without being asked: two pages in his own hand saying what he did on the beach and what he did afterwards. No proclamation was read and no countersignature given before he left the ground, as the Rule on Royal Force requires. He signs it as king. Quarrie reads it twice and opens a review, because the law says he must.

The review sits in public. Declan's knights give evidence, with the knights' radio log. Runa gives hers with Imani beside her, and Quarrie, as at the gate three years ago, is careful to say her nose is evidence and not a verdict. The two men she marked are brought from Turnstone, and they describe their leader's eyes changing over the years, flecks first and then gold, and his strength coming back every winter he dosed. That is the world's first public proof that the blood puts gold in the eyes. On Friday 8 August the findings are read. The rescue was force without the proclamation or the countersignature, reasonable for a child taken by armed men, and not censured. The burning was force on men already held and harmless, after the child was safe: not defence, but an execution without the Final Review, which Kaldo does not allow. The king is censured on the Assembly's record, read aloud in the chamber, and the Rule is amended to forbid any force by the king's own person on anyone already held. No penalty can be laid on a king's body until the Second Charter, and the review says that too. Emeric says he accepts the censure and will keep the amended Rule. He doesn't say he was wrong, and everyone notices. The Core's legation sends the findings home in full; Albin Keswick adds one sentence: the king signed the complaint against himself.

Through Ferra's eyes, the teletype terminals have reached her desk in June, and she types a figure and watches the counting house print it back. The ore carts on Saban's closed pass road ran at night on a feast day, under tarpaulins. That night she says it to Emeric plainly: Kaldo executes no one without the Final Review, and he did. He doesn't argue, and he doesn't say he was wrong. She writes the case in her book under a heading she has kept since the Titles Act, "when it's the king": the question the Second Charter will have to answer when the crown ends and a guard against tyrants is founded. She doesn't tell him the heading exists. On Saturday 9 August Ilenne calls the first meeting of the Shore Compact from Aumere: the merfolk, the dwarves, the orcs, the Sabir and the werewolves agree to warn each other the moment something surfaces and to lend hunters across borders. The winged ones listen and don't sign. Kaldo signs last, as one member among many, at Ilenne's asking, and Ilenne chairs.

The first figure Ferra asks the terminal for is the grid's: what the dams, the wind row and the solar roofs make against what Kaldo draws. The difference is about nothing. She prints it in her quarter as one line, and nobody reads it but Tess. Her office moves the hydrogen works to night power and the rain surplus, and the aluminium pot lines sign a drought clause: they shut first when the river is low, and are paid for the days. It costs output, not power.

The spring sitting has passed an income tax in bands, with the first band free, and a competition law that stops any house holding more than a third of a trade without the Assembly's leave. Ferra drafted neither and is glad. The old houses' counsel calls the second one a war by other means.

The same sitting passes the Offices Act, and through Ferra's eyes it bites the king's own people first. Saban keeps the Trade office and puts his shop and his debtors' book into a sealed holding, having written the eleven ways round the act for the committee, and is the first man the Inspector writes to under it, for a cousin's chandlery, the twelfth way. Waylon's horse interests and his Isle works share go sealed. Bram cannot pay himself half again, and laughs, then does not. The king's own works go under the sworn audit panel. Under the spouse clause Ferra's audit of anything that touches the king's holdings passes to the panel; she drafted none of it, signs her own disclosure first, and writes the date in the book with no comment. The crews' member's Works Act passes beside it: the right to refuse unsafe work as law, wage standards, notice before dismissal, apprentices' terms, a cap on debts to a works' store, a crews' seat on any works over five hundred hands, and the right to organize.

Chapter 112: The Ring

On Saturday 9 August, through Ferra's eyes, Ilenne calls the first meeting of the Shore Compact by set from Aumere and chairs it. Five peoples sign, the winged ones listen, and Kaldo signs last at her asking. The Compact's first trade is Aumere's: chroma-cloth, the merfolk's living colour-changing weave, with a weaver to teach its keeping, for iron plate and forty pumps a year that run without a stone. Ilenne adds one clause: Kaldo may lay the cloth but not grow its cells without Aumere's leave. Ferra writes the clause into her book.

On Thursday 21 August, through Arthur's eyes, the first Axion walked in the Cradle's bay on 2 August, the crown on his head and his pilot sphere floating inside its head. The guest towers on the civic ring were finished in July. Grayrock's city shield was lit on 16 August. The second rack ship is running beside the first. The officers from every crown arrived over the week and were lodged on the guest floors, and nobody told them there would be anything to see. On the morning tide a Stage 2 swimmer comes in from the strait for the harbour's shard barges and trips the bay's ring of sentinels. Nobody lured it. The observers watch from the K Sorrel behind the ring, and Mira flies the Outrider overhead. The sentinels' nets and crystal bolts slow it, Wren's Bulwark holds it against the outer pylons, and Arthur's Axion goes in with the claw-crystal blade and a shard round from the rail rifle and finishes it in the shallows, in about six minutes. A gash opens across the Axion's forearm plate. Arthur's sphere never moves.

From the ring Arthur can see Milford's second wind row going up toward about a hundred and twenty machines, and the first towers of a new row on the headland past the bay. He counts them and loses count.

Through Wren's eyes, the observers have seen two machines and the sentinels kill one beast carefully, with nobody posing: no sales, no mention of exports. A Duvrenne colonel asks how many cores Kaldo has. Ward says two Stage 2, which is true, and the colonel writes it down. Eighteen young have now been killed, and three remain in the Isle's hollows, all still small. The swimmer's core goes to Bren's Axion, laid down the next week. The Isle mother has still never been seen.

Chapter 113: The Act

On Monday 1 September, through Imani's eyes, August has gone well. The three crewmen who have been on her register since the first carcass chose the cure, lost the little strength the blood had given them, and were given their mana back by Mabyn Hoskin, the first nurse Emeric taught to kindle it. In July Imani rerouted the nerves of the docker with the first electric hand so that it reads them cleanly. Now, in the Treasury's room, Vantic and the Treasury's undersecretary propose licensing sealed beast blood for building work and stone-growing abroad: it's being sold badly already, and the revenue would pay for the clinics. Imani says the blood kills anyone who is slow to stop, and a Kaldo seal on it puts Kaldo's name on every corpse that rises. She wins. Kaldo will sell only the cure, through licensed clinics, and no sealed blood leaves Kaldo except to its own works. Vantic concedes, and says the smugglers will prove her right. A week later a plain-words notice goes into every paper and onto every set: what a dose does, the greying, the risen dead, the burning rule, and the cure.

That same week a new teacher at a Works Town school, only a year out of the senior class himself, runs an acid demonstration wrong. Dagny pulls the front bench clear and takes the splash on her forearm. Imani mends it without a scar the next morning, and that week the Polytechnic makes its teaching certificate harder. Sela signs the change without arguing.

On Wednesday 10 September, through Emeric's eyes, Countess Henrike Marrant, Velmoor's envoy, brings her court's reply to the invitation and says it to his face: "Your border towns are taking our nurses and machinists. You call it freedom of movement. We call it the hollowing of our country." She is right, and he says so. Kaldo will fund training places in Velmoor's own towns, chosen by Velmoor under its own guild, and local wages on the works Kaldo sells there, with Velmoor in control of both. He won't close Randillia's border to anyone who wants to cross it. She writes every word down and says her court will call it a bribe. He says it is one, and an honest one.

On Tuesday 16 September, through Ferra's eyes, the member for the Tarrowmede hills asks before the Assembly's committee why his parishes have been blank three quarters running. Ferra defends the allocation: the Corps works on land it may lawfully enter, the duke owns the hill land and hasn't answered a letter in two years, and the ledger prints "blank" with the reason because that is what the ledger is for. Asked whether he will go up there himself, Emeric says no: he can't conjure a duke's permission, and if he could he shouldn't. The committee finds the lawful remedy: a parish council may petition the Corps directly for works on its own common ground. At the same sitting Ferra lays her sheet before the grove committee: six dots, gaps of a hundred and twelve, ninety-six, ninety-one, eighty-four and seventy-nine days, moving with the air. Above it she has written that it shows the beasts come faster as the air thickens, and doesn't show why. The vote on slowing the planting will come with next year's budget. In the margin is the line she wants them to read: the Assembly can slow Kaldo's groves, but the old peoples' groves abroad will keep seeding the next, and it cannot slow the world. The same week Saban asks for the city model on the screens to show who is on each guest floor; Ferra gives him loads, doors and power, never names, and Emeric backs her. On Thursday 18 September the Blood Act passes. A seller answers for every harm his blood does, victim by victim, and sellers whose sales kill can be sent to the Final Review. Only the cure is sold, and the citizen's band is free to any duchy, town or neighbour that asks. The guard on Emeric and Harrold doubles that week, because a kidnapped royal is a cure factory. Ward doubles Runa's without asking anyone.

The licensed clinics' standing list now includes pads and tampons from the sulphite line, issued free with the dressings. Imani counts what women lose in work days without them, and Vantic stops arguing that item.

On Imani's bench sits the ultrasound, which the ward calls the echo: a quartz transducer, a valve pulser and one of Tess's oscilloscopes. It shows a child's heartbeat before birth for a woman who can't lie in the tank's bath, and it would still run in a dead zone, where the tank's stones stop. Its picture is grey and poor beside the tank's, and that is why it exists.

Chapter 114: You Can Look at the Wall

On Wednesday 1 October, through Tess's eyes, the Assembly renews the beast line for three years, to October Year 25, with the review's amendment cited twice in the debate and the observers' hunt once. Brask's river power station went on in September, and Doctor Penmarric's telegram was three words long. Lithium buses run the ring. The Library has catalogue terminals in its lobby, and the first hill parish's petition is on Ferra's desk with a date beside it.

The K Sorrel's tanks are filled for the first time with kerosene made from Milford's oil crops at the Works Town's still. Sorrel signs for it and asks the clerk whether the fuel rule is satisfied now. He says he'll find out.

On Friday 3 October, through Maeve's eyes, the merfolk weaver Saelle lays a strip of the cloth under clear coat along an Outrider's wing. At 9,000 metres it goes grey and stiff and doesn't come back. It's alive, Saelle tells them, and they froze it and dried it. Tess and Wren lay a thin layer of agar jelly under the next strip to keep it wet, and run the engine cooler's waste heat through the skin to keep it warm, and the strip flies and lives. It needs no wire and no stone: Maeve, in the back seat, sets her palm on a plate, and Saelle teaches her not to think of a colour but of what's behind her. First she gets the hangar wall. Then sky.

At dusk on Saturday 11 October Mira flies the trial and Maeve runs the skin: bare metal, matte black, then the cloth alive, a blue-violet scale like a fish's side that takes the sky's colour a breath later. The crowd on the stand loses the jet twice. Then the real test: stones off, and the two strongest senses in Kaldo on the ground. Armand can't find it at all. Emeric finds it only because he knows where to look; it feels like a flock of gulls, a faint scatter of life he can't tell from birds at range. Kaldo's own radar still paints it every sweep, the engines are as loud as ever, and in a dead zone the cloth holds its last colour. It's harder to see and feel, not gone. That evening Ward gives the order: every jet carries it, the four Outriders before the Gathering and every jet the Sky Yard builds from now on. Maeve keeps her hand on the plate a moment longer than she needs to, and the cloth goes the colour of her own sleeve.

On Saturday 4 October, through Imani's eyes, the clinics have been open under the Act for a fortnight, and a monthly draw from two men will never fill them. Imani has done the arithmetic and doesn't like it. On the Cradle's iron shed floor, the biggest clean floor in Kaldo, she asks Emeric to take his dragon form, on the understanding that she is taking a little. Then she brings out the blood flask's bleed tap, the needle made for carcasses. The dragon tries to back out of the shed and can't, because he is bigger than the door. Ferra laughs until she has to sit down. Harrold, who gave his own pint that morning without a word, watches from the doorway with his arms folded. "You can look at the wall," says Imani. He looks at the wall. Twelve flasks. Afterwards he sulks for an hour in his own shape and eats everything Maren sends. That afternoon Imani strikes "Do not draw" off his card and writes underneath it: the gold type.

On Sunday 26 October, the eve of the flight to Ostrel, the K Sorrel stands fuelled on the Field's apron with the crater lake's charts on her captain's seat.

Where the Story Stands After Chapter 114

Kaldo has found the cure for the beasts' blood, and found it in its own king's veins. It is ordinary underneath, a kidney machine and a few drops of gold blood, until someone kills a mother beast. The Blood Act makes every seller answer for what their blood does, Imani's licensed clinics sell only the cure, and the king has given twelve flasks while looking at a wall. The first Axion has fought in front of every crown's officers, a second is being built, and only three of the Isle mother's young remain. The Isle mother herself has never been seen.

The year's hardest scene was the king's own. He took his daughter back from men who wanted her for a tracking dog, then went back and burned the six who were still standing. His own review called the rescue reasonable and the burning an execution, censured him, and changed the law. He accepted it and did not say he was wrong. Ferra has the case filed under "when it's the king", for a charter that will not be written for years.

Past the wall, five peoples and Kaldo have signed a compact to warn each other, with Ilenne in the chair, and Ilenne alone believes, on the evidence of one beast's head, that the beasts aren't Kaldo's. The gaps between attacks keep shrinking with the air, and the Assembly will debate slowing the groves while knowing it cannot slow the world. Velmoor has told the king to his face that his towns are hollowing out its country, and he agreed. In Cinderhale, Corrick said no to the old way and admitted to Kezia what he had made of himself, and Jago Crewe has his job back without his year.

The invitations have gone out. The guest towers are finished, the dragons are coming, and tomorrow the K Sorrel flies to Ostrel to fetch an emperor's family off a crater lake.
