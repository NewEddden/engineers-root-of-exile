Book 3, Batch 20: The Line Going Up

Chapters 213–219 | Monday 3 July - Monday 25 December, Year 25

By the summer of Year 25 the Church endgame was over. Ostrella and Oruse were in separate bodies, Augustine was buried under his own name, and the Church had no pope. Ostrella's body still needed a stone to finish rebuilding it, and she had told Ferra where the pope had hidden the last tree for two thousand years.

This is the last batch of Book 3. In it the world's institutions do what Emeric hoped they would: a hearing grants a stone, a foreign crown sets the terms for its own mountains, a convention binds future rulers to a procedure, and the Assembly renews the law that makes the beast trade possible on its own schedule. Then on Hollow Night the world's mana crosses the line its makers set, and something comes up out of the ground under the Isle that no institution can vote on.

Chapter 213: A Hearing's Yes

On Monday 3 July, through Ostrella's eyes, she applies to the Stone Hearing on medical grounds, with Imani's report. She is asked the same question Runa was asked, and gives a longer and worse answer, and knows it. The Hearing grants the stone. One member votes no and explains why: the register of stones exists because one woman once decided who got them, and she is that woman. Ostrella agrees with him in front of everyone. She sleeps for four days while the stone rebuilds her, and wakes in her prime with the burn on her wrist still there.

Through Ferra's eyes, Kaldo files its account of the separation with the Imperial Core's court a day early, with the physician's and observer's notes attached unedited. The emptied relic goes home to Harrowmere, and the bishop writes that it feels lighter. At Stillwater the freed juggernauts begin choosing names and taking work. Two refuse every name offered and choose numbers instead. In September the Core's commission finishes its judgements on the seventeen cardinals: eleven cleared, six convicted of acts done willingly, none to death. Gregory, the second cardinal from the war, is one of the seventeen.

On Wednesday 12 July, behind the glass in the Stone Hearing's dense room, Ferra watches Emeric sit the examination he means to propose for anyone who would lead Randillia, to try it on himself first. A week passes inside it in a few hours outside. In the built last year of his term a frightened Assembly offers to suspend the limit on how long he can serve, for the war's duration, and he accepts, because he is right that he is needed. He fails the part about leaving, and has it printed in Kaldo News in one line. Ferra is watching his face, not the overlay, and sees something the observers don't: in the middle of a scene that has nothing to do with a council, he stops, and then goes on.

That night on the castle roof he tells her and Imani one thing. "I remember a table." One of the closed books has opened. It is a long table at the Common, the elven council across it, and himself, as Avarran, keeping one name back from them. He knew whose. Ostrella's account had made him the man deceived, which was true, and kinder than this. Ferra writes it under the line she wrote in the Westgone's old towns, and for the first time it is an answer and not a lead. Imani asks whether it hurt. He says the chest, no. The rest, yes.

Chapter 214: The High Valley

On Monday 7 August, through Runa's eyes, Kaldo enters Velmoor's high country. The valley Ostrella named lies on Velmoor's land, and Velmoor admits Kaldo under the Kesrath compact on its own terms: its rangers lead, its countess's man holds the papers, and half of whatever seeds they find stay in Velmoor under a seed law it passed in June. Ward leads Kaldo's half and Runa tracks for them. By the second day the rangers have stopped calling her the king's daughter.

Through Ostrella's eyes, she leads them in. She hasn't seen the valley with her own eyes in two thousand years, though she has dreamed of it every year. The last tree stands where she left it, enormous, over a valley floor carpeted with fallen seed. They count about 1,400 sound seeds, and Velmoor takes seven hundred. The Keeper of the Passing, travelling home that way, sits under the tree for an hour and says the valley smells the way the Common used to.

Under the valley's east wall sleeps the cradle where Oruse was first printed, its broken door crusted shut. Nobody opens it. Velmoor's rangers take the watch by their own choice, with a Kaldo relay drum to send word home. Asked whether it is dangerous, Oruse says it's asleep, that she made sure of that, and that she doesn't know how long "sure" lasts.

Velmoor's head ranger, whose crown has never given the relay eye leave over its country, asks what Kaldo has that isn't the sky. Tess's crew brings the second Kestrel, and Runa carries it up the goat path to a ledge above the crusted door. It perches with its stones off, because the makers' machines read magic and are blind to plain machinery, and Oruse's "asleep" isn't worth testing. At night an infrared lamp and the camera do the watching. The picture and the microphone run down a cable to the rangers' hut, and Tess hands the ground station to the head ranger: Velmoor's own eye on Velmoor's own ground, with no copy kept in Kaldo. The rangers will climb up every morning to change its cells. He writes that down, and so does the countess's man.

The summer before, Runa spent a month learning the wolf slowly, footing and turning and stopping and doorways, and in Ward's yard she stopped a charge early when a first-year stepped into her line. She sets the birthday problem for the first-years now. On Saturday 10 June she came in through the castle kitchen door as the wolf for the first time, filling the doorway, placed one paw and then the other, and bent her head down to Emeric without scraping anything. He said Runa, ears, and she dropped flat on the flags with her paws over them. On the night before the valley, at ten she told him she'd keep going till she fell; now she tells him she'll keep going, and she'll come home.

Chapter 215: A Procedure

From Monday 4 to Friday 15 September, through Corrick's eyes, the Convention meets in the Assembly's hall at Kaldo and opens with a fight over how to draw the next districts. Wystan's coalition wants the furnace towns grouped so they carry more weight. Smaller communities fear being outvoted. Corrick objects that handing the question to a committee could delay justice for years. Emeric warns against last-minute changes by the crown or by any party. Maudie demands a timetable and public records.

A motion to settle the boundaries immediately fails. The delegates adopt a procedure instead: published maps, deadlines, a right to challenge, and an independently checked recommendation. The boundaries themselves are left for the next session. Emeric raises a procedural objection, and Corrick agrees it's valid even though it delays the result he wants. Emeric almost laughs, but the chair calls the next item first. Through the eyes of the widow Tench in the gallery, she is glad the courts will hear a widow and that politicians must defend their rules in public, and she still thinks the Hearth League's attack on the watch-house was frightening. The clerk adds another open question to the public agenda. The hall door stays open.

The Convention's only substantive motion, a tax on great estates when they pass to heirs, is referred to the Assembly's spring sitting under the procedure the delegates have just made. The procedure works on its first case; the vote will come next year.

Chapter 216: Two Years

On Wednesday 4 October, through Ferra's eyes, the Assembly votes nine days before the beast-trade law lapses. The debate covers the Clean Ground Service scandal, the dead at Brannick's Ford, and the fact that the Aegisframe export depends on the law existing. Morwen Thorvey moves to renew for two years instead of three, so the law will be voted on again before the first machines have served a full year. It passes by fourteen votes. Ferra had wanted three years and admits Thorvey was right.

On Monday 16 October, through Imani's eyes, Oswy Tamsk gets his second brain array, with a new chip that shields its own noise inside his skull. He thinks about closing his hand, and it closes. The first egg breaks and the second holds. He asks what he's supposed to do with an egg now, and Imani tells him to eat it.

Through Tess's eyes, the Aegisframe's certification report is in. Seven of eight trials have passed, but the counter-tampering trial is still open, and the emperor's pilots are only four months into a year of training. The first deliveries move to the spring of Year 26 rather than ship with a safety check unfinished. The Larkspur has flown over the bay, and Duvrenne has laid the frame for its own first machine at Kesrath. All autumn Tess's crew, listening on the Islanders' coil outside the fence (the review never allowed a post on the cap), has heard the slow beat in the hollow under the Isle speeding up in step with Ferra's mana readings. Emeric decides to go and see on Hollow Night. Ferra, Imani and his father go with him.

In Tess's hall Merrin shows the first processor on a single chip: a few thousand switches on a sliver of glass the size of a fingernail. Out in Dunholt's hills the Mage Corps makes the first cut for the Farstrike's first battery. Under the north quarry, a third room is open, cut to the king's pattern.

Chapter 217: Hollow Night

On the evening of Tuesday 31 October, through Imani's eyes, the Islanders light their lanterns and gather at the stone for the people with no graves. At about nine the beat under the Isle stops. Then the hollow floods with mana until Imani can see nothing else. The old guardian golems by the chamber doors wake and turn on whatever is nearest, which is the Isle works' night shift: about forty Islanders and Kaldo workers in the lower gallery. The roof begins to give way. The night foreman, Brice Gaskin, gets his crew moving toward the stair and is killed before anyone from above can reach him.

Through Harrold's eyes, watching from the gallery at the top of the shaft, the flood has broken the review's cap from below. Both locks are still shut and nobody opened anything. Mother Sabbe comes up from the Islanders' stone holding one of the two keys to a door that isn't there any more, and Emeric asks her anyway, in front of the king, because it's her people's hole. She tells him to go. Petrok is told by radio within the hour. The Duvrenne witness the seal requires is fetched from the treaty-house and doesn't get there in time; Emeric doesn't wait, and says so in the record. She signs the entry at dawn. Emeric goes down, and in that air he changes into something no one has seen. It isn't the dragon they know. It is every kind of magic he has at once, first in a human shape and then in the full form, with every colour of light running through him. Harrold sees his own gold in every line of it. Ferra, beside him, says nothing for a long time, and then just "oh."

Through Arthur's eyes, the knights' night glasses are useless in the glow, and he says so over the line. Emeric tears the guardian golems apart with fire, lightning and gravity together, and holds the gallery roof up while the Crestless bring the crew up the stair three at a time. Twenty-eight are out and twelve are still trapped behind a rockfall when Tess calls down the shaft that the tank at the centre is opening.

Tess's crew brought the first Kestrel to the Isle, but it stays in its case on the quay: rock swallows its radio picture, and there is no straight line down into the hollow.

Chapter 218: The Auditor

Through Emeric's eyes, the cradle at the heart of the hollow finishes making a body: tall, hairless, grey-lavender skin, a whole collar of crystal scales at the neck, unmarked hands. It is everything Oruse's failed body should have been. It opens its eyes and looks at him, at all of him, with what he will later describe to Imani as interest. It says one word no one there knows. Then it turns and walks down the old water channel into the sea under the Isle.

Emeric could follow it. The roof over the last twelve workers is being held up by him alone. He holds it until Mira's team has the last of them out, and only then lets it fall. He chooses the twelve. Tess knows roughly what the convoy's "halt" command looks like from Oruse's notes, but nobody has yet built a way to send it.

Through Ferra's eyes, Zale brings the Undertow round by dawn and finds nothing in the deep water past the Isle. Thirty-nine of the forty came up. Brice Gaskin didn't. The Core's gazette reports a collapse at Kaldo's Isle works on Hollow Night, one dead, and a light seen under the Isle. The creature appears in no newspaper. Emeric is back in his own shape by midnight, sitting on the quay steps beside the Islanders' stone, and Imani won't let anyone near him for an hour.

Velmoor's rangers send their log over the relay drum the same day. All through Hollow Night the Kestrel perched above the high valley's cradle saw nothing and heard nothing, and the crusted door is as it was. Ferra lays it beside the Isle's chart: one cradle woke, not two. She files it under the line and doesn't underline it.

Chapter 219: The Line Going Up

On Wednesday 1 November, through Ferra's eyes, she lays out her chart: the mana in the air, the sightings of the beasts' watchers, the beat under the Isle, and the old ice-core line that only ever fell. Hollow Night's reading crosses it. One line climbs past where the world stood before the pope drained it. She writes nothing underneath. The same morning she files the breach with the review's clerk: the cap broken from below at about nine, the keyholder asked, the witness late, forty in the gallery, thirty-nine up. On Monday 4 December Keller rules the entry a lawful rescue through a breach nobody made and orders the cap poured again with both keys present. Petrok comes over on the trade boat. Emeric reads the order and doesn't argue with it.

Among the sky's entries on Ferra's board goes a line from Junia at the First Glass: a star that dims by about one part in a hundred every eleven days. It is the first planet anyone has found around another star. Ferra writes it down and doesn't underline it.

On Monday 6 November, through Imani's eyes, she tells Oruse what came out of the cradle and repeats the word as best she can. Oruse's golem body locks at every joint. The word is her own name in her makers' language, the name every auditor is taught to honour because they were told she died a martyr. The creature is Seyr, she says, her student. It came to finish her mission, and it believes she is dead. "It won't know what I am now," she says. She doesn't say whether she wants it to find her, and Imani writes that down. Ostrella hears it all from the doorway and leaves without a word to tell Emeric. Oruse adds that an auditor carries a line home through the cradle that made it, and no one can yet tell whether Seyr has used it.

On Monday 25 December, through Harrold's eyes, the Long Table seats Ostrella as a guest of the house, beside Jerome, who is teaching her which fork the Randillians think matters. Oruse asked not to come and is in Tess's shop mending a hinge. Adana has a year to go. Runa sits beside Ward. Brice Gaskin's widow is there at Harrold's own invitation, and he sits with her.

Late that night, through Emeric's eyes, he stands on the wall looking at the black sea past the Isle. Ferra and Imani find him there. He tells them the Common will hear all of it in the spring: the pope, the two women, Avarran, the makers, the convoy, and the creature in the sea. Ferra says the Assembly will want to hear it first. He agrees: the Assembly first. Imani takes his arm.

Where the Story Stands After Chapter 222

Book 3 ends here. Kaldo's courts, its Assembly, its hearings, its Convention and its neighbours now act on their own clocks and sometimes against Emeric, and he has learned to live with every ruling. The pope is gone, the Church has no head, Ostrella is whole and answering for what she did, and Oruse lives in a body Kaldo can watch. Most of the world still knows none of this.

The world's mana has climbed back past the line it fell from long ago, and the old machinery under the Isle answered by printing an auditor, which has walked into the sea. It is Oruse's own student, sent to finish her mission. Kaldo knows roughly how to stop it and has no way yet to do so. Book 4 opens with Kaldo telling the Assembly, and then everyone else, the truth.
