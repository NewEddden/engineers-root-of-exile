Book 3, Batch 19: Whole

Chapters 204–212 | Monday 2 January - Monday 26 June, Year 25

By the start of Year 25 Kaldo had put back together every piece of Ostrella it could reach except one, the stone that held her name. That stone sat in a cathedral that owned it lawfully, and a court had ordered it lent only once the separation itself was authorised. Two women still shared one stolen body in a cell under Ostrel.

This batch spends the spring finishing other people's stories: a priest's hands, a family's bell, a girl's eighteenth birthday, a pilot's cup of water. Then it spends one Thursday in June in Kaldo's dome, where two minds go into two bodies and the first thing one of them says is her own name. Even then, a court sets the terms, a cathedral's dean watches, and the freed woman's first question is about the people she harmed.

Chapter 204: His Own Hands

On Thursday 19 January, through Saban's eyes, the man whose flask turned the Brannick's Ford carter into a Crownborn is sentenced under the Blood Act to twenty-four years at Stillwater. He will be cured and kept without mana until he is rehabilitated, and like anyone he can apply for the Final Review. The other four get four to nine years at Turnstone. The carter's widow testifies without looking at him.

On Monday 6 February, through Jerome's eyes, he is bridged out of the golem and into his own healed body, about sixty years after the chapel night when the pope took it. For a week he can't hold a cup. Amara holds it for him two mornings running, and on the third he knocks it out of her hand on purpose. Through Adana's eyes, her body needs about two more years. She says so first at breakfast, so nobody else has to.

Chapter 205: The Bell

On Thursday 2 March, through Mistress Brenmoor's eyes, the family's new bell rings for the first time. Jerome helped cast it, working the bellows in his borrowed body every few weeks and, on the last visit, in his own. She gives Kaldo the twenty-ninth tomb's stone on one condition: she must be in the room when it's opened. A week later she watches from behind the glass as the pair rejoins. All she sees is a priest's hand on a stone and a body on a bed. Her ancestor's name goes into Ferra's log as the first cardinal who learned what Jerome learned.

Emeric came with Jerome to hear the bell, and brought one of Tess's men with a sound gauge, a microphone on a needle meter. He asks her leave to try something on the second stroke. When it rings, the note goes thin where he stands, thin but not gone, while her palm on the bell's frame still feels it humming the whole time. The needle drops by a third. She asks what he did, and he says he took some of the sound out of the air. She tells him never to do that to her bell again: a bell is for being heard half a county off. He agrees, and goes home with the needle's number.

On Monday 13 March, through Edda Rowe's eyes, the Assembly holds the pay hearing. A member says the Clean Ground wage is too high for honest work and too low to stop the dishonest. Edda says the wage is the reason nine in ten crews never touched a flask, and names the crews who searched their own wash lines without being asked. The wage stays.

The same week the Ore Handling Act passes. Imani's handling rules become law, the sorting sheds are licensed, the Clean Ground Service inspects them, and Edda's crews carry the ore counter.

Chapter 206: Eighteen

On Monday 6 March, through Runa's eyes, she goes before the Stone Hearing like anyone else. The Hearing asks something Emeric never would: does she want the stone for herself, or because everyone expects it? She says for herself, and that she isn't sure. The register grants her a seed for her eighteenth birthday. Emeric isn't allowed in the room, and she's glad of the rule.

On Sunday 2 April she turns eighteen, eats the seed and sleeps for three days while it rebuilds her. A letter from Liane waits on her pillow. The werewolves' council has accepted the new law's clause letting werewolves try their own on imperial land.

On the night of Saturday 22 April, through Emeric's eyes, Runa changes for the first time, in the gravity hall. He takes his own dragon form so she isn't the only one, and talks her through the fear of a body that might not come back. It comes back as a wolf six feet at the shoulder, dark brown with rust at the tips of her ruff and tail, with her mother's pale grey-green eyes looking out of it. Her tail, never trimmed in this form, is the first thing she looks at, and she laughs, which comes out as something else. The crescent bite on her forearm shows as a pale curve in the fur of her foreleg, and she noses it once. She can't speak as the wolf, and the first time she tries to change back she can't find the way and panics for a breath, until he tells her to do it the way she'd stop holding her breath. Back in her own body she empties the hall's food locker and sleeps fourteen hours. Afterwards she runs the length of the hall faster than either of them expected, and he has to look away so she doesn't see his face.

On Wednesday 5 April Runa wakes from the rebuild with Emeric in the chair, where Imani finally made him sit on the second night. He can't speak. She knocks on his chest, two quick and one slow, her father's knock, and he laughs.

Chapter 207: Precision

On Monday 24 April, through Imani's eyes, Oswy Tamsk volunteers for the first brain interface, an array that should read his intention to move and drive his artificial hand. Imani places it with Tess's chip behind his ear. It reads nothing usable, because the chip's own noise buries the signal. She takes it out that week and he heals cleanly. He says to try again in the autumn. On Thursday 4 May, Bettany grows her first finger, a Saltreach net-maker's ring finger, with Imani's hand on her wrist the whole four hours. The net-maker asks whether it will grow a nail. It does, on the ninth day.

On Monday 8 May, through Bram's eyes, five days before the show, Tess flies the first Kestrel on the proving strip at dawn. It is a drone: a flat charcoal ring 1.4 metres across with two stacked rotors inside it, a camera that sends a live picture to a case on the ground, four of Emeric's damping stones in its lip and two gravity beads in its keel. With the stones on (the switch reads QUIET) it hangs forty feet up with a sound like wind in a hedge. With them off (COLD) it sounds like a hornet's nest. Bram says to leave the stones off. Emeric says then everyone hears it, and it can only stay up thirteen minutes. Bram says then build it so thirteen minutes is enough. Bram also remembers Sorrel turning a propeller on its back years ago and Bren saying the deck stones already do that; Emeric points at the field where a forced stone killed the visors last September. Not in a dead zone they don't. Tess perches the Kestrel on the empty stand's roof and takes a photograph of Bram's face, which goes up on the hall's board beside the memory chip. That week Ferra puts the Kestrel under the eye's written order: no flight over anyone's ground without her order, every flight logged, and none over Velmoor without Velmoor's leave.

On Saturday 13 May, through Bram's eyes, the Aegisframe's public show finally happens, a season late, on its own proving strip. Every safety rule in it comes from Bram having said no. Hattie Roan carries a full cup of water a hundred paces in the machine's hand and sets it on a post without spilling it. The emperor's officer asks for the engineering report before he claps. The emperor's order stays at two.

Chapter 208: The Court's Yes

On Monday 22 May, through Ferra's eyes, every pair from the crypt has been rejoined except the last, and the Imperial Core's high court authorises the separation in Kaldo's dome. Its conditions: joint custody continues, the imperial physician and one Church observer attend the procedure, Harrowmere's dean stays beside the relic, the court gets a written account within thirty days, and anything done to Augustine's body afterwards needs its own written order. Ferra accepts every condition before Emeric can look at her. The bodies travel south inside dead ground, with the relic under the dean's seal on its own wagon.

Through Imani's eyes, Ostrella's body comes out of its crystal in the dome. Imani spends twelve days mending what she can. A control stone will finish the rebuild in July, once Ostrella is inside it, because the rebuild follows its owner's own pattern. Tess finishes a golem body wearing the face of Oruse's failed print. Kaldo can read it, and stopping it takes two keys, the crown's and the Final Review's. Sennel, the Keeper of the Passing, arrives from the elves' forest with a stone built to carry knowledge without the person. Looking at the pope's body, she says what she told them in the forest two summers ago: "There's no he."

Chapter 209: The Last Pair

On the morning of Thursday 15 June, for the only time, the page belongs to Oruse. The relic is opened, and she is asked. Refusing would keep Ostrella nameless inside herself forever, and that is the last leverage Oruse has. For one breath she considers it: the mission is lost, the body is about to go, and the life ahead of her will be watched. Then she says yes. Ostrella says yes too, and the pair closes. Nobody claps.

Through Imani's eyes, Ostrella is offered the years that went to dust, as Avarran saw them, including feelings that were never what she hoped. She gives a separate yes. Through Emeric's eyes, the sealed part of his memory moves for the first time. He feels it flow into her without seeing any of it, and afterwards the closed books are still shut, only lighter. The Keeper's stone sorts what is Oruse's knowledge from who Ostrella is, and with Oruse's consent keeps a copy of what she knows.

Adana walls the room and Jerome holds the bond while Emeric leads. Before she goes, Oruse tells Imani and Tess where the back doors are in the bodies the convoy grows: the commands to recall and to halt. Tess writes until her pencil breaks. Then the two signatures part. Augustine's body lies empty, breathing. The golem sits up with Oruse's face. Ostrella's own body takes a breath. The oldest pieces are dust and the burned register is gone. Ostrella is whole; the evidence isn't.

Chapter 210: Her Name

That afternoon, after the court's witnesses leave, Ostrella breathes with lungs unused for two thousand years, and her first word is her own name. Through Imani's eyes, Imani asks before every check. Ostrella agrees to one and says "not yet" to another, and Imani waits. Then Ostrella looks at Imani for a long time and asks for "the other one." Amara goes to fetch Ferra.

Through Amara's eyes, the first real question comes from her: what did you do to people? Ostrella answers honestly, covering the war cult, the rite and the uprooting of the groves, and doesn't let herself be only a victim. She tells the story of Avarran and her failed annulment. Emeric catches a detail that doesn't match a recovered memory, and she corrects herself, saying it's the first time anyone could. She helped raise his two sons, so they are in the telling: the elder, who checked the record of his mothers' deaths as a young man and reopened it forty years on, and whose finding she never had to stop, because she held his heirs' stones and outlasted him; and the younger, who crossed the sea, whose stone she kept and never touched. She says their names. The ache comes for all three at once, and for the first time it has a cause. Ferra remembers a warden in the steward's room in January of Year 18, and a statue at a council stair. It was never Emeric's face on that statue. It was their son's.

Through Ferra's eyes, Ostrella tells the rest. When the twins she loved were killed, she caught their dying minds in two stones and carried them through every body for two thousand years. Jerome and Adana explain that two women shared their stones until those women left, the year Emeric was born. Ferra and Imani already know they were born the same morning. The room puts it together unevenly. Each of the three has a piece of the forest to set beside it: Ferra, a charge put to her in an office one of the twins once kept; Imani, two cups poured before anyone said who was coming; Emeric, a reading he shut out at that table without asking either of them. Imani asks what was done to them, and whether anything is being done to her now. Ferra asks whether Ostrella had any right to carry them, then asks for the evidence and the names of anyone else it was done to. Ostrella tells them she can say what followed them into this life, but not who they chose to become. Emeric doesn't say what any of it means for the three of them. Imani asks to stop for the night, and he lets it stop.

In a short last section Ostrella names one thing she doesn't know and one thing she did that she can't justify. Among the years that came back to her through Emeric is one look: Avarran glancing up from his bench with plain affection and no desire at all. She says so plainly. It hurts, and she keeps it. Ferra asks her whether the two boys were happy, and she is asking the only person alive who knows. Ostrella starts to answer, and one of the three stops her. Emeric has every day he spent with those boys shelved in his own head, in books that do not open. Ferra leaves first, before anything is tidy, to start the list.

Chapter 211: Free That Man

On Friday 16 June, through Amara's eyes, three conditions are met on paper. Emeric's reading of Augustine's dead mind has been on record since Book 1. Imani confirms at noon that the body carries no signature at all. The court has sent its written order, and Harrold signed the crown's that morning. The court's clerks found no living kin. That night Imani lets Augustine's body go, gently, and the page doesn't say how. Doran says the rites in Augustine's own name.

Amara chose to be there, and she says nothing. Years ago her magic took this man's right arm, and for years that hand stayed closed around the stone that held her mind. She unfolds the fingers, finds the hand empty, and leaves it open. Emeric says nothing either. The rite doesn't settle anything between them.

Chapter 212: The Fitting

On Monday 19 June, through Harrold's eyes, Emeric tells his father all of it, the two sons included: the one Harrold's own line comes down from, and the one who left. Harrold asks one question about Avarran and seven about his son. Then he asks whether the woman who threw his son's mind across the sky meant to send it home. Emeric says yes. Harrold says he'll thank her himself, once, and never again.

On Thursday 22 June, through Imani's eyes, Oruse is fitted into her golem body in Tess's shop, with Ostrella at the end of the bench. Imani asks why Oruse never grew herself a sound body. "Do you think I haven't tried?" Every attempt came out as broken as she is. Ostrella shut the route through pregnancy until, after many bodies, she allowed the juggernauts, and she never let Oruse into her child. Then Oruse jokes that if the material had come from Avarran, Ostrella might have allowed it. Ostrella goes red to the ears and tells her to shut up, and Tess drops a screwdriver.

On Saturday 24 June Augustine is buried in Randillia City as a cardinal and a man, and his grave says Augustine. On Monday the Imperial Core's gazette reports that the Holy Father died in custody after a long illness, still calling the pope "he." The Church's claimants call a conclave to elect a new pope and can't gather enough cardinals to hold it, so the papacy stays empty. The juggernauts go to Stillwater to be treated as the people they are. In her first testimony Ostrella tells Ferra where the high valley is.

Where the Story Stands After Chapter 215

Ostrella and Oruse are separated. Ostrella is whole in her own body, waiting for a stone to finish its rebuild, and her accounting has begun with the people she harmed. Oruse lives in a body Kaldo can read and stop, though only with two keys. She has handed over the convoy's back doors, and a copy of her knowledge is kept the elves' way. Augustine is free and buried under his own name.

The inner circle now knows Emeric was Avarran, and Ferra and Imani know whose daughters they once were. All three know there were two sons, that one line runs down to Harrold and the other founded the Westgone, and nobody has yet told them whether those sons were happy. The public knows a pope died in custody. The Church has no pope, and its people are going where they choose. Jerome has his own hands back, Runa has her stone, and the empire's machine has shown it can be careful. What's left of Year 25 belongs to the wider world: a vote, a convention, and a line on Ferra's chart that keeps rising.
