Book 3, Batch 15: The Everywhere Night

Chapters 141-153 | Monday 15 November - Saturday 25 December, Year 23

By the middle of November in Year 23 the gaps between attacks abroad had fallen from a hundred and twelve days to forty. Fourteen pins stood on Ferra's board, and at that pace the next was due. The Capital Line was running, every one of Kaldo's baronies was under a dome, two soldiers' mechs stood in their bays, and a box of new sets sat in the line room's locker. Zale was in Kaldo with a sea mech that had no core in it.

This batch is not a skip. It follows the week the attacks came all at once, a day at a time, and then the weeks after it to Christmas. It is the week Kaldo's royal line went to war on five shores. At every front the people whose shore it was made the decisive move, kept their own dead, and gave or sold Kaldo only what they chose.

Chapter 141: Five Sets

On the evening of Monday 15 November, through Ferra's eyes, the sets in the line room ring from five places in four hours. Ilenne calls first: something huge has been in the Rim's harbour since dusk, shell over a soft body, and the watch is holding the inner basin. Then Jofrid: a siege beast is breaking at the Sea Hold's outer door. Then Kettrin: a brood at the Sun River ford, with orc wardens and Sabir boatmen on the banks together. Then Hila: a Glassrunner on the caravan road, two wagons already gone. Tiel calls last, near midnight, through static. There is a storm over the Guano Isles, small fliers in swarms, and the winged ones are shut inside. Five pins in one evening. Ferra stops measuring gaps.

By midnight the decision is made. The fleet can't be in five places, but the line can, and inside each sibling's own domain nobody beats them but a dragon. Emeric, Zale, Maeve and Corbin will go to Aumere first, because the Rim's beast is the biggest. Waylon will go straight from Duvrenne to the caravan road with Alina. Ward, the two Axions, Wren's Bulwark and two Outriders go on the K Amara, and Imani goes as the line's surgeon. Harrold takes Kaldo's chair. Ferra stays to run the board, with Runa beside her by Ward's order and her own choice.

Through Corbin's eyes, Emeric finds him at the castle half dressed and asks him to come. Corbin says yes before Emeric has finished asking. He has never been past the wall. The line doesn't need his firepower, and both of them know it. Every one of them is handed a set from the new run, scrambler on, no position sent in clear.

Chapter 142: Full Burn

Before dawn on Tuesday 16 November, through Zale's eyes, Emeric rings Sohra's set from the K Amara's bridge. He tells her where they are going and why, and asks for nothing. She says she'll tell her grandmother. No answer comes, and that is the answer. The K Amara climbs into the Band and goes flat out, afterburner lit and the Outriders tucked under her wings. She draws nothing: every rack burns down from its own store, the necks stay shut, and Ferra's clerk logs the draw at zero under Kaldo's own sky. Somewhere over the dragons' country a dragon flies alongside them for a few minutes, high and wide, and peels off, and nobody aboard can say which one. Zale knows it is the first time a dragon has let a human ship through its sky without a word. That night Sohra adds one thing on the set, unasked: her uncle Corvan has left the cone and won't say where, because his mother let the gold-eyed king fly over it.

Through Ilenne's eyes, the K Amara comes down on the Rim's outer water by mid-afternoon with her racks nearly empty. She will have to charge from the sea for two days. Zale goes over the side before she has settled. Ilenne meets her husband in the water at the watch's line, says nothing, and holds his wrist hard enough to bruise.

Chapter 143: The Shallows

On Wednesday 17 November, through Ilenne's eyes, the plan is the watch's, not Kaldo's. The Trenchglass is safe in the deep inner basin and helpless in the shallows over the reef flat, so the queen's watch drives it up with their boils and noise lines, the way they herd fish. Zale, in his sea form, holds the water at the basin's edge so it can't sound back into the deep. The Bulwark stands in the harbour mouth with Wren in the sphere and shuts it. The Axions on the breakwater fire into the water ahead of the beast to turn it. Maeve's lightning and Corbin's stillness pin its tail on the flat for the minute that matters. The queen's watch kills it with their own spears through the breathing folds, and Ilenne's goes in first. Two of the watch die.

Zale holds the basin's edge whole. The partial form is not enough to hold a basin, so in the water he asks for all of him, the way Ilenne taught him to ask on land, and the sea dragon comes, the first whole sea dragon of the line in two thousand years. Nobody forced it. It empties him like every first, and he is a man again on the reef flat by dusk.

The dry folk went down with the watch and stayed down for minutes with no breathing packs. Ilenne, showing nothing, asks how. Emeric tells her it is a new method of holding air with the body's own mana, that they are still training it, and that he'll teach her and her watch. She says she will learn it first herself.

That evening at the gate, through Corbin's eyes, Emeric introduces him. "Our brother Corbin. We almost fought to the death once. Long story." Ilenne, colourless: "Are you going to?" Corbin: "Not this week." Zale laughs, which in merfolk company is rude, and does it anyway.

Chapter 144: The Queen's Hall

On Thursday 18 November, through the eyes of Velis, a kinsman of the merfolk house the crown struck for its crimes, the charge is made in the open, and Velis believes it. The beasts have half-gold eyes and the king has gold eyes. The beasts haunt the dense waters that feed the merfolk and leave the thin continent alone. A dry king would have every reason to want a harvester he never has to feed. The hall answers every point, with Ilenne presiding and saying almost nothing. The merfolk's listeners hear no people and no family in the carcass on the flat. Ferra's log of the crossing, read out by set, shows the draw at zero. Kaldo names its own dead. The marks read so far point at labs on the merfolk's own islands. Velis loses the hall. He keeps his grudge, says so, and is still a voice in the hall. Nobody strikes him for it.

Through Emeric's eyes, the Trenchglass died in Aumere's water, so its core is Aumere's. Ilenne gives it for Zale's mech, the Undertow, which will guard her harbour before it guards anything else, and Ferra writes the gift down by set while Aumere's council witnesses it. Then Emeric tells her the goal: collect every core, read each one's mark to find the labs the beasts come from, silence the hidden note in every core that lets the cradles listen through them, keep the cores off the black market, and then break the cradles. Ilenne puts the Shore Compact behind it by set that night. Kettrin, Hila, Jofrid and Tiel answer from their own fights that they have heard.

Chapter 145: The Pen

The same Thursday night, through the eyes of Tamas, a young imperial soldier on the stair under Ostrel's cathedral close, a beast comes up through the undercroft floor. It is a burrower, grown far bigger than any wild young on something the Church has been feeding it. It doesn't go out. It goes down, following the mana, into a crypt nobody outside the Church knew existed: rows of crystal casings with bodies in them. It drains the oldest bay to dust and smashes a preservation bank, and the casings in the next bay begin to fail. A page of the bay's register is drifting toward its mouth. Tamas could save the page or the two failing casings. He throws his lamp's charged stone to draw the beast off the casings. The two bodies live. The page is gone, and one person in that bay will never be named. He did the right thing, and it wasn't enough.

Through the eyes of Anton Kesh, an imperial sapper Saban has paid for years, he writes down the bay number, sketches a casing, and notes the two bodies with no tomb plates: a man wearing the ring of Randillia's see and a woman in a tower mage's coat. He thinks they are recently dead. The Church's lay guard takes him on the stair and holds him. The beast breaks out into the lower city, and the Church's engineered soldiers at the gate don't stop it. Then something walks out of the cathedral's lower gate after it: a figure of crude grey iron twice the height of a house, moving like an animal, with a man's shape in a cage in its chest. It and the emperor's guns kill the beast in the lower market at dawn. The emperor's soldiers see it, and Anton sees it from the guardroom window. It walks back inside. Nobody outside Ostrel has ever seen a Church machine. Now dozens have. A priest of the close, Father Orlan, opens its kitchens to the families burned out of the market and feeds them through the morning without anyone's leave.

Chapter 146: Where Nobody Wore a Band

Ostrellin is the quiet ground, but not quiet everywhere. On Thursday night, through Edda Rowe's eyes, the last two of the Isle's young beasts come ashore, grown on the thickening air. One comes up the beach at Saint Ilda's to the healing spring, where the mana is. Nobody there wears the king's band, so there is no warning buzz and no distress call, and the abbot's pilgrims at the night office hear it last. Four of them die before anyone in Kaldo knows. A shepherd rings from the toll house on the coast road. Rowena and six knights get there by road before dawn and kill it on the shingle, their night glass down because a warm body shows clearly on a cold shore. Edda, a mender since June, comes on the first ambulance and closes three wounds the abbey's infirmarian can't. On Friday morning the second beast comes up the causeway into Coldharbour's moor and takes two peat cutters in the fog. Merryn Pellow's licensed crew come by the Capital Line to Wayford and by truck from there, with Hattie and the dog, and kill it in the bog by noon.

Through Mabyn Hoskin's eyes, the hospital's hall has every Kaldo clinic on its board with a mender at each, and the Capital Line's night trains run menders and stretchers to Wayford and back. Imani is an ocean away. Mabyn runs the hall, and nothing waits for Imani, because nothing has to. All twenty-one of the Isle's young are now dead, though their mother has still never been seen. Six people died that week in the two places that never took the band. Kaldo says nothing about it in public. On Monday the abbot of Saint Ilda's asks for the band, and Coldharbour's parishes ask the same week. Nobody in Kaldo says "we told you".

Chapter 147: One Rider

At dusk on Friday 19 November, through Maeve's eyes, she reaches the Guano Isles alone in her storm form after a day and a night of flying. The storm over the stacks becomes hers. The swarms of small fliers can't hold air that belongs to her, and they tire without the lift they need. The winged ones' young at the arrow slits kill what falls. Maeve doesn't fight the swarm. She takes its sky away.

She flew the day in the partial form, making her weather as she went. Over the stacks, inside her own storm, she goes whole for the first time, and holds it a night and a day: the storm she made is dense with rain and mana, like the one that woke her, and it feeds her for exactly as long as the weather lasts.

On Saturday, through Tiel's eyes, the Nestwardens' council stands by Tiel's request: one rider, asked for by name, their own choice and not a breach of their ban on Kaldo's fliers. Maeve passes on Emeric's question, whether the winged ones would move to a people who could shelter them. The answer is no. These are the stacks their ancestors chose when they lost the sky. They will take help on the council's terms: rifle nests on the ledges, sold to the council as a government and tagged under Kaldo's arms law; nets and guns; warning bands; and listening posts inside the stacks, every one keyed to the winged ones' own locks. Tiel says the cost plainly to Maeve. A foreign people is putting guns and ears inside their homes, and if the old war ever comes again, where else can they go? Kaldo will hold no key to any post, and that is true. The posts aren't the only eyes Kaldo has, but Maeve doesn't know that.

Chapter 148: Still Water

Through Kettrin's eyes, the brood has held the Sun River ford for three days, its young in the shallows and a Broodmother laying her eggs on a reed island below them. At dawn on Friday Corbin comes down the river as a shard dragon and holds it still. The current stops, the shallows go flat and glassy, and the young can't swim away, nor can their mother slide into the deep channel. The orc wardens and the Sabir boatmen do the killing, in their own order: the boatmen pole across water that doesn't move, and the wardens take the island. They kill the mother while she is laying, which is when she is helpless, and that was their whole plan. Three wardens and a boatman die. Corbin holds the river all day without moving.

The shard dragon that comes down the river is the whole of one, and nobody has seen it before. Corbin has had it since a night on the old bridge two and a half years ago.

That night he lets the water go a hand's width at a time on the boatmen's count. An orc warden asks him whether he was really the king's enemy. "Yes." "And now?" "Now I'm holding his river." "It's ours." "I know." Everything from the kill belongs to the orcs' roll and the boatmen, because it died on their ground. The roll sells the Broodmother's glands to Imani's clinics, since they can make the cure for drinkers of beast blood a simple injection, and it has read Kaldo's plain-words notice on the blood. The cores go into Kaldo's shut cases with the owners' title kept, and the head is sent to be read.

Chapter 149: Soft Sand

On Friday 19 November, through Waylon's eyes, he is already on the caravan road. Alina came from Duvrenne by Kaldo hull with the Lift Compact's envoy, and he came on his own wings. The Glassrunner has run the road for four days, following the wagons by the shaking of the ground. Waylon makes the sand his: soft where it runs, hard where it turns, a hundred yards of desert that won't let it keep its feet. It was never good at turning tight, and on his sand it can't run straight either.

Through Hila's eyes, the Sabir riders turn it, with orc wardens lent across the border under the Shore Compact riding behind them. On the third pass the Sabir lances go into the fragile seams along its ribs. Hila's brother is one of the riders, and the kill is theirs. The road is the Lift Compact's ground, so the beast is the Lift Compact's. It sells the Glassrunner's core and its springy tendons to Kaldo at an assessed price, to pay for its wells. The tendons will finally give the whip sword its proper cord, and the core will run Rowena's mech. Alina writes the escrow herself.

Chapter 150: The Hold and Under It

On Saturday morning, through the eyes of Torvild of Deepforge, the recharged K Amara comes down off the Sea Hold's sea gate with Emeric, Ward and the two Axions aboard, and the fight is already over. A siege beast broke the outer door on Monday. The hold's hand-piloted wardens held the inner gate for four days and killed it on the quay on Friday night while it rested after a charge, with Torvild's warden on its rear knee. The hold lost two wardens, the quay and eleven dwarves, and Hulda is there keeping the tally. The beast's core is the Sea Hold's, and the hold keeps it for its own next warden. Nobody from Kaldo asks for it.

Through Ward's eyes, the Murr come up through the hold's lowest door and ask for help, which they have never done before. Something is in their caves under the hold, coming up beneath the dwarves' foundations. Emeric, Ward, two knights and the Murr's guides go down, while the Axions, too big for the galleries, hold the tunnel mouths. Here the night glass is the best tool they have, its heat picture finding the beast's warmth in the cold rock at a hundred yards. It is a burrower, slow away from the surface, in a gallery the Murr sound out by ear. The Murr bring the gallery roof down on the thin seams behind its shoulders with their echo-stones, and Ward finishes it through the seams. The Murr sell its core to Kaldo for lamps and a sounding duct up to the dwarves' hold, so the next thing can be heard coming. Torvild and two smiths sail for Kaldo to learn the mind-cap that pilots Kaldo's machines. Ward's own mech will have to keep waiting for a siege beast's core: the only one this week was the Sea Hold's, and the hold kept it.

Chapter 151: Points on a Map

On Saturday 27 November, through Tess's eyes in Kaldo's line room, the marks from the night's cores are read. Each core's grain is described to her by set and checked against the two cores on her bench, and Ferra pins the labs on the board: under the islands off the Sun River's mouth again, under the High Shore's interior again, under a chain off the Guano Isles, under the deep shelf off the Rim, and under the dry edge's salt pans. For the first time the board shows where the beasts come from, not only where they land. Ferra writes one line under it: five places, on five peoples' own ground.

Through Maeve's eyes, the line compares scars at the queen's table at Aumere, as it has done since the first voyage. Zale has his shoulder. Maeve's hands are burned from holding a storm for a day. Corbin has nothing at all, which annoys everyone. Waylon, on the set, refuses to describe his. Emeric has his forearm from the tunnel roof. Ilenne says the merfolk don't show their scars, and then shows one. She and Corbin have a conversation about holding still that nobody else can follow. The next book's war is on that map. Nobody says the word "makers".

Then Emeric looks at the five pins and says what he'll say to the Assembly, from nothing but what the table saw: five fronts in one week, and five fronts' worth of machines. Next time there will be more of them than there are machines. Ilenne asks what he'll build. Something that doesn't have to get there first, he says.

Chapter 152: Lies, and a Button

Through Amara's eyes, Ostrel's answer comes first. On Monday 22 November Deacon Varro, the cathedral close's administrator, signs the order shutting the lower level and a statement that the soldiers' reports are lies, and by Sunday the pulpits are blaming the beasts of the crown for the lower market. On Tuesday Saban brings her Anton Kesh's report, out of the guardroom since the emperor's men freed him: the ring of Randillia's see and a tower mage's coat. Amara won't confirm her parents from a ring and a coat. She writes down one private detail, a single shaky line and then procedure, and has it sealed in front of Keswick for the Core's legation and Ferra for the crown before anyone goes back in. She tells nobody what it says, not even Harrold. On Monday 29 November the emperor's hazard order reopens the lower level to his own engineers so they can make the failing casings safe, and an imperial magistrate who distrusts Kaldo on principle goes in with them and writes down what he sees on the woman's coat. On Thursday 2 December Keswick opens the envelope beside the magistrate's sworn page. It says: the button she sewed on her mother's coat in the wrong colour thread, the night before her parents left for Ostrel, when she was about five. It matches. The magistrate signs, and doesn't become an admirer. It unlocks only the next step, her lawful claim to the bodies. Amara goes out and walks the river wall alone, and Harrold waits at the end of it.

Through Severin's eyes, every pulpit of the Core forbids the faithful to wear the king of Randillia's band on Sunday 5 December. On Monday 6 December, in his own council and in his own voice, he breaks with the Church. The Core will believe its soldiers. He orders their signed reports printed in the Core's gazette under his own seal, by his own register clerks rather than the parish's. He tells the cardinal on his council that a Church which kept a beast under its holiest ground and a machine behind its gate has lost his trust, not his faith. He writes to the king of Randillia asking for the band for the Core's garrisons and the lower city, over the pope's refusal, and in the same writ suspends relations with the Holy See. He does not let Kaldo into Ostrel. A beast under his holiest ground hurts him, and so would a foreign king inside it. That waits for a second independent report and terms of his own. The empress sends Maren the tiramisu's recipe back, with one change of her own.

Chapter 153: The Long Table

Through Ferra's eyes, the weeks after the night have their own work. On Saturday 20 November Mochi was brought to the carcass on Saint Ilda's shingle and wouldn't touch it, and neither would the recycling works' slime colony. From that day no slime in Kaldo will clear a dead beast. On Monday 29 November the Trenchglass's core went into the Undertow on the Cradle, and Zale sat in the sphere with a working machine under him for the first time. Torvild and the two smiths began learning the cap beside Hattie in the ride room. From Monday 6 December Kaldo's installers began fitting the winged ones' posts, working only where a winged warden stood beside them and handing over every key as each post was finished.

On Friday 10 December Bram braids the whip sword's new cord from beast tendons and takes the steel spring out, and the Broodmother's glands reach Imani's lab, where the new injection will be tried on rats before anyone else. On Monday 13 December, at the Claim's core room, Tess finds the hidden listening note. It is in every core, from the smallest to the largest, and it answers the call of the beasts' signalling kind. It is the same in all of them, which nobody could see when there were only one or two to compare. She silences it in each core before any goes back to its owner, with the owner's witness at the bench. The same day Alina opens talks at Kesrath between the Core's legation and Velmoor's harbour masters about the Core's shipping, and Kaldo isn't at the table.

On Tuesday 14 December, through Ferra's eyes, Emeric brings drawings to the Assembly: the Farstrike, a mass driver, batteries of long guns high in the mountains that can put a guided rod on a big beast a thousand kilometres away in about eight minutes. The objections are fair and get their time: the cost against schools and the rail, mountains given over to firing ranges, a weapon that could reach other peoples' lands. Ferra writes a condition into the motion: no shot outside Randillia's borders except at a host's written request. Ward asks what happens when one misses. It is steered to empty ground, Emeric says; every target box has a cleared fall line behind it, and a miss makes a crater, not a ruined town. Tess asks by set whether the rod can find the beast's signature on the way down. It can, and that's the part she'll build. On Thursday the budget gives the Farstrike its survey and nothing more.

On Thursday 16 December the Assembly passes the Year 24 budget and, with it, the act founding the Clean Ground Service. It sits under the hospital and answers to the Assembly, works by Imani's rules, and has Edda Rowe running the field crews, with hazard pay, a clinic band on every worker, and two signatures to seal any flask of blood and two to open it. The groves' cap is weighed again, as Emeric's amendment requires. Ward says the Watch could guard four thousand new seeds next spring. Emeric says nothing at all. Morwen Thorvey moves to hold it at three thousand, because nobody can yet say why the two lines move together and the night has made the question bigger, not smaller. It holds by four votes. On Monday 20 December Edda's first cordon clears the carcass in Coldharbour's bog: tested, harvested under custody, the blood sealed for Imani's lab, the rest burned in a lined pit. By Christmas there are three hundred names on the waiting list for the Service's first crews.

On Saturday 25 December, through Corbin's eyes, the whole line sits at one table for the first time since the war that made them a line. Harrold and Amara are there, Emeric with Ferra and Imani, Zale and Ilenne, who came on the K Amara's return by her own choice, Waylon and Alina, Maeve and Mira, the six, Runa, Tess and Osric. Torvild doesn't know the custom and brings a gift anyway, which turns out to be the custom. Emeric hands Corbin the knife, and Corbin carves. Nobody makes a speech. Amara keeps her hand on the copy of the envelope in her pocket all evening and doesn't mention it.

After the carving Ferra puts the sheet on the table between the dishes, which she has never done at a meal. Six rows, three columns, the partial form, the full form and mastery, and Emeric's row has a fourth box nobody else has, pieces. She fills Maeve's whole, 19 November, and Zale's, 17 November, and stops at Zale's last box: on land, no wings. Zale stands up in the dry long hall, asks, and for the first time his wings open on dry ground, knocking a candle into the gravy. Ferra writes the date. Corbin watches them find his, whole, 9 June, Year 21, and Emeric reads it twice. Waylon says he was first to master it and would like that noted; Ferra says it is noted, it is in ink. Harrold's last box is the drop, dated the night the dragons went home, and he does not explain it.

Where the Story Stands After Chapter 153

The beasts came on five shores in one night, and five peoples beat them, each in its own way and on its own ground. The line was there as help. At the Sea Hold it arrived after the fight was won. Every core is in a shut case with its owner's title kept, and every hidden listening note in them has been silenced. For the first time the board in Kaldo shows where the beasts come from: five places, all on the peoples' own ground. An old merfolk family still blames the gold-eyed king. The winged ones have Kaldo's guns in their homes and say plainly what that costs them.

At home the healing held without Imani, and two towns that never asked for the king's band paid for it and have asked now. Nothing will eat a dead beast anymore, so Kaldo has built a service to do it. The Assembly held the groves where they were.

Under Ostrel the Church's secret is out. It kept a beast under its holiest ground and a machine behind its gate, and a crypt nobody knew of holds two bodies Amara is now sure are her parents. The emperor has broken with the Church and asked for the band the pope forbade, but he hasn't yet let Kaldo in. That waits for the new year.
