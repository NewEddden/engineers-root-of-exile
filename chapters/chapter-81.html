Chapter 81 - Over the Top of It

Aldane learned from the ford, which was the thing Emeric had feared more than losing it, because a duke who lost a fight and learned nothing was a nuisance and a duke who lost a fight and understood why was a war.

He understood why. His hammers had broken on Emeric's steel and his knights had spent themselves against a line that would not break, and he did not try that again, because he was not a fool, he was an old and clever man with a real hatred and now real information. He stopped attacking held positions. He went mobile instead, and mobile was the exact thing the dispersed holding could least answer, and Emeric watched him find the seam and could not fault the finding. Aldane raided. He put small fast columns across the frontier at the soft points, a border hamlet, an ore convoy on the Grayrock road, an outlying work at the edge of a town, hit and gone before a ground force could close, and every raid pulled Emeric's hundred wider to cover more, and a force spread to cover everything defended nothing well. The Inkhands knew the country, knew which lane a convoy took and which hamlet lay a day from the nearest guard, and their local knowledge made Aldane's mobility sharper than a stranger's would have been, so that the men Emeric had made small guided the blows against him with the intimacy of people who had lived on that ground their whole lives.

The paired line and the relay gave Emeric warning now, which was the thing the comms had been built for and did the thing they had been built to do. He knew a raid was falling almost as it fell, knew it in Kaldo while it happened at a hamlet a day's ride off, and knowing was not reaching, because a message crossed the holding at the speed of resonance and a man crossed it at the speed of a horse, and the gap between those two speeds was the whole cruelty of the mobile war. He could hear the hamlet burning and he could not get a single armored man there in under a day, and by the day's end the column was gone and the hamlet was ash and the warning had served only to let him grieve in advance. Speed of knowing without speed of reaching was a torment dressed as an advantage.

So he built the reach, and he built it because Declan's shoulder had already told him he needed it and the mobile war was now telling him again, louder, that the barony's whole problem was that its men moved at the pace of feet in a war being fought at the pace of clever columns. The flight suit was the answer that had been in the docket since before the ford, the next rung, and he had held it for a necessity to justify it and the necessity had arrived twice over, once as an extraction he could not make and now as a distance he could not cross, and he stopped holding it and built it.

He built few of them. That was the first honesty of it, that flight was not a thing he could issue to the hundred, that the suits were complex and the crystal was finite and the training was real, so the flight element was a handful of men and not a company, a small sharp capability and not a new standing arm. And he flew the first one himself, and he did it over Ward's flat objection, because he would not send a man to test an unproven suit in a real fight while he stood on the ground and watched, the same rule that had put him behind the line at the ford, the rule that a man who made a thing tested it on his own body before he asked another man to trust his life to it. He had made the first batch of everything himself. He would make the first flight into a fight himself, and if the suit had a lie in it he had not found on the range, he would rather find it in his own fall than in another man's.

The first use was an extraction and a reinforcement both, a raid on an ore convoy that Ward's nearest ground element could reach in half a day and the flight element could reach in the time it took to cross the sky, and Emeric went up into the air over his own land for the first time in a fight and felt the thing he had built work, felt the gravity system take his weight and the thrust answer his hands, and for a few seconds, crossing the holding faster than any man in this world had ever crossed it under his own power, he understood why the old church guarded the sky as something not meant for common men, because it did not feel common, it felt like a thing a person was not supposed to be able to do.

Then the fight, and the cost, and the cost was the one he had written into the design and named to Ward and now paid in the air the way he had paid the dead zone on the ground, in fact and not theory. The thrust came from his hands. That was the mechanism, that a flying man directed his flight through his arms and his hands, the thrust vectored where he pointed, and it meant a flying man's hands were his wings, and a man's hands could not be his wings and his weapons at the same time. The moment he drew a blade one-handed he lost half his control and most of his lift, dropped and slewed, a flying man made suddenly clumsy in the air the instant he tried to be a fighting man in it, and the moment he needed both hands for something other than flight he was not flying at all, he was falling with intention. He had known this. He had cashed it now, in the middle of a raid, feeling his own lift die as he reached for a weapon and having to choose, in the half-second the choice allowed, between striking and staying up, and there was no having both, and the constraint that had been a clean line on a drawing was a man in the sky learning he could fly or fight and not do the two together.

The extraction taught it worse, and taught it about someone other than himself, which was how the real lessons always came. When a guard went down in the raid, Emeric did the thing the whole suit existed to do, dropped, took the man up, pulled him vertically out of the killing space in the seconds a ground extraction would have spent in bleeding minutes, up and over the top of the fight the way he had sworn beside Declan's cot he would learn to do. And carrying the man took both his arms, both his hands, all his thrust committed to the lift and the hold, and so the man doing the rescue was, for the whole length of the rescue, a man who could not fight, could not strike, could not so much as raise a hand to guard himself, a defenseless thing in the air with a wounded man in his arms and every eye below turning up to the easy target he made. He got the man out. He got him out because two other guards on the ground read the moment and put themselves and their fire between him and the men who would have brought him down, covered the flyer who could not cover himself, and the getting-out cost their exposure the way Declan's rescue had cost, the same shape again, that a man saved was a man saved by other men spending their own safety to buy the saving. The flight suit did not remove the cost of extraction. It moved it, from the downed man's long slow bleed to the flyer's naked helplessness and the covering men's exposure, and moving a cost was not erasing it, and he came down from that first flight knowing he had bought reach and paid for it in a new coin, not gotten reach for free.

It changed the war anyway. That was the honest other half, that the cost was real and the capability was also real and both were true at once. A handful of flying men who could cross the holding in minutes turned the mobile war from a torment into a contest, let him answer a raid while it happened instead of grieving it after, let him pull his downed out of the killing space and reinforce a soft point before the column got clear, and Aldane felt it within a week, felt his mobility stop being a free advantage and start being a gamble, because a raiding column that used to be gone before help could come now had to reckon with help coming out of the sky. The seam Aldane had found closed, not all the way, nothing closed all the way in this war, but enough that the raids grew costlier for him and rarer, and the front stopped bleeding at every soft point at once.

And there Emeric stopped climbing, and stopping was the discipline the whole thing turned on, and he felt the pull to keep climbing every day the war went on. He knew how to build the next rung. The heavy suit, the one that partitioned the crushing forces of true speed off a man's body and let him fly at velocities that would pulp an ordinary flyer, sat fully formed in his head, and a squad of those would end Aldane inside a month, would make the whole mobile war a thing of the past, would let him fall on a raiding column at a speed no defense could answer. He wanted it the way a man wants the tool that ends the fight he is tired of fighting. And he did not build it, and denying himself was harder than building anything, because the reason not to build it was not that he could not but that he should not, and should-not was a leash a man had to hold himself. The heavy suit drank crystal. It used a whole second gravity system, heavy per man, and the Grayrock vein that covered a hundred base harnesses and a handful of flight suits would not cover a squad of the heavy ones and the other things that same finite vein was already promised to, and there were no more veins, one deposit and not a world's worth. A squad of heavy suits was his entire strategic reserve of the rarest material he had, spent to win faster against a domestic duke, and the domestic duke was the rehearsal and not the real war, and a man who spent his reserve on the rehearsal had nothing left for the performance. He would beat Aldane at MK2. He would hold the heavy suit for the war that Aldane was only the first shape of, the larger thing climbing toward him one tier up that would need every crystal he had not wasted. He capped the ladder, on purpose, with the means to climb it sitting right there, and left the top rung unbuilt.

Which meant, he understood, standing in the yard as the flight element came down from the raid and the covering guards came in on the ground and the whole costly imperfect answer to the mobile war reassembled itself in front of him, that he could not gear his way to the end of this. He had reached the top of what he would let himself build, and it was enough to contest Aldane and not enough to finish him, and finishing him was going to take the other thing, the thing that was not a weapon, the intelligence he had planted in a merchant's ink a season ago and never yet aimed at the man it was made for. He had climbed the ladder as far as the ladder went. The rest of the war was not going to be won by a better suit. It was going to be won by knowing what Aldane knew, and he had, sitting in a batch of tagged ink already inside the disgruntled network, the means to know it, waiting for exactly the suspicion this war had finally made worth aiming it at.
