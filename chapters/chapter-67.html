Chapter 67 - Off the Books

The road went first, which Emeric had half expected, because the road was the softest large target he owned and Aldane, whatever else he was, was not a fool, and a man denied the lawful road and pushed into the dark reaches, from the dark, for the thing he can actually touch. The crystal came down the graded road out of the deep working in tarped wagons, and a graded road is a made thing, banked and drained and cut into a hillside where the hill did not want a road, and a made thing has a place where it is holding back more than it comfortably can, a cut or a fill or a culvert where the whole engineered patience of it is doing quiet work against the weight of a wet slope, and a man who knows where that place is can undo a season's grading in a night with a barrel of the right sort and a match. They found the barrel before the match. That was the whole of the difference between a disaster and an incident, and the difference had a name, and the name was the thing Emeric could not feel clean about even as it saved him.

The apparatus caught it. Not the wall, not the guard, not any of the honest defenses a barony was supposed to have. The apparatus, the quiet layered thing he had been building underneath the honest defenses for two years, the readers and the watchers and the small keyed devices and the network of ordinary-seeming people who reported ordinary-seeming things up a chain until the ordinary things, laid side by side by Ferra, stopped being ordinary. A contractor new to the district had bought materials a contractor had no honest use for. A carter had taken a load off his usual round to a place off any round. A man had been seen twice at the road cut who had no reason to be seen there once. None of it was proof of anything. All of it, together, was a shape, and the shape said someone means to bring down the road at the cut, and the apparatus said it in time, and men were waiting in the dark above the cut when the barrel came, and took the man who brought it before he could set his match, and Emeric slept that night unbroken while the thing that would have ruined his season sat defused in a shed under guard.

He should have felt only the relief. He examined himself for it in the morning and found the relief there, real, a season saved and a supply protected and an enemy's first physical blow turned aside before it landed, all of it good, all of it exactly what the apparatus was for. And underneath the relief, in the place he had learned not to look away from however much he wanted to, he found the other thing, which was that the apparatus had worked precisely as the thing it was modeled on worked, and the thing it was modeled on was the enemy. He had built a machine that read a district the way a jealous state reads a district, that turned carters and contractors and half the honest population into unwitting reporters, that assembled the small movements of free men into shapes their movers never knew they were making, and it had just saved him, and it would save him again, and every time it saved him it would be a little larger and a little deeper into the lives of people who had done nothing but live near a man who could not afford to be surprised. Aldane had reached for the road. Emeric had caught the reach with a net cast over the whole barony, and the net had held, and he lay in the morning knowing that the net was the price and that he kept paying it in a currency he had told himself, once, he would never spend.

The captured man knew nothing, which Emeric had also expected and which confirmed, in the confirming, everything the survey and the factor and the handler had already told him. They questioned him, properly, without the delving that Emeric held in reserve for a graver day, and it was not needed, because the man had nothing to hold back that was worth holding. He had been hired by a contractor. The contractor had been paid by an agent the man had seen twice and could not name past a face. The agent answered to no one the man had ever met. He did not know the duke's name in connection with any of it and would not have believed it if told, because the whole architecture above him had been built for exactly this, so that the rung a man could catch was a rung that led nowhere, the same principle Emeric had read at the foot of Marrow's ladder, the same principle he used in his own house, insulation, layers, a thing arranged so that catching the hand that acted told you nothing about the hand that willed it. He held the barrel-man and had, in holding him, caught precisely as much of Aldane as the law would have let him catch, which was nothing, a hireling of a hireling of a face, and the duke sat in his own hall that night unmarked and undiminished and free to reach again.

That was the shape of the war he was in now, and the barrel made it plain in a way the paper had not. He could defend, and defend well, better than any lord in the kingdom, because he had built the finest defensive apparatus this world had seen and it worked. He could not strike back, because there was nothing to strike, the enemy having taken the same care Emeric took to leave nothing between the willing and the acting that a blow could land on. He would catch every barrel. He would catch them because the net was good and would get better, and he would spend, catching them, a little more of the barony's freedom each time, a little more of the thing that separated what he was building from what he was fighting, and the enemy would keep reaching, at no cost to himself, from a hall Emeric could not touch, secure in the knowledge that a besieging avenger who loses nothing when his blows are caught can afford to throw them forever. The barrel had cost Aldane a hireling and a contractor and a face. It had cost Emeric a night's watch and a shed and one more turn of the screw on the machine he did not like owning. The exchange was in his favor, this once, on the count that mattered least, which was the barrel, and against him on the count that mattered most, which was the slow steady darkening of the thing he was becoming in order to keep winning.

He gave the order to widen the watch on the road, because there was nothing else sane to do, and the apparatus grew by exactly the amount required and not one man less, and Ferra logged it and Ward posted it and the barony was that much more watched by morning, and safer, and Emeric stood at the road cut in the afternoon looking at the place where the season would have ended and felt the safety and the cost sitting in him side by side, refusing, as they always refused, to cancel out. His tools worked. He slept well. The part of him that kept the accounts, the future-brain that had spent its last living thoughts cataloguing everyone else's negligence, sat up in the dark long after the rest of him had gone quiet, adding the road to a column it had been keeping for two years, the column of small necessary betrayals of the thing he had meant to build, and finding the column longer than he wanted, and getting no answer from anyone, because there was no one keeping it but him, and no one he had told it to, and no bottom to it yet in sight.
