Book 3, Batch 3: Struck (ch 19-28)

November yr18 to the last day of February yr19. Synopsis v1, awaiting your approval, not drafted. The batch has one spine: the note goes live and the review rules, and every lever the king would reach for in a squeeze already belongs to somebody else.



Ch 19. The Law of the Note

On Wednesday 17 November the Assembly passes the Issue Act. From Monday 10 January yr19 the crown note is good for every tax and public fee. For 120 days old contracts written in coin stay in coin, and on day 120 (Monday 8 May) old debts convert at par, as long as the redemption guarantee holds. Vantic's House prints the notes under its charter, and the Issue Board takes over printing when the charter renews. Tullio sits in the gallery for the vote and doesn't take a single note, and Ferra says that's worse than if he'd spoken.

On South Sentinel, Tess remaps the Bulwark's grip into separate fingers, and Wren curls one finger and only that finger moves. The core is still not certified. On the March the first frost comes down and the young stop growing: everything they could eat is behind iron or a fence. A Saltreach chandlers' guild applies to be the first sealing house the crown doesn't own.



Ch 20. Whatever's on the Forms

On Sunday 5 December Fulk Garside visits his sister Mabel at her mill in Cinderhale. He brings a cast-iron flour scale he couldn't have afforded two years ago. He glances at the door when the mill whistle goes, jokes that foremen are expendable when they ask questions, and when she asks what Wystan's works is making he says too quickly, "Whatever's on the forms." She doesn't ask again. After he leaves she works out on her slate how much coin she'll need to hold back when the note comes.

Merryn Pellow shoots one of the two lost pups in the salt marsh. Kesrath gives written leave to read the tideshell's core, but not to sell it. Tess reads it at the Claim with Oskel and Alina watching: born, under the Isle, the word she still can't read, and a new sign that says it became a tideshell because of the shore it grew on. It's a swimming core, the Undertow's kind, so it isn't Ward's. Ward says then he'll wait for a Rampart.



Ch 21. The Long Table

Saturday 25 December is the first Long Table at home since the voyage. Zale and Ilenne come up from Aumere, so the first Long Table's guest is back at the same table. Waylon and Alina come from the Isle, and Runa is at her first, overwhelmed by a present. There are gifts all round, and Wren sings Alina's cradle song, and this time Alina stays in the room. Emeric tries to steal both his wives off to the stair before the pudding. Ferra says the pudding first, Imani says the pudding and the toast, he says that's two things, and Ferra says she can count. Harrold toasts whoever's sitting in the review's room next month, and the table drinks without asking what he means.



Ch 22. Day One

On Monday 3 January the first clinic bands go on the four flecked crewmen. Then, on Monday 10 January, the note goes live. At the League's hall in Cinderhale, Maudie's chest takes notes for real dues and nothing else. She refuses a man trying to pay a year ahead with notes he bought cheap and a stranger who wants coin, and a family buys a loaf at the posted price with paper.

At Pryor's Gate the changers take the note at seven parts in eight. Sayer's weigh-house at Fairhythe holds grain settlements, and the Halverne harbour duke takes the note at face in the first hour. Tullio files suit in Keller's court that conversion takes a creditor's contracted right, and the king has it printed whole. The next day Tullio calls 1,900 harvest notes in coin on a thirty-day demand, about 7,400 Gold Rand, all of it lawful. Ferra tells Emeric: "One town. Don't build a speech on it."



Ch 23. Part One

On Friday 28 January the Isle Works Review reads part one in full. It recommends striking the emergency exception from the crown's prerogative, and it puts the crown's share of the Isle works in trust for ten years for the families of the fourteen and the Islanders. The awards are paid from the crown's own purse besides. The trustees are Mother Sabbe, two members of the families' bench (Dacey one of them) and Keller's clerk.

The king says once in the room why he dislikes it: four armed houses are testing the Act, the Ostrel letter warned of more than one room, and Kaldo loses its own oil for a decade. He keeps every line and signs the trust deed that afternoon. That night he says the thing he couldn't say in the room, that the Quiet's clock won't wait for a committee. Imani answers that a day late is better than a day wrong, and he lies awake longer than they do. On Tuesday he reads the recommendation to the Assembly word for word and argues neither way. From that day Ferra buys Kaldo's own oil back from the trust at Duvrenne's price.



Ch 24. The Rolls

Pelham's agents demand coin in Cinderhale for dues people have already paid, and keep demanding after Maudie's written complaint. Maudie gathers her thirty-one tenants. On the night of Thursday 3 February the Hearth burns Pelham's working collection rolls in the courtroom hearth and leaves the court's register under a cloth. Gethin asks who chose the pages, and this time Corrick can't say the town. Maudie is furious at having to rebuild her evidence kitchen by kitchen, a neighbour is relieved, and they're both right.

The next day Keller's court upholds conversion at par, narrowly, and rules that a creditor can still get relief if redemption fails. Vantic reads that part twice.



Ch 25. Friday

Tullio's calls fall due on Thursday 10 February and coin goes short across the western plains. Tom Ashlock delivers a load of cheese under an invoice payable in coin. On Monday the shipper offers him notes, and Tom can't pay his two hands with paper on Friday. The crews' man brings a standstill bill, but it will take nine days and can only stop new calls. Vantic opens her House's thirty-day window on Monday 14 February instead. It buys verified claims once each, pays Tom's hands and his supplier in coin on the Tuesday, and puts her glass tower at risk.

The king had argued against the window. He tells her he was wrong about the week and right about the danger. Mabel defends her cautious acceptance of notes to a merchant who has his own good reason not to follow her. On Friday Tom pays his hands in coin and doesn't know whose it is.



Ch 26. Four Chairs

The March's reform committee, Agatha Vell among them, petitions the Assembly for guaranteed seats and a referendum. The Assembly's majority offers four elected seats and a rights hearing without waiting for the state map, because Pryor's guards hold the only road to Ostrel. In his hall Pryor refuses to let the March vote: "You will not trade three hundred years of protection for four chairs." Agatha answers: "They were never your years to trade." In Cinderhale, Corrick pins the March's offer beside the minute that deferred Maudie's petition and writes nothing under either. A carter reads both twice and says, to nobody, "so that's what it takes."



Ch 27. The Hour

On Friday 25 February the circuit holds its first sitting at Cinderhale, at the table where the rolls burned, and Maudie wins her first order. The next day the king comes to the League's hall with Ferra, and there's no dais. Gethin won't come in. The king sees the new mana channels in Gethin's hand and asks who rebuilt it, and gets your lines word for word.

Corrick lays out the documents: "You chose Wayford." The king answers yes, and that he was wrong about what it would cost. Corrick asks for a seat for Cinderhale by royal order and is refused. He asks for representation by a general law instead, and the king admits the state map has slipped twice. Corrick asks what keeps the next king from taking it all back: "Not enough. Not yet." Outside on the step, Ferra tells him to say what he means to do with the crown. He won't. "Then he has nothing to hold you to." "I know."



Ch 28. Struck

On Monday 28 February the Assembly debates the exception for a day. A member from the Tarrow argues to keep it, because Sayer's porters stand on his town's quay every morning, and the chamber treats it as the honest fear it is. The crews' man answers that if they keep it for the houses they keep it for the next king. The mill lord, for once voting with the majority, says he signed the finding and won't vote against his own signature. The exception is struck by name, and from now on the next emergency is the Assembly's to authorise. The king signs the crown's notice and leaves by the members' door. On the steps Ferra says, "That's one." He says yes.



What I closed

Sixteen open items and conflicts were ruled in this batch, listed in section 6 of the md. The main ones:

1. The Issue Act's vote was never placed anywhere, so I put it on 17 November.

2. The "standstill bill (9 days)" was never explained. It now stops new coin calls on harvest notes but can't touch calls already served, which is why Vantic's window still matters.

3. Nothing said whether yr19 has a 29 February. Since every weekday so far matches Earth's calendar, it does, so day 120 is Monday 8 May.

4. Ward's Bastion needs a Rampart's core, not the tideshell's, so I fixed Batch 2 (now v1.2).

5. The other main rulings: Duvrenne's leave to read the core, but not sell it; where the young spend the winter; the first private sealing house; and who makes the March's offer and why.

6. Where the hour happens, and whether the king argues the recommendation. He doesn't; he reads it word for word.
