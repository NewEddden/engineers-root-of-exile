Book 2, Batch 11: Grounded

Chapters 84–87 | Thursday 15 October – Thursday 22 October, Year 17

The desert stop is over. The Deep Lift is held by a Sabir rota, Hadra is serving her sentence on it, the relief pair has turned for home, and the fleet is flying down the Sun River toward the sea with Gisela and her slab aboard and Runa newly speaking Kaldo's tongue. To heal the scalds he took walking into Hadra's water, Emeric spent two days hanging off a relay drum five miles up in the Band, the densest air on that side of the world. The fleet called it a weekend.

Every dragon within a long flight felt it: a man with the old face drinking from the Band for two days. One dragon, Corvan, took it as a summons. The desert drums begin to die one at a time, each last signal reading as a grab, and the deaths walk down the river toward the fleet. Sohra told her house two things: the old face stops, and there is a gun you cannot feel. So Corvan does not go near a hull. He waits until Kaldo's people are a day up an orc valley with a cart of pumpkins and nothing in their hands but knives, because orc law says that is all a guest may carry, and he goes through them to make Emeric come.

Emeric comes, and does something deliberate and humbling to a dragon to keep him on the ground. It works. It also troubles a farmer, a young winged courier, and Imani, who says so to his face. Then the matriarch who set her house's seal on the old promise comes down under a truce sign, pays the orcs for the harm on their land, pays Kaldo nothing, and tells its king to come to her house before his ships leave these waters.

Chapter 84: Last Gasp

On Thursday 15 October the fleet comes down the Sun River, and by Friday noon it is at the river mouth, where Zale's ship and the K Tam have waited. Ferra writes one line of reasons for the week there: rejoin the surface pair, fit Tess's gravity anchors to Zale's ship, buy food, and fill the seed hand's crates before the cliffs. The anchors' crates come up out of the K Endeavor's hold and onto Zale's deck, where Dagny and Bram's crew start fitting them and Zale wants to know what every dial does. At the orc treaty-house, Kettrin brings the mail, including word from Brega's family: the door families will choose who comes to see their dead on the Isle after the king is home and the protective zone has held a winter. The market sells grain at the October harvest. The house's guano factor sells Kaldo a forward lot of about sixty tonnes of Guano Isles guano, already sacked on the landing wharf there, to be lifted as the fleet passes, Kaldo's first guano contract, for Milford's fields and the powder master's beds, half paid in bar iron now. Emeric asks aloud who digs it and from which cliffs, because he has read of islands dug bare on his other world. The factor says hired crews under the Nestwardens' concession, which is true on paper, and Ferra writes the clause Emeric asks for: from ledges open under the Nestwardens' law. Varn, the seed hand, arranges to fetch ripe pumpkins from a valley farm under the house's escort writ. Grau goes ashore for news of Coldspring. After Friday's dusk, Runa will not go on deck. She says the air smells of burnt stone, and nobody else can smell anything.

On Friday night, about two in the morning, the drum at the court's anchorage on the river misses its beat, and its neighbour, the gorge drum, logs the anchorage drum's last gasp: the pin load jumped hard while the shell stayed intact. In Ferra's reading of how drums die, that is a grab, not lightning, a fade or a hull's shell. She logs it at the night set while the watch's night glass sweeps a river mouth where nothing moves, four hundred miles from the anchorage, and lets Emeric sleep.

On Saturday the seed party leaves at first light with Ward's leave, since one dead drum four hundred miles inland is no threat to the river mouth. Emeric spends a quiet hour with Ferra and Imani on the K Discovery's deck talking about nothing. On Saturday night the gorge drum dies the same way.

Before dawn on Sunday Ferra plots the two deaths on the chart in the K Endeavor's chart room: a line walking down the river, and the next drum along the chain is the one over the fleet. Armand points out that a drum can be felt, not heard; a dragon feels the pin and lift stones at range, and a man drawing on the Band beside a drum for two days is louder than any drum. Emeric works it out before Armand finishes the sentence, and does not say it aloud. Ferra watches him not say it. She says one thing aloud herself: Hila's gift set now rings nothing, because the dead anchorage drum was the court's line to the fleet, four days after Emeric told Hadra to use it. Ward lays down his rules: the 20 mm guns and the dragon rifles to the rails on every hull, Arthur on the K Endeavor's; no climbs and no flights; every hull on the water under the river-mouth drum; everybody inside the boundary stones by noon. The seed party is a day up the valley under the house's escort, and gets the recall by field set.

Chapter 85: Where the Rifles Aren't

The valley farm, about twenty-five miles up the river road on the house's land and off its neutral ground, is an orc family's terraced plots on the valley side, pumpkins in rows between the bellfruit. The seed party is the seed hand, Bren and Mira as the party's knights, a K Amara knight, and a K Endeavor deckhand driving the cart, all in field dress with knives, since nothing else may pass the stones; the suits stay aboard with the rifles. Two orc wardens of the house escort them. Varn wants ripe fruit at harvest rather than a sack of seed, so Milford can pick its own seed from the best and know the strain. On Saturday afternoon he and the farm's matron argue for an hour over which fruit to cut for seed, each sure the other is wrong, and Mira enjoys it more than anything in a month. The party sleeps in the drying barn. At dawn on Sunday the recall comes on the field set, and the cart is loaded and on the road by first light, the matron and the seed hand still arguing over one last fruit.

Mid-morning at the farm gate, Corvan walks out of the pumpkin rows in his humanoid form, gold-eyed, and asks which of them is the king's. He is about two thousand and fifty years old, Sohra's mother's brother, not his house's heir, and he did not wait for his house to decide what to do about the old face. He felt the river relay from his own sky in the first week of October and came, taking the drums because dragon law allows it and because they led him to the fleet. He has heard the old face stops, so he means to give it a reason not to. He has also heard of the gun nobody can feel, so he will not go near a hull; a Kaldo party a day up a valley carrying only knives is the one place the gun is not. He does not touch the orcs, the wardens or the farm. He has no quarrel with them, and he wants them to see that.

The two wardens plant themselves in front of the farm's people, as the law asks of them, which is no help to anyone else. Corvan walks round them. The K Amara knight is thrown across the yard into the barn wall, concussed. The deckhand's arm is broken when he swings the cart whip. Bren puts himself between Corvan and the seed hand, and Corvan throws him into the terrace wall: ribs broken, left forearm broken, and then his breathing goes wrong. Mira gets the field set open. Corvan lets her. He wants the call made.

Emeric comes off the K Discovery's deck by air, suitless, his eyes gold before he is over the valley, and the K Discovery lifts behind him with Imani aboard to follow the river up. He warns once from the yard's edge: let him go. Corvan answers by putting his foot on Bren's chest, then flares into his partial form, wings and horns, to meet Emeric in the air, where a dragon is strongest. Emeric uses what will become known as the flash step. It is not a new magic. His legs go to scale for one stride, bursting his boots, and the pull aims him at a point just past his target, so the stride becomes a fall sideways. The first step takes Corvan off Bren. The second puts Emeric behind him. He cannot chain a third, and he is winded afterward.

In the partial form, a dragon's wings root at the shoulders. Emeric takes Corvan's right arm out of its socket, then the left, knowing from a boxing poster and the square where the joint gives. With both out, Corvan cannot fly, and cannot change form without tearing himself: grounded, and stuck in the partial form. The choice is plain. Emeric could kill him and does not. He does not do the gentle thing either, the pull alone, which might not hold a body as dense as a dragon's. Then he puts Corvan's face in the dirt between the pumpkin rows, his knee in the dragon's back, and quietly tells him three things: whose arm that was, whose people these are, and that the next one comes back without the arms.

Then he leaves the dragon there for Bren. Bren's breathing is a tension pneumothorax from the broken ribs, a lung leak trapped under pressure that kills within minutes. Emeric takes the kit's needle, decompresses Bren's chest between the second and third ribs, and holds him sitting up until the K Discovery comes down on the river below the farm with Imani. Corvan lies in the rows with the two wardens standing over him, planted. The seed hand watched all of it from behind the cart and will not look at Emeric on the way back. He is a Milford man who grows things, and nobody makes him explain.

Chapter 86: Something Less Humiliating

In the farmyard Imani sets her order: Bren first, then Corvan, then the deckhand's arm and the knight's head. A patient is a patient. She takes over Bren's needle and begins mending the ribs and the leak. Then she turns to Corvan, because one of his hands has gone cold, which can happen when a dislocated shoulder presses on the artery beneath it. She checks pulse and feeling in both hands, the cold one first, and reduces both joints by traction and countertraction, with Emeric doing the pulling on her count, since nobody else there can move a dragon's arm against a dragon's muscle, and Mira and a warden bracing the body. Both joints go back. She binds both arms to his body and straps his wings flat to his back with the K Discovery's canvas. Her terms to Corvan: no change of form and no flight until the joints hold, for weeks, or he tears what is holding them. She offers to mend the joints with mana. He refuses to let a human put anything in him. She writes the weeks down for whoever comes for him.

Then, in the yard, with the farm watching, she tells Emeric to his face that something less humiliating may have been available. He does not argue. He does not settle it either. She means it as a doctor and as his wife, and neither the reader nor Emeric can separate the two. At the river mouth Ward meets the K Discovery and says nothing, which from Ward is approval. Imani sees it and is angrier at that than at anything Emeric did.

The attack happened on the house's land under the house's writ, so Corvan is not Kaldo's prisoner. On Sunday evening the first warden takes him into the door's custody, under the house's roof on neutral ground, and the house's healers keep him. Kaldo keeps no prisoner. Emeric writes his account for the house that night. Bren lies in Imani's ward on the K Endeavor, his ribs and lung mending and his forearm splinted, off duty for three or four weeks. Mira sits beside him, and they argue about whether he was stupid. He was. That night Imani writes her objection in full in her diary. Emeric comes to bed late. She does not take it back, and he does not ask her to. Ferra's hand rests on his back and stays there. On the K Endeavor's rail, Mochi leans toward the shore and the house and will not settle until Runa takes it below. Nobody reads what it means.

On Monday Kettrin enters two lines in the house's roll with equal weight: a dragon struck the house's guests on the house's land without cause, and the guests' king took a dragon's arms out on the house's land and laid him in the dirt. Ferra reads it and does not ask for a word to be changed. The house splits three ways. The farm's matron wants Kaldo gone before the dragon's kin come. One warden says the house's writ failed and Kaldo paid for it with a knight's breath. The first warden says the door exists for exactly this, and opens it.

Emeric goes to Corvan's cell, a warden present, because he wants to know why. Corvan tells him without being asked twice. The drum: Emeric hung on it for two days like a tick, drinking, and every dragon within a long flight felt it. The gun: Sohra told them of a gun nobody can feel, so he came where Kaldo's people had nothing in their hands. And his father. Corvan's memory is sensory and broken. He was small. The ground went quiet under the nests. His father fell out of the sky. On the hill stood a man with Emeric's face. Corvan knows no more than that, and does not need to. Emeric says it was not him. "You have his face. That's enough for me." Resemblance is not guilt, and the scene does not argue it. Emeric has nothing to say that would change it. Corvan does not mention his house's matriarch's age, that she knew the face as family, Sohra's mother, or the succession, and he would not to a human.

Chapter 87: Under the Truce Sign

At first light on Tuesday 20 October, the wardens find a single shed scale laid on the stone ring outside the door, where the werewolves' moot sat in July. Nobody saw it put there. Every people past the wall reads it the same way: a dragon asks to speak, and has given something first, since shed scale is only ever given, never taken. Saldis walks down from the headland in her humanoid form, visibly old, never helpless, and the wardens step back without being told. Two adults of her house wait in full form on the headland behind her, to carry. Sohra stays in the air over the bay and does not land, the stump of her horn plain to anyone with a glass. Arthur, at the K Endeavor's rail with the 20 mm covered on Ward's order, has one. Following Ward's rule of the fewest people near a dragon's house, only Emeric, Ward and Ferra go down to the ring, with the first warden and Kettrin with the roll. Imani stays with Bren and sends her written terms for Corvan's arms by a warden.

Saldis says one thing at a time. She has come for her son, not for Emeric. She put her house's seal to the promise his ships broke, and that is all she says about the Leaving Terms. Ferra asks to read them, and Saldis says the text is not hers to give. To the house, not to Kaldo, she pays for the harm on its land in scale, given and set on the ring for the house's roll, the best light armour there is and lawful only when given. To Kaldo she pays nothing, and says why: his people's blood was the price of his breach. Kaldo asks nothing, and Ferra enters it as she said it. Then the summons: before his ships leave these waters, Kaldo's king is to come to her house. Where it is, she tells the first warden, not him.

Emeric makes a promise there, the first conduct before any terms. Until he comes to her house, the fleet will not climb to the Band, will launch no drum, and will lay nothing new in it. The racks are full, and the thick air at sea level fills them slowly. He cannot pull down the drums already up, because they are the fleet's only voice home. Saldis says, "Then they're yours to lose." She does not accept the promise. She notes it.

The wardens bring Corvan out on his feet, arms bound and wings strapped. Sohra comes down for the first time, lands at the ring's edge and puts her hand to her uncle's face, and the relief on her is the plainest thing anyone sees all morning. Then she is back in the air without once looking at the ships. Corvan does not look at her. He looks at Emeric: "If you come to her house, I'll be there." The two adults take him up between them in their forefeet, the way dragons carry young, and fly low over the water to the north-east with Sohra above. In one family there are three stances: Corvan resentful, Sohra relieved, and Saldis afraid. Only Ward reads the fear, an old soldier seeing an old soldier who knows the next war is hers to stop. Nobody says the word.

At home in Cinderhale, Kezia's crew of the Hearth has watched the mill lords' watch house on the mill road for nine nights from the lane, learning the hours of the mill's riot bell, which is what the watch is paid to answer. On Tuesday night the house guard does not leave his post, and Kezia calls off the first attempt. Gethin swears at her the whole way back to the old fire-stone workings, and she lets him. The next night they start counting again.

On Wednesday Harrold replies to Ferra's one-line cipher about Corvan. The relief pair landed in Kaldo's bay on Saturday, the burned are in hospital, and Tess has the turbine page and the memory-metal drawings. Then: "Is the knight alive?" Ferra answers yes, and nothing else, since secrecy rules keep names off the line. Ferra sends the water court and Hila a letter by the house's post explaining that the line to the court is down, why, and that Kaldo will put it back when it can do so lawfully. Emeric signs it. It is the first promise of the voyage he has had to break within a week. The anchors are tested on Zale's hull at the moorings. Bren is moved to a bunk with a window. At Thursday's dusk Runa, at the rail, catches Bracken's pack on the wind, high in the hills, driven up after the deer by the dragon's smell. She knows where they are. She does not ask to go, and Grau does not offer.

On Wednesday night, on a ledge on the headland, a young winged courier named Tiel writes a letter. He is grey-brown, his flight feathers not yet full, set by the Guano Isles cliffs' elders to watch the fleet since it came down the river. From the valley's ridge on Sunday he saw a dragon walk into people with nothing in their hands and break them. Then he saw a gold-eyed man come out of the sky, the very thing every song of the falling sky is about, do to a dragon what the old princes did, and talk to him in the dirt. He writes the letter twice and tears up the first, because it held only one account. The second holds both: the dragon began it, and the people had knives; and the gold-eyed man frightened him more than the dragon did, and he does not know if that is fair. He does not decide. Before dawn on Thursday he flies north-east, ahead of the fleet, with a letter that tells the cliffs two true things about the king who is coming and cannot say which matters more.

Where the Story Stands After Chapter 87

Emeric rang the bell himself. Healing for two days on a relay drum in the Band drew Corvan to the fleet, and the dragon destroyed two desert drums, cutting the line to the water court and leaving Hila's gift set silent. Corvan then struck where the rifles were not, on a seed party carrying only knives, and Bren took the worst of it. Emeric answered by dislocating both of Corvan's shoulders with a new flash step and talking to him with his face in the dirt, deliberately choosing neither killing nor gentleness. Imani objected to his face that something less humiliating may have been available, and that objection now stands unanswered in her diary. Corvan believes a man with Emeric's face stood on the hill when his father fell from the sky, and resemblance is enough for him.

The matriarch Saldis came under a truce sign, paid the orcs in scale for the harm on their land, paid Kaldo nothing because its breach of the promise she sealed cost its people their blood, and summoned Kaldo's king to her house before the fleet leaves these waters. Emeric has promised that until then the fleet will not climb to the Band or launch or lay anything new in it, so the next stop must run on full racks and the slow fill of the thick air. Corvan has gone home bound and grounded for weeks, vowing to be there when Emeric comes. Sohra is relieved, and Saldis is afraid.

Bren will be off duty until about mid-November, with Mira beside him. Tiel's letter, holding both accounts, is flying ahead of the fleet to the Guano Isles, on top of Sefa's report of the festival, and the winged peoples will meet the fleet having seen the worst thing it could show them. The fleet carries ripe pumpkins for Hollow Night, a contract for sixty tonnes of guano with a clause about lawful ledges, seven unlaunched drums, and about 102 tonnes of food. At home, the Hearth's first attempt on the Cinderhale watch house was called off, and they are counting again.

This short batch makes Emeric pay for being careless with his power. He saves his people and spares the dragon, but the method he chose, and the summons he has accepted, now travel ahead of him.
