Chapter 80 - What the Range Could Not Teach

The first fight came at a nameless ford on the Grayrock road, where Aldane's advance guard tried to cross onto Emeric's land and Ward's detachment was waiting to say they could not, and it went, in the first four minutes, exactly the way the range had promised it would not.

Emeric was there, in the base harness, close behind the line where a commander could see and be reached and not be the first man a blade found, and he had told himself he was there to read the enemy and to steady his own, and both of those were true, and the truer thing underneath was that he could not send half-trained men into the first real test of everything he had built and sit safe in a hall while they paid for his arithmetic. He had made the weapons. He would watch what they did.

Aldane's force came in two kinds and Emeric read the two kinds in the first seconds, because reading systems was the thing his mind did whether he asked it or not, even here, even with his own pulse loud in his ears. The bulk were the Inkhands and the levy around them, men with grievance and reach and no training, dangerous the way a crowd is dangerous and not the way a soldier is. And threaded through them, the spine of the thing, were Aldane's own knights, a score of them in capital-forged armor, and their armor told Emeric everything about how the old world made war. It was offense armor. Every piece of it spent its whole magical budget on the strike, on making a blow land harder and faster than a man could throw it unaided, and it carried that budget in a single stone per suit, one stone, unmonitored, feeding the whole harness. It made each knight a hammer. It also made each knight a hammer with one point of failure, one stone that if it failed or emptied or was caught in the wrong place left the man inside suddenly ordinary, and Emeric filed that the way he filed everything, in the middle of the fight, without meaning to, a weakness banked for a later day he did not yet have the reach to spend it on.

His own men held the ford well at first, and that was the doctrine working, the base harness doing the job he had built it to do. It spent no budget on offense. It spent all of it on keeping a man alive, the good steel floor that held when every rune failed, the small distributed weight-offset so a man in full plate moved like a man in none, and the reflex-assist that read a coming blow off the ambient mana and sharpened a man's own dodge a fraction past what fear would let him manage. His men took blows from Aldane's hammers that should have broken them and did not break, and they slipped strikes they should have been too slow to slip, and for a minute and more the ford was his, and he let himself feel, for that minute, that the theory held.

Then the theory met the frightened man and the fouled action and the thing the range could not teach, and it met all three in the space of a single exchange around one guard named Osric, and Emeric watched the whole chain of it happen and could not reach any link in time.

Osric's gun jammed. That was the first link, and it was the plainest, and it was the one Emeric had named to Ward three days before and had still not been able to prevent. Osric had cleared a jam a hundred times on the range, calm, in his own time, the motion clean in his hands, and on the range there had never been a knight bearing down on him while he did it, and now there was, and his hands did the range motion and the range motion was built for a range and not for this, and the action stayed fouled, and Osric had a dead weight of iron in his hands where a weapon should have been and a hammer closing on him. He did the right next thing, he reached to drop the gun and draw his blade, and reaching to drop the gun took the half-second the fight did not give him, and in that half-second the second link fell.

The second link was the dead zone, and it was Emeric's own weapon that made it, and the cruelty of that would sit with him a long time. A guard down the line, meaning well, doing exactly what he had been drilled to do, threw a taser-grenade into a knot of Inkhands to put them down without killing them, because Emeric had ordered the Inkhands taken alive where they could be, because Emeric could not stomach slaughtering the men he had made small by making things well, and the humane order had a cost the humane order's giver had accounted for in theory and now watched paid in fact. The grenade dumped its charge through its rune the way it was built to, and dumping the charge emptied the ambient mana at the point of discharge, a hole in the world a few paces wide where for a handful of seconds there was no mana for any rune to draw, and the crystal that had thrown the charge sat inert in that dead space, and everything else in that dead space that lived on ambient mana went quiet with it. Including the reflex-assist in Osric's harness. Osric, already fouled, already reaching, lost in the same instant the one thing that might have saved a fouled man, the small sharpening of his own dodge that the ambient mana fed, because the ambient mana was gone, drunk by his own side's mercy, and the hammer came down into a man whose armor had just, through no fault of his and every fault of the design, stopped helping him.

Declan saw it. That was the third link and the last one and the one that turned a bad exchange into a wound the barony would carry for weeks. Declan was beside Osric and Declan saw the hammer fall on a man who had gone suddenly slow, and Declan did the thing that no drill teaches and no design accounts for, the thing men do, and put himself into the blow that was meant for another man. He took it on the shoulder and the side where Osric's head would have been, and his own harness held the steel floor of it so it did not kill him, and the force of a capital hammer with its whole budget behind it went through even good steel as a broken thing, and Declan went down hard and did not get up, and the ford, for one terrible moment, had a hole in it where two guards had been fighting and now one lay in the mud and the other stood over him with a dead gun in his hands.

Ward closed the hole. That was Ward's whole art, closing holes, and he closed this one, drove the knights back off the two downed men with the guards nearest, and the line reformed and the ford held and the fight, in the accounting that mattered least, was won. Aldane's advance guard did not cross. They fell back with their own losses and left the ford to Emeric, and by every ledger of ground and objective it was a victory, and Emeric stood in it and felt no victory at all, because the ledger of ground was not the ledger he read. He read the other one. Declan down and not getting up. Osric standing white-faced over him with a weapon that had failed him. A dead zone still faintly guttering in the trampled grass where his own mercy had disarmed his own man. A won ford that had cost him a guard for weeks and had cost that guard something that might not come all the way back, and had cost all of it not to bad luck but to design, to costs he had named aloud three days before and had fielded the weapons knowing, and knowing had not spared a single link in the chain.

They carried Declan back through the Grayrock gate on a stretcher that evening, and Emeric walked beside it the whole way, which changed nothing for Declan and was the only thing he could do, and the promise the arc had been making since the census walked in through his own gate exactly as promised, the cost of every good thing he had built arriving on a litter with a face he knew. Imani took Declan and told him the truth, which was that the shoulder was broken in a way that would knit and the man inside it would be weeks from a line and months from certain of the arm, and that he would likely be whole, likely, in time, and that likely was the best any physician gave and she would not pretend to better. Weeks sidelined. A hinge of a man, laid up, because a fight had gone the way first fights go.

And it was a hinge in the other sense too, the sense Emeric could not stop his mind from working even standing beside a broken man, because the fight had shown him precisely where the ladder had to climb next and precisely why he had held it until a fight showed him. The base harness worked. It kept men alive who should have died, and that was proven now in fact and not theory, and it was not enough, and the ford had shown him the exact not-enough. His men could hold. They could not finish, not fast, not without grinding, and grinding was bodies. And when a man went down in the middle of a held line, there was no way to get him out except through the fight, slow, on foot, under the hammers, the very thing that had nearly cost a second guard when Declan fell, because getting a downed man clear of a contested ford on the ground meant more men in the killing space for longer. He needed reach the ground could not give him. He needed to be able to pull a broken man up and out of a fight vertically, over the top of it, in seconds instead of the long bleeding minutes a ground extraction cost. He needed the flight suit, the next rung, the one that had been waiting in the docket for exactly the necessity a real fight would expose, and the real fight had exposed it, had written it in Declan's shoulder, and Emeric hated that this was how he learned it and did not pretend there had been another way to learn it that the war would have allowed.

He went to Declan before he went to the drawings, because the drawings would keep and the man would remember who came first. Declan was grey with Imani's draught and half under it, and he looked up at Emeric and said the thing that guards always said, that he was sorry to be off the line, as though the man who had taken a hammer meant for another owed anyone an apology, and Emeric told him the only true thing he had, which was that the weapons had failed and the design had failed and the order had failed and not one of those failures was Declan's, that Declan had done the one thing in the whole chain that no one could fault, and that the failures were his own, Emeric's, named in advance and fielded anyway, and that he would fix the ones that could be fixed and carry the ones that could not. Declan, half under, said something that might have been that he would do it again, and slept.

Emeric stood in the sick-house a while longer, in the quiet after, and let himself feel the full weight of the thing he had known abstractly since the scale-up and now knew in his hands, that adversarial war was not a machine he could perfect on a range and deploy clean, that every rung of the ladder he climbed would be climbed on the back of a fight that had gone wrong, that the flight suit he would now build was going to be paid for by Declan's shoulder the way the next rung after it would be paid for by some cost he could not yet see, and that this was the true price of the whole enterprise laid bare, not the coin and not the labor but this, a man on a cot who had blocked a blow because Emeric's mercy had disarmed his friend. He had built a barony worth being proud of and an army sized to the land and weapons range-proven and honestly fielded, and the war had taken all of it and shown him, in four minutes at a nameless ford, that being right about every system did not spare the men inside them, and that the ledger he answered to was not the one that counted fords.

Then he went to build the flight suit, because Declan was down and the next fight was coming and grief that did not become work was only grief, and a man who could pull the next Declan up and out of the killing space in seconds was a man worth becoming before the next ford.
